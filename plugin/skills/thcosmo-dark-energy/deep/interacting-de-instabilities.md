## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->
<!-- num: not a value -->

- Valiviita, Majerotto & Maartens (arXiv:0804.0232, Valiviita et al. 2008, v3) label dark energy x and cold dark matter c, write the transfer four-vector $Q^{\mu}_{c}=-Q^{\mu}_{x}$ [src: Sect. III], use conformal time with $\mathcal{H}$, and give the dark-sector equations "in longitudinal (Newtonian) gauge" (B = E = 0), with φ in the lapse and ψ in the spatial metric [src: Sect. III, "Dark sector perturbations", text before Eq. 47]. Their model Eq. 37 has background $Q=\Gamma\rho_{c}\,.$ (arXiv:0804.0232) [src: Eq. 38], and "The case" Γ > 0 "corresponds in the dark matter frame to the decay of dark matter into dark energy" [src: Sect. III, item (3)].
- Gavela et al. (arXiv:0901.1611, Gavela et al. 2009, v1) work "in the synchronous gauge" "comoving with dark matter", dots being conformal-time derivatives [src: Sect. 2], with $\dot{\rho}_{dm}+3\mathcal{H}\rho_{dm}=Q\,,$ [src: Eq. 3] and $\dot{\rho}_{de}+3\mathcal{H}\rho_{de}(1+w)=-Q\,,$ (arXiv:0901.1611) [src: Eq. 4], so Q > 0 feeds dark matter, as in Wang et al. and opposite to Li et al. (`formalism.md#interacting-dark-energy`). In their second-order equations the prime is $\partial/\partial a$ [src: Sect. 2.2, footnote 8].
- Clemson et al. (arXiv:1109.6234, Clemson et al. 2012, v3) take $Q_{x}=\Gamma\rho_{x}=\Gamma\bar{\rho}_{x}(1+\delta_{x})=-Q_{c},$ (arXiv:1109.6234) [src: Eq. 26], "treating" Γ "as a universal constant" [src: Sect. III, "Our IDE models"].
- Li, Zhang & Zhang (arXiv:1404.5220, Li et al. 2014, v4) work in the comoving gauge "defined by" $B=v_{T}$ and $H_{T}=0$, T denoting "total matters except dark energy", with $\zeta\equiv H_{L}$, $\xi\equiv A$, $V\equiv v$ in that gauge [src: text after Eq. 10]; their test model is $Q^{\mu}_{c}=-Q^{\mu}_{de}=-3\beta H\rho_{c}u^{\mu}_{c},$ (arXiv:1404.5220) [src: Eq. 21].
- `[der]` Γ is an interaction rate in Valiviita et al. and Clemson et al., and the PPF dynamical variable in Li et al.; ξ is a coupling in Gavela et al. and the comoving-gauge lapse in Li et al.

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — the coupled non-adiabatic pressure
<!-- key: step-1-the-coupled-non-adiabatic-pressure -->

The sound speed is fixed in the rest frame of each fluid, $c_{s{A}}^{2}=\left.{\delta P_{A}\over\delta\rho_{A}}\right|_{\rm rf}\,.$ (arXiv:0804.0232) [src: Eq. 22], with $c^2_{s\varphi}=1$ for a scalar field [src: Eq. 23]. A constant-w fluid "at face value" is barotropic, but then "the sound speed" "would be imaginary", so "it is necessary to impose" $c_{sx}^2>0$ "by hand", and $c_{sx}^{2}=1\,,\quad\quad c_{ax}^{2}=w_{x}=\,\mbox{const}\,<0\,.$ (arXiv:0804.0232) [src: Eq. 25], "what is done in the CAMB and CMBFAST codes" [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 25]. Transforming from the rest frame to a general gauge gives $\delta P_{A}=c_{a{A}}^{2}\delta\rho_{A}+\big(c_{s{A}}^{2}-c_{a{A}}^{2}\big)\left[\delta\rho_{A}+{\rho_{A}^{\prime}}\left(v_{A}+B\right)\right]=c_{a{A}}^{2}\delta\rho_{A}+\delta P_{{\rm nad}\,{A}}\,,$ (arXiv:0804.0232) [src: Eq. 28], and, because $\rho'_A$ now contains the coupling, $\delta P_{A}=c_{s{A}}^{2}\delta\rho_{A}+\big(c_{s{A}}^{2}-c_{a{A}}^{2}\big)\Big[3\mathcal{H}(1+w_{A})\rho_{{A}}-aQ_{A}\Big]{\theta_{A}\over k^{2}}\,.$ (arXiv:0804.0232) [src: Eq. 29]. "This corrects the expression used in [17], which omits the" $Q_A$ "term", so earlier $\delta_x'$ and $\theta_x'$ equations were in error [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 29].

### Step 2 — a covariant transfer and the run-away dark-energy velocity
<!-- key: step-2-a-covariant-transfer-and-the-run-away -->

Valiviita et al. choose $Q^{\mu}_{c}=-Q^{\mu}_{x}=\Gamma\,T^{\,\nu}_{c\,\,\nu}\,u_{c}^{\mu}=-\Gamma\rho_{c}\,u_{c}^{\mu}\,,$ (arXiv:0804.0232) [src: Eq. 37]: a local rate, and "In the rest frame of the dark matter, there is no momentum transfer" [src: Sect. III, items (1)–(2)]. Then $\delta Q_{c}=-\Gamma\rho_{c}\delta_{c}=-\delta Q_{x}\,,\quad\quad f_{c}=\Gamma\rho_{c}(v-v_{c})=-f_{x}\,,$ (arXiv:0804.0232) [src: Eq. 46], and, with $c_{sx}^2=1$ and constant $w_x$, in Newtonian gauge, $\delta^{\prime}_{x}+3\mathcal{H}(1-w_{x})\delta_{x}+(1+w_{x})\theta_{x}+9\mathcal{H}^{2}(1-w_{x}^{2})\frac{\theta_{x}}{k^{2}}-3(1+w_{x})\psi^{\prime}=a\Gamma\frac{\rho_{c}}{\rho_{x}}\left[\delta_{c}-\delta_{x}+3\mathcal{H}(1-w_{x})\frac{\theta_{x}}{k^{2}}+\phi\right]\!,$ [src: Eq. 47], $\theta^{\prime}_{x}-2\mathcal{H}\theta_{x}-\frac{k^{2}}{(1+w_{x})}\delta_{x}-k^{2}\phi=\frac{a\Gamma}{(1+w_{x})}\frac{\rho_{c}}{\rho_{x}}\left(\theta_{c}-2\theta_{x}\right),$ [src: Eq. 48], $\delta^{\prime}_{c}+\theta_{c}-3\psi^{\prime}=-a\Gamma\phi\,,$ [src: Eq. 49] and $\theta^{\prime}_{c}+\mathcal{H}\theta_{c}-k^{2}\phi=0\,.$ (arXiv:0804.0232) [src: Eq. 50]; the dark-matter Euler equation is unchanged "due to the fact that there is no momentum transfer in the dark matter frame" [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 50].

In the early radiation era the coupled background obeys $a\Gamma{\rho_{c}\over\rho_{x}}=(3w_{x}+2)\tau^{-1}\,,\quad\quad w_{x}<-{2\over 3}\,.$ (arXiv:0804.0232) [src: Eq. 43] (the step from the background equations to Eq. 43 was not read). The $Q_x$ term of Eq. 29 then puts on the right of Eq. 48 the driving term $-2\frac{a\Gamma}{(1+w_{x})}\frac{\rho_{c}}{\rho_{x}}\theta_{x}=\frac{-2(3w_{x}+2)}{1+w_{x}}\mathcal{H}\theta_{x}$ (arXiv:0804.0232) [src: Eq. 51], whose multiplier "is a positive number (since" $w_{x}<-2/3$ ") – and it becomes very large if" $w_x$ "is close to −1": "momentum balance requires a run-away growth of the dark energy velocity" [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 51].

### Step 3 — the growing non-adiabatic mode
<!-- key: step-3-the-growing-non-adiabatic-mode -->

With power-law ansätze (Eq. 62) and $n_\psi\neq 0$, the system is solved "to leading order in" kτ; the curvature perturbation is $\zeta=-{1\over 2}{(n_{\psi}+J+2)}\,\psi.$ (arXiv:0804.0232) [src: Eq. 71], and the index is $n_{\psi}=n_{\pm}=\frac{-(1+2w_{x})\pm\sqrt{3w_{x}^{2}-2}}{1+w_{x}}\,.$ (arXiv:0804.0232) [src: Eq. 73]. The modes are regular if Re $n_\pm\geq 3$ [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 73], i.e. $n_{+}~\mbox{regular if}~-1<w_{x}\leq-{4\over 5}\,,~~~~n_{-}~\mbox{regular if}~-{9\over 11}<w_{x}\leq-{4\over 5}$ (arXiv:0804.0232) [src: Eq. 74], and near −1, $w_{x}\sim-1~~\Rightarrow~~n_{+}\sim{2\over 1+w_{x}}\gg 1\,.$ (arXiv:0804.0232) [src: Eq. 75]: "This large exponent signals the blow-up of" ψ "and therefore of all perturbations, including the gauge-invariant curvature perturbation" [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 75]. Three properties, as stated there: "The instability occurs no matter how weak the coupling is. A smaller value of" $\vert\Gamma\vert$ "simply moves the blow-up to earlier times"; it "is non-adiabatic"; and its origin "is not simply the fact that the dark energy fluid is non-adiabatic" — "In the uncoupled case, the same non-adiabatic fluid behaviour is also present, but there is no instability" [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 75]. It "cannot be removed by simply re-scaling" $A_\psi$, because the spectra "will exhibit a strong scale-dependence in violation of observations" [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 77].

### Step 4 — the same instability for H-proportional kernels
<!-- key: step-4-the-same-instability-for-h-proportional -->

For $aQ=\alpha\mathcal{H}\rho_c$ with the covariant form $aQ^{\mu}_{c}=-aQ^{\mu}_{x}=-\alpha{\mathcal{H}}\rho_{c}u_{c}^{\mu}\,.$ (arXiv:0804.0232) [src: Eq. 81], "following the implicit assumption made in other work that" αH "is an approximation to an interaction rate that varies with time but not in space" [src: Sect. IV, text after Eq. 81], the fastest index is $n_{+}=\frac{3\alpha+(\alpha-6)w_{x}+\sqrt{(\alpha^{2}+16\alpha+40)w_{x}^{2}-2(\alpha^{2}+6\alpha+8)w_{x}+(\alpha^{2}-4\alpha-20)}}{2(1+w_{x})}\,.$ (arXiv:0804.0232) [src: Eq. 85], and for small $\vert\alpha\vert$ and $w_x$ near −1, $n_{+}\sim\frac{6}{1+w_{x}}\gg 1\,.$ [src: Eq. 86], "triple the corresponding index for the" Γ "model" [src: Sect. IV, text after Eq. 86]. For $aQ=\beta\mathcal{H}(\rho_c+\rho_x)$ the four-velocity in $Q^\mu$ is more ambiguous, and "It appears that" the earlier perturbation equations "do not conform to momentum balance" (arXiv:0804.0232) [src: Sect. IV, second subsection].

### Step 5 — the doom factor: a model-independent criterion
<!-- key: step-5-the-doom-factor-a-model-independent -->

Gavela et al. rewrite the rest-frame relation $\delta P_{de}=\hat{c}_{s\,de}^{2}\delta\rho_{de}-(\hat{c}_{s\,de}^{2}-c_{a\,de}^{2})\dot{\rho}_{de}\frac{\theta_{de}}{k^{2}}\,,$ (arXiv:0901.1611) [src: Eq. 11] with the coupled background Eq. 4; the two rows of one display give $\frac{\delta P_{de}}{\delta\rho_{de}}=\hat{c}_{s\,de}^{2}+3(\hat{c}_{s\,de}^{2}-c_{a\,de}^{2})(1+w^{eff}_{de})\frac{{\mathcal{H}}\theta_{de}}{k^{2}\delta_{de}}=\hat{c}_{s\,de}^{2}+3(\hat{c}_{s\,de}^{2}-c_{a\,de}^{2})(1+w)\left(1+{\bf d}\right)\frac{{\mathcal{H}}\theta_{de}}{k^{2}\delta_{de}}\,,$ (arXiv:0901.1611) [src: Eq. 12], with the doom factor ${\bf d}\equiv\frac{Q}{3\mathcal{H}\rho_{de}(1+w)}\,,$ (arXiv:0901.1611) [src: Eq. 13], "precisely this extra factor, proportional to the dark coupling Q, which may induce non-adiabatic instabilities" [src: Sect. 2.1, text after Eq. 13]. The dark-energy velocity equation becomes $\dot{\theta}_{de}=-{\mathcal{H}}\left(1-3\hat{c}_{s\,de}^{2}-3{\bf d}(\hat{c}_{s\,de}^{2}+1)\right)\theta_{de}+\frac{k^{2}}{1+w}\hat{c}_{s\,de}^{2}\delta_{de}\,,$ (arXiv:0901.1611) [src: Eq. 16]. A positive d turns Hubble friction into anti-friction `[der]`. Combined into second order, $\delta_{de}^{\prime\prime}=A_{e}\,\frac{\delta_{de}}{a^{2}}\,+\,B_{e}\,\frac{\delta_{de}^{\prime}}{a}\,+\,{\mathcal{G}}(\rho_{i},\delta_{i},\delta_{i}^{\prime};{i\neq de})\,,$ (arXiv:0901.1611) [src: Eq. 18]; "for A and B both positive, the solution may enter in an exponentially growing, unstable, regime" [src: Sect. 2.2, item 1], and in the strongly coupled regime the sign of $B_e$ "reduces to the sign of the doom factor" "Assuming" $\hat{c}_{s\,de}^{2}>0$ [src: Sect. 2.4]. The criterion, as the conclusions state it: "when d is positive and sizeable, d > 1, the dark-coupling dependent terms may dominate the evolution of dark energy perturbations, which will then enter a runaway, unstable, growth regime" (arXiv:0901.1611) [src: Sect. 5, text after Eq. 60]. <!-- num: not a value -->

The two kernels differ by a density ratio. For $Q=\xi\mathcal{H}\rho_{de}$, ${\bf d}=\frac{\xi}{3(1+w)}\,.$ (arXiv:0901.1611) [src: Eq. 40], constant; for $Q\propto\rho_{dm}$, ${\bf d}=\frac{\xi}{3(1+w)}\,\frac{\rho_{dm}}{\rho_{de}}.$ (arXiv:0901.1611) [src: Eq. 56], and "At early times, the doom factor will always thus be large even for tiny values of" ξ, "as the ratio" $\rho_{dm}/\rho_{de}$ "is very large then" [src: Sect. 4.2, text after Eq. 56].

### Step 6 — which Q^μ avoid it
<!-- key: step-6-which-q-mu-avoid-it -->

- **Kernel ∝ ρ_de, opposite signs of coupling and 1+w (Gavela et al.).** With Q = ξℋρ_de, the region w > −1 and ξ < 0 "ensures a negative doom factor" and "spans an instability–free region" (arXiv:0901.1611) [src: Sect. 3.5]; models "with negative dark-coupling" ξ < 0 "and positive" (1+w) are "stable as" d < 0 [src: Sect. 5]. In their convention ξ < 0 transfers energy from dark matter to dark energy `[der]` (from Eq. 3–4 with Q = ξℋρ_de, Eq. 29–30).
- **Kernel ∝ ρ_de, energy against momentum frame (Clemson et al.).** "the simplest physical choice is that there is no momentum transfer in the rest frame of either DM or DE", giving transfer four-vectors parallel to $u_c$ or to $u_x$ [src: Sect. III, "Our IDE models", Eqs. 27–28]. The momentum transfer is $f_{x}=\Gamma\bar{\rho}_{x}(v_{c}-v)=-f_{c}$ for $Q\parallel u_c$ [src: Eq. 30] and $f_{x}=\Gamma\bar{\rho}_{x}(v_{x}-v)=-f_{c}$ for $Q\parallel u_x$ (arXiv:1109.6234) [src: Eq. 31]; the dark-matter Euler equation "has the standard form" for $Q\parallel u_c$ and "is modified" for $Q\parallel u_x$, $\theta_{c}^{\prime}+\mathcal{H}\theta_{c}-k^{2}\phi=a\Gamma\frac{\bar{\rho}_{x}}{\bar{\rho}_{c}}(\theta_{c}-\theta_{x}),$ (arXiv:1109.6234) [src: Eq. 36]. On large scales the dark-energy velocity grows as $\frac{\theta_{x}}{\theta^{(\Gamma=0)}_{x}}=\exp\left[-\alpha\frac{\Gamma}{1+w}(t-t_{0})\right],$ (arXiv:1109.6234) [src: Eq. 38], with α = 2, 1 for the two frames, so $-\frac{\Gamma}{(1+w)}>0~~\Rightarrow~~\mbox{instability}.$ [src: Eq. 39] and $w>-1~\mbox{and}~\Gamma>0~~\Rightarrow~~\mbox{no instability},$ (arXiv:1109.6234) [src: Eq. 40], "for both" frames [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 40]. The instability "is still present in the velocity difference, which is gauge invariant" [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 39]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Kernel ∝ ρ_c.** For constant w the Γρ_c and αℋρ_c models are unstable on super-Hubble scales whatever the coupling (Steps 3–4), and, for Q ∝ ρ_dm, Gavela et al. find that ρ_de "is negative in the past for negative" ξ, which also fixes the sign of d (arXiv:0901.1611) [src: Sect. 4.3]. Clemson et al. note that for constant w this model "suffers from an instability" and that "The instability can be cured by allowing w to vary in time" (their Ref. 83, not read) (arXiv:1109.6234) [src: Sect. II].
- **The Wang et al. summary** for ξ₁ (∝ ρ_c) and ξ₂ (∝ ρ_de) kernels with $C_e^2=1$ is in `formalism.md#interacting-dark-energy`; Jackson, Taylor & Berera's small-coupling condition for Q ∝ Hρ_de is cited there from its abstract. <!-- num: not a value -->

### Step 7 — the ePPF closure
<!-- key: step-7-the-eppf-closure -->

Li, Zhang & Zhang keep the conservation equations of Eqs. 9–10 but drop the fluid pressure closure: "we also replace the condition on the dark energy pressure perturbation with a direct relationship between the momentum density of dark energy and that of other components on the large scales" (arXiv:1404.5220) [src: text after Eq. 10], $\lim_{k_{H}\ll 1}{4\pi G\over H^{2}}(\rho_{de}+p_{de}){V_{de}-V_{T}\over k_{H}}=-{1\over 3}c_{K}f_{\zeta}(a)k_{H}V_{T},$ (arXiv:1404.5220) [src: Eq. 11], giving $\lim_{k_{H}\ll 1}\zeta^{\prime}=\xi-{K\over k^{2}}k_{H}V_{T}+{1\over 3}c_{K}f_{\zeta}k_{H}V_{T}\,,$ [src: Eq. 12] with the lapse now carrying the coupling, $\xi=-{\Delta p_{T}-{2\over 3}c_{K}p_{T}\Pi_{T}+{a\over k}[Q_{c}(V-V_{T})+f_{c}]\over\rho_{T}+p_{T}}\,.$ (arXiv:1404.5220) [src: Eq. 13]. The interpolating variable is defined on all scales by $\Phi+\Gamma={4\pi G\over c_{K}k_{H}^{2}H^{2}}\Delta_{T}\rho_{T}$ (arXiv:1404.5220) [src: Eq. 15], with $\lim_{k_{H}\ll 1}\Gamma^{\prime}=S-\Gamma\,,$ [src: Eq. 16] and a source in which the interaction appears explicitly, in two rows, $S={4\pi G\over k_{H}^{2}H^{2}}\Big\{[(\rho_{de}+p_{de})-f_{\zeta}(\rho_{T}+p_{T})]k_{H}V_{T}+{3a\over kc_{K}}[Q_{c}(V-V_{T})+f_{c}]+\frac{1}{Hc_{K}}(\Delta Q_{c}-\xi Q_{c})\Big\}.$ (arXiv:1404.5220) [src: unnumbered display after Eq. 16]. The full-scale equation is the PPF one, $(1+c_{\Gamma}^{2}k_{H}^{2})[\Gamma^{\prime}+\Gamma+c_{\Gamma}^{2}k_{H}^{2}\Gamma]=S\,.$ (arXiv:1404.5220) [src: Eq. 17], after which the dark-energy density and momentum follow algebraically, $\rho_{de}\Delta_{de}=-3(\rho_{de}+p_{de}){V_{de}-V_{T}\over k_{H}}-{k^{2}c_{K}\over 4\pi Ga^{2}}\Gamma\,,$ (arXiv:1404.5220) [src: Eq. 18], and the pressure perturbation is derived, not assumed [src: Eq. 20]. Settings: Γ = 0 at a → 0, $f_\zeta=0$, and $c_\Gamma=0.4c_s$, to which "the dark energy perturbation evolution is insensitive" "With a careful test" [src: text after Eq. 20]. The abstract's verdict: "The large-scale instability can be successfully avoided and the well-behaved density and metric perturbations are obtained within this framework", and the framework "is applicable to all the interacting dark energy models" (arXiv:1404.5220) [src: abstract]. Li et al. (2026) describe the ePPF as avoiding "directly specifying the DE pressure perturbation on large scales, where the conventional fluid treatment may become ill defined", implemented for coupled fluids in IDECAMB (arXiv:2606.21826) [src: Sect. III.1]; their Ref. 207 for the ePPF is "Li, Y.-H., Zhang, J.-F., & Zhang, X. 2014, Phys. Rev. D, 90, 063005" [src: References]. By authors, year and content it is arXiv:1404.5220 `[der]`.

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **Fluid with $c_{sx}^2=1$ (Valiviita et al., Clemson et al.).** Simple; unstable for the sign combinations above and singular at w = −1: Clemson et al. "must exclude the value" w = −1 (arXiv:1109.6234) [src: Sect. III, "Instability", inline]; their code was adapted "to suppress perturbations when" $\vert 1+w\vert<0.01$ [src: Sect. IV, inline], which App. A describes as imposing $\vert 1+w\vert<0.01$ [src: App. A, inline].
- **Doom-factor bookkeeping (Gavela et al.).** No new closure; a criterion to choose the parameter region. Cost: the stable region is a restriction of parameter space (for Q ∝ ρ_de, sign(ξ) opposite to sign(1+w)), not a cure.
- **Scalar-field dark energy instead of a fluid.** In Valiviita et al.'s Γ > 0 fluid model the dark-energy density becomes negative in the past, "The rigidity of the assumption that" $w_x$ "is constant leads to this problem"; "When dark energy is modelled as a scalar field, it remains positive for all times" (arXiv:0804.0232) [src: Sect. III, "Background dynamics", text after Eq. 43]. The coupled-quintessence route is in `models.md#coupled-quintessence`.
- **ePPF (Li et al. 2014).** Removes the fluid pressure closure; costs one function $f_\zeta$, one parameter $c_\Gamma$ and the smoothness assumption below the transition scale (Step 7). Exact in the sense of PPF only where that assumption holds (`deep/dark-energy-perturbations-ppf.md`).
- **ePPF with momentum transfer along $u_{de}$ (Dai & Xia).** In the ePPF the source contains "an additional part" "which is not proportional to" 1+w, "So" $v_{de}$ "would diverge when w is very close to −1"; this is harmless for $Q_{de}^{\mu}=3\beta H\rho_{c}u_{c}^{\mu}$, where the dark-matter Euler equation is unchanged, but for $Q_{de}^{\mu}=3\beta H\rho_{c}u_{de}^{\mu}$ "the perturbation of dark matter and the matter power spectrum will diverge" (arXiv:1904.04149, Dai & Xia 2019) [src: Sect. IV.1, inline; Sect. V, inline]. Their remedy relates the transfers to $\delta T^{0}_{~0}$ and $\delta T^{i}_{~0}$ "rather than" δρ and $v_i$ [src: Sect. V].

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Dropping $Q_A$ from the non-adiabatic pressure.** The term $-aQ_A$ in Eq. 29 of arXiv:0804.0232 is what drives the instability; earlier equations that omit it are in error (arXiv:0804.0232) [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 29]. Check: rederive δP from the rest frame with the coupled background.
- **An unspecified $Q^\mu$.** A background Q such as Eq. 36 does not fix δQ and f; "What is the covariant form of" $Q^\mu$ "that reduces to Eq. (36) in the background? This has not been made explicit in previous work" (arXiv:0804.0232) [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 36]. Check: the paper states the four-velocity along which energy flows and whether H is perturbed.
- **Weak coupling read as safe.** For constant w and Q ∝ ρ_c the blow-up only moves earlier as Γ decreases (arXiv:0804.0232) [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 75]; the ρ_dm/ρ_de factor in d makes it large at early times "even for tiny values of" ξ (arXiv:0901.1611) [src: Sect. 4.2].
- **Rescaling the amplitude.** It does not save the model: the instability is scale dependent (arXiv:0804.0232) [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 77].
- **Gauge choice hiding the effect.** Setting $\theta_x=0$ by a frame choice leaves the instability "in the velocity difference, which is gauge invariant" (arXiv:1109.6234) [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 39].
- **Negative dark-energy densities.** For Γ > 0 in the constant-w Γρ_c model, for α > 0 in the αℋρ_c model, and for β > 0 in the βℋ(ρ_c+ρ_x) model, $\rho_x$ becomes negative in the past (arXiv:0804.0232) [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 43; Sect. IV, text after Eqs. 79 and 89]; a sign change of $\rho_{de}$ also flips d and can trigger a late instability (arXiv:0901.1611) [src: Sect. 4.3].
- **w = −1 inside a coupled fluid.** The Euler equations of the fluid are singular there (arXiv:1109.6234) [src: Sect. III, "Instability"]; in the ePPF, a transfer along $u_{de}$ diverges as w → −1 (arXiv:1904.04149) [src: Sect. IV.1].
- **ePPF taken as physics.** It fixes a closure, not a model; for coupled fluids it is the closure IDECAMB uses (arXiv:2606.21826) [src: Sect. III.1]. Check: for a coupled scalar field, compare with its own field equations rather than with the ePPF.
