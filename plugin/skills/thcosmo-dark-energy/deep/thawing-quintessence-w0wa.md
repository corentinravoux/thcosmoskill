## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- Scherrer & Sen (arXiv:0712.3450, Scherrer & Sen 2007, v4) "work in units for which" 8πG = 1 [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 3]; the prime "will always denote the derivative with respect to" ln a [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 10]; they write $\gamma\equiv 1+w=\frac{2x^{2}}{x^{2}+y^{2}}.$ (arXiv:0712.3450) [src: Eq. 12] and $\Gamma\equiv V\frac{d^{2}V}{d\phi^{2}}/\left(\frac{dV}{d\phi}\right)^{2}.$ (arXiv:0712.3450) [src: Eq. 16]. <!-- num: not a value -->
  - `[der]` Their γ is 1 + w of the field, whereas in Table I of Copeland et al. (`formalism.md#quintessence`) γ is the barotropic index of the background fluid; their Γ is the Copeland et al. Γ. Their x has no factor κ/H because 8πG = 1 and the prime is an e-fold derivative.
- Dutta & Scherrer (arXiv:0809.4441, Dutta & Scherrer 2008, v4) also set 8πG = 1 and assume a flat universe [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 7]; their k is "the constant k" of $u=A\sinh(kt)+B\cosh(kt)$, not a wavenumber [src: Eqs. 19–20].
- Shlivko & Steinhardt (arXiv:2405.03933, v2) use $N\equiv-\ln(1+z)$, primes for derivatives with respect to N, and a spatially flat universe [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 9]; their hilltop curvature is quoted as $k^{2}M_{pl}^{2}$ [src: Sect. 5]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- CPL is written $w(a)=w_{0}+w_{a}(1-a)$ (`theoretical-cosmology/references/conventions.md#dark-energy-and-modified-gravity`); Shlivko & Steinhardt write it as $w(z)= w_0+ w_a z/(1+z)$ (arXiv:2405.03933) [src: abstract]. de Putter & Linder quote the standard $w_a$, "defined by [24]" (Linder 2003), as "$w_{a}=-w^{\prime}(a_{\star}=0.5)/0.5$" (arXiv:0808.0189) [src: Sect. III, inline, text before Eq. 10].

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — slow-roll thawing: the field equation in Ω_φ
<!-- key: step-1-slow-roll-thawing-the-field-equation-in -->

Scherrer & Sen impose the slow-roll conditions on the potential "at" $\phi=\phi_0$, $\left(\frac{1}{V}\frac{dV}{d\phi}\right)^{2}\ll 1,$ (arXiv:0712.3450) [src: Eq. 6] and $\frac{1}{V}\frac{d^{2}V}{d\phi^{2}}\ll 1.$ [src: Eq. 7], "along with the requirement that w be close to -1 today", and not the slow-roll approximation, which "fails for the case of quintessence" because it "requires the scalar field to dominate the expansion"; "Effectively, we are deriving the analog of the slow-roll approximation for the case where the expansion is not dominated by the scalar field" [src: Sect. III]. In a flat universe of matter and the field, with $\Omega_{\phi}=x^{2}+y^{2},$ [src: Eq. 11], the autonomous system $x^{\prime}=-3x+\lambda\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}y^{2}+\frac{3}{2}x[1+x^{2}-y^{2}],$ [src: Eq. 13], $y^{\prime}=-\lambda\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}xy+\frac{3}{2}y[1+x^{2}-y^{2}],$ [src: Eq. 14], $\lambda^{\prime}=-\sqrt{6}\lambda^{2}(\Gamma-1)x,$ (arXiv:0712.3450) [src: Eq. 15] is rewritten, with "we assume that" $x^{\prime}>0$ ("our results generalize trivially to the opposite case"), in the observables γ and $\Omega_\phi$: $\gamma^{\prime}=-3\gamma(2-\gamma)+\lambda(2-\gamma)\sqrt{3\gamma\Omega_{\phi}},$ [src: Eq. 17], $\Omega_{\phi}^{\prime}=3(1-\gamma)\Omega_{\phi}(1-\Omega_{\phi}),$ [src: Eq. 18], $\lambda^{\prime}=-\sqrt{3}\lambda^{2}(\Gamma-1)\sqrt{\gamma\Omega_{\phi}}.$ (arXiv:0712.3450) [src: Eq. 19]. Changing the independent variable from a to $\Omega_\phi$ gives $\frac{d\gamma}{d\Omega_{\phi}}=\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{\Omega_{\phi}^{\prime}}=\frac{-3\gamma(2-\gamma)+\lambda(2-\gamma)\sqrt{3\gamma\Omega_{\phi}}}{3(1-\gamma)\Omega_{\phi}(1-\Omega_{\phi})}.$ (arXiv:0712.3450) [src: Eq. 20], valid "only if" $\Omega_\phi$ "is a monotonic function of the scale factor" [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 20]. Eqs. 19–20 "are an exact description of the scalar field evolution for" $x^{\prime}>0$ [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 20]. <!-- num: not a value -->

### Step 2 — the two approximations and the universal w(Ω_φ)
<!-- key: step-2-the-two-approximations-and-the-universal -->

Two assumptions: γ ≪ 1, and λ approximately constant, $\lambda=\lambda_{0}=-(1/V)(dV/d\phi)\biggr|_{\phi=\phi_{0}},$ (arXiv:0712.3450) [src: Eq. 21]. To lowest order in γ, $\frac{d\gamma}{d\Omega_{\phi}}=-\frac{2\gamma}{\Omega_{\phi}(1-\Omega_{\phi})}+\frac{2}{3}\lambda_{0}\frac{\sqrt{3\gamma}}{(1-\Omega_{\phi})\sqrt{\Omega_{\phi}}}.$ (arXiv:0712.3450) [src: Eq. 22], which "can be transformed into a linear differential equation with the change of variables $s^{2}=\gamma$" [src: Sect. III, inline, text after Eq. 22]; with the thawing boundary condition γ = 0 at $\Omega_\phi=0$, the two rows of one display give $1+w=\frac{\lambda_{0}^{2}}{3}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Omega_{\phi}}}-\left(\frac{1}{\Omega_{\phi}}-1\right)\tanh^{-1}\sqrt{\Omega_{\phi}}\right]^{2},=\frac{\lambda_{0}^{2}}{3}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Omega_{\phi}}}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\Omega_{\phi}}-1\right)\ln\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{\Omega_{\phi}}}{1-\sqrt{\Omega_{\phi}}}\right)\right]^{2}.$ (arXiv:0712.3450) [src: Eq. 23]. The step from Eq. 22 to Eq. 23 (solving the linear equation) is skipped in the paper. "for sufficiently flat potentials, all thawing quintessence models with w near −1 approach a single generic behavior, with w(a) determined entirely by" $\Omega_\phi(a)$ "and the (constant) initial value of" (1/V)(dV/dφ) [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 23].

The constancy of λ is then checked: 1 + w is of order $\lambda_0^2$, so Eq. 6 ensures 1 + w ≪ 1; requiring $\vert\lambda'/\lambda\vert\ll 1$ with γ of order λ² in Eq. 19 gives $\frac{1}{V}\frac{d^{2}V}{d\phi^{2}}-\left(\frac{1}{V}\frac{dV}{d\phi}\right)^{2}\ll 1.$ (arXiv:0712.3450) [src: Eq. 24], which "The two slow-roll conditions, taken together, insure" [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 24].

### Step 3 — from w(Ω_φ) to w(a)
<!-- key: step-3-from-w-omegaphi-to-w-a -->

To the same order, Eq. 18 becomes the ΛCDM relation, $\Omega_{\phi}=\left[1+\left(\Omega_{\phi 0}^{-1}-1\right)a^{-3}\right]^{-1},$ (arXiv:0712.3450) [src: Eq. 25]. Eliminating $\lambda_0$ in favour of $w_0$, the three rows of one display give $1+w=(1+w_{0})\Biggl[\sqrt{1+(\Omega_{\phi 0}^{-1}-1)a^{-3}}-(\Omega_{\phi 0}^{-1}-1)a^{-3}\tanh^{-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\Omega_{\phi 0}^{-1}-1)a^{-3}}}\Biggr]^{2}\times\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Omega_{\phi 0}}}-\left(\frac{1}{\Omega_{\phi 0}}-1\right)\tanh^{-1}\sqrt{\Omega_{\phi 0}}\right]^{-2}.$ (arXiv:0712.3450) [src: Eq. 26], "a unique function of" $w_0$ and $\Omega_{\phi0}$ [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 25]. "A sufficiently accurate determination" of $w_0$ and $\Omega_{\phi0}$ "uniquely determines the value of" $\lambda_0$ "for these models": for $\Omega_{\phi 0}=0.7$ and $w_{0}=-0.9$, $\lambda_{0}=0.8$ (arXiv:0712.3450) [src: Sect. III, text after Fig. 1].

### Step 4 — the hilltop case: a ΛCDM-background linear equation
<!-- key: step-4-the-hilltop-case-a-lambdacdm-background -->

Dutta & Scherrer take a different route that does not assume a flat potential. With $\phi(t)=u(t)/a(t)^{3/2},$ (arXiv:0809.4441) [src: Eq. 9] the friction term disappears, and with total pressure $p_{T}\approx-\rho_{\phi 0}$ (w near −1) the field equation becomes $\ddot{u}-\frac{3}{4}\rho_{\phi 0}u+a^{3/2}V^{\prime}(u/a^{3/2})=0.$ (arXiv:0809.4441) [src: Eq. 13], expected "to provide a good approximation" for "any model in which the kinetic term" is "dominated by the potential term" [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 13]. Expanding about the maximum to second order and setting $\rho_{\phi0}=V(\phi_*)$ gives a linear equation with $k\equiv\sqrt{(3/4)V(\phi_{*})-V^{\prime\prime}(\phi_{*})},$ (arXiv:0809.4441) [src: Eq. 19]; with the ΛCDM scale factor and φ = $\phi_i$ at t = 0, $\phi=\frac{\phi_{i}}{kt_{\Lambda}}\frac{\sinh(kt)}{\sinh(t/t_{\Lambda})}.$ (arXiv:0809.4441) [src: Eq. 24]. With $1+w=\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{\rho_{\phi}}.$ [src: Eq. 25] and the curvature parameter $K=\sqrt{1-(4/3)V^{\prime\prime}(\phi_{*})/V(\phi_{*})}.$ (arXiv:0809.4441) [src: Eq. 30], the main result is $1+w(a)=(1+w_{0})a^{3(K-1)}\frac{[(F(a)+1)^{K}(K-F(a))+(F(a)-1)^{K}(K+F(a))]^{2}}{[(\Omega_{\phi 0}^{-1/2}+1)^{K}(K-\Omega_{\phi 0}^{-1/2})+(\Omega_{\phi 0}^{-1/2}-1)^{K}(K+\Omega_{\phi 0}^{-1/2})]^{2}},$ (arXiv:0809.4441) [src: Eq. 31], with $F(a)=\sqrt{1+(\Omega_{\phi 0}^{-1}-1)a^{-3}}.$ [src: Eq. 32], $F=1/\sqrt{\Omega_\phi(a)}$ [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 32]. "in the limit where w is close to -1", "all of the hilltop quintessence models with a given value of" $w_0$ "form a single set of models parametrized by the value of K, which depends only on" $V''(\phi_*)/V(\phi_*)$ [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 32]. In the limit K → 1 Eq. 31 reduces to the slow-roll form, $1+w=(1+w_{0})\frac{\Biggl[F(a)-\left[F(a)^{2}-1\right]\tanh^{-1}\left[F(a)^{-1}\right]\Biggr]^{2}}{\left[\Omega_{\phi 0}^{-1/2}-\left(\Omega_{\phi 0}^{-1}-1\right)\tanh^{-1}\sqrt{\Omega_{\phi 0}}\right]^{2}}.$ (arXiv:0809.4441) [src: Eq. 33], the reduction being stated, not shown ("It is straightforward to show") [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 33]. For K = 2, $1+w=(1+w_{0})a^{3},$ (arXiv:0809.4441) [src: Eq. 34]. For the PNGB, $K=\sqrt{1+(2/3)(1/f^{2})}$ (arXiv:0809.4441) [src: Sect. III, inline, text after Eq. 36].

### Step 5 — the Caldwell–Linder thawing bounds
<!-- key: step-5-the-caldwell-linder-thawing-bounds -->

The rendering of arXiv:astro-ph/0505494 carries no equation numbers; the bounds are inline. Upper bound: "Since the Hubble damping limits the scalar field acceleration," $\ddot{\phi}<\dot{\phi}/t\approx(3/2)H\dot{\phi}$, "then the equation of motion implies such models will lie at" w' < 3(1 + w) (arXiv:astro-ph/0505494) [src: text after Fig. 1]. Lower bound: suggested by "the fact that dark energy is not entirely dominant today", with $\Omega_{de}\lesssim 0.8$; "A study of several classes of thawing models, such as a pseudo Nambu-Goldstone boson (PNGB) or polynomial potentials, indicates the bound" w' > (1 + w) [src: text after Fig. 1]. The lower bound is therefore empirical over the models studied, not derived. de Putter & Linder find PNGB trajectories "that lie outside the conventional thawing region" $3(1+w)>w^{\prime}>1+w$, "although these start along" $w^{\prime}=3(1+w)$ "at early times"; "The exceptions have" $f\ll 1$ (arXiv:0808.0189) [src: Sect. II.1]. Scherrer & Sen's two-exponential potential, built to violate Eq. 7, "also violates the upper bound on" w′ "postulated in" Caldwell & Linder (arXiv:0712.3450) [src: Sect. III, text after Fig. 5]. <!-- num: not a value -->

### Step 6 — mapping onto w0–wa: three procedures
<!-- key: step-6-mapping-onto-w0-wa-three-procedures -->

1. **Fit w(a) directly at low redshift (Scherrer & Sen).** For $a$ between 0.5 and 1, w(a) "is a nearly linear function of a", "although the linear behavior breaks down for" z > 1; for $\Omega_{\phi 0}=0.7$ "the linear fit to equation (26)" for $z\lesssim 1$ "is roughly" $w=w_{0}-1.5(1+w_{0})(1-a)$, "so that" $w_{a}\approx-1.5(1+w_{0})$; "in our case" $w_a$ "is not a free parameter" (arXiv:0712.3450) [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 26]. For hilltops, "Except for this limiting case" (K → 1), "w(a) is poorly fit by linear evolution in a" (arXiv:0809.4441) [src: abstract].
2. **Calibrate (de Putter & Linder).** A constant stretch of the time variation, $w^{\prime}(a)\to\frac{w^{\prime}(a)}{a_{\star}}\to\frac{w^{\prime}(a_{\star})}{a_{\star}}\,.$ (arXiv:0808.0189) [src: Eq. 9], gives the calibrated form $w(a)=w_{0}-\frac{w^{\prime}(a_{\star})}{a_{\star}}(1-a)=w_{0}+w_{a}^{\rm(d)}(1-a)$ (arXiv:0808.0189) [src: Eq. 10], with "a single stretch parameter" $a_{\star}=0.8$ "for the entire thawing class" and $a_{\star}=0.85$ for freezing fields [src: Sect. III]; "Thawing models, despite their differences in" w–w′, "are nearly identical once calibrated" [src: Fig. 10 caption]. Linder later calls this calibration "the foundation of" $w_0$–$w_a$, which was "never" meant "as an arbitrary parametrization or 'Taylor expansion'" (arXiv:2410.10981) [src: Sect. II, Fig. 3 caption and text after it].
3. **Match observables (Shlivko & Steinhardt; Wolf et al.).** Shlivko & Steinhardt choose "the fit that best matches the evolution of the Hubble parameter" rather than w(z), minimising an error E, the maximum over z < 4 of the fractional difference between the fitted and the model H(z) (arXiv:2405.03933) [src: Eq. 5], with the fit model $\Omega_{\text{fit}}^{\prime}(N)=3w_{\text{fit}}(N)\Omega_{\text{fit}}(N)(\Omega_{\text{fit}}(N)-1),$ [src: Eq. 10], $H_{\text{fit}}^{\prime}(N)=-\frac{3}{2}H(N)(1+w_{\text{fit}}(N)\Omega_{\text{fit}}(N)),$ [src: Eq. 11], $w_{\text{fit}}(N)\equiv w_{0}+w_{a}(1-e^{N}).$ (arXiv:2405.03933) [src: Eq. 12], scanning $\{w_0,w_a,\Omega_{\rm fit}(0),H_{\rm fit}(0)\}$; "fitting all four parameters, rather than just" $w_a$ "as in the approach of de Putter and Linder, is necessary" for "a wider range of quintessence models than those considered in" their Ref. 14 [src: Sect. 2]. Wolf et al. go one step closer to the data: with $H^{2}(a)=H_{0}^{2}\left[\Omega_{\mathrm{m}}a^{-3}+\left(1-\Omega_{\mathrm{m}}\right)e^{3w_{a}(a-1)}a^{-3(1+w_{0}+w_{a})}\right],$ (arXiv:2408.17318) [src: Eq. 7] they minimise, per survey S, $\chi^{2}_{\rm S}=\left(\mathcal{O}^{\rm CPL}-\mathcal{O}^{\varphi}\right)^{T}\left(\frac{\mathcal{O}^{\rm data}}{\mathcal{O}^{\varphi}}\right)^{T}{{\rm Cov}}^{-1}\frac{\mathcal{O}^{\rm data}}{\mathcal{O}^{\varphi}}\left(\mathcal{O}^{\rm CPL}-\mathcal{O}^{\varphi}\right),$ (arXiv:2408.17318) [src: Eq. 8], on compressed BAO, SN and CMB observables [src: Sect. III].

### Step 7 — why non-phantom models land in the phantom-looking sector
<!-- key: step-7-why-non-phantom-models-land-in-the -->

- **The CMB "mirage" (Linder 2007).** Matching the distance to last scattering $d_{\rm lss}$ of ΛCDM at fixed $\Omega_m$, "we can derive a quantity" $A=\int_{1}^{Y}dy\,\frac{\ln y}{(\Omega_{m}y^{3}+1-\Omega_{m})^{3/2}}\Big/\int_{1}^{Y}dy\,\frac{\ln y+y^{-1}-1}{(\Omega_{m}y^{3}+1-\Omega_{m})^{3/2}},$ (arXiv:0708.0024) [src: Eq. 1], with Y = 1 + z, and forces a crossing of w = −1 at $a_{w}=1-A^{-1}.$ (arXiv:0708.0024) [src: Eq. 3]; equivalently, "to match LCDM for some model with current EOS" $w_0$ "one has" $w_{a}=-A(1+w_{0})$ [src: Sect. II, inline, text after Eq. 3]. Consequence: "a high redshift distance measurement consistent with LCDM virtually forces (within the picture so far) the value" $w(z\approx 0.4)=-1$, "irrespective of true time variation", so the averaged EOS of low-redshift measurements "insufficiently sensitive to time variation" "will necessarily appear to be" w ≈ −1 [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 3]. Linder (2024) calls the family "mirage dark energy", "along the line" $w_{a}\approx-3.66(1+w_{0})$ (arXiv:2410.10981) [src: Sect. III]; his source for the line is his Ref. 11, the 2007 paper above, printed in his list as "The Mirage of Λ" [src: References].
- **Extrapolation of a linear w beyond the data.** Shlivko & Steinhardt find that "all the quintessence models we tested" (exponential, plateau, hilltop) "obey the NEC and have" w(z) > −1 "for all z", yet "are best fit by curves or regions on the" $w_0$–$w_a$ "plane that satisfy" $w_0+w_a<-1$; this "is a manifestation of the fact that even large differences in w(z) over a wide range of" z ≫ 1 "(where" $\Omega_{Q}(z)$ "is negligibly small)" "have a negligible impact on cosmological observables such as H(z)" (arXiv:2405.03933) [src: Sect. 5]. Wolf et al., for their $m^2<0$ example (V₀, m²) = (0.9725, −40.0): the inferred CPL parameters "seemingly imply that this dark energy model becomes phantom in the past", "just an artifact of extrapolating the linear parameterization of w(a) outside the range where the relevant distance observations are fit" (arXiv:2408.17318) [src: Sect. III].
- **The fitted $w_0$ is not w today.** "the best-fit values of" $w_0$ "obtained when matching H(z)'s need not be equal to—or even close to—the actual value" $w_Q(0)$, "specifically the case for the hilltop and plateau models" (arXiv:2405.03933) [src: Sect. 5]; for the same example, "the fitted" $w_0$ "can differ quite substantially from the true value of the equation of state today" (arXiv:2408.17318) [src: Sect. III].
- **Survey dependence.** For a thawing model with $m^2<0$, "the best fit" $(w_0,w_a)$ "can differ quite substantially depending on the redshifts the observables are sensitive to"; "The more recent the epoch probed, the steeper the region inferred"; the $m^2>0$ branch "evolve far more linearly" (arXiv:2408.17318) [src: Sect. III].
- **A phantom mirage from a mis-assigned component (Liu, Zhu, Hu & Miranda 2025).** If an axion with $m_{\rm a}\gtrsim H_0$ is counted as cold dark matter today, the inferred dark energy is $\rho_{\rm eff}(z)\equiv\rho_{{{\rm de}}}(z)+\rho_{\rm a}(z)-\rho_{\rm a}(0)(1+z)^{3}$ (arXiv:2510.14957) [src: Eq. 1], with, in the two rows of one display, $1+w_{\rm eff}=\frac{1}{3}\frac{d\ln\rho_{\rm eff}}{d\ln(1+z)}=\frac{(1+w_{{\rm de}})\rho_{{\rm de}}+(1+w_{\rm a})\rho_{\rm a}-\rho_{\rm a}(0)(1+z)^{3}}{\rho_{\rm eff}},$ (arXiv:2510.14957) [src: Eq. 2]; "for higher z when" $w_{\rm a}<0$ "and approaches" −1, "while" $\rho_{\rm eff}>0$, "the negative contribution from the incorrect matterlike assumption for axions drives" $w_{\rm eff}<-1$ "which implies the mirage of a phantom", and "If" $w_{\rm de}>-1$, "then this also implies a phantom crossing" [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 2]. The axion version "does not require an additional coupling", unlike the coupled-quintessence mirage [src: Sect. III].

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **Slow-roll thawing (Scherrer & Sen) against hilltop (Dutta & Scherrer).** Slow roll gives one w(a) per $(w_0,\Omega_{\phi0})$; the hilltop solution adds K and so "more complex behavior"; for $V''/V\gg 1$ "w increases more slowly at high redshift, but much more rapidly at low redshift" (arXiv:0809.4441) [src: Sect. III, text after Fig. 1 caption]. Cost of slow roll: having dropped terms of order 1 + w, "we expect the fractional error in 1+w to be on the order of 1+w" (arXiv:0712.3450) [src: Sect. III, text after Fig. 2]. The PNGB is covered by slow roll only for f > 1 [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 27 of arXiv:0712.3450], and by the hilltop expansion "when" $\phi\ll f$ (arXiv:0809.4441) [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 17].
- **Crittenden et al.'s κ(φ) scheme**, reprinted by Scherrer & Sen: $\kappa(\phi)=\frac{dV/d\phi}{V(1+\ddot{\phi}/3H\dot{\phi})}.$ [src: Eq. 28] with $\kappa(\phi)=\kappa_{0}+\kappa_{1}(\phi-\phi_{0}).$ [src: Eq. 29] gives $1+w=\frac{2}{3}\kappa_{0}^{2}\Omega_{\phi}\left(\frac{1-\Omega_{\phi}}{1-\Omega_{\phi 0}}\right)^{4\kappa_{1}/3}.$ (arXiv:0712.3450) [src: Eq. 30], "clearly distinct from" Eq. 23 "for all values of" $\kappa_0$ and $\kappa_1$ [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 30].
- **Fit w, fit H, or fit the survey.** A fit to w(a) at z ≲ 1 (Scherrer & Sen), a stretch calibration (de Putter & Linder, one parameter $a_\star$), a max-norm fit to H(z) over z < 4 (Shlivko & Steinhardt), or a survey-weighted χ² (Wolf et al.). Shlivko & Steinhardt: choosing "a mean-square error instead of a maximum, or matching" $D_M$'s "instead of" H's "can affect the slope of best-fit lines by" ≲ 10 % "depending on the model", the lines pivoting about the ΛCDM point (arXiv:2405.03933) [src: Sect. 3]. Wolf et al. note that Ye et al. "simplified the mapping to" $(w_0,w_a)$ "by fitting the equation of state directly" (arXiv:2408.17318) [src: Sect. III].
- **Numerical integration instead of analytic forms.** The analytic w(a) are "approximate and only valid in the regime where" $1+w_{Q}(z)\ll 1$; Shlivko & Steinhardt integrate the full equations instead, starting from $\Omega_{Q}=10^{-6}$ with zero initial velocity (arXiv:2405.03933) [src: Sect. 2]. Wolf et al. set initial conditions with "an analytic ansatz" found by symbolic regression (arXiv:2408.17318) [src: Sect. I].
- **Algebraic thawing form.** Linder fits the data with "the algebraic thawing form" (his Ref. 12), which keeps w(a) > −1; for thawing-region values it agrees with $w_0$–$w_a$ "to" $\mathcal{O}(0.1\%)$, but "this does not work for the strong mirage case where" $\vert w_{a}\vert\gtrsim 1$ (arXiv:2410.10981) [src: Sect. III].

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Reading $w_0+w_a<-1$ as NEC violation.** The NEC-satisfying quintessence models tested there land there (arXiv:2405.03933) [src: Sect. 5]. Check: map the model with an observable-matching procedure (Step 6.3) before comparing with a contour.
- **Reading the fitted $w_0$ as w today.** In hilltop and plateau models the two "need not be equal to—or even close to" each other; in the k²M_pl² = 100 hilltop of Fig. 4 the w(z) of model and fit "differ by" $\mathcal{O}(100\%)$ (arXiv:2405.03933) [src: Sect. 5, text with Fig. 4]. Check: quote $w_Q(0)$ from the model, not $w_0$ of the fit.
- **Using the slow-roll w(a) outside its assumptions.** It assumes $x'>0$ ("our results generalize trivially to the opposite case"), a monotonic $\Omega_\phi$, γ ≪ 1, constant λ and no radiation (arXiv:0712.3450) [src: Sect. III]; a potential that violates Eq. 7, such as two joined exponentials, stays thawing "but it does not produce a functional form for w(a) resembling that of a nearly flat potential" [src: Sect. III, text after Fig. 5]. Check Eqs. 6–7 at $\phi_0$; for a hilltop with $V''/V$ not small use Eq. 31 of arXiv:0809.4441.
- **Freezing potentials treated as freezing by default.** "while negative power law potentials usually give rise to 'freezing' models, they can be made to act as thawing models by an appropriate choice of" $\phi_0$ (arXiv:0712.3450) [src: Sect. III, text after Fig. 2].
- **The Caldwell–Linder lower bound taken as a theorem.** It is read off "A study of several classes of thawing models" (arXiv:astro-ph/0505494) [src: text after Fig. 1], and small-f PNGB trajectories leave the region (arXiv:0808.0189) [src: Sect. II.1].
- **One mapping for all surveys.** The best-fit $(w_0,w_a)$ of the same model moves with the redshifts probed (arXiv:2408.17318) [src: Sect. III]; the best-fit line itself has a "∼10%" uncertainty in $w_a$ at given $w_0$ for plateau and hilltop curves (arXiv:2405.03933) [src: Sect. 5]. For models "not well fit by any" $(w_0,w_a)$ "the safer approach would be to perform a separate analysis specific to the model" [src: Sect. 5].
- **Fine tuning invisible on a $w_0$–$w_a$ plot.** "plots like Fig. 3 carry no information about fine-tuning of either parameters or initial conditions" (arXiv:2405.03933) [src: Sect. 5]; the tiny $\phi_i/f$ needed at small f "may run into physical problems such as a tachyonic instability" (arXiv:0808.0189) [src: Sect. II.1].
- **The opposite reading.** Linder argues that, taking DESI DR1 at face value, "the data favors that the phantom crossing is indeed a real property", the non-crossing algebraic form being ∼3σ worse (arXiv:2410.10981) [src: Sect. III]; Cortês & Liddle, using "a pivot scale for the equation of state", find that the evidence "is concentrated in the derivative of w rather than its mean offset from −1", and argue that "conclusions on dark energy evolution are strongly driven by the assumed parameter priors" (arXiv:2404.08056) [src: abstract]. A dossier answer on "phantom or not" quotes both sides.
- **A mis-assigned component.** An axion with $m_{\rm a}\gtrsim H_0$, dark-energy-like early and matter-like late, counted as CDM today produces a phantom $w_{\rm eff}$ with no phantom field (arXiv:2510.14957) [src: Sect. III]; the coupled-dark-sector version is in `formalism.md#interacting-dark-energy` (Eq. 9 of arXiv:2606.21826).
