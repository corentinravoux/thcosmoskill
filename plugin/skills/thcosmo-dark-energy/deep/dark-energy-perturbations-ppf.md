## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- Fang, Hu & Lewis (arXiv:0808.3125, Fang et al. 2008, v2) write ${}^{\prime}\equiv d/d\ln a$, $w_{e}=p_{e}/\rho_{e}$, $k_{H}=k/aH$, A "the gravitational potential in an arbitrary gauge" [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 1], with B the space–time and $H_L$ the space–space curvature piece of the metric "in an arbitrary gauge" [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 4], and $c_K=1-3K/k^2$ with K the background curvature [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 6]. The dark-energy variable is the momentum density $u_e$, $(\rho_{e}{\bf u}_{e})_{i}\equiv T^{0}_{\hphantom{0}i}$, not the velocity: "the relative fluid velocity" is $v_{e}=u_{e}/(1+w_{e})$ [src: Sect. II]. "T" denotes "all other components excluding the dark energy" [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 6]. <!-- num: not a value -->
  - `[der]` These are Hu's gauge-general variables, not the (δ, θ) of Ma & Bertschinger used by Ren et al. in `formalism.md`; no paper read converts one set into the other.
- Hu & Sawicki (arXiv:0708.1190, Hu & Sawicki 2007, v2) use the same $'$ and $k_H$, with $\Phi_-$ the combination of potentials related to Φ and Ψ through "the metric ratio g" [src: Sect. II.1].
- Yang et al. (arXiv:2511.22478, v3) write PPF in synchronous gauge, with $\alpha=a(\dot{h}+6\dot{\eta})/(2k^{2})$ with h and η "the metric perturbations in synchronous gauge", $\mu_{K}=1-3K/k^{2}$, and set $\mu_K=1$, $f_\zeta=0$ [src: App. A, text after Eq. 4]. <!-- eq: notation -->

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — why a single component cannot cross: the momentum equation
<!-- key: step-1-why-a-single-component-cannot-cross-the -->

Momentum conservation of the dark energy, in an arbitrary gauge: $u_{e}^{\prime}=(3w_{e}-1)u_{e}+k_{H}{\delta p_{e}\over\rho_{e}}+(1+w_{e})k_{H}A\,,$ (arXiv:0808.3125) [src: Eq. 1]. The sound speed is defined in the dark-energy rest frame, reached by a change of time slicing, with the two rows of one display $\delta\rho^{\rm(rest)}=\delta\rho_{e}+3\rho_{e}{u_{e}\over k_{H}}\,,\delta p^{\rm(rest)}=\delta p_{e}+3{p_{e}^{\prime}\over\rho_{e}^{\prime}}\rho_{e}{u_{e}\over k_{H}}\,,$ (arXiv:0808.3125) [src: Eq. 2] and $c_{s}^{2}\equiv{\delta p_{e}^{\rm(rest)}\over\delta\rho_{e}^{{\rm(rest)}}}\,,$ [src: Eq. 3], which brings Eq. 1 to $u_{e}^{\prime}=3\left(w_{e}+c_{s}^{2}-{p_{e}^{\prime}\over\rho_{e}^{\prime}}-{1\over 3}\right)u_{e}+k_{H}c_{s}^{2}\delta_{e}+(1+w_{e})k_{H}A\,,$ (arXiv:0808.3125) [src: Sect. II, unnumbered display after Eq. 3]. The continuity equation, in two rows: $\delta_{e}^{\prime}+3(c_{s}^{2}-w_{e})\delta_{e}+9\left(c_{s}^{2}-{p_{e}^{\prime}\over\rho_{e}^{\prime}}\right){u_{e}\over k_{H}}=-k_{H}u_{e}-(1+w_{e})(k_{H}B+3H_{L}^{\prime})\,,$ (arXiv:0808.3125) [src: Eq. 4]. For a canonical scalar $c_s^2=1$ [src: Sect. II, text after the unnumbered display]. "When" $w_{e}=-1$, $p_{e}^{\prime}/\rho_{e}^{\prime}$ "will generally diverge leading to an instability in the evolution of perturbations if" $c_s^2$ "is held fixed"; "the change in the time slicing required to reach the rest or constant field gauge becomes infinite when the field has no kinetic energy", and "Viewed as a fluid", $v_e$ "becomes undefined if the momentum remains finite" (arXiv:0808.3125) [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 6].

### Step 2 — several components: the singularity moves into the components
<!-- key: step-2-several-components-the-singularity-moves -->

For a sum of minimally coupled fields each with sound speed $c_e^2$, the two rows of one display give $\delta p_{e}=c_{s}^{2}\rho_{e}\delta_{e}+3\left(c_{s}^{2}-{p_{e}^{\prime}\over\rho_{e}^{\prime}}\right){\rho_{e}u_{e}\over k_{H}}=c_{e}^{2}\rho_{e}\delta_{e}+3\left(c_{e}^{2}{\rho_{e}u_{e}\over k_{H}}-\sum_{\alpha}{p_{e\alpha}^{\prime}\over\rho^{\prime}_{e\alpha}}{\rho_{e\alpha}u_{e\alpha}\over k_{H}}\right)\,,$ (arXiv:0808.3125) [src: Eq. 7], which "implicitly defines" $c_s^2$ "as a function of the individual momenta"; "As long as no individual component crosses the phantom divide", "the pressure fluctuations are no longer singular" [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 7]. Two-field constructions exist but are "cumbersome for obtaining a general function" $w_{e}(\ln a)$ "constrained to match cosmological distances" [src: Sect. II]. The PPF idea follows: "Models that cross the phantom divide must have internal degrees of freedom to ensure" $u_e$ "remains finite through the crossing. Provided they do, energy momentum conservation and the requirement that the dark energy is smooth compared with the matter for" $c_{e}k_{H}\gg 1$ "impose nearly unique constraints on their parameterization" [src: Sect. II, last paragraph].

### Step 3 — the smoothness condition and the Γ variable
<!-- key: step-3-the-smoothness-condition-and-the-variable -->

With the Newtonian-gauge Poisson equation $c_{K}k^{2}\Phi=4\pi Ga^{2}\sum_{i}\rho_{i}\delta_{i}^{{\rm(rest)}}$ (arXiv:0808.3125) [src: Eq. 5], with $c_s^2>0$ making the dark energy "Jeans stable in the regime" $k_{H}c_{s}\gg 1$ "so long as" $p_{e}^{\prime}/\rho_{e}^{\prime}$ "remains finite" [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 4], "In the matter dominated epoch, matter density fluctuations continue to grow and so the Poisson equation" "becomes dominated by the matter component, i.e. the dark energy is relatively smooth compared with the matter", $\rho_{e}\delta_{e}^{\rm(rest)}\ll\rho_{T}\delta_{T}^{\rm(rest)}\,,$ [src: Eq. 6]; this "is not the same as setting all dark energy perturbations to zero which causes inconsistencies between the four scalar Einstein equations", and in synchronous gauge "some care must be taken even at" $k_{H}c_{s}\gg 1$ [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 6]. PPF "replaces the density and momentum components with a single joint dynamical variable" Γ "but retains strict conservation of energy and momentum in its equation of motion" [src: Sect. III]. With vanishing anisotropic stress and a normal small-scale Poisson equation, "(see [7] Eq. 30)" — Hu 2008, not read here — $\Gamma\equiv{4\pi Ga^{2}\over c_{K}k^{2}}\rho_{T}\Delta_{T}-\Phi\,,$ (arXiv:0808.3125) [src: Eq. 8], with the comoving-gauge matter density $\Delta_{T}\equiv\delta_{T}^{{\rm(rest)}}=\delta_{T}+3u_{T}/k_{H}$ [src: Eq. 9]; by Eq. 5 this is $\Gamma=-{4\pi Ga^{2}\over k^{2}c_{K}}\rho_{e}\delta_{e}^{{\rm(rest)}}\,.$ (arXiv:0808.3125) [src: Eq. 10]. "The condition that the dark energy becomes smooth relative to the matter in their respective rest gauges then becomes a direct requirement on the evolution of" Γ [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 10].

In the modified-gravity form of the same idea, Hu & Sawicki add Γ to the modified Poisson equation, $k^{2}[\Phi_{-}+\Gamma]=4\pi Ga^{2}\rho_{m}\Delta_{m}\,.$ (arXiv:0708.1190) [src: Eq. 16].

### Step 4 — the large-scale closure: f_ζ
<!-- key: step-4-the-large-scale-closure-f -->

On large scales "the curvature" $\zeta\equiv H_{L}^{(T)}$ "in the total matter gauge is conserved up to order" $k_{H}^{2}$ "in a flat universe with adiabatic fluctuations" [src: Sect. III]. Its Einstein equation, $\zeta^{\prime}=\xi-{K\over(aH)^{2}}{V_{T}\over k_{H}}-{4\pi G\over H^{2}}\rho_{e}{U_{e}\over k_{H}}\,,$ (arXiv:0808.3125) [src: Eq. 11], with $\xi=-{\Delta p_{T}-{2\over 3}c_{K}p_{T}\pi_{T}\over\rho_{T}+p_{T}}\,,$ [src: Eq. 12], is enforced by tying the dark-energy momentum to the matter velocity, $\lim_{k_{H}\ll 1}U_{e}=-{H^{2}\over 12\pi G\rho_{e}}c_{K}k_{H}^{2}V_{T}f_{\zeta}\,,$ (arXiv:0808.3125) [src: Eq. 13], $f_\zeta(\ln a)$ "a function of time only"; "The scaling requirement is that to first order in" $k_H$, "the dark energy and matter rest frames are the same at large scales", and "Once" $U_e$ "and its evolution are determined", $\delta p_e$ "follows by momentum conservation with no singularities encountered as" $w_e$ "crosses the phantom divide" [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 13]. For the metric, "even this is not necessary as long as" $f_{\zeta}\lesssim{\rho_{e}/(\rho_{T}+\rho_{e})}$, and, since "ultra large scales where the dark energy is not smooth are generally probed gravitationally", "it suffices for most purposes to simply take" $f_{\zeta}=0$ [src: Sect. III]. In the modified-gravity version the same freedom is $\lim_{k_{H}\rightarrow 0}\zeta^{\prime}={1\over 3}f_{\zeta}k_{H}V_{m}\,.$ (arXiv:0708.1190) [src: Eq. 17], "the exact choice of" $f_\zeta$ "is rarely important for observable quantities"; "Only observables that depend on the comoving density on large scales beyond the quasi-static regime are affected" [src: Sect. III.1, text after Eq. 19].

### Step 5 — the interpolating equation and the transition scale
<!-- key: step-5-the-interpolating-equation-and-the -->

"to assure that the dark energy becomes smooth relative to the matter inside a transition scale" $c_{e}k_{H}=1$ "while exactly conserving energy and momentum locally", Fang et al. take $(1+c_{\Gamma}^{2}k_{H}^{2})[\Gamma^{\prime}+\Gamma+c_{\Gamma}^{2}k_{H}^{2}\Gamma]=S\,,$ (arXiv:0808.3125) [src: Eq. 14], with $S=-{4\pi G\over H^{2}}\left[f_{\zeta}(\rho_{T}+p_{T})-(\rho_{e}+p_{e})\right]{V_{T}\over k_{H}}\,.$ [src: Eq. 15]. This "explicitly guarantees that" $\Gamma\ll V_{T}/k_{H}={\cal O}(\Phi)$ "for" $c_{\Gamma}k_{H}\gg 1$; "the specifics of how rapidly the dark energy becomes negligible" "depend on the specific form of" Eq. 14, but "the net impact on observable quantities of this choice is small" [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 15]. The calibration of the transition is $c_{\Gamma}=0.4c_{e}\,$ (arXiv:0808.3125) [src: Eq. 16], which "matches the evolution of scalar field models" [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 16]. Fang et al. write Eq. 14 "by taking [6, 7]", their Refs. 6 (Hu & Sawicki, arXiv:0708.1190) and 7 (Hu 2008, arXiv:0801.2433, not read) [src: Sect. III; References]; the modified-gravity form of Ref. 6 is $(1+c_{\Gamma}^{2}k_{H}^{2})\left[\Gamma^{\prime}+\Gamma+c_{\Gamma}^{2}k_{H}^{2}\left(\Gamma-f_{G}\Phi_{-}\right)\right]=S\,.$ (arXiv:0708.1190) [src: Eq. 21], which interpolates between $\Gamma^{\prime}+\Gamma=S\,,\qquad(k_{H}\rightarrow 0)\,,$ [src: Eq. 18] and $\Gamma=f_{G}\Phi_{-}\,,\qquad(k_{H}\rightarrow\infty)\,.$ (arXiv:0708.1190) [src: Eq. 20], with initial conditions Γ = Γ′ = 0 "when the mode was above the horizon" "For models where" S → 0 as a → 0 [src: Sect. III.1, text after Eq. 21]. Dark energy is Eq. 21 without its $f_G$ term `[der]`: Eq. 14 of arXiv:0808.3125 is Eq. 21 of arXiv:0708.1190 with that term dropped.

The parameters, then: $w_e(\ln a)$ for the background; $f_\zeta(\ln a)$ on large scales (set to 0 in practice); $c_\Gamma$, fixed to 0.4 $c_e$; zero anisotropic stress. In the modified-gravity framework, "given an expansion history H(a)", "3 functions and 1 parameter": g, $f_\zeta$, $f_G$ and $c_\Gamma$ (arXiv:0708.1190) [src: Sect. III.1, last paragraph]. <!-- num: not a value -->

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **Explicit two-field (quintom) perturbations.** Exact for a given model `[der]`; Fang et al. use a two-field model approximating $w_0=-1.15$, $w_a=0.5$ as the check of PPF (arXiv:0808.3125) [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 16; Fig. 2 caption]. Cost: "cumbersome" for a general $w_e(\ln a)$ [src: Sect. II].
- **Matching across a thin layer (Zhao et al. 2005; Li et al. 2011).** In Ren et al.'s account (their Ref. 197, which proposed the treatment, is Zhao et al.; their Ref. 96, which "later revisited" it, is Li et al. [src: References]), a narrow interval $\vert 1+w\vert<\epsilon$ splits the evolution in three; outside, standard equations; inside, perturbations "assumed to remain finite and continuous" and matched by imposing $\dot{\delta}=0$ and $\dot{\theta}=0$; validated "by comparing the resulting CMB and LSS power spectra with those from a full two-field Quintom model, finding negligible differences" (arXiv:2606.01360) [src: paragraph beginning "To handle such crossings in practice", inline] (the rendering has no section headings); Zhao et al. find, "Compare with those assuming no dark energy perturbations", that "the parameter space which allows the equation of state to get across −1 will be enlarged in general when including the perturbations" (arXiv:astro-ph/0507482) [src: abstract]. Li et al. justify the matching from continuity of "the induced 3-metric on the hypersurface of" $w_D=-1$ "and its extrinsic curvature", showing that $\delta_D$ and $\theta_D$ "are continuous on the matching hypersurface" (arXiv:1008.1684) [src: abstract, inline].
- **Fluid with frozen velocity near w = −1 (CAMB `DarkEnergyFluid`).** "The velocity equation is singular as w -> -1, so the fluid velocity perturbation is held fixed where abs(1 + w) <= 5e-7 and switched on smoothly up to abs(1 + w) = 1e-6"; models "whose w(a) only comes within ~1e-6 of -1 over part of the evolution can therefore be significantly biased on large scales"; a $w_0$–$w_a$ or tabulated w that crosses −1 raises "fluid dark energy model does not support w crossing -1" [doc: CAMB `camb/dark_energy.py`, class `DarkEnergyFluid`, GitHub master, 2026-11-04].
- **PPF in CAMB (`DarkEnergyPPF`).** Implements arXiv:0808.3125; "Note PPF is not a physical model and just designed to allow crossing -1 in an ad hoc smooth way" [doc: CAMB `camb/dark_energy.py`, class `DarkEnergyPPF`, GitHub master, 2026-11-04]. The Fortran sets `c_Gamma_ppf = 0.4_dl*sqrt(this%cs2_lam)` and stops with "DarkEnergyPPF does not support varying sound speed" when `cs2_lam` ≠ 1; above a threshold in $(c_\Gamma k/aH)^2$ it relaxes Γ to "the algebraic quasi-static fixed point of the same PPF equation", a choice made "to stay closish to fluid and well behaved" for "some extreme models" [doc: CAMB `fortran/DarkEnergyPPF.f90`, GitHub master, last commit 2026-08-10, read 2026-11-04]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- **PPF in CLASS.** "The flag 'use_ppf' is 'yes' by default, to use the PPF approximation (see 0808.3125 [astro-ph]) allowing perturbations to cross the phantom divide. Set to 'no' to enforce true fluid equations for perturbations"; `c_gamma_over_c_fld` is "eq. (16) in 0808.3125", default 0.4 "as recommended by that reference, and implicitely assumed in other codes"; `cs2_fld` is "the sound speed defined in the frame comoving with the fluid", default 1, with the CLP form "p/rho = w0_fld + wa_fld (1-a/a0)" [doc: CLASS `explanatory.ini`, Sect. 8.a, GitHub master, 2026-11-04]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **PPF with a free sound speed (Yang et al.).** In synchronous gauge, the two numbered rows $\Gamma\equiv-\frac{4\pi Ga^{2}}{k^{2}\mu_{K}}\rho_{\rm de}\Delta_{\rm de}^{\rm rest},$ (arXiv:2511.22478) [src: Eq. 3] and $S\equiv-\frac{4\pi G}{H^{2}}\Big[f_{\zeta}(t)(\rho_{\rm T}+p_{\rm T})-\rho_{\rm de}(1+w)\Big]\frac{v_{\rm T}+k\alpha}{k_{H}},$ [src: Eq. 4], and $\left(1+c_{\Gamma}^{2}k_{H}^{2}\right)\left(\frac{\dot{\Gamma}}{H}+\Gamma+c_{\Gamma}^{2}k_{H}^{2}\Gamma\right)=S,$ (arXiv:2511.22478) [src: Eq. 5], with "$c_{\Gamma}=0.4\,c_{s}$ following Ref. [33]" [src: App. A, text after Eq. 5]; models "with" $c_s^2=1$ "in PPF framework correspond to smooth dark energy" [src: Sect. II]. They compute with "modified versions of CLASS [18] and hi_class [15, 78]" [src: App. C]. `[der]` This uses a sound speed that the CAMB PPF module read above refuses.
- **EFT of dark energy.** Yang et al. contrast PPF, which "models dark energy as a non-interacting fluid", with the EFT of dark energy, in which "perturbations can be consistently analyzed independently of the assumed background expansion history" (arXiv:2511.22478) [src: Sects. I and III]; the EFT itself is in `thcosmo-modified-gravity`.

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **A fluid closure run through a crossing.** $p'_e/\rho'_e$ "will generally diverge" and the perturbations are unstable at fixed $c_s^2$ (arXiv:0808.3125) [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 6]; CAMB refuses the case for its fluid module [doc: CAMB `camb/dark_energy.py`, 2026-11-04]. Check: which perturbation module produced the chains.
- **A fluid that only approaches −1.** No crossing, yet CAMB's frozen-velocity patch can bias large scales "significantly" [doc: CAMB `camb/dark_energy.py`, class `DarkEnergyFluid`, 2026-11-04]; a hand-written fluid code has the same singular velocity equation (Eq. 1–3 of arXiv:0808.3125).
- **Smoothness confused with zero perturbations.** Setting dark-energy perturbations to zero "causes inconsistencies between the four scalar Einstein equations" (arXiv:0808.3125) [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 6]; PPF instead conserves energy and momentum and enforces "all of the Einstein equations removing potential ambiguities to the meaning of a 'smooth' dark energy component" [src: Sect. IV].
- **PPF read as a physical model.** It is a closure; CAMB's documentation states it "is not a physical model" and that "For models with w>-1 but far from cosmological constant, it can give quite different answers to the fluid model with c_s^2=1" [doc: CAMB `camb/dark_energy.py`, class `DarkEnergyPPF`, 2026-11-04]. Check: for a non-crossing model, run the fluid module too and compare.
- **Transition outside the calibrated regime.** The accuracy statement holds "as long as the transition scale to relative smoothness is comparable to the horizon" (arXiv:0808.3125) [src: Sect. IV]; $c_\Gamma=0.4c_e$ "matches the evolution of scalar field models" [src: Eq. 16 and text after]. For a small sound speed the transition moves inside the horizon, and a small-$c_s^2$ PPF result is an extrapolation of that calibration `[der]`; no paper read tests it against a clustering two-field model.
- **The sound speed in a PPF code.** CAMB's PPF module runs only with `cs2_lam` = 1 [doc: CAMB `fortran/DarkEnergyPPF.f90`, 2026-11-04]; a quoted PPF bound on $c_s^2$ (arXiv:2511.22478) comes from "modified versions of CLASS [18] and hi_class" [src: App. C], not from that module `[der]`. Check the code version before comparing constraints.
- **$f_\zeta$ and observables of comoving density.** $f_\zeta$ does not affect redshift and lensing potentials but does affect "observables that depend on the comoving density on large scales beyond the quasi-static regime" (arXiv:0708.1190) [src: Sect. III.1, text after Eq. 19].
- **Synchronous gauge.** "some care must be taken even at" $k_{H}c_{s}\gg 1$, since "the dark energy momentum is no longer negligible in comparison" (arXiv:0808.3125) [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 6].
- **Interacting dark energy.** PPF as above assumes "the energy and momentum of the dark energy is separately conserved" (arXiv:0808.3125) [src: Sect. IV]; with an interaction use the ePPF (`deep/interacting-de-instabilities.md`).
