Scope: definitions and governing equations of dark-energy models within general relativity: vacuum energy and the Λ problem, quintessence, k-essence, phantom fields and the crossing of w = −1, interacting dark energy, early dark energy, phenomenological classes.

## The cosmological constant and vacuum energy
<!-- key: the-cosmological-constant-and-vacuum-energy -->

- Observed value, in Copeland, Sami & Tsujikawa (2006, arXiv:hep-th/0603057), with the full Planck mass $m_{\rm pl}=1.22\times 10^{19}$ GeV [src: Sect. IV.2, text after Eq. 69]: $\Lambda\approx H_{0}^{2}=(2.13h\times 10^{-42}\,{\rm GeV})^{2}\,.$ (arXiv:hep-th/0603057) [src: Eq. 66], and $\rho_{\Lambda}=\frac{\Lambda m_{{\rm pl}}^{2}}{8\pi}\approx 10^{-47}\,{\rm GeV}^{4}\,.$ [src: Eq. 67].
- Zero-point energy of a field of mass m: $\rho_{\rm vac}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm d}^{3}{\bf k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sqrt{k^{2}+m^{2}}=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}k\,k^{2}\sqrt{k^{2}+m^{2}}\,.$ (arXiv:hep-th/0603057) [src: Eq. 68]; with a cut-off, $\rho_{\rm vac}\approx\frac{k_{\rm max}^{4}}{16\pi^{2}}\,.$ [src: Eq. 69], and $k_{\rm max}=m_{\rm pl}$ gives $\rho_{\rm vac}\approx 10^{74}\,{\rm GeV}^{4}\,,$ [src: Eq. 70], printed as "about $10^{121}$ orders of magnitude larger" than Eq. 67 (as printed: the exponent, not the number of orders, is 121) [src: Sect. IV.2, text after Eq. 70]; a QCD cut-off still gives $\rho_{\rm vac}\approx 10^{-3}\,{\rm GeV}^{4}$ [src: Sect. IV.2, text after Eq. 70].
- For spin j: $\rho_{\rm vac}=\frac{1}{2}(-1)^{2j}(2j+1)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm d}^{3}{\bf k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sqrt{k^{2}+m^{2}}=\frac{(-1)^{2j}(2j+1)}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}k\,k^{2}\sqrt{k^{2}+m^{2}}\,.$ (arXiv:hep-th/0603057) [src: Eq. 71]; exact supersymmetry cancels the sum, but broken supersymmetry around $10^{3}$ GeV leaves the value "still far away" from the observed Λ [src: Sect. IV.2].
- Renormalised estimate (Martin 2012, arXiv:1205.3365): the method that introduces a cut-off "is not appropriate" [src: Sect. I, outline paragraph]; with dimensional regularisation and renormalisation, $\rho_{{}_{\rm vac}}=\sum_{i}n_{i}\frac{m_{i}^{4}}{64\pi^{2}}\ln\left(\frac{m_{i}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\right)+\rho_{{}_{\rm B}}+\rho_{{}_{\rm vac}}^{{}_{\rm EW}}+\rho_{{}_{\rm vac}}^{{}_{\rm QCD}}+\cdots,$ (arXiv:1205.3365) [src: Eq. 515], with $n_i$ counting degrees of freedom with sign (Higgs $n_{\rm H}=1$, quarks $-4$) and $\rho_{\rm B}$ the bare constant [src: Sect. IX, text after Eq. 515]. With the renormalisation scale $\mu\simeq 3\times 10^{-25}$ GeV argued from supernova photons and $H_0$, the Standard-Model fields give $\rho_{{}_{\rm vac}}\simeq-2\times 10^{8}\,\mbox{GeV}^{4}+\rho_{{}_{\rm B}}+\rho_{{}_{\rm vac}}^{{}_{\rm EW}}+\rho_{{}_{\rm vac}}^{{}_{\rm QCD}}+\cdots.$ [src: Eq. 516]; "regardless of the precise value of μ" this is very far from the $10^{72}$ GeV⁴ "always mentioned in the literature" [src: Sect. IX, text after Eq. 516].
- Martin's statement of the problem is a mismatch of "something like 54 orders of magnitude (but much less than the 122 orders of magnitude often quoted in the literature)" between Eq. 516 and the measured value (arXiv:1205.3365) [src: Sect. X.2]. It rests on homogeneity and isotropy and on general relativity, and it is not removed by replacing Λ by dynamical dark energy, because the bound ρ_vac < ρ_cri still applies [src: Sect. X.2].
- The classical part of the problem: in the presence of gravity, absolute energies, not only differences, are observable, so each phase transition (electroweak, QCD) shifts the vacuum energy (arXiv:1205.3365) [src: Sect. I, outline paragraph; Sect. III].

## Quintessence
<!-- key: quintessence -->

- Action $S=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\phi)^{2}-V(\phi)\right]\,,$ (arXiv:hep-th/0603057) [src: Eq. 97]; field equation $\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}+\frac{{\rm d}V}{{\rm d}\phi}=0\,.$ [src: Eq. 98]; $\rho=-T_{0}^{0}=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}+V(\phi)\,,~~~p=T_{i}^{i}=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}-V(\phi)\,.$ [src: Eq. 101].
- Background (arXiv:hep-th/0603057): $H^{2}=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\left[\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}+V(\phi)\right]\,,$ [src: Eq. 102] and $\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}=-\frac{8\pi G}{3}\left[\dot{\phi}^{2}-V(\phi)\right]\,.$ [src: Eq. 103], so acceleration requires $\dot{\phi}^{2}<V(\phi)$ [src: Sect. V.1, text after Eq. 103].
- Equation of state $w_{\phi}=\frac{p}{\rho}=\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}-2V(\phi)}{\dot{\phi}^{2}+2V(\phi)}\,.$ (arXiv:hep-th/0603057) [src: Eq. 105], which "ranges in the region $-1\leq w_{\phi}\leq 1$" [src: Sect. V.1, text after Eq. 106]: a canonical field cannot be phantom.
- Power-law expansion $a\propto t^{p}$ comes from $V(\phi)=V_{0}\exp\left(-\sqrt{\frac{16\pi}{p}}\frac{\phi}{m_{\rm pl}}\right)\,,$ (arXiv:hep-th/0603057) [src: Eq. 111], accelerating for p > 1 [src: Sect. V.1, text after Eq. 108].
- The inflationary slow-roll conditions "are not completely trustworthy" for dark energy because dark matter is present; $\epsilon=-\dot{H}/H^{2}$ is the better measure of acceleration (arXiv:hep-th/0603057) [src: Sect. V.1, text after Eq. 104].

Dynamical system (Copeland et al., with $\epsilon=+1$ for quintessence and $\epsilon=-1$ for a phantom): <!-- num: not a value --> <!-- eq: notation -->

- Lagrangian density as printed, ${\cal L}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon\dot{\phi}^{2}+V(\phi)\,,$ (arXiv:hep-th/0603057) [src: Eq. 179] (as printed, with $+V$, while Eq. 180 counts V in the energy density with a plus sign and Eq. 97 has $-V$ in the action); $H^{2}=\frac{\kappa^{2}}{3}\left[\frac{1}{2}\epsilon\dot{\phi}^{2}+V(\phi)+\rho_{m}\right]\,,$ [src: Eq. 180], $\dot{H}=-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2}\left[\epsilon\dot{\phi}^{2}+(1+w_{m})\rho_{m}\right]\,,$ [src: Eq. 181], $\epsilon\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}+\frac{{\rm d}V}{{\rm d}\phi}=0\,.$ [src: Eq. 182].
- Variables $x\equiv\frac{\kappa\dot{\phi}}{\sqrt{6}H}\,,~~y\equiv\frac{\kappa\sqrt{V}}{\sqrt{3}H}\,,$ $\lambda\equiv-\frac{V_{,\phi}}{\kappa V}\,,~~\Gamma\equiv\frac{VV_{,\phi\phi}}{V_{,\phi}^{2}}\,,$ (arXiv:hep-th/0603057) [src: Eq. 183]; $\frac{{\rm d}\lambda}{{\rm d}N}=-\sqrt{6}\lambda^{2}(\Gamma-1)x\,,$ [src: Eq. 186]; constraint $\epsilon x^{2}+y^{2}+\frac{\kappa^{2}\rho_{m}}{3H^{2}}=1\,.$ [src: Eq. 187]; $w_{\phi}\equiv\frac{p_{\phi}}{\rho_{\phi}}=\frac{\epsilon x^{2}-y^{2}}{\epsilon x^{2}+y^{2}}\,,$ [src: Eq. 188]; $\Omega_{\phi}\equiv\frac{\kappa^{2}\rho_{\phi}}{3H^{2}}=\epsilon x^{2}+y^{2}\,.$ [src: Eq. 189].
- Exponential potential $V(\phi)=V_{0}e^{-\kappa\lambda\phi}\,.$ (constant λ) (arXiv:hep-th/0603057) [src: Eq. 191]. Fixed points for quintessence, with γ the barotropic index of the fluid as used in Table I [src: Table I]:

Thawing, freezing and tracking:

- Caldwell & Linder (arXiv:astro-ph/0505494) split the simplest scalar-field models by whether the field accelerates or decelerates down its potential [src: abstract]. Thawing fields are frozen by Hubble damping until recently, start at w ≈ −1 and have w′ ≡ ẇ/H > 0, lying at w' < 3(1+w) with a lower bound w' > (1+w) found for several classes; these bounds hold for (1+w) ≪ 1, "and so w ≲ −0.8 is a practical limit of applicability" [src: text after Fig. 1]. For freezing models the line w' = 1.5w(1+w) "is a possible lower bound" [src: Fig. 2 caption]. The rendering carries no equation numbers.
- Tracker fields avoid the initial-conditions problem of the ratio of dark-energy to matter density (Steinhardt, Wang & Zlatev, arXiv:astro-ph/9812313) [src: abstract].
- Thawing with a nearly flat potential (slow-roll conditions on V): all such models "converge to a unique relation" between 1+w, $\Omega_\phi$ and the initial slope, and w(a) "depends only on the present-day values for w and Ω_φ", accurate to δw ≲ 0.005 for w < −0.9, and well fitted at z ≲ 1 by the linear CPL form (Scherrer & Sen, arXiv:0712.3450) [src: abstract]. Unlike trackers, they depend only on the initial conditions and not on the rest of the potential [src: Sect. V].

## k-essence and non-canonical fields
<!-- key: k-essence-and-non-canonical-fields -->

- Action $S=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,p(\phi,X)\,,$ with $X\equiv-(1/2)(\nabla\phi)^{2}$, the Lagrangian density being a pressure (Copeland et al., arXiv:hep-th/0603057) [src: Eq. 115; Sect. V.2, text before Eq. 115]; k-essence is "usually" restricted to $p(\phi,X)=f(\phi)\hat{p}(X)\,.$ [src: Eq. 116]. With this X the phantom is written $p=-X-V(\phi)$ [src: Sect. V.5, text after Eq. 147], and the action includes quintessence [src: Sect. V.2, text after Eq. 115]. The sign and factor of X differ between papers: see the hub's atlas, "Kinetic term X".
- Low-energy string motivation: $p(\phi,X)=K(\phi)X+L(\phi)X^{2}\,.$ (arXiv:hep-th/0603057) [src: Eq. 121], which a field redefinition brings to $p(\phi,X)=f(\phi)(-X+X^{2})\,,$ [src: Eq. 123], with $\rho=2X\frac{\partial p}{\partial X}-p=f(\phi)(-X+3X^{2})\,.$ [src: Eq. 124] and $w_{\phi}=\frac{p}{\rho}=\frac{1-X}{1-3X}\,.$ [src: Eq. 125]; w = −1 at X = 1/2 and acceleration for X < 2/3 [src: Sect. V.2, text after Eq. 125].
- Stability (Copeland et al., arXiv:hep-th/0603057, following their Ref. 39): the Hamiltonian of fluctuations is positive if $\xi_{1}\,\equiv\,p_{,X}+2Xp_{,XX}\,\geq\,0,~~\xi_{2}\,\equiv\,p_{,X}\,\geq\,0,$ [src: Eq. 145] and $\xi_{3}\,\equiv\,-p_{,\phi\phi}\,\geq\,0\,.$ [src: Eq. 146]; sound speed $c_{s}^{2}\equiv\frac{p_{,X}}{\rho_{,X}}=\frac{\xi_{2}}{\xi_{1}}\,,$ [src: Eq. 147]. Classical stability needs $c_s^2>0$; quantum stability needs both $\xi_1>0$ and $\xi_2\geq 0$; the authors adopt Eq. 145, not Eq. 146 (an infrared instability), as the consistency criterion [src: Sect. V.5, text after Eq. 147].
- Attractor k-essence: a dynamical attractor makes k-essence act as a cosmological constant "only at the onset of matter-domination", with two classes of solutions, acceleration forever or of finite duration (Armendariz-Picon, Mukhanov & Steinhardt, arXiv:astro-ph/0006373) [src: abstract].
- Chaplygin gas: $p=-\frac{A}{\rho}\,,$ (arXiv:hep-th/0603057) [src: Eq. 151], integrating to $\rho=\sqrt{A+\frac{B}{a^{6}}}\,,$ (arXiv:hep-th/0603057) [src: Eq. 152]: dust-like for a ≪ (B/A)^{1/6} and Λ-like for a ≫ (B/A)^{1/6} (arXiv:hep-th/0603057) [src: Eqs. 153–154]. It is a tachyon with a constant potential [src: Sect. V.6, text after Eq. 151]; introduced by Kamenshchik, Moschella & Pasquier (arXiv:gr-qc/0103004) [src: abstract].

## Phantom fields and the crossing of w = −1
<!-- key: phantom-fields-and-the-crossing-of-w-1 -->

- A phantom has $w<-1$; Caldwell's toy phantom component was "compatible with most classical tests of cosmology" of 1999 (arXiv:astro-ph/9908168) [src: abstract]. Its density grows; it "becomes infinite in finite time", ending in a "Big Rip" (Caldwell, Kamionkowski & Weinberg, arXiv:astro-ph/0302506) [src: abstract].
- Ghost instability: for $p=-X-V(\phi)$, $\xi_1=\xi_2=-1$, so the system "is quantum mechanically unstable even though the speed of sound is positive" (arXiv:hep-th/0603057) [src: Sect. V.5, text after Eq. 147]. A ghost interacting at least gravitationally needs a Lorentz-violating cutoff Λ < 3 MeV to agree with the cosmic gamma-ray background, and ghosts with a Lorentz-conserving cutoff "are completely excluded" (Cline, Jeon & Moore, arXiv:hep-ph/0311312) [src: abstract].
- Terminology (Ren et al. 2026, arXiv:2606.01360, v2): crossing of w = −1 is "Quintom behavior", also called phantom crossing or crossing the phantom divide; Quintom-A crosses from above to below with time, Quintom-B from below to above [src: text before Eq. 1].
- No-go theorem: for a single perfect fluid, or a single field with $L=L(\phi,\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi)$ minimally coupled to general relativity in FRW, w cannot cross −1 (arXiv:2606.01360) [src: inline, text before Eq. 2]. Vikman's original analysis: transitions are "either realized by a discrete set of trajectories in the phase space or are unstable with respect to the cosmological perturbations", for a dominant dark energy; without that assumption the result holds for Lagrangians quadratic in the field derivatives (arXiv:astro-ph/0407107) [src: abstract].
- The fluid argument, in conformal Newtonian gauge (Ren et al., arXiv:2606.01360): $\delta^{\prime}=-\Big(1+\frac{\bar{p}}{\bar{\rho}}\Big)(\theta-3\Phi^{\prime})-3\mathcal{H}\Big(\frac{\delta p}{\delta\rho}-\frac{\bar{p}}{\bar{\rho}}\Big)\delta~,$ [src: Eq. 2], $\theta^{\prime}=-\Big(\mathcal{H}+\frac{\bar{p}^{\prime}}{\bar{\rho}+\bar{p}}\Big)\theta+k^{2}\Big(\frac{\delta p}{\bar{\rho}+\bar{p}}+\Psi\Big)~,$ [src: Eq. 3]; the adiabatic sound speed $c_{a}^{2}=\frac{\delta p}{\delta\rho}\Big|_{\rm{adiabatic}}=\frac{\bar{p}^{\prime}}{\bar{\rho}^{\prime}}=w-\frac{w^{\prime}}{3\mathcal{H}(1+w)}.$ [src: Eq. 4] diverges when w crosses −1; with the rest-frame sound speed $c_{s}^{2}=\frac{\delta\hat{p}}{\delta\hat{\rho}}.$ [src: Eq. 5] and $\delta p=c_{s}^{2}\delta\rho+(c_{s}^{2}-c_{a}^{2})\Big[3\mathcal{H}(1+w)\bar{\rho}\Big]\frac{\theta}{k^{2}}.$ [src: Eq. 8], keeping the entropy perturbation finite forces $\delta p=c_a^2\delta\rho$ at the crossing, incompatible with the non-adiabatic perturbations assumed; "the perturbation system becomes ill-defined around the crossing point" [src: text after Eq. 10].
- Ways out: additional degrees of freedom — multiple fields, higher derivatives, modified gravity, interactions (arXiv:2606.01360) [src: paragraph "Due to the No-Go theorem", after Eq. 10]. The first two-field Quintom model (2004) has one canonical field and one with the opposite kinetic sign: $S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla^{\mu}\phi+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\mu}\sigma\nabla^{\mu}\sigma-V(\phi,\sigma)\right].$ (arXiv:2606.01360) [src: Eq. 11]; Quintom was proposed by Feng, Wang & Zhang as w > −1 in the past and w < −1 today (arXiv:astro-ph/0404224) [src: abstract].
- Crossing in Horndeski, generalised Proca and other theories of modified gravity: `thcosmo-modified-gravity` (`open-questions.md`, "Can shift-symmetric Horndeski gravity cross the phantom divide?"). An apparent crossing from an energy exchange with dark matter is treated below under interacting dark energy.

## Interacting dark energy
<!-- key: interacting-dark-energy -->

- Covariant form (Li et al. 2026 review, arXiv:2606.21826, v2): $\nabla_{\nu}T^{\nu}_{\mu,{\rm de}}=-\nabla_{\nu}T^{\nu}_{\mu,{\rm c}}=Q_{\mu},$ [src: Eq. 2]; the choice of $Q_\mu$ fixes the background rate Q, its perturbation δQ and the momentum transfer f [src: Sect. III.1, text after Eq. 2]. Background, in conformal time: ${\rho}_{\rm de}^{\prime}+3\mathcal{H}(1+w)\rho_{\rm de}=aQ,$ [src: Eq. 3], ${\rho}_{\rm c}^{\prime}+3\mathcal{H}\rho_{\rm c}=-aQ,$ [src: Eq. 4].
- Common kernels: $Q=\beta H\rho_{\rm c},\qquad Q=\beta H\rho_{\rm de},$ (arXiv:2606.21826) [src: Eq. 5], and, on the view that a local interaction should not depend on the global expansion rate, $Q=\beta H_{0}\rho_{\rm c},\qquad Q=\beta H_{0}\rho_{\rm de}.$ [src: Eq. 6]; these "remain phenomenological parametrizations rather than unique consequences of an underlying microscopic theory" [src: Sect. III.1, text after Eq. 6].
- Effective equations of state seen by an observer who assumes separate conservation: $w_{\rm de}^{\rm eff}=w-\frac{aQ}{3\mathcal{H}\rho_{\rm de}},$ (arXiv:2606.21826) [src: Eq. 9], $w_{\rm c}^{\rm eff}=\frac{aQ}{3\mathcal{H}\rho_{\rm c}}.$ [src: Eq. 10]; even with w = −1, an interaction "may even mimic a time-varying or phantom-crossing EoS" [src: Sect. III.1, text after Eq. 10]. With w = −1 (interacting vacuum) the vacuum density is not constant: ${\rho}_{\rm de}^{\prime}=aQ$ [src: Sect. III.1, inline, text after Eq. 6].
- Sign conventions differ. In Li et al. Q > 0 adds energy to dark energy (Eqs. 3–4 above). In the review of Wang et al. (arXiv:1603.08299, v2), $\dot{\rho}_{c}+3H\rho_{c}=Q,$ [src: Eq. 12] and $\dot{\rho}_{d}+3H(1+\omega_{d})\rho_{d}=-Q\,,$ [src: Eq. 13], so Q > 0 adds energy to dark matter, with kernels $Q=H\xi_{1}\rho_{c};\quad Q=H\xi_{2}\rho_{d};\quad Q=H\xi(\rho_{d}+\rho_{c}).$ [src: Eq. 14]. A coupling β or ξ is quoted with its paper's sign convention.
- Coincidence problem: with $Q=H\xi(\rho_{d}+\rho_{c})$, $\frac{dr}{dt}=-3\Gamma Hr,\qquad\qquad\Gamma=-\omega_{d}-\xi^{2}\frac{(\rho_{c}+\rho_{d})^{2}}{3\rho_{c}\rho_{d}},$ (arXiv:1603.08299) [src: Eq. 15], with stationary ratios $r^{\pm}_{s}=-1+2b\pm 2\sqrt{b(b-1)},\qquad b=-\frac{3\omega_{d}}{4\xi}>1.$ [src: Eq. 16]: the past solution $r_s^+$ is unstable and the future one $r_s^-$ stable [src: Sect. 2.1.1, text after Eq. 16].
- Coupled quintessence: a scalar field with an exponential potential and a linear coupling to matter, conformally equivalent to Brans–Dicke with a power-law potential, with a "φ-matter-dominated epoch" (φMDE) in which the field has a finite, almost constant energy density (Amendola, arXiv:astro-ph/9908023) [src: abstract]. In coupled-fluid models Q is imposed on the conservation equations; in coupled quintessence the coupling is set in the action (arXiv:2606.21826) [src: Sect. III.1].

Large-scale instability:

- Constant w with a coupling proportional to the dark-matter density: "the curvature perturbation blows up on super-Hubble scales", even with adiabatic initial conditions and "regardless of how weak the coupling is"; it is a non-adiabatic instability, different from the small-scale strong-coupling instabilities found earlier (Valiviita, Majerotto & Maartens, arXiv:0804.0232) [src: abstract].
- Wang et al. show that with constant ω_d and ξ₁ ≠ 0 the curvature perturbation diverges for ω_d > −1 but not for ω_d < −1, and that a coupling proportional to the dark-energy density (ξ₂ ≠ 0) gives stable solutions for both signs of 1 + ω_d (arXiv:1603.08299) [src: Sect. 4.2], assuming C_e² = 1 for the effective sound speed, which is "correct for scalar fields, but it is not obvious for other cases" [src: Sect. 4.2]. For Q ∝ Hρ_de, the instability "can be avoided" if the coupling is small enough for a given w (Jackson, Taylor & Berera, arXiv:0901.3272) [src: abstract].
- The extended parametrised post-Friedmann (ePPF) framework avoids specifying the dark-energy pressure perturbation on large scales and "removes the non-physical large-scale instability" over a broad parameter range; it is implemented in IDECAMB (arXiv:2606.21826) [src: Sect. III.1].

## Early dark energy
<!-- key: early-dark-energy -->

Formalism only; the status of early dark energy as a response to the H0 tension belongs to `thcosmo-tensions-model-building` (planned), and measured values to cosmoskill `cosmo-tensions-anomalies`.

- Background (Poulin et al. review, arXiv:2302.09032, v3): $\rho_{\rm EDE}(a)=\rho_{\rm EDE,0}e^{3\int_{a}^{1}[1+w_{\rm EDE}(a)]da/a}\,.$ [src: Eq. 20]; phenomenological equation of state $w_{\rm EDE}(a)=\frac{1+w_{f}}{1+(a_{c}/a)^{3(1+w_{f})}}-1\,,$ [src: Eq. 21], with $w_f=(n-1)/(n+1)$ for a field oscillating in $V\propto\phi^{2n}$ around its minimum [src: Sect. IV.1, text after Eq. 20]. The EDE fraction is $f_{\rm EDE}\equiv\rho_{\rm EDE}/\rho_{\rm tot}$, peaking at the critical redshift $z_c$ [src: Sect. IV.1, text after Eq. 21].
- Perturbations (arXiv:2302.09032; prime = conformal time η): $\frac{d}{d\eta}\left(\frac{\delta_{\rm EDE}}{1+w_{\rm EDE}}\right)=-\left(\theta_{\rm EDE}+h_{\delta}^{\prime}\right)-3\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}(c_{s}^{2}-c_{a}^{2})\left(\frac{\delta_{\rm EDE}}{1+w_{\rm EDE}}+3\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\frac{\theta_{\rm EDE}}{k^{2}}\right)$ [src: Eq. 22] and $\theta_{\rm EDE}^{\prime}=-\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\left(1-3c_{s}^{2}\right)\theta_{\rm EDE}+c_{s}^{2}k^{2}\frac{\delta_{\rm EDE}}{1+w_{\rm EDE}}+k^{2}h_{v},$ [src: Eq. 23], with $h_{\delta}=h_s/6$, $h_v=0$ in synchronous gauge and $h_\delta=\phi_N$, $h_v=\psi_N$ in conformal Newtonian gauge [src: Sect. IV.1, text after Eq. 23].
- Adiabatic sound speed as printed: $c_{a}^{2}=\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{\rm EDE}}{P^{\prime}_{\rm EDE}}=w_{\rm EDE}-\frac{1}{3}\frac{dw_{\rm EDE}/d\ln a}{1+w_{\rm EDE}}$ (arXiv:2302.09032) [src: Eq. 24] — as printed, the first ratio is $\rho'/P'$, while the text below defines $c_a^2$ by $\dot{p}=c_{a}^{2}\dot{\rho}$ [src: Sect. IV.1, text after Eq. 24]. For Eq. 21, c_a² → −(2 + w_f) for a ≪ a_c and w_f for a ≫ a_c [src: Sect. IV.1, text after Eq. 24]. The effective sound speed $c_s^2$ depends in general on k and a [src: Sect. IV.1].
- Axion-like EDE: $V(\theta)=m^{2}f^{2}[1-\cos(\theta)]^{n},$ with $\theta\equiv\phi/f$ in $[-\pi,\pi]$ (arXiv:2302.09032) [src: Eq. 6]; the field is frozen until H ∼ ∂²_θV and then oscillates [src: Sect. III.1]. It is "conventional" to trade m and f for $z_c$ and $f_{\rm EDE}(z_c)$, with $\theta_i$ controlling mostly the perturbations through $c_s^2$; sampling the theory parameters instead changes the effective priors [src: Sect. III.1].
- New early dark energy (NEDE): $V(\psi,\phi)=\frac{\lambda}{4}\psi^{4}+\frac{1}{2}\beta M^{2}\psi^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\alpha M\psi^{3}+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\phi^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma\phi^{2}\psi^{2}.$ (arXiv:2302.09032) [src: Eq. 10]; when $H\lesssim m$ the trigger field φ rolls, the ψ = 0 configuration becomes unstable and a first-order phase transition releases the NEDE energy [src: Sect. ## Dark-energy perturbations and clustering
<!-- key: dark-energy-perturbations-and-clustering -->

- Fluid variables: the adiabatic sound speed $c_a^2$ (Eq. 4 of arXiv:2606.01360, or Eq. 24 of arXiv:2302.09032 as printed) and the rest-frame sound speed $c_s^2$ (Eq. 5 of arXiv:2606.01360) are distinct; their difference sources the non-adiabatic pressure of Eq. 8 of arXiv:2606.01360 (see "Phantom fields" above). For k-essence $c_{s}^{2}\equiv\frac{p_{,X}}{\rho_{,X}}$ (arXiv:hep-th/0603057) [src: Eq. 147].
- $c_a^2$ diverges when w crosses −1, which "signals a breakdown of the fluid description" (arXiv:2606.01360) [src: text after Eq. 4]; a fluid code that integrates through a crossing needs a different closure.
- Parameterised post-Friedmann (PPF) closure: single-field models cannot cross; a PPF description gives "a simple but accurate description of multiple scalar field crossing models", conserves energy and momentum, and is "exact in the metric evolution on scales well above and below the transition scale to relative smoothness" (Fang, Hu & Lewis, arXiv:0808.3125) [src: abstract]. The extended PPF for interacting dark energy is under "Interacting dark energy" above.
- Clustering matters for the CMB: for constant w, perturbations increase the large-scale power when w > −1 and partially cancel the background ISW effect when w < −1, which "increases the degeneracies" (Weller & Lewis, as summarised in arXiv:hep-th/0603057) [sec: Sect. XIII.3]; with SN Ia they found $w=-1.02\pm 0.16$ at 1σ for $c_s^2=1$, not much changed by other sound speeds [sec: Sect. XIII.3].
- First constraint on c_s² for time-varying w with DESI DR2 + Planck 2018 + Union3, in the PPF framework: log₁₀ c_s² = −3.00 (+2.9, −0.99), with constant-w models "unconstrained"; an EFT analysis favours c_s² ∼ 0.3 or 0.4 (Yang et al., arXiv:2511.22478) [src: abstract].
