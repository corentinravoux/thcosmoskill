## Live debates

### How large is the cosmological-constant problem, and is it solved by any mechanism?
<!-- key: how-large-is-the-cosmological-constant-problem -->

- Size: the "commonly cited discrepancy of 120 orders of magnitude" rests on "an estimate that does not account for regularization and renormalization", while "fundamental principles of quantum field theory nevertheless lead to a huge mismatch" (arXiv:2606.20778) [src: abstract]; Martin's renormalised free-field estimate gives "something like 54 orders of magnitude" (arXiv:1205.3365) [src: Sect. X.2].
- Sequestering: Kaloper & Padilla make the dimensional parameters of the matter sector functionals of the 4-volume, which "completely sequesters from gravity all of the vacuum energy from a matter sector, including all loop corrections"; for these parameters to be nonzero "the universe should be finite in spacetime", w_DE ≃ −1 is "a transient", and the universe "will collapse in the future" (arXiv:1309.6562) [src: abstract]. A 2026 variant obtains a sequestering-like global constraint from a lapse function in a 5d anisotropic theory, with radiative contributions "cancelled at all orders" (Khoury, Muntz & Padilla, arXiv:2604.08659) [src: abstract].
- Unimodular gravity de-gravitates the vacuum energy but "traditionally trades the problem of vacuum energy for a fine-tuning of initial conditions" (Kaplan & Rajendran, arXiv:2511.12897) [src: abstract].
- Relaxation: "because relaxation is not confined to the vacuum energy alone it can have consequences for the rest of low-energy physics", suppressing "'slow' physics" relative to a timescale set by the relaxon mass; in "yoga" relaxation models conventional QCD axions "are unlikely to remain viable in their standard form" (van de Bruck, Burgess & Smith, arXiv:2603.20008) [src: abstract].

### Do the data prefer thawing quintessence?
<!-- key: do-the-data-prefer-thawing-quintessence -->

- No, with wider priors: a preference appears "only if we use priors which are heavily informed by the data itself", and "if we extend the priors to physically better motivated ranges, the evidence for thawing quintessence disappears" (Payeur, McDonough & Brandenberger, arXiv:2411.13637, v4) [src: abstract].
- Yes, with theory-motivated priors on the Padé-w parameters: "consistently preferred" with DESI DR2 + Planck+ACT + any major SN compilation, including DES-Dovekie, but "contingent on the inclusion of supernovae" (Shlivko, arXiv:2512.20832) [src: abstract].
- `[der]` The two papers differ in the parameterisation of thawing and the data release; the dependence on SN Ia is common ground.

### Is the DESI preference for dynamical dark energy physical, and does it require a phantom phase?
<!-- key: is-the-desi-preference-for-dynamical-dark -->

- For a genuine late-time signal: the preference "does not disappear when the CPL assumption is relaxed" (arXiv:2606.21826) [src: Sect. II.2]; in a joint EDE + CPL analysis including DESI BAO it is "a robust signature that cannot be absorbed by modifying the physics of the early Universe", per Malekjani & Pourojaghi (arXiv:2608.19432) [src: abstract].
- Against, or for caution: driven by low-z DES Y5 SNe Ia (arXiv:2512.10585) [src: Sect. V]; sensitive to SN calibration residuals (arXiv:2602.05368) [src: abstract].
- Phantom phase: "an artifact of a parametrization that is not based on a physical model" per Dinda & Maartens (arXiv:2504.15190) [src: abstract]; KMIX "may account for the DESI preference without invoking true phantom behavior" (arXiv:2511.23463) [src: abstract]; an interaction "may even mimic a time-varying or phantom-crossing EoS" (arXiv:2606.21826) [src: Sect. III.1, text after Eq. 10]. Against: genuine crossings built without ghosts in modified gravity (`thcosmo-modified-gravity`) or with a spatially covariant scalar "avoiding ghost and gradient instabilities in linear perturbations" (arXiv:2508.01378) [src: abstract].

### Is interacting dark energy preferred, and by which kernel?
<!-- key: is-interacting-dark-energy-preferred-and-by -->

- For: Q = βH₀ρ_de at 3.4σ with SPT + DESI DR2 + DES Y5 (arXiv:2510.11363) [src: abstract].
- Against: 2.1σ with Pantheon+ instead (arXiv:2510.11363) [src: abstract]; sign-switching solutions imply negative past dark-energy densities "which may be unphysical" (arXiv:2601.03122) [src: abstract]; with GRBs and DESI DR2, no significant deviation from ΛCDM (Zhu et al., arXiv:2511.16032) [src: abstract].

## Unresolved calculations
<!-- key: unresolved-calculations -->

- A first-principles interaction kernel Q: "in the absence of a fundamental theory the quantity Q cannot be derived from first principles" (arXiv:1603.08299) [src: Sect. 2.1].
- The vacuum energy beyond free fields and flat space: Martin's estimate is for a free theory (arXiv:1205.3365) [src: abstract]; expanding-universe ambiguities (choice of vacuum, comoving or physical cut-off) remain (arXiv:2606.20778) [src: abstract].
- A UV completion of the axion-like early-dark-energy potential, which "suffers from fine-tuning issues that makes it challenging to embed in a UV-complete theory" (arXiv:2302.09032) [src: Sect. VII.2].
- Scalar-field realisations of sign-switching histories: some have none in a one-field action (arXiv:2609.09261) [src: abstract].

## What next data or calculations will decide
<!-- key: what-next-data-or-calculations-will-decide -->

- Sound speed of dark energy: current data already give the first constraint for time-varying w (arXiv:2511.22478) [src: abstract].
- Euclid: pre-launch **forecast** constraints on time-varying dark energy and specific modified-gravity models are reviewed in arXiv:2512.09748 [src: abstract]; numbers there are forecasts.
- Thawing versus freezing needs σ(w') ≈ (1+w) (arXiv:astro-ph/0505494) [src: text after Fig. 1].
- The DESI-era preference for evolving w is "particularly sensitive to redshift-dependent SN calibration/selection residuals at the few×10⁻² mag level", and the review derives "an explicit calibration requirement" for such claims (arXiv:2602.05368) [src: abstract].

## Stale claims and misconceptions
<!-- key: stale-claims-and-misconceptions -->

- "The vacuum energy is 120 (or 122) orders of magnitude too large": an estimate "that does not account for regularization and renormalization" (arXiv:2606.20778, abstract); Martin's renormalised free-field value is "far from being 122 orders of magnitude larger than the critical energy density" (arXiv:1205.3365, abstract).
- "Dynamical dark energy solves the cosmological-constant problem": it does not (arXiv:hep-th/0603057, Sect. IV.2; arXiv:1205.3365, Sect. X.2).
- "A CPL fit crossing w = −1 shows phantom dark energy": a canonical field "ranges in the region" −1 ≤ w ≤ 1 (arXiv:hep-th/0603057, Sect. V.1); thawing quintessence with curvature "can fit the recent data as well" (arXiv:2504.15190) and KMIX "may account for the DESI preference without invoking true phantom behavior" (arXiv:2511.23463) [src: abstracts].
- "Dynamical dark energy after DESI eases the H0 tension": ω(z) > −1 "drives H0 towards lower values rather than higher ones" (arXiv:2512.10585) [src: Sect. V].
- "Constant-w interacting fluids are fine at the background level, so they are viable": they can be unstable at the perturbation level (arXiv:0804.0232) [src: abstract].
