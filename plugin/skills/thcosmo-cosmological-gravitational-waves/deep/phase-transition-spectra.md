## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- The sound-shell derivation of Hindmarsh & Hijazi 2019 (arXiv:1909.10040) works in flat spacetime and cosmic time: "We assume that the phase transition completes in much less than a Hubble time, and so that we can neglect the expansion of the Universe", with the metric in "the synchronous gauge" [src: Sect. 3.1]. The skill's conventions block (`formalism.md#conventions`) uses conformal time and the scale factor in the metric; the two differ by name only here, and no conversion is made.
- Roper Pol, Procacci & Caprini (arXiv:2308.12943, Roper Pol et al. 2023) and Stomberg & Roper Pol (arXiv:2508.04263, Stomberg & Roper Pol 2025) use conformal time in a radiation-dominated expanding universe, with ${\cal H}_{*}$ the conformal Hubble rate at production (arXiv:2508.04263) [src: Sect. 2]. Guo, Sinha, Vagie & White (arXiv:2007.08537, Guo et al. 2020) write $H_s$ for the Hubble rate "when the source becomes active" (arXiv:2007.08537) [src: Sect. 1]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- The wall speed is $v_{\rm w}$ in the sound-shell papers and the 2019 report, $\xi_w$ in Caprini et al. 2024 (arXiv:2403.03723), where the fluid amplitude enters as the kinetic-energy fraction $K$ [src: Sect. 2.2.1]; in the numerical simulations cited by arXiv:1909.10040, $\overline{U}_{\text{f}}$ "denotes the enthalpy-weighted RMS fluid velocity", a difference "negligible" for the non-relativistic flows it considers [src: arXiv:1909.10040, Sect. 3.3, footnote 3]. Their relation is Eq. 22 of the 2019 report (`formalism.md#first-order-phase-transitions`). <!-- num: not a value -->
- Redshift factors are printed with different normalisations: compare Eq. 20 of the 2019 report (`formalism.md#first-order-phase-transitions`) with $F_{\rm GW}^{0}=\left(a_{\ast}/a_{0}\right)^{4}\left(H_{\ast}/H_{0}\right)^{2}\simeq 1.6\times 10^{-5}\left(g_{\ast}/100\right)^{-1/3}$ in arXiv:2508.04263 [src: Sect. 2, inline, text after Eq. 1]. Whether a factor $h^2$ separates them is not stated in the places read; check it before combining amplitudes from the two `[der]`.

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — gravitational waves from a shear-stress correlator
<!-- key: step-1-gravitational-waves-from-a-shear-stress -->

The tensor perturbation is sourced by the transverse-traceless part of the stress, and the power spectrum is $\frac{1}{12H^{2}}$ times that of $\dot h$ [src: Eq. 18]. Only the off-diagonal part of the stress is kept, $\tau_{ij}=\gamma^{2}wv_{i}v_{j}+\partial_{i}\phi\partial_{j}\phi$ [src: Eq. 19]. With the unequal-time correlator (UETC) $U_\Pi$ of Eq. 22, and "Averaging over a number of oscillations at wavenumber $k$", the spectral density of $\dot h$ is $\displaystyle P_{\dot{h}}(k,t)=(16\pi G)^{2}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}dt_{1}\int_{0}^{t}dt_{2}\cos[k(t_{1}-t_{2})]U_{\Pi}(k,t_{1},t_{2}).$ (arXiv:1909.10040) [src: Eq. 24]. Everything that follows is a model for $U_\Pi$.

### Step 2 — Gaussian, irrotational, non-relativistic sound waves
<!-- key: step-2-gaussian-irrotational-non-relativistic -->

Three assumptions reduce $U_\Pi$ to the velocity power spectrum [src: Sect. 3.2]. First, the fluid dominates the stress and velocities are non-relativistic, $\tau_{ij}\simeq\bar{w}v_{i}v_{j}.$ [src: Eq. 25]; the footnote states the condition: this "is a very accurate assumption unless the bubble walls are sufficiently weakly coupled that they run away", and "The sound shell model does not apply in this case" [src: Sect. 3.2, footnote 2]. Second, "Based on the results of numerical simulations [10, 11, 12], it is justifiable to assume that the velocity field is irrotational and statistically homogeneous", so that $\left\langle\tilde{v}^{i}_{\textbf{q}_{1}}(t_{1})\tilde{v}^{*j}_{\textbf{q}_{2}}(t_{2})\right\rangle=\hat{q}_{1}^{i}\hat{q}_{1}^{j}G(q_{1},t_{1},t_{2}){(2\pi)^{3}}\delta({\textbf{q}_{1}-\textbf{q}_{2}}).$ [src: Eq. 27]. Third, the four-point function is Wick-expanded: "Any non-Gaussianity in the velocity field will lead to an extra contribution from the connected four-point correlator; we will assume that this is negligible" (arXiv:1909.10040) [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 31]. The result is Eq. 30 of the paper, a convolution of two factors G.

The velocity is then a superposition of linear sound waves with $\omega=c_{\text{s}}q$ [src: Sect. 3.2, inline, text after Eq. 37]. The correlator of the plane-wave amplitudes has a stationary part $P_v$ and a part $Q_v$ that depends on $t_1+t_2$; because the collision times $t_{\rm i}$ are spread, $Q_v$ involves an average over $e^{2i\omega t_{\text{i}}}$ and "One therefore expects $Q_{v}(q)$ to be less than $P_{v}(q)$, and significantly less for frequencies $\omega\gg 1/\Delta t_{\text{i}}$" [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 43]. Neglecting $Q_v$ gives $G(q,t_{1},t_{2})=2P_{v}(q)D(\omega,t_{1},t_{2}),$ [src: Eq. 44] with the decoherence function "approximately given by" $D(\omega,t_{1},t_{2})\simeq\cos[\omega(t_{1}-t_{2})].$ (arXiv:1909.10040) [src: Eq. 45]. The stationarity "breaks down for low frequencies, $\omega\ll 1/\Delta t_{\text{i}}$", where the authors state that these terms "contribute subdominantly" [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 45]; Step 5 shows that this is where later work disagrees.

### Step 3 — the long-time limit and the kernel
<!-- key: step-3-the-long-time-limit-and-the-kernel -->

Inserting Eq. 44 into Eq. 24 leaves a time integral of three cosines whose rate "asymptotes at large times to a δ-function", $\dot{\Delta}(t,k,q,\tilde{q})\to\frac{\pi}{8}\sum_{\pm\pm\pm}\delta(\pm k\pm\omega\pm\tilde{\omega}).$ [src: Eq. 53], of which "Only $k-\omega-\tilde{\omega}$ can vanish": the gravitational wave at wavenumber $k$ is produced by pairs of sound waves with $\tilde{q}=k/c_{s}-q$ [src: Eq. 56]. The non-stationary $Q_v$ terms "will produce only oscillatory contributions" and are dropped [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 54]. With the scaled spectral density $P_{v}(q)=L_{\text{f}}^{3}\overline{U}_{\text{f}}^{2}\tilde{P}_{v}(qL_{\text{f}}),$ [src: Eq. 57] the dimensionless kernel is $\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{\text{gw}}(y)=\frac{1}{4\pi yc_{\text{s}}}\left(\frac{1-c_{s}^{2}}{c^{2}_{s}}\right)^{2}\int_{z_{-}}^{z_{+}}\frac{dz}{z}\frac{(z-z_{+})^{2}(z-z_{-})^{2}}{(z_{+}+z_{-}-z)}\bar{P}_{v}(z)\bar{P}_{v}(z_{+}+z_{-}-z).$ [src: Eq. 61], with $z_{\pm}=y(1\pm c_{s})/{2c_{s}}$ [src: Sect. 3.3, inline, text after Eq. 58]. A stationary velocity field of lifetime $\tau_{\rm v}$ then gives ${\mathcal{P}}_{\text{gw}}(k)=3\left(\Gamma\overline{U}_{\text{f}}^{2}\right)^{2}(H\tau_{\text{v}})\left(HL_{\text{f}}\right)\frac{(kL_{\text{f}})^{3}}{2\pi^{2}}\tilde{P}_{GW}(kL_{\text{f}})$ (arXiv:1909.10040) [src: Eq. 62]. The factor $H\tau_{\rm v}$ is the linear growth with source duration that Step 5 revisits; the paper states that "If $H\tau_{\text{nl}}\gg 1$, it can be shown that $H\tau_{\text{v}}\to 1$", and that "A better estimate of $\tau_{\text{v}}$ for higher speed flows, which may be the most relevant ones [19], is clearly vital" [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 62].

### Step 4 — the velocity spectrum from bubbles, and the power laws
<!-- key: step-4-the-velocity-spectrum-from-bubbles-and -->

The sound-shell model computes $\tilde P_v$ <!-- eq: notation --> by superposing single-bubble fluid shells, released as free sound waves at the collision times drawn from an exponential or a simultaneous nucleation history [src: Sects. 4.1–4.2]; that hydrodynamics is in `thcosmo-baryogenesis-phase-transitions` (planned). Two scales appear: $L_{\rm f}$ "set by the mean bubble separation $R_*$", and the shell width, "a factor $\lvert c_{\text{s}}-v_{\text{w}}\rvert$ smaller than $R_*$" (arXiv:1909.10040) [src: Sect. 3.4]. A velocity spectrum $\propto k^n$ maps to "$\mathcal{P}_{\text{gw}}(k)\propto k^{2n-1}$"; causality and analyticity give $n=5$ at low $k$, hence "$\mathcal{P}_{v}(k)\propto k^{5}$ and ${\mathcal{P}}_{\text{gw}}(k)\propto k^{9}$", while the earlier claim of $k^5$ of their Ref. [13] is stated to be incorrect [src: Sect. 3.4]. The same section notes that at low $k$ the simulated spectra "are dominated by long-wavelength modes generated during the bubble collision phase", so that the acoustic production "is obscured" there [src: Sect. 3.4]. <!-- num: not a value -->

The 2026 analysis of Caprini, Midiri, Procacci & Roper Pol (Caprini et al. 2026) revises the location of the two breaks of the velocity spectrum: "the breaks in the velocity spectrum are not associated to the bubble size and the sound shell thickness, as previously proposed, but to the position of the discontinuities", which matters "particularly" for supersonic deflagrations (arXiv:2604.02240) [src: abstract]. Barni, Blasi, Madge & Vanvlasselaer apply the model to inverse transitions, in which "the plasma is sucked in by the expanding bubbles" (arXiv:2510.21439, Barni et al. 2025) [src: abstract].

### Step 5 — expansion, source duration and the suppression factor
<!-- key: step-5-expansion-source-duration-and-the -->

The flat-space result grows linearly with the source duration. Guo et al. redo the derivation in an expanding universe, for radiation and matter domination [src: abstract]. After the integral over the mean time $y_+$, the integrand takes the form $\displaystyle\left[\int dy_{+}\cdots\right]=\frac{1}{2}\Upsilon(y)\cos\left(\tilde{\tilde{k}}y_{-}\right).$ (arXiv:2007.08537) [src: Eq. 166], and keeping the dominant non-oscillatory term, $\displaystyle\Upsilon_{\text{RD}}=1-\frac{1}{y}.$ [src: Eq. 168] and $\displaystyle\Upsilon_{\text{MD}}=\frac{2}{3}\left(1-\frac{1}{y^{3/2}}\right).$ (arXiv:2007.08537) [src: Eq. 170], which approach "1 for RD and 2/3 for MD, irrespective of how long the source lasts", because of "the dilution of the source over time" [src: Sect. 5.2, text after Eq. 166]. In terms of the sound-wave lifetime, for radiation domination, $\Upsilon=1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+2\tau_{\text{sw}}H_{s}}},$ (arXiv:2007.08537) [src: Eq. 3], entering $\displaystyle h^{2}\Omega_{\text{GW}}=8.5\times 10^{-6}\left(\frac{100}{g_{s}(T_{e})}\right)^{1/3}\Gamma^{2}\bar{U}_{f}^{4}\left[\frac{H_{s}}{\beta(v_{w})}\right]v_{w}\times\Upsilon.$ (arXiv:2007.08537) [src: Eq. 2]. The asymptote is not reached when shocks and turbulence disrupt the source "at around $\tau_{\rm sw}H_{s}\sim H_{s}R_{\ast}/\bar{U}_{f}$", so that "there is a suppression to the standard spectrum" (arXiv:2007.08537) [src: Sect. 1, text after Eq. 3].

The shock time is the estimate $\tau_{\rm sh}\sim L_{f}/\bar{U}_{f}\,,$ (arXiv:2003.07360) [src: Eq. 2.20] of Ellis, Lewicki & No (arXiv:2003.07360, Ellis et al. 2020), with $\bar{U}_{f}\simeq\sqrt{\frac{3}{4}\frac{\kappa\alpha}{1+\alpha}}\,.$ [src: Eq. 2.19]; the comparison with the Hubble time decides "whether the linear evolution of the fluid flow and the GW sources associated with it can be considered long-lasting ($H\,\tau_{\rm sh}\gtrsim 1$)" [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 2.20]. They argue that "in models without a zero-temperature barrier and in scale-invariant models" the sound-wave period "lasts only for a fraction of a Hubble time after a generic first-order cosmological phase transition, whereas it may last longer in some models with a zero-temperature barrier that feature severe supercooling" (arXiv:2003.07360) [src: abstract].

Roper Pol et al. 2023 locate the approximation behind Υ. Ref. [59] of their paper (arXiv:2007.08537) approximates the product of the two conformal times by the square of their mean (arXiv:2308.12943) [src: Sect. V.1, text before Eq. 84], which yields Eq. 84 with $\Upsilon(\delta\tau_{\rm fin})=\int_{\tau_{*}}^{\tau_{\rm fin}}\frac{{\rm\,d}\tau_{1}}{{\cal H}_{*}\tau_{1}^{2}}=1-\frac{1}{\tau_{\rm fin}{\cal H}_{*}}.$ (arXiv:2308.12943) [src: Eq. 85], justified "only in the asymptotic limit $k\delta\tau_{\rm fin}\to\infty$" [src: Sect. V.1, inline, text after Eq. 86]. The full integral in the $k\to 0$ limit gives instead $\tilde{\Delta}_{0}(\delta\tau_{\rm fin})\simeq A\ln^{2}(\tau_{\rm fin}{\cal H}_{*}),$ (arXiv:2308.12943) [src: Eq. 87] with "$A\in[0.5,1]$", quadratic in the duration for a short source [src: Sect. V.1, text after Eq. 87]. Their conclusion, as stated: the linear growth and Υ "are valid for stationary processes only under specific assumptions", which "do not hold in the $k\to 0$ limit", and "the causality tail, proportional to $k^{3}$, is also proportional to $\ln^{2}(1+\delta\tau_{\rm fin}{\cal H}_{*})$" (arXiv:2308.12943) [src: Sect. IV]. Υ remains usable "when $k\gg 1/\delta\tau_{\rm fin}$", and around the peak if in addition "$\delta\tau_{\rm fin}/R_{*}\gg 1$" [src: Sect. V.1, text after Eq. 87]. <!-- num: not a value -->

The same paper traces the $k^9$ of Step 4 to the definition of the growth rate: arXiv:1909.10040 defines $\dot\Delta$ <!-- eq: notation --> as a derivative in cosmic time of a quantity "defined after averaging over time and ... valid only in the free propagation regime at late times", which the authors "consider ... to be misleading"; with the full time dependence they find $k^3$ as $k\to 0$, "in general" in agreement with simulations, with the footnote that "This agreement is not always completely clear, since the numerical studies of the IR regime of the GW spectrum are computationally challenging" (arXiv:2308.12943) [src: Sect. IV.3 and footnote 8]. <!-- num: not a value -->

### Step 6 — beyond linear sound waves: a decaying, locally stationary source
<!-- key: step-6-beyond-linear-sound-waves-a-decaying -->

For strong transitions the kinetic energy decays during sourcing. Stomberg & Roper Pol model the UETC as locally stationary, $E_{\Pi}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},k)=2\,k^{2}\,K^{2}(\eta_{+})\,f(\eta_{-},k)\,,$ (arXiv:2508.04263) [src: Eq. 2], where $f$ "characterizes the stationary UETC, already considered in the sound-shell model"; this "reduces to the stationary one assumed in the sound-shell model when the kinetic energy does not decay in time" [src: Sect. 2]. The amplitude then depends on $K_{\rm int,exp}^{2}={\cal H}_{\ast}^{-1}\int_{\eta_{\ast}}^{\eta_{\rm fin}}\frac{K^{2}(\eta_{+})}{\eta_{+}^{2}}{\rm\,d}\eta_{+}\,.$ [src: Eq. 3], through $\Omega_{\rm GW}(k)=\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathrm{tot}}}\frac{d\rho_{\mathrm{GW}}}{d\ln k}=3\,F_{\rm GW}^{0}\,\tilde{\Omega}_{\rm GW}\,K_{\rm int,exp}^{2}\,{\cal H}_{\ast}{\cal R}_{\ast}\,S(k{\cal R}_{\ast})\,,$ (arXiv:2508.04263) [src: Eq. 1], with "$\tilde{\Omega}_{\rm GW}\sim{\cal O}(10^{-2})$" [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 1]. The GW production from non-linear compressional motion "seems to be well described" by this assumption, according to the model and Higgsless simulations the authors review (arXiv:2508.04263) [src: Sect. 2].

`[der]` For a constant kinetic-energy fraction the integral defining $K_{\rm int,exp}^{2}$ reduces to the square of that fraction times the suppression factor of Roper Pol et al. 2023; the decay of the fraction is what replaces the cut at the shock time. <!-- num: not a value -->

### Step 7 — turbulence
<!-- key: step-7-turbulence -->

Vortical turbulence is not a superposition of free waves, and its UETC is modelled instead. Auclair et al. (arXiv:2205.02588, Auclair et al. 2022) show with simulations "that the unequal time correlation function (UETC) of the Fourier components of the velocity field is Gaussian in the time difference, as predicted by the 'sweeping' decorrelation model" [src: abstract]. Since the production at wavenumber $k$ takes a time of order $1/k$, "the SGWB signal on the scales of interest – specifically, around the peak – is fully established before the turbulence has decayed appreciably", while "On even larger scales, one expects the $k^{3}$ increase typical of uncorrelated sources" (arXiv:2205.02588) [src: Sect. 7.2.3]. They therefore treat the source as constant for $\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cut}}\tau_{\xi_{*}}$ eddy times, with $\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cut}}$ set "to 7 as it gives the best fit to the result of the numerical integration" [src: Sect. 7.2.3], and approximate the time integral as $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{gw}}(k,\tau_{*},\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cut}}\tau_{\xi_{*}})\approx\begin{cases}\ln^{2}\quantity(1 + \ncut\tauxist/ \tdevel)&\text{ for}~\sqrt{2}\,k<1/(\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cut}}\tau_{\xi_{*}})\,,\\ \ln^{2}\quantity[1 + (\sqrt{2}k\tdevel)^{-1}]&\text{ for}~\sqrt{2}\,k>1/(\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cut}}\tau_{\xi_{*}})\,.\end{cases}$ (arXiv:2205.02588) [src: Eq. 104], the macros as printed in the rendering. The logarithm squared below the inverse source lifetime is the same structure as Eq. 87 of arXiv:2308.12943 for sound waves; that paper lists the quadratic dependence for turbulence among its references (arXiv:2308.12943) [src: Sect. IV]. Earlier direct MHD simulations without the "simplifying assumptions of earlier analytic work" place the peak "in the mHz frequency range for a signal produced at the electroweak scale", if the energy-carrying scale is "about a hundredth of the Hubble radius" (arXiv:1903.08585, Roper Pol et al. 2019) [src: abstract].

### Step 8 — what changed between the LISA templates of 2019 and 2024
<!-- key: step-8-what-changed-between-the-lisa-templates -->

The two templates are in `formalism.md#first-order-phase-transitions`. The changes, with the reason given in the papers read:

1. **Shape.** 2019 uses the single-peak ansatz $C(s)=s^{3}\left(\frac{7}{4+3s^{2}}\right)^{7/2},$ (arXiv:1704.05871) [src: Eq. 36], fitted to scalar-plus-fluid lattice simulations of Hindmarsh et al. 2017, "$\propto k^{3}$" at small $k$ "turning over to $\propto k^{-4}$" [src: Sect. VI]. 2024 uses a double broken power law with breaks at $R_*$ and the shell thickness, and slopes from "the result of Ref. [42]" (arXiv:2403.03723) [src: Sect. 2.2.1], Ref. [42] being the Higgsless simulations of Jinno et al. (arXiv:2209.04369, Jinno et al. 2022). Their comparison table gives IR, intermediate and UV slopes 9, 1, −3 for the sound-shell model, "–", 1, −3 for scalar-plus-fluid lattices, and 3, 1, −3 for Higgsless simulations (arXiv:2209.04369) [src: Table 2]. <!-- num: not a value -->
2. **Source duration.** 2019 assumes a source lasting about a Hubble time and multiplies the amplitude by the shock-time factor when shocks form earlier (`approximations.md#phase-transition-templates`); 2024 writes $H_{*}\tau_{\rm sw}={\rm min}\left[H_{*}\tau_{\rm sh},1\right]$ inside the amplitude, with $\bar{v}_{f}^{2}=\Gamma^{-1}\,K$ "valid in the subrelativistic bulk velocity limit" (arXiv:2403.03723) [src: Sect. 2.2.1, inline, text after Eq. 11]. Neither template uses the Υ of Step 5 or the logarithm squared of Eq. 87 of arXiv:2308.12943. <!-- num: not a value -->
3. **Efficiency.** 2024 takes $K\simeq 0.6\,\kappa\,\alpha/(1+\alpha)$, the 0.6 accounting "for the efficiency in producing kinetic energy in the bulk fluid motion with respect to the single bubble case, as found in Ref. [42]", "a conservative estimate" relative to 2019 (arXiv:2403.03723) [src: Sect. 2.2.1, inline, text after Eq. 11].
4. **Numerical method behind the fit.** 2019 rests on simulations that couple the scalar field to the fluid "via a phenomenological friction parameter"; the Higgsless approach integrates out the Higgs field so that shocks "are also resolved" (arXiv:2403.03723) [src: Sect. 2.2.1]. Jinno et al. find that "for weak phase transitions the scale $q_{1}$ seems to follow the shell thickness $1/\xi_{\rm shell}$ closely, while for stronger phase transitions it does not", the "most plausible explanation" being "nonlinear effects during the first collision of sound shells" (arXiv:2209.04369) [src: Sect. 5].
5. **Amplitude prefactor.** The 2017 lattice paper carries an erratum: "There is a factor of 3 missing from the right hand side of Eq. 39", whose coefficient becomes 2.061 instead of 0.687, the same factor is "missing from Eq. 45", and the corrected SNR is "a factor of 0.687×0.5≈0.34 lower" than in its original figure; the tabulated amplitudes "are correct and include this factor" (arXiv:1704.05871) [src: Erratum].
6. **Check before reuse.** The sound-wave template of the 2019 report is written with the coefficient of its Eq. 29 (`formalism.md#first-order-phase-transitions`); whether that normalisation already absorbs the factor of the 2017 erratum was not checked in the places read `[der]`. <!-- num: not a value -->

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **Sound-shell model (analytic).** Linear, Gaussian, stationary sound waves (Step 2); gives the shape from single-bubble profiles and the nucleation history. Its "gravitational wave peak power and frequency are in good agreement with published numerical simulations, where bubbles are nucleated simultaneously" (arXiv:1909.10040) [src: abstract]. Cost: no shocks, no vorticity, no runaway walls [src: Sect. 3.2, footnote 2]; the flat-space version misstates the IR tail (Step 5).
- **Scalar field plus fluid on a lattice.** Resolves the wall and couples it by a friction term; the 2015 simulations "confirm that the dominant source of gravitational waves is sound waves" (arXiv:1504.03291, Hindmarsh et al. 2015) [src: abstract]. In the shape paper, the $k^{-3}$ of the model "emerges clearly for detonations", while deflagrations "exhibit a steeper high-$k$ decay and an extra feature" (arXiv:1704.05871) [src: abstract]. Cost, as Jinno et al. put it: "Numerically, this is a rather demanding task since several length scales enter the dynamics" (arXiv:2209.04369) [src: abstract].
- **Higgsless simulations.** The Higgs field "only enters as a boundary condition in the equation of state" and the hydrodynamics is "solved fully nonlinearly" (arXiv:2209.04369) [src: Sect. 5]; this is the basis of the 2024 slopes and amplitude constant (Step 8).
- **Hybrid simulations.** Spherical 1D profiles embedded in 3D; according to Jinno et al. this "has many limitations but no clear advantages", the embedding being linear (arXiv:2209.04369) [src: Sect. 5].
- **Expanding-universe lattices.** For slow, strong transitions, the normalised amplitude is "of order $\mathcal{O}(10^{-2})$, in alignment with Minkowski-spacetime results", while the spectra "differ markedly from those scenarios" (arXiv:2610.14806, Zou et al. 2026) [src: abstract] (`approximations.md#phase-transition-templates`).
- **Scalar-only sources (bubble collisions).** Envelope, bulk-flow and scalar-lattice slopes (IR, UV) are 3 and −1, 1 and −3, 3 and −1.5 respectively (arXiv:2209.04369) [src: Table 1]; these feed Template I of arXiv:2403.03723 and matter only when the walls run away. <!-- num: not a value -->

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **The $k^9$ infrared tail.** The flat-space sound-shell result $\mathcal{P}_{\rm gw}\propto k^9$ (arXiv:1909.10040) [src: Sect. 3.4, inline] is contradicted by the $k^3$ found with the full time dependence (arXiv:2308.12943) [src: Sect. IV.3] and by Higgsless and hybrid simulations (arXiv:2209.04369) [src: Table 2]. Check: a spectrum steeper than $k^3$ below the peak needs a justification. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Υ used at low frequency.** Eq. 85 of arXiv:2308.12943 holds only for $k\delta\tau_{\rm fin}\to\infty$; below $1/\delta\tau_{\rm fin}$ the duration enters as $\ln^2$ [src: Sect. V.1]. Check: the regime of the peak relative to $1/\delta\tau_{\rm fin}$.
