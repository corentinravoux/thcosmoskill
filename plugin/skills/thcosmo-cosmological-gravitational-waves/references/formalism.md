# Formalism of cosmological gravitational waves

Scope: definitions and governing equations of tensor perturbations and stochastic gravitational-wave backgrounds of cosmological origin: the tensor equation of motion and the energy density of a background, the irreducible inflationary background and its transfer function, inflationary sources beyond vacuum fluctuations, preheating, first-order phase transitions, topological defects, scalar-induced waves, and propagation on cosmological backgrounds and in modified gravity.

## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

The hub's atlas (`theoretical-cosmology/references/conventions.md`, sections "Metric signature and curvature signs", "Units, Planck mass and where 8πG appears", "Fourier convention and power-spectrum normalisation" and "Tensor-to-scalar ratio and the pivot scale") collects the general choices. The anchor review of this skill, Caprini & Figueroa 2018 (arXiv:1801.04268), fixes the following; other sources are compared with it in the table at the end of this block. <!-- num: not a value -->

- Metric, signature $(-,+,+,+)$, conformal time, tensor perturbation inside the spatial metric: $ds^{2}=a^{2}(\eta)\,\left[-d\eta^{2}+(\delta_{ij}+h_{ij})\,dx^{i}dx^{j}\right]\,.$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 66]; $h_{ij}$ is transverse and traceless, and $G$ appears explicitly (no reduced Planck mass in the definitions below).
- Fourier convention: $\Pi_{ij}(\mathbf{x},t)=\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\Pi_{ij}(\mathbf{k},t)\,e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\,.$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 59], the same for $h_{ij}$ in Eq. 62.
- Polarisation tensors $e^{+}_{ij}(\mathbf{\hat{k}})=\hat{m}_{i}\,\hat{m}_{j}-\hat{n}_{i}\,\hat{n}_{j}\,,\;e^{\times}_{ij}(\mathbf{\hat{k}})=\hat{m}_{i}\,\hat{n}_{j}+\hat{n}_{i}\,\hat{m}_{j}\,,$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 63], normalised as $e_{ij}^{r}(\mathbf{\hat{k}})\,e_{ij}^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbf{\hat{k}})=2\,\delta_{rr^{\prime}}\,,$ [src: Eq. 64]. The factor 2 in this normalisation propagates into every amplitude below; other papers normalise to 1. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Present-day frequency $f=k/(2\pi\,a_{0})$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Sect. 3.2, inline, text after Eq. 90]. <!-- num: not a value -->

## The tensor equation of motion and free propagation
<!-- key: the-tensor-equation-of-motion-and-free -->

All from Caprini & Figueroa 2018 (arXiv:1801.04268), Sect. 2.4, in the conventions above.

- Equation of motion with a source: $\ddot{h}_{ij}(\mathbf{x},t)+3\,H\,\dot{h}_{ij}(\mathbf{x},t)-\frac{\mathbf{\nabla}^{2}}{a^{2}}\,h_{ij}(\mathbf{x},t)=16\pi G\,\Pi_{ij}^{TT}(\mathbf{x},t)\,,$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 57], with the anisotropic stress $a^{2}\,\Pi_{ij}=T_{ij}-p\,a^{2}\,(\delta_{ij}+h_{ij})\,,$ [src: Eq. 58].
- Transverse–traceless projection: $\Pi_{ij}^{\mathrm{TT}}(\mathbf{k})=\mathcal{O}_{ij,lm}(\mathbf{\hat{k}})\,\Pi_{lm}(\mathbf{k})=\left[P_{il}(\mathbf{\hat{k}})\,P_{jm}(\mathbf{\hat{k}})-\frac{1}{2}\,P_{ij}(\mathbf{\hat{k}})\,P_{lm}(\mathbf{\hat{k}})\right]\Pi_{lm}(\mathbf{k})\,,$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 60], with $P_{ij}(\mathbf{\hat{k}})=\delta_{ij}-\hat{k}_{i}\,\hat{k}_{j}\,,$ [src: Eq. 61].
- In conformal time, with $H_{ij}(\mathbf{k},\eta)=a\,h_{ij}(\mathbf{k},\eta)\,,$ [src: Eq. 67], the equation becomes $H_{ij}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{k},\eta)+\left(k^{2}-\frac{a^{\prime\prime}}{a}\right)\,H_{ij}(\mathbf{k},\eta)=16\pi G\,a^{3}\,\Pi_{ij}^{TT}(\mathbf{k},\eta)\,,$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 68].
- For a power-law scale factor $a(\eta)=a_{n}\eta^{n}$ (radiation $n=1$, matter $n=2$, de Sitter $n=-1$) the free solution is $h_{r}({\bf k},\eta)=\frac{A_{r}(\mathbf{k})}{a(\eta)}\eta\,j_{n-1}(k\eta)+\frac{B_{r}(\mathbf{k})}{a(\eta)}\eta\,y_{n-1}(k\eta)\,,$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 70].
- Sub-Hubble limit: $h_{r}(\mathbf{k},\eta)=\frac{A_{r}(\mathbf{k})}{a(\eta)}\,e^{ik\eta}+\frac{B_{r}(\mathbf{k})}{a(\eta)}\,e^{-ik\eta}\,,\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{for }k\gg\mathcal{H}\,.$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 71]: the amplitude decays as $1/a$. Super-Hubble limit: $h_{r}(\mathbf{k},\eta)=A_{r}(\mathbf{k})+B_{r}(\mathbf{k})\,\int^{\eta}\,\frac{d\eta^{\prime}}{a^{2}(\eta^{\prime})}\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{for }k\ll\mathcal{H}\,,$ [src: Eq. 74]: a constant mode and a decaying mode.

## Characterisation of a stochastic background
<!-- key: characterisation-of-a-stochastic-background -->

All from Caprini & Figueroa 2018 (arXiv:1801.04268), Sects. 3.2–3.3.

- For a statistically homogeneous, isotropic, unpolarised and Gaussian background, $\langle h_{r}(\mathbf{k},\eta)\,h^{*}_{p}(\mathbf{q},\eta)\rangle=\frac{8\pi^{5}}{k^{3}}\,\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})\,\delta_{rp}\,h_{c}^{2}(k,\eta)\,,$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 84], normalised so that $\langle h_{ij}(\mathbf{x},\eta)\,h_{ij}(\mathbf{x},\eta)\rangle=2\,\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{dk}{k}\,h_{c}^{2}(k,\eta)\,,$ [src: Eq. 85]. The factor 2 is "a convention motivated by the fact that the LHS involves contributions from two independent polarizations", and the convention of their Ref. [54] "differs by a factor 2" [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 85]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Energy density, defined only after an average over several wavelengths or periods [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 87]: $\rho_{\rm GW}\,=\,\frac{\langle\dot{h}_{ij}(\mathbf{x},t)\,\dot{h}_{ij}(\mathbf{x},t)\rangle}{32\pi G}\,=\,\frac{\langle h^{\prime}_{ij}(\mathbf{x},\eta)\,h^{\prime}_{ij}(\mathbf{x},\eta)\rangle}{32\pi G\,a^{2}(\eta)}\,=\,\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{dk}{k}\,\frac{d\rho_{\rm GW}}{d\mathrm{log}k}\,,$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 87]. For sub-Hubble modes $\frac{d\rho_{\rm GW}}{d\mathrm{log}k}=\frac{k^{2}\,h_{c}^{2}(k,\eta)}{16\pi G\,a^{2}(\eta)}\,.$ [src: Eq. 90], which dilutes as $\rho_{\rm GW}\propto a^{-4}$ [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 90].
- Spectral density and normalised spectrum: $S_{h}(f)=\frac{h_{c}^{2}(f)}{2f}\,,$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 92]; $\Omega_{\rm GW}(f)=\frac{1}{\rho_{c}}\,\frac{d\rho_{\rm GW}}{d\mathrm{log}f}\,,$ [src: Eq. 93], with $\rho_{c}=3H^{2}/(8\pi G)$; $h^{2}\Omega_{\rm GW}^{(0)}$ is used "because it is independent of the observational uncertainty on the value of $H_{0}$" [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 93].
- Relations to the detector variables, "note the factor two difference with respect to e.g. [54]" [src: Sect. 3.2, text before Eq. 94]: $\Omega_{\rm GW}^{(0)}(f)=\frac{4\pi^{2}}{3H_{0}^{2}}\,f^{3}\,S_{h}(f)\,.$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 94]; $S_{h}(f)=7.98\times 10^{-37}\,\left(\frac{\mathrm{Hz}}{f}\right)^{3}\,h^{2}\,\Omega_{\rm GW}(f)\,\frac{1}{\rm Hz}\,,$ [src: Eq. 95]; $h_{c}(f)=1.26\times 10^{-18}\,\left(\frac{\mathrm{Hz}}{f}\right)\,\sqrt{h^{2}\,\Omega_{\rm GW}(f)}\,.$ [src: Eq. 96].
- Redshifting from production (subscript p) to today: $h^{2}\,\Omega_{\rm GW}(k)=\frac{h^{2}}{\rho_{c}}\,\left(\frac{a_{p}}{a_{0}}\right)^{4}\,\rho_{p}\,\left(\frac{1}{\rho}\,\frac{d\rho_{\rm GW}}{d\mathrm{log}k}\right)_{p}\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{for }k\gg\mathcal{H}\,.$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 101]; with $x_{k}=\frac{k/a_{p}}{H_{p}}$ [src: Eq. 102], $f=\frac{1}{2\pi}\,\frac{k}{a_{0}}=\frac{x_{k}}{2\pi}\,\frac{a_{p}}{a_{0}}\,H_{p}\,.$ [src: Eq. 103]. A source at $t_p$ "cannot be correlated on length/time scales larger than $H_{p}^{-1}$", so the peak "certainly satisfies $x_{k}\geq 1$" [src: Sect. 3.3, inline, text after Eq. 103]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- For production during the radiation era: $h^{2}\,\Omega_{\rm GW}=1.6\times 10^{-5}\,\left(\frac{100}{g_{*}(T_{p})}\right)^{1/3}\,\left(\frac{1}{\rho}\,\frac{d\rho_{\rm GW}}{d\mathrm{log}k}\right)_{p}$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 104] and $f=2.6\times 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{Hz}\,\,\,x_{k}\,\left(\frac{g_{*}(T_{p})}{100}\right)^{1/6}\,\frac{T_{p}}{\mathrm{GeV}}$ [src: Eq. 105], with $g_{*}(T_{p})=g_{S}(T_{p})$ assumed for $T_p$ above an MeV [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 105]. A source that keeps $\rho_{\rm GW}$ a constant fraction of $\rho$ during radiation domination gives an "approximately flat" spectrum [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 103].
- Frequency bands quoted with Fig. 1: "$1\,{\rm Hz}\lesssim f\lesssim 10^{3}\,{\rm Hz}$ for ground-based interferometers, $10^{-5}\,{\rm Hz}\lesssim f\lesssim 0.1\,{\rm Hz}$ for LISA, $3\times 10^{-9}\,{\rm Hz}\lesssim f\lesssim 10^{-6}\,{\rm Hz}$ for Pulsar Timing Arrays, and $3.4\times 10^{-19}\,{\rm Hz}\lesssim f\lesssim 7\times 10^{-18}\,{\rm Hz}$ for the CMB" (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Fig. 1 caption].

## The irreducible inflationary background
<!-- key: the-irreducible-inflationary-background -->

From Caprini & Figueroa 2018 (arXiv:1801.04268), Sects. 5.1–5.2, which use the reduced Planck mass $m_{\mathrm{Pl}}=1/\sqrt{8\pi G}$ in this section [src: Sect. 5.1, text after Eq. 165] and the tensor normalisation of Eq. 64 above. <!-- num: not a value -->

- Quadratic tensor action and canonical variable: $S_{g}^{(2)}=-\,\frac{m_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}}{8}\,\int d\eta\,d^{3}\mathbf{x}\,a^{2}(\eta)\,\eta^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_{\mu}h_{ij}\,\partial_{\nu}h_{ij}=\frac{m_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}}{4}\,\sum_{r=+,\times}\,\int d\eta\,{d^{3}\mathbf{k}\over(2\pi)^{3}}\,a^{2}(\eta)\,\left[|h^{\prime}_{r}(\mathbf{k},\eta)|^{2}-k^{2}\,|h_{r}(\mathbf{k},\eta)|^{2}\right]=\frac{1}{2}\,\sum_{r=+,\times}\,\int d\eta\,{d^{3}\mathbf{k}\over(2\pi)^{3}}\,\left[|v^{\prime}_{r}|^{2}-k^{2}\,|v_{r}|^{2}+\frac{a^{\prime\prime}}{a}\,|v_{r}|^{2}\right]\,,$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eqs. 171–172], with $v_{r}(\mathbf{k},\eta)=\frac{m_{\mathrm{Pl}}}{\sqrt{2}}\,a(\eta)\,h_{r}(\mathbf{k},\eta)\,.$ [src: Eq. 173].
- Mode equation $v^{\prime\prime}_{k}+\omega_{k}^{2}(\eta)\,v_{k}=0\,,~~~~~~{\rm with}~~~\omega_{k}(\eta)^{2}\equiv k^{2}-{a^{\prime\prime}\over a}~~.$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 179]; in quasi-de Sitter $v_{k}^{\prime\prime}+\left[k^{2}-{1\over\eta^{2}}\left(\nu^{2}-{1\over 4}\right)\right]v_{k}=0\,,~~~~~\nu\equiv{3\over 2}+\epsilon~~.$ [src: Eq. 183]; the Bunch–Davies solution is $v_{k}={\sqrt{\pi}\over 2}e^{{i\over 2}\left(\nu+{1\over 2}\right)}\sqrt{-\eta}\,H_{\nu}^{(1)}(-k\eta)\,,~~\forall\,k,\eta~~.$ [src: Eq. 185] (the constant phase is printed as $e^{\frac{i}{2}(\nu+\frac{1}{2})}$, without a factor π; as printed, no erratum found).
- Super-horizon amplitude: $|h_{k}(\eta)|\simeq\frac{H}{m_{\mathrm{Pl}}\,k^{3/2}}\,f(\epsilon)\left({k\over aH}\right)^{-\epsilon}\,,\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{ for }\hskip 14.22636ptk\ll aH$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 190], with $f(\epsilon)\simeq 1-0.27\epsilon$ [src: text after Eq. 190].
- Tensor power spectrum, defined by $\langle 0|\hat{h}_{ij}(\mathbf{k},\eta)\hat{h}^{*}_{ij}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\eta)|0\rangle={2\pi^{2}\over k^{3}}\mathcal{P}_{h}(k)\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\,,$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 192], so that $\langle 0|\hat{h}_{ij}(\mathbf{x},\eta)\hat{h}_{ij}(\mathbf{x},\eta)|0\rangle=\int{{\rm d}k\over k}~\mathcal{P}_{h}(k)\,.$ [src: Eq. 193]: $\mathcal{P}_{h}(k)\simeq\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\,\frac{H^{2}}{m_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}}\,f^{2}(\epsilon)\left({k\over aH}\right)^{-2\epsilon},\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{ for }\hskip 14.22636ptk\ll aH\,.$ [src: Eq. 194]; at horizon crossing $\mathcal{P}_{h}(k)\simeq~\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\,\frac{H_{k}^{2}}{m_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}}\,~,\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{ for }\hskip 14.22636ptk=a_{k}H_{k}\,.$ [src: Eq. 195].
- `[der]` Eq. 192 sums over $i,j$, and Eq. 193 has no factor 2, unlike Eq. 85 for $h_c^2$: $\mathcal{P}_h$ and $h_c^2$ are normalised differently in the same review.
- Tilt and consistency relation: $n_{T}(k)=\frac{d\mathrm{log}\mathcal{P}_{h}(k)}{d\mathrm{log}k}\,.$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 196]; $n_{T}\simeq-2\epsilon\,.$ [src: Eq. 197]; with $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}\simeq\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{H_{k}^{4}}{\dot{\phi}_{k}^{2}}\simeq{H_{k}^{2}\over 8\pi^{2}\epsilon m_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{ for }\hskip 14.22636ptk=a_{k}H_{k}\,.$ [src: Eq. 200] and $r=\frac{\mathcal{P}_{h}(k)}{\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)}\,.$ [src: Eq. 201], $r_{k}=16\,\epsilon\,.$ [src: Eq. 202] and $n_{T}(k)=-\frac{r_{k}}{8}\,.$ [src: Eq. 203], for single-field slow roll.
- Re-entry: in radiation domination $h_{r}^{\rm RD}({\bf k},\eta)=h^{\rm inf}(k)\,j_{0}(k\eta)\,,~~~~~~r=+,\times$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 205] with $h^{\rm inf}(k)\simeq\frac{H}{m_{\rm Pl}k^{3/2}}$ [src: Eq. 206]; in matter domination $h_{r}^{\rm MD}({\bf k},\eta)=h^{\rm inf}(k)\,\frac{3\,j_{1}(k\eta)}{k\eta}\,,~~~~~~r=+,\times~.$ [src: Eq. 207]. Equality scales: $\eta_{\rm eq}=\frac{2(\sqrt{2}-1)\sqrt{\Omega_{\rm rad}}}{a_{0}\,H_{0}\,\Omega_{\rm mat}}\simeq 86\,{\rm Mpc}\,,$ [src: Eq. 209]; $k_{\rm eq}=\frac{\sqrt{2}\,a_{0}\,H_{0}\,\Omega_{\rm mat}}{\sqrt{\Omega_{\rm rad}}}\simeq 1.3\cdot 10^{-2}\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\,.$ [src: Eq. 210]. The matching assumes an instantaneous radiation–matter transition and is expected "to fail for modes which are comparable to the inverse duration of the transition" [src: Sect. 5.2, text after Eq. 208].
- Energy spectrum today, with the transfer function $T$ defined by $h_{r}({\bf k},\eta_{0})=h^{\rm inf}(k)T(k,\eta_{0})\,,$ [src: Eq. 213]: $\Omega_{\rm GW}(k)=\frac{1}{12\,H_{0}^{2}\,a_{0}^{2}}\,[T^{\prime}(k,\eta_{0})]^{2}\mathcal{P}_{h}(k)\,,$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 218]; the approximation $\Omega_{\rm GW}(k)=\frac{3}{128}\,\Omega_{\rm rad}\,\mathcal{P}_{h}(k)\,\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{k_{\rm eq}}{k}\right)^{2}+\frac{16}{9}\right]\,,$ [src: Eq. 223] inserts a factor 1/2 for the oscillations, and its prefactor 3/128 and pivot $k_{\rm eq}$ "have been chosen in order to recover Eq. (4) of [84]" [src: Sect. 5.2, text after Eq. 223]. For modes entering in radiation domination, $\Omega_{\rm GW}\big|_{\rm rad}={\Omega_{\rm rad}\over 24}{\mathcal{P}}_{h}(k)$ [src: Sect. 5.2, inline, text after Eq. 230].
- Change of relativistic degrees of freedom at time $\bar\eta$, for modes that entered before it: $\frac{\rho_{\rm GW}(k,\eta_{0})|_{\bar{g}}}{\rho_{\rm GW}(k,\eta_{0})|_{g^{0}}}\simeq\left(\frac{g_{S}^{0}}{\bar{g}_{S}}\right)^{4/3}\left(\frac{\bar{g}_{*}}{g_{*}^{0}}\right)\,.$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 227]; the full Standard-Model treatment is Watanabe & Komatsu 2006 (arXiv:astro-ph/0604176), who state that "these features must exist" [src: abstract].
- A stiff epoch ($w>1/3$) before radiation domination, for $k>k_{\rm RD}$: $\Omega_{\rm GW}(k)={\Omega_{\rm rad}\over 24}{\mathcal{P}}_{h}(k)\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm RD}}\right)^{\frac{6w-2}{3w+1}}\,~~~~{\rm for}~k>k_{\rm RD}\,.$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 230]. The text after Eq. 230 prints the exponent as $(6w-2)(3w+1)$, a product, where Eq. 230 has the ratio; as printed, no erratum found.
- Neutrino free streaming adds a source: $h_{ij}^{\prime\prime}({\bf k},\eta)+2\mathcal{H}h_{ij}^{\prime}({\bf k},\eta)+k^{2}h_{ij}({\bf k},\eta)=-24\frac{\rho_{\nu}}{\bar{\rho}}\mathcal{H}^{2}\int_{\eta_{\nu}}^{\eta}d\tau\left[\frac{j_{2}(k(\eta-\tau))}{k^{2}(\eta-\tau)^{2}}\right]h_{ij}^{\prime}({\bf k},\tau)\,,$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 235], derived from the analytic anisotropic stress of free-streaming neutrinos by Weinberg 2004 (arXiv:astro-ph/0306304) [src: abstract]. Boyle & Steinhardt 2008 (arXiv:astro-ph/0512014) derive a transfer function that incorporates time-varying w(z), tensor anisotropic stress from free-streaming particles and departures from w = 1/3 in the radiation era [src: abstract].

## Inflationary sources beyond vacuum fluctuations
<!-- key: inflationary-sources-beyond-vacuum-fluctuations -->

Sustained gauge-field production by a pseudoscalar (axion) inflaton, from Caprini & Figueroa 2018 (arXiv:1801.04268), Sect. 6.1.2, which follows Sorbo 2011 (arXiv:1101.1525) and Barnaby & Peloso 2011 (arXiv:1011.1500):

- Coupling $\Delta{\cal L}=-\frac{1}{4\,\Lambda}\,\phi\,F_{\mu\nu}\,\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}\,,$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 238]; helicity modes obey $A_{\pm}^{\prime\prime}({\bf k},\,\tau)+\left[k^{2}\pm 2\,\xi\,\frac{k}{\tau}\right]A_{\pm}({\bf k},\,\tau)=0\,,~~~~\xi\equiv\frac{\dot{\phi}}{2\,\Lambda\,H}\,,$ [src: Eq. 239]; for $\xi>0$ "only $A_{+}$ is amplified", by a factor $\sim e^{\pi\,\xi}$ for $\xi\gtrsim{\cal O}(1)$ [src: Sect. 6.1.2, text after Eq. 239].
- Total tensor spectrum, vacuum plus particle production, for $\xi\gtrsim{\cal O}(1)$: $\mathcal{P}_{h}^{(tot)}\left(k\right)\equiv\frac{k^{3}}{2\pi^{2}}\sum_{a=\pm}\left|h_{a}\left(k\right)\right|^{2}=\mathcal{P}_{h}^{\rm(vac)}(k)+\mathcal{P}_{h}^{\rm(pp)}(k)=\mathcal{P}_{h}^{\rm(vac)}\left(1+\Delta\mathcal{P}_{h}\right)\simeq\frac{2\,H^{2}}{\pi^{2}\,m_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\left(1+4.3\cdot 10^{-7}\frac{H^{2}}{m_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\frac{e^{4\pi\xi}}{\xi^{6}}\right)\,.$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eqs. 240–241]; $H$ and $\xi$ are evaluated at horizon exit of each mode [src: Sect. 6.1.2, text after Eq. 241].
- Chirality: $\mathcal{P}_{h,+}^{\rm(pp)}\simeq 8.7\cdot 10^{-8}\frac{H^{4}}{m_{Pl}^{4}}\frac{e^{4\pi\xi}}{\xi^{6}}$ and $\mathcal{P}_{h,-}^{\rm(pp)}\simeq 2.1\cdot 10^{-3}\mathcal{P}_{h,+}^{\rm(pp)}$, so "the sourced GW background is highly chiral" (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Sect. 6.1.2, inline, text after Eq. 241].
- Energy fraction and local tilt: $h^{2}\Omega_{\rm GW}^{\rm(pp)}\simeq 1.5\cdot 10^{-13}(H/m_{\rm Pl})^{4}(e^{4\pi\xi}/{\xi^{6}})$ for $\xi>1$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Sect. 6.1.2, inline, text after Eq. 241]; $n_{T}\simeq-4\epsilon+\left(4\pi\xi-6\right)\left(\epsilon-\eta\right)\;,$ [src: Eq. 242], with $\epsilon=\epsilon_H$ and $\eta=\eta_\phi$ [src: text after Eq. 242]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- The scalar bispectrum is near equilateral with $f_{NL}^{\rm{equil}}\sim 10^{6}\,(H^{6}/|\dot{\phi}|^{3})(e^{6\pi\xi}/\xi^{9})$, and the non-detection by Planck gives $\xi\lesssim 2.5$ at 95% C.L. at CMB scales (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Sect. 6.1.2, text after Eq. 239]; a large signal at short scales needs $\xi$ to grow during inflation [src: same place].

Other inflationary sources that the review treats in Sects. 6.1.1 (transient particle production), 6.2 (spectator fields, EFT of inflation with enhanced small-scale tensors), 6.3 (modified gravity during inflation) and 6.5 (pre-Big-Bang, string gas, ekpyrotic) are not transcribed here; see "Not verified". <!-- num: not a value -->

## Preheating and non-perturbative sources
<!-- key: preheating-and-non-perturbative-sources -->

Parametric resonance after inflation, from Caprini & Figueroa 2018 (arXiv:1801.04268), Sect. 7.1:

- Inflaton $\phi$ coupled to a daughter field $X$ through $g^2\phi^2X^2$: $\ddot{X}-\frac{1}{a^{2}}\nabla^{2}X+3H\dot{X}+g^{2}\phi^{2}X=0\ ,$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 283]; in rescaled variables ${d^{2}\over dz^{2}}\chi_{k}+\left(\kappa^{2}+q_{\rm i}\varphi^{2}\right)\chi_{k}\simeq 0\,,~~~~~\kappa\equiv{k\over\omega_{\rm i}}\,,~~~z\equiv\omega_{\rm i}\int_{t}{dt^{\prime}\over a(t^{\prime})}\,,$ [src: Eq. 287] with resonance parameter $q_{\rm i}\equiv{g^{2}\Phi_{\rm i}^{2}\over\omega_{\rm i}^{2}}\,.$ [src: Eq. 288].
- The source is the TT part of the gradients, $\Pi_{ij}^{\rm TT}={1\over a^{2}}\left\{\partial_{i}\chi\,\partial_{j}\chi\right\}^{{\rm TT}}$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Sect. 7.1, inline, text after Eq. 290], and $\frac{d\rho_{\mathrm{\rm GW}}}{d\log k}\left(k,\tau\right)=\frac{2}{\pi}{Gk^{3}\over a^{4}(\tau)}\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau}d\tau^{\prime}d\tau^{\prime\prime}\,a(\tau^{\prime})\,a(\tau^{\prime\prime})\cos[k(\tau^{\prime}-\tau^{\prime\prime})]\,\Pi^{2}(k,\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime}),$ [src: Eq. 289].
- Redshifting to today through an epoch of equation of state $w$ before radiation domination: $h^{2}\Omega_{\rm GW}=h^{2}\Omega_{\rm rad}\left(a_{{\rm f}}\over a_{{}_{\rm RD}}\right)^{1-3w}\left(g_{S,0}\over g_{S,_{\rm RD}}\right)^{4\over 3}\left(g_{{}_{\rm RD}}^{*}\over g_{0}^{*}\right)\Omega_{{\rm GW}}^{({\rm f})}\,,$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 293].
- Peak amplitude at the end of production, from scaling arguments calibrated on lattice runs: $\Omega_{{\rm GW}}^{({\rm f})}(\kappa_{p})\simeq{C^{2}\over 8\pi^{4}}\frac{\omega_{\rm i}^{6}}{\rho_{\rm i}m_{Pl}^{2}}\,q_{\rm i}^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta}\,,$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 296]; "the peak amplitude of the GW background from parametric resonance, decreases with the resonance parameter $q_{\rm i}$" [src: Sect. 7.1, text after Eq. 298]. For $\frac{1}{2}m^2\phi^2$ preheating with $q_{\rm i}\gg 1$, a single peak at $f_{p}=\epsilon_{\rm f}^{1/4}\left(\frac{q_{\rm i}}{10^{4}}\right)^{0.67}\times 2.0\cdot 10^{8}\ {\rm Hz}\,,$ [src: Eq. 299].

## First-order phase transitions
<!-- key: first-order-phase-transitions -->

The dynamics of nucleation, bubble walls and the effective potential belong to `thcosmo-baryogenesis-phase-transitions` (planned); here only the parameters that enter the spectra and the spectral templates. The parameters $(H_{*},\beta,\alpha,v_{\rm w})$ are defined in Caprini et al. 2019 (arXiv:1910.13125), Sect. 2. <!-- num: not a value -->

- Nucleation probability per unit volume and time, and inverse duration: $\mathcal{P}=A(t)e^{-S_{\text{c}}(t)}.$ (arXiv:1910.13125) [src: Eq. 1]; $\beta(T)=\frac{d}{dt}\ln\mathcal{P}(T)\,,$ [src: Eq. 2]. Mean bubble separation: $R_{*}=\frac{(8\pi)^{\frac{1}{3}}}{\beta}\,\text{Max}(v_{\text{w}},c_{\text{s}})\,.$ [src: Eq. 6].
- Strength in the bag model, with $p_{+}=\frac{1}{3}\,a_{+}T^{4}-\epsilon\,,\quad e_{+}=a_{+}T^{4}+\epsilon\,,\quad p_{-}=\frac{1}{3}\,a_{-}T^{4}\,,\quad e_{-}=a_{-}T^{4}\,,$ (arXiv:1910.13125) [src: Eq. 8]: $\alpha=\frac{\epsilon}{a_{+}T^{4}}\,.$ [src: Eq. 9]; beyond the bag model, the trace-anomaly and energy definitions $\alpha_{\theta}\equiv\frac{4}{3}\frac{\Delta\theta(T_{\text{n}})}{w_{+}(T_{\text{n}})}\,,$ [src: Eq. 12] and $\alpha_{e}\equiv\frac{4}{3}\frac{\Delta e(T_{\text{n}})}{w_{+}(T_{\text{n}})}\,.$ [src: Eq. 13].
- Kinetic energy: the single-bubble efficiency $\kappa=\frac{3}{\epsilon\,v_{\text{w}}^{3}}\int\,w(\xi)\,v^{2}\gamma^{2}\xi^{2}d\xi\,.$ (arXiv:1910.13125) [src: Eq. 10] and the kinetic energy fraction $K=\frac{\kappa\alpha}{1+\alpha}.$ [src: Eq. 11]; equivalently $K=\frac{\langle{w\gamma^{2}v^{2}}\rangle}{\bar{e}}=\Gamma\bar{U}_{\rm f}^{2},$ [src: Eq. 22], with Γ ≃ 4/3 for an ultra-relativistic fluid [src: Sect. 3.1, text after Eq. 22].
- The efficiencies in deflagrations, detonations, hybrids and runaways are computed in Espinosa et al. 2010 (arXiv:1004.4187) [src: abstract].
- Redshift factor: $F_{\text{gw},0}=\Omega_{\gamma,0}\left(\frac{g_{s\,0}}{g_{s\,*}}\right)^{\frac{4}{3}}\frac{g_{*}}{g_{0}}.$ (arXiv:1910.13125) [src: Eq. 19], numerically $F_{\text{gw},0}=(3.57\pm 0.05)\times 10^{-5}\left(\frac{100}{g_{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}.$ [src: Eq. 20].

For sound waves, $\Omega_{\text{gw}}^{\text{ac}}\propto K^{2}\,(H_{*}R_{*}/c_{s})\,.$ [src: Eq. 25] for long-lasting acoustic production, and $\Omega_{\text{gw}}^{\text{ac}}\propto K^{3/2}\,(H_{*}R_{*})^{2}/c_{s}\,.$ [src: Eq. 26] when shocks develop within a Hubble time; for turbulence, $\Omega_{\text{gw}}^{\text{tu}}\propto K_{\perp}^{3/2}\,(H_{*}R_{*}).$ [src: Eq. 27] or $\Omega_{\text{gw}}^{\text{tu}}\propto K_{\perp}\,(H_{*}R_{*})^{2}.$ [src: Eq. 28], under two different assumptions on the turbulence lifetime [src: Sect. 3.2].
- The 2019 sound-wave template, "for wall speeds away from the Chapman-Jouguet speed" [src: Sect. 3.3, text before Eq. 29]: $\frac{d\Omega_{\text{gw},0}}{d\ln(f)}=0.687F_{\text{gw},0}K^{2}(H_{*}R_{*}/c_{s})\tilde{\Omega}_{\text{gw}}C\left(\frac{f}{f_{\text{p},0}}\right)\,,$ (arXiv:1910.13125) [src: Eq. 29], with $\tilde{\Omega}_{\text{gw}}\sim 10^{-2}$ from simulations [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 29], $C(s)=s^{3}\left(\frac{7}{4+3s^{2}}\right)^{\frac{7}{2}}$ [src: Eq. 30] and $f_{\text{p},0}\simeq 26\left(\frac{1}{H_{*}R_{*}}\right)\left(\frac{z_{\text{p}}}{10}\right)\left(\frac{T_{*}}{100\,\text{GeV}}\right)\left(\frac{g_{*}}{100}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\;\mu\text{Hz}.$ [src: Eq. 31], $z_{\text{p}}\simeq 10$ [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 31]. When the shock time is shorter than a Hubble time, $\frac{d\Omega_{\text{gw},0}}{d\ln(f)}=0.687F_{\text{gw},0}K^{3/2}(H_{*}R_{*}/\sqrt{c_{s}})^{2}\tilde{\Omega}_{\text{gw}}C\left(\frac{f}{f_{\text{p},0}}\right).$ [src: Eq. 32]; "most models of interest for LISA predict $H_{*}\tau_{\text{sh}}<1$, and so (32) is often the relevant expression" [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 32].
- The 2024 LISA templates (Caprini et al. 2024, arXiv:2403.03723). ## Topological defects
<!-- key: topological-defects -->

Network formation and evolution belong to `thcosmo-pbh-defects` (planned); here only what enters the spectra.

Cosmic-string loops, from Caprini & Figueroa 2018 (arXiv:1801.04268), Sect. 9.2, Nambu–Goto picture:

- The spectrum depends on "i) the string tension $G\mu$, ii) the size of cosmic string loops relative to the horizon at birth $\alpha$, iii) the spectral index $q$ of the emission spectrum, iv) the cut-off $n_{*}$ in the emission spectrum (mimicking radiation backreaction [612]), and finally v) the intercommutation probability $p$" (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Sect. 9.2]. For field-theory strings "typically as $G\mu=\pi(v/M_{Pl})^{2}$ (though the prefactor $\pi$ depends on conventions)" [src: Sect. 9.2, inline, text after Eq. 378]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Loop production rate from the long-string network: $\dot{N}_{<}={\rho_{>}\over\alpha\mu H(t)}\left(1-3w_{>}\right)\,.$ (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Eq. 379], which with the simulated $\langle v^{2}\rangle$ and $\rho_{>}=A\mu H^{2}(t)$ gives $\dot{N}_{<}\Big|_{\rm RD}\simeq 59.3H(t)$ and $\dot{N}_{<}\Big|_{\rm MD}\simeq 39.1H(t)$ [src: Sect. 9.2, inline, text after Eq. 379].
- Loops lose energy at $\dot{E}_{\rm GW}=\Gamma G\mu^{2}$, with $\Gamma\simeq 50$ "normally used" (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Sect. 9.2, inline, text after Eq. 379]; the number density per unit length is $n(l,t)\equiv{1\over V_{H}}{dN_{<}\over dl}={1\over V_{H}}{\dot{N}_{<}\over\dot{l}}={1\over V_{H}}{-\dot{N}_{<}\over(\alpha\dot{H}/H^{2}+\Gamma G\mu)}\,.$ [src: Eq. 380]. The inline expression for $\dot l$ just before Eq. 380 is printed with $\Gamma G$ where Eq. 380 has $\Gamma G\mu$; as printed, no erratum found.
- Emission into harmonics $f_{n}\equiv 2n/l$ with $P_{n}\equiv D_{q}/n^{q+1}$, "$q\approx 1/3$ for any string loop which has a cusp" (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Sect. 9.2, inline, text after Eq. 380]; spectral power $dP_{\rm GW}(f)=\Gamma G\mu^{2}l\mathcal{P}(f)df\,.$ [src: Eq. 381]; spectrum today ${d\rho^{(0)}\over df}\equiv\Gamma{G\mu^{2}}\int_{t_{*}}^{t_{0}}dt\left({a(t)\over a_{0}}\right)^{3}\int_{0}^{\alpha/H(t)}dl\,l\,n(l,t)\,\mathcal{P}((a_{0}/a(t))fl)\,.$ [src: Eq. 383].
- The $P_n\propto 1/n^{q+1}$ form "is based on the asymptotic behavior expected for large n" and "might be inaccurate for the lower harmonics, specially for $n=1$" (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Sect. 9.2, text after Eq. 383]. The harmonic expansion and the burst superposition "converge on the scale-invariant spectrum plateau" and diverge on the rising low-frequency branch (Zhang et al. 2026, arXiv:2610.02590) [src: abstract].
- The latest simulations quoted by the review prefer large loops, "of the order of $\mathcal{O}(0.1)$ times the size of the horizon at their time of birth" (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Sect. 9.2, text after Eq. 383]. In Abelian-Higgs field-theory simulations loops instead "decay by the emission of massive radiation within a Hubble time", so they "emit much less gravitational radiation than in the Nambu-Goto scenario" (Hindmarsh et al. 2017, arXiv:1703.06696) [src: abstract].

Domain walls, from Roshan & White 2024 (arXiv:2401.04388), Sect. IV.2, in signature $(+,-,-,-)$ [src: Eq. 118]:

- For $V(\phi)=\frac{\lambda}{4}(\phi^{2}-v^{2})^{2},$ (arXiv:2401.04388) [src: Eq. 114], the wall tension is printed as $\sigma=\int_{\infty}^{\infty}dx\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg[\frac{\partial\phi(x)}{dx}\bigg]^{2}+V(\phi(x))\bigg)=\sqrt{\frac{8\lambda}{9}}v^{3}.$ [src: Eq. 116]; the lower limit is printed as $+\infty$, as printed, no erratum found.
- Scaling network: $\rho_{\rm DW}=\sigma\frac{\mathcal{A}}{t},$ (arXiv:2401.04388) [src: Eq. 120], with $\mathcal{A}\simeq 0.8\pm 0.1$ [src: Sect. IV.2, text after Eq. 120].
- Annihilation by a potential bias $V_{\text{bias}}=V(-v)-V(+v)=\frac{4}{3}\epsilon v^{4}.$ (arXiv:2401.04388) [src: Eq. 122] at $t_{\text{ann}}=C_{\text{ann}}\frac{\sigma\mathcal{A}}{V_{\text{bias}}},$ [src: Eq. 123], that is $T_{\rm ann}=3.41\times 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{GeV}\,C_{\rm ann}^{-1/2}\mathcal{A}^{-1/2}\left(\frac{g_{*}(T_{\rm ann})}{10}\right)^{-1/4}\left(\frac{\sigma}{\mathrm{TeV}^{3}}\right)^{-1/2}\left(\frac{V_{\rm bias}}{\mathrm{MeV}^{4}}\right)^{1/2},$ [src: Eq. 124] in radiation domination.
- For annihilation by a population bias, lattice simulations find T_ann ∼ T_s B_s^0.8 (Barbini et al. 2026, arXiv:2607.18107) [src: abstract].

## Scalar-induced gravitational waves
<!-- key: scalar-induced-gravitational-waves -->

Second-order tensor modes sourced by first-order scalar perturbations, introduced for the radiation era by Ananda, Clarkson & Wands 2007 (arXiv:gr-qc/0612013) [src: abstract] and over "all observable wavelengths" by Baumann et al. 2007 (arXiv:hep-th/0703290) [src: abstract]; formulas below from the review of Domènech 2021 (arXiv:2109.01398), which uses the reduced Planck mass $M_{\rm pl}$ [src: Eq. 5.1]. The gauge dependence of second-order tensors is a question for `thcosmo-cosmological-perturbations` (planned); its essentials are in `approximations.md`. <!-- num: not a value -->

- For an epoch of constant $w$ and $c_s$ after inflation, a peak entering during it and an instantaneous transition to radiation domination, for $k\gg k_{\rm rh}$: $\Omega_{\rm GW,rh}=\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm rh}}\right)^{-2b}\int_{0}^{\infty}dv\int_{|1-v|}^{1+v}du\,{\cal T}(u,v,b,c_{s}){{\cal P}_{\cal R}(ku)}{{\cal P}_{\cal R}(kv)}\,,$ (arXiv:2109.01398) [src: Eq. 10.2], with $b=\frac{1-3w}{1+3w}\quad{\rm and}\quad y=1-\frac{1-c_{s}^{2}(u-v)^{2}}{2c_{s}^{2}uv}\,.$ [src: Eq. 10.5]; Eq. 10.3 gives the general transfer function and "is not valid for $c_{s}=0$" [src: Sect. 10.1, text after Eq. 10.5].
- Radiation domination ($b=0$): ${\cal T}_{RD}(u,v,c_{s},w=1/3)=\frac{y^{2}}{3c_{s}^{4}}\left(\frac{4v^{2}-(1-u^{2}+v^{2})^{2}}{4u^{2}v^{2}}\right)^{2}\times\left\{\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}y^{2}\Theta[c_{s}(u+v)-1]+\left(1-\frac{1}{2}y\ln\left|\frac{1+y}{1-y}\right|\right)^{2}\right\}\,,$ (arXiv:2109.01398) [src: Eq. 10.6], which "recovers the result of Kohri and Terada [63] after taking into account all numerical factors in (2.7)" [src: Sect. 10.1.1, text after Eq. 10.6]; Kohri & Terada 2018 (arXiv:1804.08577) computed the universal integral analytically [src: abstract].
- Kination ($b=-1/2$, $w=1$): ${\cal T}_{KD}(u,v,c_{s},w=1)=\frac{4}{3\pi c_{s}^{4}|1-y^{2}|}\left(\frac{4v^{2}-(1-u^{2}+v^{2})^{2}}{4u^{2}v^{2}}\right)^{2}\times\Bigg\{\left(1+3y^{2}\right)\Theta[c_{s}(u+v)-1]+\left(1-3y^{2}+3y\sqrt{|1-y^{2}|}\right)^{2}\Theta[1-c_{s}(u+v)]\Bigg\}\,.$ (arXiv:2109.01398) [src: Eq. 10.7].
- Today: $\Omega_{\rm GW,0}h^{2}=\Omega_{r,0}h^{2}\frac{1}{\rho_{r,0}}\frac{d\rho_{\rm GW,0}}{d\ln k}=1.62\times 10^{-5}\left(\frac{\Omega_{r,0}h^{2}}{4.18\times 10^{-5}}\right)\left(\frac{g_{*}(T_{\rm rh})}{106.75}\right)\left(\frac{g_{*s}(T_{\rm rh})}{106.75}\right)^{-4/3}\Omega_{\rm GW,rh}\,.$ (arXiv:2109.01398) [src: Eq. 5.2].
- Infrared tail: $\frac{d\ln\Omega_{\rm GW}^{\rm induced}}{d\ln k}(k_{\rm rh}\ll k\ll k_{p})\approx 3-2|b|\,.$ (arXiv:2109.01398) [src: Eq. 2.22], with a logarithmic correction for integer b and a slope $2-2|b|$ for an exact Dirac delta [src: Sect. 2.2.1, text after Eq. 2.22]; for modes entering in radiation domination $\frac{d\ln\Omega_{\rm GW}^{\rm induced}}{d\ln k}(k\ll k_{\rm rh})\approx 3\,,$ [src: Eq. 2.23]; "the far most IR tail of the induced GW spectrum indeed must go as $k^{3}$" [src: Sect. 2.2.1, text after Eq. 2.23]. For $b>3/2$ ($w<-1/15$) the spectrum peaks at $k\sim k_{\rm rh}$ [src: Sect. 2.2.1, text after Eq. 2.23]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Beyond second order, the infrared scaling "only depends on the expanding background", and non-Gaussianities "do not modify the infrared scaling" (Yuan 2026, arXiv:2609.17928) [src: abstract].
- In matter domination at third order the kernels grow secularly, Ω_GW^(3) ∝ x^6 against Ω_GW^(2) ∝ x^2 at x ≡ kη ≫ 1 (Zhou 2026, arXiv:2610.08045) [src: abstract]; "higher-order SIGWs inevitably become nonperturbative during a sufficiently long MD era" [src: abstract].

## Propagation on cosmological backgrounds and in modified gravity
<!-- key: propagation-on-cosmological-backgrounds-and-in -->

Two-home rule: the speed of tensors as a constraint on theories (GW170817, the Horndeski and DHOST conditions, graviton decay) is in `thcosmo-modified-gravity`, file `deep/speed-of-tensors-constraints.md`; standard-siren measurements are in cosmoskill `cosmo-multimessenger`. Here only the propagation equations. <!-- num: not a value -->

- Parameterised propagation on FLRW, in which "tensor perturbations can only mix with other tensor perturbations": $h^{\prime\prime}_{ij}+(2+\nu)\mathcal{H}h^{\prime}_{ij}+(c_{g}^{2}k^{2}+m^{2}a^{2})h_{ij}=\Pi_{ij}\,,$ (Ezquiaga & Zumalacárregui 2018, arXiv:1807.09241) [src: Eq. 55], with ν "an additional friction term", $c_g$ "an anomalous propagation speed", m "an effective mass" and $\Pi_{ij}$ "a source term originated by the additional fields" [src: Sect. III.3.2, text after Eq. 55]. The corresponding luminosity distance is $d_{L}^{{}_{\text{MG}}}=(1+z)\frac{c_{g}(z)}{c_{g}(0)}\text{exp}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\nu}{1+z^{\prime}}dz^{\prime}\right]\int_{0}^{z}\frac{c_{g}(z^{\prime})}{H(z^{\prime})}dz^{\prime}\,.$ [src: Eq. 57].
- With friction written as δ(η) (Belgacem et al. 2018, arXiv:1805.08731): $\tilde{h}^{\prime\prime}_{A}+2{\cal H}[1-\delta(\eta)]\tilde{h}^{\prime}_{A}+k^{2}\tilde{h}_{A}=0\,,$ (arXiv:1805.08731) [src: Eq. 12]; then $d_{L}^{\,\rm gw}(z)=\frac{a(z)}{\tilde{a}(z)}\,d_{L}^{\,\rm em}(z)\,.$ [src: Eq. 18] and $d_{L}^{\,\rm gw}(z)=d_{L}^{\,\rm em}(z)\exp\left\{-\int_{0}^{z}\,\frac{dz^{\prime}}{1+z^{\prime}}\,\delta(z^{\prime})\right\}\,.$ [src: Eq. 20].
- `[der]` The two papers name the friction differently: ν in Eq. 55 of arXiv:1807.09241 enters as (2+ν)ℋ, δ in Eq. 12 of arXiv:1805.08731 as 2ℋ(1−δ); neither paper states the relation, so none is given here. <!-- num: not a value -->
- The $(\Xi_0, n)$ parametrisation: $\frac{d_{L}^{\,\rm gw}(z)}{d_{L}^{\,\rm em}(z)}=\Xi_{0}+\frac{1-\Xi_{0}}{(1+z)^{n}}\,.$ (arXiv:1805.08731) [src: Eq. 22], which tends to $\Xi_0$ at large redshift and to 1 at $z=0$ [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 22]; the values $n=5/2$ and $\Xi_{0}=0.970$ printed after it are those of the nonlocal RR model used as the example [src: Sect. II, inline, text after Eq. 22]. The effect of modified propagation "dominates over that of $w_{\rm DE}(z)$" in generic modified-gravity models, "even in theories where the speed of gravity is equal to $c$" [src: abstract].
- In GR the sub-horizon amplitude scales as $\tilde{h}_{A}(\eta,{\bf k})\propto\frac{1}{d_{L}(z)}\,,$ (arXiv:1805.08731) [src: Eq. 11].
- Free-streaming neutrinos damp tensor modes in GR (Eq. 235 above; arXiv:astro-ph/0306304). Shear viscosity of the post-inflationary plasma adds a damping term to the tensor equation; for the electron–photon–baryon plasma the effect "corresponds to a fractional difference of order $10^{-3}$" (Fanizza, Pavone & Tedesco 2025, arXiv:2512.04011) [src: abstract].
