# Open questions on cosmological gravitational waves

Scope: live debates with both sides cited, unresolved calculations, what the next data or the next calculation will decide, forecasts (labelled), and stale claims with the paper that corrects them.

Snapshot: 2026-11-05

## Live debates
<!-- key: live-debates -->

### Is the PTA background cosmological?
<!-- key: is-the-pta-background-cosmological -->
- NANOGrav 15-year: many cosmological models "seem to provide a better fit resulting in Bayes factors in the range from 10 to 100" against supermassive black-hole binaries, but "these results strongly depend on modeling assumptions about the cosmic SMBHB population and, at this stage, should not be regarded as evidence for new physics" (arXiv:2306.16219) [src: abstract].
- Cosmic strings as the source: three of four loop-distribution models are precluded by LVK bounds (arXiv:2610.02590) [src: abstract]; post-BBN matter or kination domination is one route proposed for strings (Co, Lam & Lee 2026, arXiv:2610.10674) [src: abstract]. Measured spectra and their fits: cosmoskill `cosmo-early-universe-exotica`.

### Nambu–Goto or field-theory string loops?
<!-- key: nambu-goto-or-field-theory-string-loops -->
- Field theory: loops "decay by the emission of massive radiation within a Hubble time", with "much weaker gravitational wave constraints on their tension" (Hindmarsh et al. 2017, arXiv:1703.06696) [src: abstract].
- Nambu–Goto: the loop number "can be accurately predicted using recently acquired detailed knowledge of the loop production function"; PTA bound Gμ ≤ 2.8×10^-9 in that picture (Blanco-Pillado, Olum & Shlaer 2013, arXiv:1309.6637) [src: abstract].
- Consequence: bounds on $G\mu$ quoted without the loop picture cannot be compared; the review states it is "difficult to compare the results among them" (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Sect. 9.2.1, text after Eq. 384].

### The gauge question for scalar-induced waves
<!-- key: the-gauge-question-for-scalar-induced-waves -->
- No second-order description of the detector response exists, and "a gauge invariant formulation of the equations of motion of induced GWs (3.15) does not help at all" (Domènech 2021, arXiv:2109.01398) [src: Sect. 7.2]; agreement between properly fixed synchronous and Newtonian gauges holds in radiation domination and for constant $w$ other than dust [src: Sect. 7.2, text after Eq. 7.11]. The dust case remains open among the sources read. `[der]` Home: `thcosmo-cosmological-perturbations` (planned).

### Does homogeneous backreaction describe axion-inflation backgrounds?
<!-- key: does-homogeneous-backreaction-describe-axion -->
- Lattice side: the homogeneous approximation "fails to accurately describe the truly local dynamics of strong backreaction" (Figueroa et al. 2024, arXiv:2411.16368) [src: abstract]; inhomogeneity removes the oscillatory spectral features (arXiv:2303.17436) [src: abstract].
- Homogeneous side: detectable examples exist with subdominant inflaton gradient energy throughout inflation (Barbon, Ijaz & Peloso 2025, arXiv:2510.17207) [src: abstract].
- A third result constrains both: in pure Abelian axion inflation, signals within reach of future interferometers come only with strong backreaction and conflict with the $\Delta N_{\rm eff}$ bound (arXiv:2508.00798) [src: abstract], which "defines a clear target for future lattice studies" [src: abstract].
- What decides it: lattice GW spectra computed through the strong-backreaction regime for the parameter points that the homogeneous studies call detectable. `[der]`

## Unresolved calculations

### The turbulence contribution of phase transitions
<!-- key: the-turbulence-contribution-of-phase-transitions -->
- "the turbulence contribution to $\Omega_{\text{gw}}$ is the subject of ongoing study", and different decay models give "different parametric dependences" (Caprini et al. 2019, arXiv:1910.13125) [src: Sect. 3.2]. The 2024 template adds turbulence as a double broken power law with slopes 3, 1, −8/3 and a free fraction ε (arXiv:2403.03723) [src: Table 1; Eq. 13].

### Strong and supercooled transitions
<!-- key: strong-and-supercooled-transitions -->
- Kinetic-energy deficit and suppressed GWs for strong deflagrations (arXiv:1906.00480) [src: abstract]; expanding-universe lattice spectra that "differ markedly" from Minkowski ones (arXiv:2610.14806) [src: abstract]; self-consistent percolation and reheating in the supercooled regime (arXiv:2605.15259) [src: abstract]. No single template covers this regime among the sources read. `[der]`

## What next data or calculations will decide
<!-- key: what-next-data-or-calculations-will-decide -->

- LISA and phase transitions (forecast): reconstruction "with better than ∼10 % accuracy, if certain experimental and theoretical uncertainties are solved by the time LISA flies" (Caprini et al. 2024, arXiv:2403.03723) [src: abstract].
- Third-generation detectors and modified propagation (forecast): "O5-like GW data will not resolve deviations from GR smaller than 30%", and third-generation detectors "can confidently detect deviations of order 5% to 3$σ$ significance" (arXiv:2608.18310) [src: abstract].
- Chirality and non-Gaussianity would single out the axion–gauge-field mechanism: the review calls them "very distinctive properties" with "specific predictions unique to this scenario" (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Sect. 6.1.2, text after Eq. 241].
- Lattice: GW spectra through strong backreaction in axion inflation (arXiv:2508.00798) [src: abstract]; CosmoLattice 2.0 adds axion–gauge couplings and scaling defect initial conditions (Baeza-Ballesteros et al. 2026, arXiv:2607.24978) [src: abstract].
- Preheating signals "lie in the ultra-high-frequency regime at frequencies above the kHz range" (arXiv:2607.18381) [src: abstract], outside the bands of Fig. 1 of arXiv:1801.04268 [src: Fig. 1 caption].

## Stale claims and misconceptions
<!-- key: stale-claims-and-misconceptions -->

- "The 2019 sound-wave formula Eq. 29 applies to LISA models": "most models of interest for LISA predict $H_{*}\tau_{\text{sh}}<1$, and so (32) is often the relevant expression" (Caprini et al. 2019, arXiv:1910.13125) [src: Sect. 3.3, inline, text after Eq. 32]; the 2024 templates replace both (arXiv:2403.03723) [src: Sect. 2.2.1].
- "The envelope approximation describes strong transitions": the collided parts of the walls are "essential for a correct estimation" of the spectrum (arXiv:2403.03723) [src: Sect. 2.1, text after Eq. 6].
- "The kinetic efficiency κ of one bubble gives K": the 2024 template uses $K\simeq 0.6\,\kappa\,\alpha/(1+\alpha)$ for bulk flow (arXiv:2403.03723) [src: Sect. 2.2.1, inline, text after Eq. 11]; for strong deflagrations the deficit is larger (arXiv:1906.00480) [src: abstract].
- "$N_{\rm eff}=3.046$ in the radiation bound": the review prints 3.046 (arXiv:1801.04268) [src: Sect. 4.1, inline, text after Eq. 127]; the current Standard-Model value is in `thcosmo-neutrinos-light-relics`.
- "Homogeneous backreaction gives the oscillatory peaks of axion-inflation spectra": lattice studies find the oscillatory features "smoothed out" (arXiv:2303.17436) [src: abstract].
- "All Minkowski-space PT simulations transfer to an expanding universe": a 2026 expanding-universe study finds the amplitude "in alignment with Minkowski-spacetime results" but spectra that "differ markedly from those scenarios" (arXiv:2610.14806) [src: abstract].
