## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- **The letter μ.** It means four different things in the papers followed here. In Arkani-Hamed & Maldacena it is the mass parameter, $\Delta_{\pm}={3\over 2}\pm i\mu~,~~~~~~~~\mu=\sqrt{{m^{2}\over H^{2}}-{9\over 4}}$ (arXiv:1503.08043) [src: Eq. 1.2], as in Bodas, Kumar & Sundrum, who write $\mu=\sqrt{M^{2}-9/4}$ inside their Eq. 43 (arXiv:2010.04727) [src: Eq. 43]. In Assassi, Baumann & Green it is the coefficient of the cubic self-interaction ${\cal H}_{3}=\mu\sigma^{3}$ (arXiv:1204.4207) [src: Sect. 3.1, inline, text after Eq. 3.2], and the mass enters through $\nu\equiv\sqrt{\frac{9}{4}-\frac{m^{2}}{H^{2}}}$ (arXiv:1204.4207) [src: Eq. 3.7]. In An, McAneny, Ridgway & Wise it is the quadratic mixing, $\mu=2{\dot{\tilde{\phi}}_{0}}/{{\tilde{\Lambda}}}.$ (arXiv:1706.09971) [src: Eq. 14]. Philcox 2026b also writes μ for the cubic coupling $\mu\sigma^{3}$ and ρ for the mixing (arXiv:2607.12010) [src: Eq. 3]. A formula moved between these papers must be relabelled by hand. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Mixing and Goldstone normalisation.** Belrhali, Poisson & Renaux-Petel relate the canonical Goldstone to ζ by "$\zeta=-H\pi_{c}/f_{\pi}^{2}$ , with $f_{\pi}^{4}=2M_{\rm Pl}^{2}|\dot{H}|$" and write the mixing $\rho\,\dot{\pi}_{c}\,\sigma$, at unit sound speed (arXiv:2608.23243) [src: Setup, inline]. Philcox 2026b keeps the sound speed: "$f_{\pi}\equiv(2M_{\rm Pl}^{2}|\dot{H}|c_{s})^{1/4}$" and "$\zeta=-(Hc_{s}^{3/2}/f_{\pi}^{2})\pi_{c}$" (arXiv:2607.12010) [src: Sect. I, inline, text after Eq. 4]. Assassi et al. use the adiabatic field $\theta=-\sqrt{2\epsilon}\hskip 1.0ptM_{\rm pl}\,\zeta\ ,$ with $\epsilon\equiv-\dot{H}/H^{2}$ (arXiv:1204.4207) [src: Eq. 3.2]; Arkani-Hamed & Maldacena use $\zeta=-{H\over\dot{\phi}_{0}}\xi$ (arXiv:1503.08043) [src: Eq. 6.145]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Signature.** An et al. write "$ds^{2}=dt^{2}-a(t)^{2}d{\bf x}^{2}$" (arXiv:1706.09971) [src: Sect. II, inline, text after Eq. 3], the opposite of the signature of `formalism.md#conventions`; Bodas et al. write the kinetic term as $-\frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}$ (arXiv:2010.04727) [src: Eq. 23].
- **What is called $f_{\rm NL}$.** Arkani-Hamed & Maldacena compare their ratio (Step 4) with local non-Gaussianity, for which "the ratio in (6.156) is defined as ${3\over 5}f_{NL}$" (arXiv:1503.08043) [src: Sect. 6.1, inline, text after Eq. 6.158]; Assassi et al. define $f_{\rm NL}(\nu)$ through the prefactor $\frac{12}{5}$ of their Eq. 3.34 (arXiv:1204.4207); Bodas et al. call the amplitude of the non-analytic part $\lvert f_{\text{oscil}}\rvert$ (arXiv:2010.04727) [src: Eq. 79]; Belrhali et al. normalise their shapes "at the equilateral configuration" (arXiv:2608.23243) [src: Shapes and collider signal]. No paper read relates these amplitudes; none is given here. <!-- num: not a value --> <!-- eq: notation -->

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — a massive field at late times, and the origin of the Boltzmann factor
<!-- key: step-1-a-massive-field-at-late-times-and-the -->

Arkani-Hamed & Maldacena take the de Sitter two-point function of a massive scalar to late times at fixed comoving separation: $\langle\phi(\eta,\vec{x})\phi(\eta^{\prime},\vec{0})\rangle\sim{1\over 4\pi^{5/2}}\left[\left({\eta\eta^{\prime}\over|\vec{x}|^{2}}\right)^{{3\over 2}+i\mu}{\Gamma(-i\mu)\Gamma({3\over 2}+i\mu)}+\left({\eta\eta^{\prime}\over|\vec{x}|^{2}}\right)^{{3\over 2}-i\mu}{\Gamma(i\mu)\Gamma({3\over 2}-i\mu)}\right]+\cdots$ (arXiv:1503.08043) [src: Eq. 3.17]. The two terms are "two simple exponentials of proper time $e^{-({3\over 2}\mp i\mu)t}$" [src: Sect. 3, inline, text after Eq. 3.17], each behaving like a correlator of an operator of dimension $\Delta_\pm$ (Eq. 1.2). "for large $\mu$ it is suppressed as $e^{-\pi\mu}$ . If we square this we get $e^{-2\pi\mu}$ which is the Boltzman factor of a massive particle" [src: Sect. 3, text after Eq. 3.17].

Bodas, Kumar & Sundrum give the same factor from the mode function. With Bunch–Davies initial conditions, at late times $\bar{f}_{k}\xrightarrow{\eta\rightarrow 0}\bar{f}_{k}(out)\simeq\frac{(-\eta)^{3/2}}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\Gamma(-i\mu)(-k\eta/2)^{i\mu}e^{\pi\mu/2}+\Gamma(i\mu)(-k\eta/2)^{-i\mu}e^{-\pi\mu/2}\right)\xrightarrow{M\gg H}\,\frac{(-\eta)^{3/2}}{\sqrt{2\mu}}\left((-k\eta/2)^{i\mu}+e^{-\pi\mu}(-k\eta/2)^{-i\mu}\right),$ (arXiv:2010.04727) [src: Eq. 45], using $|\Gamma(i\mu)|\sim\sqrt{2\pi}|\mu|^{-1/2}e^{-\pi|\mu|/2}.$ [src: Eq. 46]. "a small negative frequency part ... has developed, with a relative amplitude $e^{-\pi\mu}$ , which corresponds to ‘cosmological particle production’. Then the probability of particle production is $\sim e^{-2\pi\mu}$ , which is approximately $e^{-M/{T_{\rm Hawking}}}$ , where the cosmological Hawking Temperature is given by $T_{\rm Hawking}=H/(2\pi)$" [src: Sect. 3.4.1, text after Eq. 46]. The non-analytic signature "is always accompanied by this exponential suppression unless there is another energy source of particle production" [src: Sect. 3.4.1, text after Eq. 46].

### Step 2 — the squeezed limit inherits the late-time dimensions
<!-- key: step-2-the-squeezed-limit-inherits-the-late -->

In the squeezed limit, ${\langle\zeta\zeta\zeta\rangle\over\langle\zeta\zeta\rangle_{\rm short}\langle\zeta\zeta\rangle_{\rm long}}\sim\epsilon\sum_{i}w_{i}\left({k_{\rm long}\over k_{\rm short}}\right)^{\Delta_{i}}~,~~~~~~~~{\rm for}~~~~{k_{\rm long}\over k_{\rm short}}\ll 1$ (arXiv:1503.08043) [src: Eq. 1.1], "a consequence of the slightly broken conformal symmetry of the late time wavefunction of the inflationary universe" [src: Sect. 1, text after Eq. 1.1]; "a single new particle of mass $m>{3H\over 2}$ and spin $s$ gives rise to a pair of terms" with the dimensions of Eq. 1.2 [src: Sect. 1, inline, text after Eq. 1.1]. The coefficients carry "an interesting calculable phase", $w_{\pm}=|w_{\pm}|e^{\pm i\delta}$, which "depends only on the mass of the particle" [src: Sect. 1, inline, text after Eq. 1.2]. The oscillation "is a quantum effect. It arises from the quantum interference between two processes", the Gaussian evolution of the inflaton and "the creation of a pair of massive particles that subsequently decay to inflaton modes" [src: Sect. 1, text after Eq. 1.2].

For a light field (real ν) the same logic gives a power law without oscillation. Assassi, Baumann & Green: the massive mode function is $v_{k}(\tau)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{H}{\sqrt{2k^{3}}}(-k\tau)^{3/2}\,{\rm H}_{\nu}^{{(1)}}(-k\tau)$ (arXiv:1204.4207) [src: Eq. 3.7], which decays as $v_{k}(\tau)\sim(-\tau)^{3/2-\nu}$ outside the horizon [src: Sect. 3.1, inline, text after Eq. 3.7], and the bispectrum scales as $\lim_{k_{1}\to 0}\frac{\langle\zeta_{{\vec{k}}_{1}}\zeta_{{\vec{k}}_{2}}\zeta_{{\vec{k}}_{3}}\rangle^{\prime}}{P_{\zeta}(k_{1})}\ \propto\ k_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}-\nu}\ .$ [src: Eq. 3.10]. Their explanation: the long σ mode exits at $\tau_1\sim k_1^{-1}$; "By this time, the amplitude of the long $\sigma$ -mode has decayed by a factor of $(\tau_{2}/\tau_{1})^{3/2-\nu}=(k_{1}/k_{2})^{3/2-\nu}$" [src: Sect. 3.1, text after Eq. 3.10].

`[der]` Continued above the threshold mass, where ν becomes imaginary, the same decay factor becomes the complex power of the Arkani-Hamed–Maldacena form, the bridge between the quasi-single-field power law and the collider oscillation.

### Step 3 — the quasi-single-field in-in calculation with perturbative mixing
<!-- key: step-3-the-quasi-single-field-in-in-calculation -->

Assassi et al. review quasi-single-field inflation (QSFI), reference [25] of their paper being Chen & Wang (arXiv:0911.3380). The free Hamiltonian is ${\cal H}_{0}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\theta)^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\sigma)^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}m^{2}\sigma^{2}\ ,$ (arXiv:1204.4207) [src: Eq. 3.1], with $m\sim H$ (arXiv:1204.4207) [src: Sect. 3.1, inline, text after Eq. 3.1], and "Following [25]" the mixing is treated as an interaction, ${\cal H}_{\rm int}=-\rho\hskip 1.0pt\dot{\theta}\sigma+\mu\sigma^{3}\ .$ (arXiv:1204.4207) [src: Eq. 3.3]; "In order to describe the mixing perturbatively we require $\rho<H$" [src: Sect. 3.1, text after Eq. 3.3]. The in-in expectation value is their Eq. 3.8 and the interaction splits into (arXiv:1204.4207) $H_{2}\equiv-\int{\rm d}^{3}x\,a^{3}\rho\hskip 1.0pt\theta^{\prime}\sigma\qquad{\rm and}\qquad H_{3}\equiv\int{\rm d}^{3}x\,a^{4}\mu\sigma^{3}\ .$ [src: Eq. 3.9], the mixing "treated as a perturbative insertion in Feynman diagrams" [src: Sect. 3.1, text after Eq. 3.9]. The bispectrum is then three mixing insertions and one cubic vertex.

In the squeezed limit they need the unequal-time mixed correlator, computed from one insertion of $H_2$ (their Eqs. 3.29–3.30) with the superhorizon mode functions $\lim_{|k\tau|\ll 1}u_{k}(\tau)\approx\dfrac{H}{\sqrt{2k^{3}}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{|k\tau|\ll 1}v_{k}(\tau)\approx-i\,\dfrac{2^{\nu}\Gamma(\nu)}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\dfrac{H}{\sqrt{k^{3}}}(-k\tau)^{3/2-\nu}\ ,$ (arXiv:1204.4207) [src: Eq. 3.31]. The result is $\langle\zeta_{{\vec{k}}_{1}}\zeta_{{\vec{k}}_{2}}\zeta_{{\vec{k}}_{3}}\rangle^{\prime}\ \xrightarrow{k_{1}\to 0}\ \frac{12}{5}f_{{\rm NL}}(\nu)\cdot P_{\zeta}(k_{1})P_{\zeta}(k_{2})\left(\frac{k_{1}}{k_{2}}\right)^{3/2-\nu}\ ,$ [src: Eq. 3.34] with $f_{{\rm NL}}(\nu)\equiv\,p(\nu)\,d(\nu)\cdot\frac{\mu\rho^{3}}{H^{4}}\cdot\Delta_{\zeta}^{-1}\ .$ [src: Eq. 3.35], where $d(\nu)$ (Eq. 3.33) and $p(\nu)$ (Eq. 3.36) are numerical integrals over the mode functions. "Eq. (3.34) precisely matches the result that one obtains by taking the limit $k_{1}\rightarrow 0$ of the full expression of the bispectrum [25]" [src: Sect. 3.2.3, text after Eq. 3.36].

`[der]` The amplitude is set by three powers of the mixing, one power of the cubic coupling and the inverse of the power-spectrum amplitude; the shape interpolates between local and equilateral through ν, the one-parameter family of `formalism.md#cosmological-collider`.

### Step 4 — the heavy field: the oscillating squeezed limit at tree level
<!-- key: step-4-the-heavy-field-the-oscillating-squeezed -->

Arkani-Hamed & Maldacena obtain the inflationary three-point function from the de Sitter four-point function of conformally coupled scalars by setting one external leg to the background, "with one external particles set to $\dot{\phi}_{0}$" and $\vec{k}_{I}=\vec{k}_{3}$, $\vec{k}_{4}=0$ (arXiv:1503.08043) [src: Sect. 6, text after Eq. 6.145]. For the coupling $\lambda\int(\nabla\phi)^{2}\sigma$, with one φ replaced by the background and one by the fluctuation, the vertex becomes $\lambda\int{d\eta\over\eta^{2}}[-\partial_{\eta}\xi\partial_{\eta}\phi_{0}\sigma]\longrightarrow\lambda\int{d\eta\over\eta^{2}}[-k_{3}^{2}\eta e^{ik_{3}\eta}]\partial_{\eta}\phi_{0}\sigma\longrightarrow\lambda k_{3}^{2}\dot{\phi}_{0}\int{d\eta\over\eta^{2}}e^{ik_{3}\eta}\sigma$ [src: Eq. 6.146], having "assumed that $\dot{\phi}_{0}$ varies slowly enough that we can take it out of the integral" [src: Sect. 6.1, text after Eq. 6.146]. Then $\langle\xi_{\vec{k}_{1}}\xi_{\vec{k}_{2}}\xi_{\vec{k}_{3}}\rangle=\dot{\phi}_{0}(\eta_{3}^{*}){\lambda^{2}2^{2}k_{3}^{2}\over 8k_{1}^{3}k_{2}^{3}k_{3}^{3}}O_{12}I_{E}(k_{12},k_{3},k_{3})+{\rm other~diagrams}$ [src: Eq. 6.147], with $I_E$ the de Sitter four-point function of their Eq. 5.93, $O_{12}$ a differential operator (their Eq. 4.61) and $\eta_3^*$ the horizon-crossing time of $k_3$ [src: Sect. 6.1, text after Eq. 6.147]. "Only the diagram explicitly described in (6.147) contributes non-analytic terms in $k_{I}$" [src: Sect. 6.1, text after Eq. 6.147].

The non-analytic part comes from the $I_{+-}$ and $I_{-+}$ contributions, which factorise, with the normalisation $F(1)={1\over\sqrt{2}}{\pi\over\cosh\pi\mu}$ [src: Eq. 6.149]; $I_{++}$ and $I_{--}$ follow by analytic continuation [src: Sect. 6.1, text after Eq. 6.149]. Acting with $O_{12}$ gives $\langle\xi_{\vec{k}_{1}}\xi_{\vec{k}_{2}}\xi_{\vec{k}_{3}}\rangle^{\prime}_{k_{3}\to 0}={\dot{\phi}_{0}(\eta_{3}^{*})\lambda^{2}\over 2k_{1}^{3}k_{3}^{3}}J(\mu,{k_{3}\over k_{1}})$ [src: Eq. 6.152] with $J(\mu,{k_{3}\over k_{1}})\equiv{1\over 4\sqrt{\pi}}{\pi^{2}\over\cosh^{2}\pi\mu}\left({k_{3}\over k_{1}}\right)^{3\over 2}\times\left[\left({k_{3}\over 4k_{1}}\right)^{-i\mu}{(1-i\sinh\pi\mu)({5\over 2}-i\mu)({3\over 2}-i\mu)\Gamma(i\mu)\over\Gamma({1\over 2}+i\mu)}+\left({k_{3}\over 4k_{1}}\right)^{i\mu}{(1+i\sinh\pi\mu)({5\over 2}+i\mu)({3\over 2}+i\mu)\Gamma(-i\mu)\over\Gamma({1\over 2}-i\mu)}\right]$ [src: Eqs. 6.154–6.155]. Converting with Eq. 6.145 and the two-point function $\langle\zeta_{\vec{k}}\,\zeta_{-\vec{k}}\rangle^{\prime}={H^{4}\over\dot{\phi}_{0}^{2}}{1\over 2k^{3}}$ [src: Eq. 6.158], $\left.{\langle\zeta_{\vec{k}_{1}}\zeta_{\vec{k}_{2}}\zeta_{\vec{k}_{3}}\rangle^{\prime}\over 4\langle\zeta_{\vec{k}_{1}}\zeta_{-\vec{k}_{1}}\rangle^{\prime}\langle\zeta_{\vec{k}_{3}}\zeta_{-\vec{k}_{3}}\rangle^{\prime}}\right|_{k_{3}\to 0}=-CJ(\mu,{k_{3}\over k_{1}})$ [src: Eq. 6.156] with $C\equiv{\dot{\phi}^{2}_{0}(\eta^{*}_{3})\lambda^{2}\over 2H^{2}}={\epsilon}M_{pl}^{2}\lambda^{2}$ [src: Eq. 6.157] (arXiv:1503.08043).

### Step 5 — reading the amplitude off the tree-level result
<!-- key: step-5-reading-the-amplitude-off-the-tree-level -->

Arkani-Hamed & Maldacena list the small factors of Eq. 6.156: "First we have the slow roll factor $\epsilon$ . The function J is also very small. First it is small because of the dilution factor $\left({k_{3}\over k_{1}}\right)^{3\over 2}$ . Second, as $\mu$ becomes large, there is the exponential suppression factor $e^{-\pi\mu}$" (arXiv:1503.08043) [src: Sect. 6.1, text after Eq. 6.158]. "All these suppression factors suggest that one is not going to be able to measure this from CMB measurements alone, unless $\lambda M_{pl}$ is particularly large. Generically we expect that $\lambda M_{pl}\sim 1$ or greater" [src: Sect. 6.1, text after Eq. 6.158]. The signal is larger than the single-field squeezed term because the latter is unobservable and "any physical effect in the single field theory would be down by $k_{3}^{2}/k_{1}^{2}$", whereas "the effect we are talking about is bigger, going like $(k_{3}/k_{1})^{3\over 2}$" [src: Sect. 6.1, text after Eq. 6.158] (see `deep/soft-limits-and-consistency.md`).

Why $e^{-\pi\mu}$ and not the Boltzmann factor $e^{-2\pi\mu}$: "the probability to create a pair of massive particles goes as $e^{-2\pi\mu}$ . We are getting something bigger, of order $e^{-\pi\mu}$ , because we have an interference effect that is sentitive to the amplitude and not the square of the amplitude for creating the pair" (arXiv:1503.08043) [src: Sect. 5.2.2], typo "sentitive" as printed. The phase of the oscillation "is determined by the mass" and its computation "required solving the equation for the massive field in order to connect the simple boundary condition at early times with the late time behavior of the propagator" [src: Sect. 5.2.2].

## What sets the amplitude
<!-- key: what-sets-the-amplitude -->

### Perturbativity at weak mixing
<!-- key: perturbativity-at-weak-mixing -->

- **Criterion.** Lee, Baumann & Pimentel: since the dominant contributions occur at horizon crossing, "Consistency of the perturbative description therefore requires that the sizes of the mixing interactions at $\omega\sim H$ are smaller than the terms in the free-field actions"; for $c_\pi=1$, "The dimensionful couplings of relevant interactions have to be less than $H$ , while those of irrelevant interactions have to be greater than $H$ . The dimensionless couplings of marginal interactions have to be less than unity" (arXiv:1607.03735) [src: Sect. 3.4.1] <!-- eq: notation -->. For $c_\pi\neq1$ "Spatial gradients of the Goldstone mode are enhanced and the correlation functions can receive contributions from a second time scale, the time of crossing of the sound horizon" [src: Sect. 3.4.1].
- **Single exchange is small.** For the single-exchange diagram of a spin-1 particle $f_{\rm NL}\sim f(\mu_{1})\hskip 1.0pt\sqrt{c_{\pi}}\,\frac{\rho_{1}^{2}}{H^{2}}\,.$ (arXiv:1607.03735) [src: Eq. 4.27]: "As long as the mixing is perturbative, $\rho_{1}<H$ , this non-Gaussianity is constrained to be less than unity", and "the same reasoning applies to the coupling to scalar fields, which is why the single-exchange diagram has been neglected in the context of quasi-single-field inflation" [src: Sect. 4.2, text after Eq. 4.27].
- **Double and triple exchange, sound speed.** "both the double- and triple-exchange diagrams can naturally produce large non-Gaussianities. The non-analytic part of the bispectrum is suppressed by $e^{-\pi\mu_{s}}$ for $c_{\pi}=1$ , but only by $e^{-\pi\mu_{s}/2}$ for small $c_{\pi}$ . Typically, we find that $f_{\rm NL}\gtrsim{\cal O}(1)$ from the non-analytic part is possible if $\mu_{s}\lesssim 5$ for $c_{\pi}=1$ and $\mu_{s}\lesssim 10$ for $c_{\pi}\ll 1$" (arXiv:1607.03735) [src: Sect. 4.2, Summary].
- **Explicit bounds.** Philcox 2026b writes the cubic Lagrangian of the Goldstone and a scalar in his Eq. 3, with single-, double- and triple-exchange bispectra that "In the weak mixing regime, defined by $\rho\ll H$ , ... scale as $\rho\Delta\rho$ , $\rho^{2}\alpha$ , and $\rho^{3}\mu$ respectively. For $\rho\gtrsim H$ , these scalings break down since the mixing modifies the propagation of $\pi_{c}$" (arXiv:2607.12010) [src: Sect. I, text after Eq. 4]. "Assuming weak mixing, perturbativity gives the (order-of-magnitude) bounds" $\frac{\rho}{H}\lesssim\mathrm{min}\left(c_{s}^{-1/2},\frac{m}{H}\right),\quad\left|\frac{\Delta\rho}{H}\right|\lesssim\frac{c_{s}^{-1/2}}{2\pi\Delta_{\zeta}},|\alpha|\lesssim c_{s}^{1/2},\qquad\qquad\qquad\,\,\left|\frac{\mu}{H}\right|\lesssim 1.$ [src: Eq. 5]. His verdict: "perturbativity restricts the collider signals to be unobservably small, except for double- and triple-exchange configurations at low masses" [src: abstract].

### Chemical potential: a second energy source
<!-- key: chemical-potential-a-second-energy-source -->

- **The operator.** Bodas, Kumar & Sundrum couple the inflaton derivatively to the current of a complex scalar, $\mathcal{L}=\sqrt{-g}\left\{\vphantom{\frac{1}{2}}\right.\left.-\frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}-V(\phi)-|\partial\chi|^{2}-M^{2}|\chi|^{2}\right.\left.-\frac{i\partial_{\mu}\phi}{\Lambda}\left(\chi\partial^{\mu}\chi^{\dagger}\,-\chi^{\dagger}\partial^{\mu}\chi\,\right)-\frac{c\,(\partial\phi)^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}|\chi|^{2}+\alpha\left(\chi+\chi^{\dagger}\right)\,\right\}\;.$ (arXiv:2010.04727) [src: Eq. 23]; the background gives $-\frac{1}{\Lambda}\partial_{\mu}\phi J^{\mu}\supset\frac{\dot{\phi}_{0}}{\Lambda}J_{0}=\lambda J_{0},$ [src: Eq. 24], with "$\dot{\phi}_{0}\simeq(60H)^{2}$" in slow roll [src: Sect. 3.2, inline, text after Eq. 23].
- **Why a symmetry-breaking term is needed.** In inflation "the charge conservation is exact", and "any non-trivial effect of the chemical potential can be eliminated by a suitable field rotation, $\chi\rightarrow e^{-i\lambda t}\tilde{\chi}$" (arXiv:2010.04727) [src: Sect. 3.1, text after Eq. 20]; a small explicit breaking α survives the rotation as a time-dependent phase, $\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{\chi}}\supset-|\partial\tilde{\chi}|^{2}-M^{2}|\tilde{\chi}|^{2}-\alpha\,(\tilde{\chi}e^{-i\lambda t}+\tilde{\chi}^{\dagger}e^{+i\lambda t})\;.$ [src: Eq. 22], which "introduces an additional explicit time-dependence with frequency $\lambda\gg H$ , and hence produces heavy fields more efficiently" [src: Sect. 3.4.1, text after Eq. 46]; the physical mass is M [src: Sect. 3.1, text after Eq. 22].
- **Result.** In the squeezed limit, from the dominant $I_{+-}$ diagrams and with the bound on α saturated (their Eq. 73), $F_{\text{squeezed}}\approx|f_{\text{oscil}}(\mu,\lambda)|\left(\,\,\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)^{-3/2+i(\mu-\lambda)}+\text{c.c.}\right),$ [src: Eq. 79] with $|f_{\text{oscil}}(\mu,\lambda)|=\frac{5\pi}{12\sqrt{2}}\times 10^{-2}\frac{\lambda^{7/2}}{\mu^{1/2}\,(\lambda^{2}-\mu^{2})}.$ [src: Eq. 80] (arXiv:2010.04727). The estimate holds where "$\lambda>\mu$", and the full calculation "smooths out the naive divergence in the stationary phase estimate as $\mu\approx\lambda$" [src: Sect. 4.3, Fig. 2 caption]. "we obtain an observable NG for a large range of physical masses with a high reach, $H<M_{\text{eff}}\lesssim\lambda\,\,(\lesssim 60H)$", "observable" meaning "$f_{\text{NL}}\gtrsim O(0.01)$" [src: Sect. 4.3, footnote 9]; the abstract quotes "$f_{\mathrm{NL}} \sim \mathcal{O}(0.01-10)$ for an extended range of scalar masses, $M \lesssim \dotφ_{0}^{1/2}$", and "The mechanism does not invoke any particular fine-tuning of parameters or breakdown of perturbation-theoretic control" [src: abstract]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- `[der]` Eq. 80 carries no exponential Boltzmann factor: the energy for particle production comes from the inflaton kinetic energy through the chemical potential, not from the de Sitter temperature of Step 1. The oscillation frequency in the logarithm of the squeezing is shifted by the chemical potential (Eq. 79), which is how the paper reads off mass and chemical potential (its Sect. 5).

### Strong mixing
<!-- key: strong-mixing -->

- **Non-perturbative quadratic theory.** An, McAneny, Ridgway & Wise write the QSFI free Lagrangian with the mixing kept, ${\cal L}_{0}={1\over 2}\left({\dot{\tilde{\pi}}}^{2}-{1\over a^{2}}{\nabla{\tilde{\pi}}}\cdot{\nabla{\tilde{\pi}}}\right)+{1\over 2}\left({\dot{s}}^{2}-{1\over a^{2}}\nabla s\cdot\nabla s-m^{2}s^{2}\right)+\mu{\dot{\tilde{\pi}}}s$ (arXiv:1706.09971) [src: Eq. 12], μ being the mixing (Conventions). In flat space "The massless mode has an unusual energy momentum relation that, for momentum in the range $m\ll q\ll\mu$ , has a non-relativistic flavor, $E_{q}=q^{2}/\mu$", and "The other mode is heavy with a mass $\sqrt{\mu^{2}+m^{2}}$ . The fact that this mode’s mass does not go to zero as $m\rightarrow 0$ is what regularizes the divergences that occur at $m=0$ when one treats $\mu$ perturbatively" [src: Sect. I]. They solve the de Sitter mode functions numerically in the region $(\mu/H)^{2}+(m/H)^{2}>9/4$ with $m/H\sim{\cal O}(1)$ or less [src: Sect. I], and compare with the large-μ effective theory of their Sect. VII. In that effective theory, with the squeezing parameter c of their Eq. 101, the squeezed bispectrum from the $1/\Lambda$ coupling goes as $B_{\zeta}^{\text{sq}}\sim c^{-1}$, "still suppressed compared to what local non-gaussianity would give", the "$c^{-1}$ behavior in the squeezed limit is also seen in equilateral non-Gaussianity" [src: Sect. VII.3, inline, text after Eq. 105].
- **The Boltzmann exponent halves.** Belrhali, Poisson & Renaux-Petel solve the strongly mixed theory, "$\hat{\rho}\equiv\rho/H\gtrsim\hat{m}\equiv m/H$ , where no expansion in powers of $\hat{\rho}$ converges" (arXiv:2608.23243) [src: Setup, text after Eq. 1], in Laplace space. For $\kappa=k_3/k_1\ll1$ the signal is "$S\simeq\mathcal{A}\,\kappa^{1/2}\cos\big(\mu_{\rm eff}\ln\kappa+\varphi\big)$" [src: Shapes and collider signal, inline, text after Eq. 19]. The accounting: "Each leg in (14) carries a weight $e^{\pm\pi\hat{\rho}/2}$ according to its channel, so the dominant term, with all three legs in the amplified channel, scales like $e^{+3\pi\hat{\rho}/2}$ , for all interactions. The collider coefficients of the soft leg ... cost $e^{-\pi\mu_{\rm eff}}$ , and the hard-leg integral (18) a further $e^{-\pi\hat{\rho}}$ . Deep in the strong-mixing regime where $\rho\simeq\mu_{\rm eff}$ , one thus finds" $\mathcal{A}\;\propto\;e^{-\pi\mu_{\rm eff}/2}\qquad\text{(strong mixing)}\,,$ [src: Eq. 20], "only half the conventional Boltzmann suppression that had been anticipated by analogy with weak mixing" [src: Shapes and collider signal, text after Eq. 20]. More precisely, at fixed $\hat m^2$, $\mathcal{A}\;\propto\;\mu_{\rm eff}^{\,n}\,e^{-\pi\mu_{\rm eff}/2}\,,\qquad n=0,\ 0,\ 2,\ 3\,,$ [src: Eq. 21] for the four cubic interactions of their Fig. 1; "each time derivative of the curvature perturbation enhances the oscillations by one power of their frequency" [src: Introduction].
- **Over the whole plane.** Pinol 2026 resums the mixing "to all orders in the dimensionless mixing strength $λ=ρ/H$" and finds that "At strong mixing the collider amplitude scales as $e^{π(λ-2μ_{\rm eff})/2}$ over the whole $(λ,μ_{\rm eff})$ plane" (arXiv:2609.38167) [src: abstract]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- **Unequal sound speeds.** When the curvature perturbation propagates with $c_s<1$ and the heavy field does not, "heavy particles that are lighter than $H/c_s$ leave smoking gun imprints in the bispectrum in the form of resonances in the squeezed limit, a phenomenon that we call the low speed collider" (Jazayeri & Renaux-Petel 2022, arXiv:2205.10340) [src: abstract]. <!-- eq: notation -->

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **In-in with perturbative mixing insertions** (Step 3). Assumes $\rho<H$ (arXiv:1204.4207) [src: Sect. 3.1, text after Eq. 3.3]; nested time integrals evaluated numerically ($p(\nu)$, $d(\nu)$). Exact in the squeezed limit for light fields; fails at strong mixing, where the perturbative series does not converge (arXiv:2608.23243) [src: Setup, text after Eq. 1].
- **Conformal building block plus weight-shifting operators** (Step 4; Eq. 3.8 of arXiv:2203.08128 in `formalism.md#cosmological-collider`). Arkani-Hamed & Maldacena take $\dot\phi_0$ constant over the integral (arXiv:1503.08043) [src: Sect. 6.1, text after Eq. 6.146]; the bootstrap version of Jazayeri & Renaux-Petel extends it to $c_s\neq1$ but "requires the analytical continuation of this seed correlator beyond the physical domain implied by momentum conservation" (arXiv:2205.10340) [src: abstract].
- **Large-mixing effective theory.** Integrating out the heavy mode for $\mu/H\gg1$ in the notation of An et al.; they "study how large $\mu/H$ must be for the effective field theory method to be quantitatively correct" (arXiv:1706.09971) [src: Sect. I] and find agreement with the numerics "In the large $\mu$ region" [src: Sect. I].
- **Exact linear solutions resummed in the mixing.** Huenupi et al. build dressed propagators from exact solutions, "recover the well-known cosmological-collider and quasi-single-field results in the weak-mixing limit", and find in the strong-mixing regime that "both the power spectrum and the bispectrum can be dramatically enhanced" (arXiv:2607.14529) [src: abstract]. Belrhali et al. reach closed-form series in Laplace space, agreeing with CosmoFlow "to relative accuracy $10^{-5}$" (arXiv:2608.23243) [src: Shapes and collider signal, inline, text after Eq. 19], and state that the exponential integral representations of Huenupi et al. and Pinol "follow, after integrations by parts, from the plane-wave superposition that the Laplace representation derives systematically" [src: Conclusion].
- **Numerical cosmological flow.** Philcox 2026b computes the bispectra numerically with CosmoFlow (arXiv:2607.12010) [src: abstract]; "Numerical computations however become prohibitive at large squeezing", and configurations with "$\kappa<0.014$" were "handled by a fitted form" (arXiv:2608.23243) [src: Shapes and collider signal, inline, text after Eq. 19].

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **μ read in the wrong paper's sense.** See Conventions: a mass parameter (arXiv:1503.08043), a cubic coupling (arXiv:1204.4207, arXiv:2607.12010) and a mixing (arXiv:1706.09971) share the letter. A bound such as $\left|\frac{\mu}{H}\right|\lesssim 1$ in Eq. 5 of arXiv:2607.12010 is on the σ³ coupling, not on the mass. <!-- eq: notation -->
- **Weak-mixing scalings used at $\rho\gtrsim H$.** The scalings $\rho\Delta\rho$, $\rho^{2}\alpha$, $\rho^{3}\mu$ "break down since the mixing modifies the propagation of $\pi_{c}$" (arXiv:2607.12010) [src: Sect. I, text after Eq. 4]; strong mixing also "breaks the factorization of the bispectrum into a free amplitude and a fixed shape" [src: abstract], and even at the smallest perturbative mixing "the true and assumed shapes differ significantly" [src: Sect. III, Fig. 2 caption]. Check: compare a weak-mixing template with a resummed calculation (arXiv:2608.23243, arXiv:2609.38167) at the mixing assumed.
- **Assuming $e^{-\pi\mu}$ everywhere.** The suppression is $e^{-\pi\mu_s/2}$ for small $c_\pi$ (arXiv:1607.03735) [src: Sect. 4.2, Summary], $e^{-\pi\mu_{\rm eff}/2}$ at strong mixing (arXiv:2608.23243) [src: Eq. 20], and absent in Eq. 80 of arXiv:2010.04727 with a chemical potential.
- **Disagreement on the size at strong mixing.** Philcox 2026b finds that larger mixing "distorts the signal, damping its amplitude and shifting the collider oscillations" (arXiv:2607.12010) [src: abstract]; Belrhali et al. find the signal "larger than widely believed" (arXiv:2608.23243) [src: abstract]. `[der]` The two statements differ in their reference (amplitude at fixed observed $f_{\rm NL}$ against the equilateral normalisation, versus the Boltzmann exponent); a referee should ask which normalisation a claim uses (Conventions).
- **Exponential enhancement outside control.** The growth "like $e^{\pi\hat{\rho}/2}$" at large mixing and fixed $\mu_{\rm eff}$ lies in a regime that "features a transient tachyonic instability and parametrically lies in a region where perturbative control is lost due to graviton loops" (arXiv:2608.23243) [src: Shapes and collider signal, inline, text after Eq. 19].
- **The stationary-phase estimate near resonance.** Eq. 80 of arXiv:2010.04727 diverges as μ → λ; the full calculation removes it, "since for such $\mu$ the approximation made in our stationary phase estimate breaks down" (arXiv:2010.04727) [src: Sect. 4.3, Fig. 2 caption].
- **Slow time dependence of $\dot\phi_0$.** Eq. 6.147 evaluates $\dot\phi_0$ at $\eta_3^*$ after taking it out of the time integral (arXiv:1503.08043) [src: Sect. 6.1, text after Eq. 6.146]; a background with features violates this.
- **Slip copied as printed.** In Philcox 2026b κ denotes the coefficient of a cubic mixing in Eq. 3, fixed by the quadratic theory in Eq. 4, and, after Eq. 6, the constant "$\kappa\equiv 2\Gamma(5/4)^{2}/\pi^{3}$" (arXiv:2607.12010) [src: Sect. I, inline, text after Eq. 6]; the κ inside the bound on the mixing in Eq. 6 is the second. None published as of the snapshot.
