## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- Power spectrum. Three normalisations meet in this dossier. Creminelli & Zaldarriaga and Cheung et al. 2007b write $\langle\zeta_{\vec{k}_{i}}\zeta_{\vec{k}_{j}}\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\vec{k}_{i}+\vec{k}_{j})P_{k_{i}}\;.$ (arXiv:0709.0295) [src: Eq. 3]. Chen 2010 and Wang 2013 use a dimensionless $P_\zeta$ with $(2\pi)^5$ and $1/(2k^3)$ (`formalism.md#conventions`), and Chen defines "$n_{s}-1\equiv d\ln P_{\zeta}/d\ln k$" (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Sect. 9.2, inline, text after Eq. 9.5]. Pajer, Schmidt & Zaldarriaga define $n_s$ "through $P_{\zeta}(k)\propto k^{-4+n_{s}}$" (arXiv:1305.0824) [src: Sect. III.2, inline, text after Eq. 28]. No paper read states the relation between these normalisations; none is given here. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Fourier convention. Cheung et al. 2007b write $\zeta^{B}(\vec{x})=\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\zeta^{B}(\vec{k})e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}\ ,$ (arXiv:0709.0295) [src: Eq. 11], "our convention for the Fourier transform" [src: Sect. 2, footnote 4].
- Which momentum is soft. Creminelli & Zaldarriaga and Wang take $k_1$ soft; Chen 2010 takes $k_3\ll k_1=k_2$ [src: Sect. 9.2]; Pajer et al. write $\mathbf{k}_L$ for the long mode and $\mathbf{k}_{S}=\mathbf{k}_{1}+\mathbf{k}_{L}/2$ for the short one (arXiv:1305.0824) [src: Sect. III.2, inline, text after Eq. 24].

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — the assumption: one clock, an attractor
<!-- key: step-1-the-assumption-one-clock-an-attractor -->

Creminelli & Zaldarriaga state the assumption: "The inflaton is the only “clock of the Universe” and it fixes the Hubble parameter: fluctuations of the inflaton are therefore equivalent to a relative rescaling of the scale factor in different parts of the Universe" (arXiv:astro-ph/0407059) [src: text before Eq. 1]. They make it precise: "we are implicitly assuming that the perturbations are just fluctuations back and forth on the same unique classical solution"; "This is the case if the classical solution is a dynamical attractor, so that only one mode of the perturbations remains after horizon crossing while the other decays exponentially"; "In other words we do not have any isocurvature component" [src: text after Eq. 3]. The relation "is not based on any slow-roll approximation and it is valid for any inflaton Lagrangian" [src: text before Eq. 1].

Cheung et al. 2007b add two facts used by the proof: outside the horizon, "if there is only one degree of freedom", N and $N^i$ go to 1 and 0 and the metric becomes $ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+a(t)^{2}e^{2\zeta(x)}dx_{i}dx_{i}$ (arXiv:0709.0295) [src: Eq. 7] with ζ constant, which can be reabsorbed "in a ’local’ rescaling of the coordinates $x^{\prime}=e^{\zeta(x)}x$" [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 7]; and once outside the horizon a mode "becomes classical, in the sense that $[\zeta_{\vec{k}},\dot{\zeta}_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}]\rightarrow 0$ exponentially fast" [src: Sect. 2, inline, text after Eq. 7].

### Step 2 — the short two-point function in a long background
<!-- key: step-2-the-short-two-point-function-in-a-long -->

The three-point function in the squeezed limit is computed "in a two step process": the two-point function of the short modes in a background $\zeta^B$, then correlated with the background (arXiv:0709.0295) [src: Sect. 2, text before Eq. 8]. Expanding in the background, $\langle\zeta\zeta\rangle_{B}(\vec{x}_{2},\vec{x}_{3})\simeq\langle\zeta\zeta\rangle_{0}(|\vec{x}_{2}-\vec{x}_{3}|)+\zeta^{B}(\frac{\vec{x}_{2}+\vec{x}_{3}}{2})\left.\left(\frac{d}{d\zeta^{B}}\langle\zeta\zeta\rangle_{B}(|\vec{x}_{2}-\vec{x}_{3}|)\right)\right|_{0}\ ,$ (arXiv:0709.0295) [src: Eq. 9], where "no slow roll approximation has been done: we have just expanded in powers of the small background field" [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 9]. Because ζ is a rescaling of the coordinates, the derivative with respect to $\zeta^B$ is traded "for a derivative with respect to the log-distance between the points" [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 9]. Creminelli & Zaldarriaga write the same step as $\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta_{1}}\langle\zeta(x)\zeta(0)\rangle\cdot\zeta_{1}=x\frac{d}{dx}\langle\zeta(x)\zeta(0)\rangle\cdot\zeta_{1}\;.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0407059) [src: Eq. 4], and stress that the relation holds "only if we generalize the linear expression $(1+2\zeta(x))$ to the exponential form", because "only with the exponential form the effect of the background wave $e^{\zeta_{1}}$ multiplies the small scale fluctuations" [src: text after Eq. 4].

### Step 3 — Fourier transform and average
<!-- key: step-3-fourier-transform-and-average -->

In Fourier space, with long and short momenta $\vec{k}_{L}=\vec{k}_{2}+\vec{k}_{3}$ and $\vec{k}_{S}=(\vec{k}_{2}-\vec{k}_{3})/2$ and an integration by parts, $\langle\zeta\zeta\rangle_{B}(\vec{k}_{2},\vec{k}_{3})=\langle\zeta\zeta\rangle_{0}(k_{S})-\zeta^{B}(\vec{k}_{L})\frac{1}{k^{3}_{S}}\frac{d}{d\log k_{S}}[k_{S}^{3}\langle\zeta\zeta\rangle_{0}(k_{S})]\ ,$ (arXiv:0709.0295) [src: Eq. 12]. Multiplying by the long mode and averaging, $\langle\zeta^{(B)}(\vec{k}_{1})\langle\zeta\zeta\rangle_{B}(\vec{k}_{2},\vec{k}_{3})\rangle=-\langle\zeta^{(B)}(\vec{k}_{1})\zeta^{B}(\vec{k}_{L})\rangle\frac{1}{k^{3}_{S}}\frac{d}{d\log k_{S}}[k_{S}^{3}\langle\zeta\zeta\rangle_{0}(k_{S})]=-(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\vec{k}_{1}+\vec{k}_{L})P_{k_{1}}P_{k_{2}}\frac{d\log[k^{3}_{2}P_{k_{2}}]}{d\log k_{2}}=-(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\vec{k}_{1}+\vec{k}_{2}+\vec{k}_{3})P_{k_{1}}P_{k_{2}}\frac{d\log[k^{3}_{2}P_{k_{2}}]}{d\log k_{2}}$ (arXiv:0709.0295) [src: Eqs. 13–15], rows joined in reading order, which is their consistency relation $\lim_{k_{1}\to 0}\langle\zeta_{\vec{k}_{1}}\zeta_{\vec{k}_{2}}\zeta_{\vec{k}_{3}}\rangle=-(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\sum_{i}\vec{k}_{i})P_{k_{1}}P_{k_{3}}\frac{d\log k_{3}^{3}P_{k_{3}}}{d\log k_{3}}\;,$ (arXiv:0709.0295) [src: Eq. 2]. "no slow roll expansion was done in this proof (which remains valid for example even if there are sharp features in the potential)", and "the only important assumption was that there is only one clock field" [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 15].

Chen 2010 gives the same argument with an explicit expansion to all orders in the long mode, $\langle\zeta_{k_{1}}\zeta_{k_{2}}\zeta_{k_{3}}\rangle\to-\frac{d\langle\zeta_{k_{1}}\zeta_{k_{2}}\rangle}{d\ln k}\Big|_{0}\langle\zeta_{k_{3}}\zeta_{k_{3}}\rangle~.$ (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Eq. 9.4], the higher terms of its Eq. 9.5 being "related to the loop diagram contributions" [src: Sect. 9.2, text after Eq. 9.5], and in its normalisation $\langle\zeta_{k_{1}}\zeta_{k_{2}}\zeta_{k_{3}}\rangle\to-(n_{s}-1)\frac{1}{4k_{1}^{3}k_{3}^{3}}P_{\zeta}(k_{1})P_{\zeta}(k_{3})(2\pi)^{7}\delta^{3}(\sum_{i}{\bf k}_{i})~.$ (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Eq. 9.6]; "Although originally derived for slow-roll inflation, the only assumption is the single field" [src: Sect. 9.2, text after Eq. 9.6].

### Step 4 — the check on the slow-roll bispectrum
<!-- key: step-4-the-check-on-the-slow-roll-bispectrum -->

Wang 2013 derives the relation through the large gauge transformation, $\zeta_{\mathbf{k}_{1}}\rightarrow\zeta_{\mathbf{k}_{1}}+\lambda(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\mathbf{k}_{1})~,\qquad\mathbf{k}\rightarrow\mathbf{k}e^{-\lambda}~,\qquad(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\mathbf{k}_{1})\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta_{\mathbf{k}_{1}}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\ln k}=-\frac{\partial}{H\partial t}~.$ (arXiv:1303.1523) [src: Eq. 207], obtaining $\lim_{k_{1}/k_{3}\rightarrow 0}\langle\zeta_{\mathbf{k}_{1}}\zeta_{\mathbf{k}_{2}}\zeta_{\mathbf{k}_{3}}\rangle=-(2\pi)^{7}\delta^{3}(\mathbf{k}_{1}+\mathbf{k}_{2}+\mathbf{k}_{3})\frac{P_{\zeta}^{2}}{4k_{1}^{3}k_{3}^{3}}(n_{s}-1)~.$ (arXiv:1303.1523) [src: Eq. 209]; the delta function in the denominator of his Eq. 208 "is not well defined", cured by a finite box [src: Sect. III.6.1, footnote 53]. The squeezed limit of the slow-roll shape is $\lim_{k_{1}/k_{3}\rightarrow 0}\mathcal{F}(k_{1}/k_{3},k_{2}/k_{3})=\frac{k_{3}}{4k_{1}}(2\epsilon+\eta)~,$ (arXiv:1303.1523) [src: Eq. 210], so that $\lim_{k_{1}/k_{3}\rightarrow 0}\langle\zeta_{\mathbf{k}_{1}}\zeta_{\mathbf{k}_{2}}\zeta_{\mathbf{k}_{3}}\rangle=(2\pi)^{7}\delta^{3}(\mathbf{k}_{1}+\mathbf{k}_{2}+\mathbf{k}_{3})\frac{P_{\zeta}^{2}}{4k_{1}^{3}k_{3}^{3}}(2\epsilon+\eta)~.$ (arXiv:1303.1523) [src: Eq. 211], and with $n_{s}-1=-2\epsilon-\eta$ "the consistency relation is indeed satisfied" [src: Sect. III.6.1, inline, text after Eq. 211]. Compared with the local template of his Eq. 212, this is $f_{NL}=-\frac{5}{12}(n_{s}-1)~.$ (arXiv:1303.1523) [src: Eq. 213], "for this minimal model". Bravo et al. write the same as "Maldacena's consistency relation f_NL = 5(1-n_s)/12", from "the standard derivation of the bispectrum's squeezed limit using co-moving coordinates" (arXiv:1711.05290) [src: abstract]. Chen 2010 reports explicit tree-level checks for general single-field models "to three different orders" and for resonance models "to the leading order" (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Sect. 9.2, text after Eq. 9.6].

### Step 5 — the next orders in the soft momentum
<!-- key: step-5-the-next-orders-in-the-soft-momentum -->

- First order vanishes for the bispectrum. "Terms proportional to the first derivative of the long wavelength mode do not contribute to the 3-point function because their effect averages to zero", so the corrections are "suppressed by $(k_{1}/k_{3})^{2}$" (arXiv:astro-ph/0407059) [src: text before Eq. 7]: $\langle\zeta_{\vec{\kappa}_{1}}\zeta_{\vec{\kappa}_{2}}\zeta_{\vec{\kappa}_{3}}\rangle\simeq-(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\sum_{i}\vec{k}_{i})(n_{s}-1+{\cal{O}}\left(\frac{k_{1}}{k_{3}}\right)^{2})P_{k_{1}}P_{k_{3}}\;.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0407059) [src: Eq. 7], with κ on the left and k on the right as printed (the same mixing as in Eq. 1, `results.md`).
- Conformal order. Creminelli, Noreña & Simonović obtain the effect of a constant gradient of the long mode from a special conformal transformation; for the 3-point function this recovers "the known result that the 3-point function does not contain terms going as $1/q^{2}$ in the squeezed limit", and "The first non-trivial relation is between the 3- and the 4-point function" (arXiv:1203.4595) [src: Sect. 1]. Beyond the decoupling limit the derivation uses "the fact that a special conformal transformation of the spatial coordinates induces a profile of ζ which is linear in $\vec{x}$" [src: Sect. 1]. Their most general relation is $\boxed{\begin{split}\langle\zeta_{\vec{q}}\zeta_{\vec{k}_{1}}\ldots\zeta_{\vec{k}_{n}}\rangle^{\prime}\overset{q\rightarrow 0}{=}&-P(q)\left[3(n-1)+\sum_{a}\vec{k}_{a}\cdot\vec{\partial}_{k_{a}}+\frac{1}{2}q^{i}D_{i}\right]\langle\zeta_{\vec{k}_{1}}\ldots\zeta_{\vec{k}_{n}}\rangle^{\prime}+{\cal{O}}(q/k)^{2}\\ &\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm with}\hskip 9.24994ptq^{i}D_{i}\equiv\sum_{a=1}^{n}\Big[6\vec{q}\cdot\vec{\partial}_{k_{a}}-\vec{q}\cdot\vec{k}_{a}\vec{\partial}_{k_{a}}^{2}+2\vec{k}_{a}\cdot\vec{\partial}_{k_{a}}(\vec{q}\cdot\vec{\partial}_{k_{a}})\Big]\;.\end{split}}$ (arXiv:1203.4595) [src: Eq. 54], which "represents the most general result of this paper" [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 54]. The infinite tower of Ward identities that contains both orders is in `results.md` (arXiv:1304.5527).
- Second order is model dependent. After the long mode exits, "there is still dependence of the two-point function on the derivative of this long wavelength mode"; these corrections "start from the second order in $k_{3}/k_{1}$" and "give an additive correction $f_{NL}^{\rm non-loc}(k_{3}/k_{1})^{2}$ to the $n_{s}-1$", so "For a large $f_{NL}^{\rm non-loc}$, therefore, the condition needs to be satisfied in a very squeezed limit"; the equilateral bispectra of $P(X,\phi)$ "are this type of examples" (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Sect. 9.2, third correction].

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **Explicit EFT verification.** Cheung et al. 2007b verify the relation by direct calculation in the EFT of inflation, for $P(X,\phi)$ at leading and next-to-leading order in slow roll and for two extrinsic-curvature operators, the second one the ghost condensate, as the headings of their Sects. 3.2–3.4 state (arXiv:0709.0295) [src: Sect. 3], "valid at leading non trivial order in slow roll parameters" [src: abstract]. Cost: model by model; the formal proof of Step 3 needs no slow-roll expansion.
- **Soft theorem with time derivatives.** Bravo & Palma (arXiv:2009.03369) use diffeomorphism invariance to derive "valid to all orders in slow roll parameters" a squeezed limit in comoving coordinates that "consists in the standard Maldacena's consistency relation ... plus additional terms containing time derivatives of the power spectrum", "by neglecting departures from the adiabatic evolution of long-wavelength modes (for instance, produced in sharp transitions between slow-roll and ultra slow-roll phases)" [src: abstract].
- **Multi-soft.** Ullah 2026 (arXiv:2604.09765) gives tree-level multi-soft theorems with the background-wave method (`results.md`).

## What violates the relation
<!-- key: what-violates-the-relation -->

Each violation removes one assumption of Step 1.

- **A second field.** Curvaton or modulated-decay models "obviously do not respect the consistency relation; fluctuations in ζ are sourced outside the horizon" (arXiv:astro-ph/0407059) [src: text after Eq. 6]. "The violation of the consistency relation does not require the existence of a second field which fluctuates during inflation. Even a field which evolves classically with negligible quantum fluctuation (for example if its mass is much bigger than H) introduces a second independent clock"; "Obviously this second field must be really independent and not triggered by the inflaton itself" [src: footnote 4]. Chen 2010: "this condition only applies to single field inflation models"; with more fields, non-Gaussianities "can be transferred from the isocurvature directions which do not respect this relation" (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Sect. 9.2].
- **Sub-horizon interactions (non-Bunch–Davies states, resonance).** The background-rescaling picture assumes "there is no interaction when these modes are all within the horizon". In the resonance model the relation holds only "in the very squeezed limit ( $k_{3}\ll k_{1}/C$ with $C=\omega/H\gg 1$ )"; for "$k_{1}/C<k_{3}\ll k_{1}$ , the left hand side of the condition is larger than the right hand side by a factor of $Ck_{3}/2k_{1}$"; for "the non-Bunch-Davies vacuum case, a similar scale is determined by the UV cutoff $\tau_{c}$" (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Sect. 9.2, inline, text after Eq. 9.6]. Wang adds that initial correlations on sub-Hubble scales, "stretched to super-Hubble scales as they were", can "happen to provide non-vanishing squeezed limit" (arXiv:1303.1523) [src: Sect. III.6.1, footnote 52].
- **Non-conserved ζ (non-attractor).** "if ζ is for some reason not conserved on super-Hubble scales, it cannot be considered as a shift of background" (arXiv:1303.1523) [src: Sect. III.6.1, footnote 51]. In non-attractor single-field models "the curvature perturbation on super-horizon scales grows as R ∝ a^3"; this is "so far the only known class of self-consistent single-field models with a Bunch-Davies initial state that can produce a large squeezed-limit bispectrum violating Maldacena's consistency relation"; three methods agree on "f^local_NL = 5(1+c_s^2)/(4c_s^2)", "valid for arbitrary values of the speed of sound parameter, $c_s$, for a particular non-attractor model" (Chen et al. 2013, arXiv:1308.5341) [src: abstract]. In the canonical case Bravo et al. quote "f_NL = 5/2" for the non-attractor regime (arXiv:1711.05290) [src: abstract].
- **The transition out of non-attractor.** Cai et al. 2017 (arXiv:1712.09998): non-attractor inflation "should be followed by a phase of slow-roll attractor", and "If the curvature perturbations keep evolving during the transition - such as in the case of smooth transition or some sharp transition scenarios - the $\mathcal{O}(1)$ local non-Gaussianity generated in the non-attractor phase can be completely erased by the subsequent evolution, although the consistency relation remains violated"; "In extremal cases of sharp transition where the super-horizon modes freeze immediately right after the end of the non-attractor phase, the original non-attractor result can be recovered" [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value --> Suyama, Tada & Yamaguchi (arXiv:2101.10682) argue that the transition phase "necessarily involves two DoF in dynamics", so that "the observed squeezed bispectrum does not vanish in general for long-wavelength perturbations exiting the horizon during a non-attractor phase" [src: abstract].
- **Loops.** The right-hand side "should receive corrections from loop contributions", associated "with higher derivatives of the two-point function" (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Sect. 9.2, first correction]; the use of the relation in loop calculations is in `formalism.md#loop-corrections`.

## Why the single-field squeezed signal is unobservable
<!-- key: why-the-single-field-squeezed-signal-is -->

### Step 6 — the size, even before projection
<!-- key: step-6-the-size-even-before-projection -->

Creminelli & Zaldarriaga: the relation "implies a very low level of non-gaussianity in the particular geometrical limit $k_{1}\ll k_{2,3}$, much below current and forecoming limits"; models with a large 3-point function for generic triangles see it "becomes very small in the limit $k_{1}\ll k_{2,3}$" (arXiv:astro-ph/0407059) [src: text after Eq. 6]. Its interest is that "it could be easily proved wrong, therefore ruling out in a model independent way the possibility that the inflaton is the only dynamical field" [src: text after Eq. 6]. They note that second-order effects in the observed CMB "will introduce corrections with $f_{\rm NL}\sim 1$" [src: text after Eq. 7].

### Step 7 — the transformation to the local observer's frame
<!-- key: step-7-the-transformation-to-the-local-observer -->

Pajer, Schmidt & Zaldarriaga give the squeezed bispectrum in a new frame, built from a coordinate transformation that "varies slowly over the spatial patch" [src: Sect. II, text before Eq. 2], as the old one plus the response of the short power spectrum to the dilation, anisotropic rescaling and time shift of the transformation (their Eqs. 7 and 9, arXiv:1305.0824). In comoving gauge for "the attractor solution of single-field inflation" the time shift is negligible "To order $k_{L}^{2}/k_{S}^{2}$", "merely a consequence of the particular gauge chosen" [src: Sect. III.2, text after Eq. 23], and in conformal Fermi coordinates the long ζ is removed "up to second derivatives" [src: Sect. III.2, text after Eq. 25]. Then $B_{\bar{\zeta}\bar{\zeta}\bar{\zeta}}(\mathbf{k}_{L},\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2})=\> P_{\zeta}(k_{L})P_{\zeta}(k_{S})\frac{\partial\ln(k_{S}^{3}P_{\zeta}(k_{S}))}{\partial\ln k_{S}}+B_{\zeta\zeta\zeta}(\mathbf{k}_{L},\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2}),$ (arXiv:1305.0824) [src: Eq. 27], rows joined, and with the comoving-gauge result $B_{\zeta\zeta\zeta}(\mathbf{k}_{L},\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2})=-[n_{s}-1]P_{\zeta}(k_{L})P_{\zeta}(k_{S})\,,$ [src: Eq. 28] "the first term in Eq. (27) exactly cancels the contribution from $B_{\zeta\zeta\zeta}$", leaving $B_{\bar{\zeta}\bar{\zeta}\bar{\zeta}}(\mathbf{k}_{L},\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2})=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k_{L}}{k_{S}}\right)^{2}\,.$ (arXiv:1305.0824) [src: Eq. 29]. The same holds for a long tensor: the comoving-gauge $B_{h_{s}\zeta\zeta}(\mathbf{k}_{L},\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2})=-P_{h_{s}}(k_{L})e^{s}_{ij}(\hat{\mathbf{k}}_{L})k_{S}^{i}k_{S}^{j}\frac{d}{dk_{S}^{2}}P_{\zeta}(k_{S})\quad=-P_{h_{s}}(k_{L})e^{s}_{ij}(\hat{\mathbf{k}}_{L})\hat{k}_{S}^{i}\hat{k}_{S}^{j}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{d\ln k_{S}}P_{\zeta}(k_{S})\,.$ (arXiv:1305.0824) [src: Eq. 33] is cancelled by the transformation, assuming "that the different polarization states are statistically independent" [src: Sect. III.2, text before Eq. 32], so $B_{\bar{h}\bar{\zeta}\bar{\zeta}}(\mathbf{k}_{L},\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2})=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k_{L}}{k_{S}}\right)^{2},$ [src: Eq. 34]. The paper's reading: the consistency relation is "equivalent to the statement that there is no correlation between infinitely long and short wavelength modes in the conformal Fermi frame", "a physical, gauge-invariant statement", "expected to hold not only at leading order in spacetime perturbations but at higher orders as well"; correlations imprinted while the long mode was inside the horizon "are exponentially suppressed" [src: Sect. III.2, text after Eq. 34].

`[der]` Steps 3 and 7 are the same fact read twice: the comoving-gauge squeezed limit is the response of the short power spectrum to a coordinate rescaling, and a local observer, whose coordinates absorb that rescaling, sees none of it.

### Step 8 — what is left
<!-- key: step-8-what-is-left -->

- Projection effects. The primordial contributions to the squeezed CMB bispectrum and to scale-dependent halo bias vanish, "except projection effects" (arXiv:1305.0824) [src: abstract]; `approximations.md#numerical-issues`.
- The floor at second order in the soft momentum. Cabass, Pajer & Schmidt 2016 (arXiv:1612.00033) find with conformal Fermi coordinates that the tilt does not disappear at all orders: "a contribution of order the tilt appears in local observables", and "the floor of physical primordial non-Gaussianity in our Universe has a squeezed-limit scaling of $k_\ell^2/k_s^2$, similar to equilateral and orthogonal shapes, and a dimensionless amplitude of order $0.1\times(n_\mathrm{s}-1)$" [src: abstract]; the floor is that of "single-field slow-roll models, where this lower bound is saturated" [src: abstract].
- Non-attractor backgrounds. Bravo et al. argue that "independently of whether inflation is attractor or non-attractor, the size of the observable primordial local non-Gaussianity is predicted to be f_NL^obs = 0", since in the CFC frame the squeezed limit "vanishes ... regardless of the inflationary regime", once "all the modes have exited the horizon" (arXiv:1711.05290) [src: abstract]. Bravo & Palma restrict this: "in the absence of sudden transitions between attractor and non-attractor regimes, observable local non-Gaussianity is generically suppressed" (arXiv:2009.03369) [src: abstract]. Suyama et al. disagree for long modes exiting during the non-attractor phase (previous section, arXiv:2101.10682).
- An earlier route. Tanaka & Urakawa (arXiv:1103.1251) reach the vanishing of the leading term with "a genuine gauge invariant variable which is naturally composed purely of the information in our local universe", arguing from the finite observable volume [src: abstract].

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Calling the comoving-gauge squeezed limit a signal.** `[der]` The value of Eq. 213 of arXiv:1303.1523 is not an observable local $f_{\rm NL}$: Eq. 29 of arXiv:1305.0824 shows it is removed by the frame of the local observer. A paper that forecasts a detection of $f_{\rm NL}$ of the size of $n_s-1$ from single-field inflation must include the coordinate transformation; check against Eqs. 27–29 of arXiv:1305.0824 and the floor of arXiv:1612.00033.
- **The linear form of ζ.** The relation holds "only if we generalize the linear expression $(1+2\zeta(x))$ to the exponential form used in the metric (2)" (arXiv:astro-ph/0407059) [src: text after Eq. 4]; a calculation in the linear variable at second order differs by a field redefinition.
- **Normalisation of $P$ and of $n_s$.** The relation is printed with $d\log k^3P/d\log k$ for a dimensionful P (arXiv:0709.0295) [src: Eq. 2], with $n_s-1$ and a dimensionless $P_\zeta$ (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Eq. 9.6] and with $P_{\zeta}(k)\propto k^{-4+n_{s}}$ (arXiv:1305.0824) [src: Sect. III.2, inline, text after Eq. 28]; mixing them changes the coefficient. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **A soft momentum that is not soft enough.** With resonance, a non-Bunch–Davies state or a large non-local $f_{NL}$, the relation is reached only far into the squeezed limit (Chen 2010, Sect. 9.2, second and third corrections); a numerical check at moderate $k_3/k_1$ can show an apparent violation.
- **Non-attractor results that ignore the transition.** The non-attractor $f_{NL}$ assumes an instant transition; Cai et al. show that the transition can erase it "although the consistency relation remains violated" (arXiv:1712.09998) [src: abstract].
- **Slips copied as printed.** Creminelli & Zaldarriaga print κ on the left and k on the right of Eqs. 1 and 7 (arXiv:astro-ph/0407059) [src: Eqs. 1, 7]; the κ of Eq. 1 is already recorded in `references/approximations.md#convention-traps`. Wang's Eq. 208 has a delta function in a denominator, flagged by the author himself [src: Sect. III.6.1, footnote 53] (arXiv:1303.1523).
