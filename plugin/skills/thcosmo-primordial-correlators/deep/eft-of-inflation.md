# From the unitary-gauge EFT to f_NL — thcosmo-primordial-correlators dossier

Scope: how the Goldstone action follows from the unitary-gauge action of the EFT of inflation by the Stückelberg trick, when the decoupling from gravity holds and what it costs, and how the equilateral and orthogonal amplitudes follow from $c_s$ and $M_3$ (or $\tilde c_3$), with the sign and normalisation conventions of each step.

## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- Cheung et al. 2007 (arXiv:0709.0293) keep $M_{\rm Pl}$ explicit, write the unperturbed metric $ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+a^{2}(t)d\vec{x}^{2}$ [src: Eq. 7] and use $\epsilon\equiv-\dot{H}/H^{2}$ [src: Sect. 3, inline, text after Eq. 26]. The Goldstone π is a time shift, related at linear order to the curvature perturbation of their Eq. 29 by $\zeta(t,\vec{x})=-H\pi(t,\vec{x})\;.$ (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Eq. 30]. Senatore, Smith & Zaldarriaga 2010 state the same relation, "valid at linear order and at leading order in the generalized slow roll parameters" (arXiv:0905.3746) [src: Sect. 2].
- $f_{\rm NL}$ variable. Senatore et al. define the bispectrum of $\Phi=\frac{3}{5}\zeta\ ,$ (arXiv:0905.3746) [src: Eq. 14], "valid, at first order, out of the horizon, during matter domination" [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 14], and $f_{NL}$ by the equilateral point, $F(k,k,k)=f_{NL}\cdot\frac{6\Delta_{\Phi}^{2}}{k^{6}}\ ,$ (arXiv:0905.3746) [src: Eq. 20], with $\Delta_{\Phi}=\frac{9}{25}\frac{H^{2}}{4\,\epsilon\,c_{s}\,M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\ ,$ [src: Eq. 18]. Planck 2018 IX uses $\Phi\equiv(3/5)\zeta$ (`formalism.md#shapes-and-templates`).
- Cubic coefficient. Cheung et al. use $M_3^4$, the coefficient of $(g^{00}+1)^3/3!$ [src: Eq. 10]. Senatore et al. use the dimensionless $\tilde c_3$, with "$\tilde{c}_{3}=c_{s}^{2}c_{3}$, where $c_{3}$ is the parameter defined in [10]" (arXiv:0905.3746) [src: Sect. 2, footnote 2]. Planck 2018 IX gives the map between them (Step 5). Grall & Melville 2020 (arXiv:2005.02366) use other coefficients $c_n$, $\alpha_1$, $\beta_1$, defined in their Eqs. 2.37 and 2.41 (not transcribed).
- Chen 2010 (arXiv:1002.1416) works instead in ζ gauge with $P(X,\phi)$ and the quantities $c_s^2$, Σ and λ of its Eqs. 6.2–6.4.

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — the unitary-gauge action
<!-- key: step-1-the-unitary-gauge-action -->

In the gauge $\delta\phi(\vec{x},t)=0$ "there are no explicit scalar perturbations, but only metric fluctuations", and "the scalar perturbation has been eaten by the metric"; the operators allowed are those invariant under "time dependent spatial diffeomorphisms", such as polynomials of $g^{00}$ and the extrinsic curvature of constant-time surfaces, multiplied by "generic functions of time" (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Sect. 2]. Only the first three terms contain linear perturbations, so the tadpole cancellation fixes $c(t)$ and $\Lambda(t)$ by the Friedmann equations (Eqs. 8–9) [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 5], and the action becomes $S\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int\!d^{4}x\;\sqrt{-g}\Big[\frac{1}{2}M_{\rm Pl}^{2}R+M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\dot{H}g^{00}-M_{\rm Pl}^{2}(3H^{2}+\dot{H})+\frac{1}{2!}M_{2}(t)^{4}(g^{00}+1)^{2}+\frac{1}{3!}M_{3}(t)^{4}(g^{00}+1)^{3}+-\frac{\bar{M}_{1}(t)^{3}}{2}(g^{00}+1)\delta K^{\mu}{}_{\mu}-\frac{\bar{M}_{2}(t)^{2}}{2}\delta K^{\mu}{}_{\mu}{}^{2}-\frac{\bar{M}_{3}(t)^{2}}{2}\delta K^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\delta K^{\nu}{}_{\mu}+...\Big]\;.$ (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Eq. 10], the two rows joined in reading order; the "+-" at the join is the end of the first row and the start of the second, as printed. This completes the transcription that `formalism.md#not-verified` lists as missing. The coefficients are assumed not to "vary significantly in one Hubble time", so that the Lagrangian is "approximately time translation invariant" [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 10]. A $P(X,\phi)$ model is of this form "with $M_{n}^{4}(t)=\dot{\phi}_{0}(t)^{2n}\partial^{n}P/\partial X^{n}$ evaluated at $\phi_{0}(t)$" (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Sect. 2, inline, text after Eq. 12].

### Step 2 — the Stückelberg trick
<!-- key: step-2-the-stuckelberg-trick -->

For two operators $\int d^{4}x\;\sqrt{-g}\left[A(t)+B(t)g^{00}(x)\right]\ .$ (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Eq. 18], a broken time diffeomorphism is performed and, wherever $\xi^0$ appears, the substitution $\xi^{0}(x(\widetilde{x}))\to-\widetilde{\pi}(\widetilde{x})\,.$ [src: Eq. 22] gives $\int d^{4}x\;\sqrt{-g(x)}\left[A(t+\pi(x))+B(t+\pi(x))\frac{\partial(t+\pi(x))}{\partial x^{\mu}}\frac{\partial(t+\pi(x))}{\partial x^{\nu}}g^{\mu\nu}(x)\right].$ [src: Eq. 23], invariant "at all orders" under diffeomorphisms when $\pi(x)\to\widetilde{\pi}(\widetilde{x}(x))=\pi(x)-\xi^{0}(x)\ .$ (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Eq. 24]. Applied to Eq. 10, this gives the full action of their Eq. 25, in which $g^{00}$ enters through the combination $(1+\dot{\pi})^{2}g^{00}+2(1+\dot{\pi})\partial_{i}\pi g^{0i}+g^{ij}\partial_{i}\pi\partial_{j}\pi$ (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Eq. 25], and where "for the moment we have neglected for simplicity terms that involve the extrinsic curvature" [src: Sect. 3, text after Eq. 25].

### Step 3 — the decoupling limit
<!-- key: step-3-the-decoupling-limit -->

The quadratic terms mixing π with the metric "contain fewer derivatives than the kinetic term of π so that they can be neglected above some high energy scale" (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Sect. 3, text after Eq. 25]. Two cases are worked out:

- Slow roll ($M_2=M_3=0$): the leading mixing is $\sim M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\dot{H}\dot{\pi}\delta g^{00}\ .$ (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Eq. 26], negligible "for energies above $E_{\rm mix}\sim\epsilon^{1/2}H$" after canonical normalisation [src: Sect. 3, inline, text after Eq. 26].
- Large $M_2$: the mixing $\sim M_{2}^{4}\dot{\pi}\delta g^{00}$ (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Eq. 27] "becomes negligible at energies larger than $E_{\rm mix}\sim M_{2}^{2}/M_{\rm Pl}$" [src: Sect. 3, inline, text after Eq. 27]; the paper adds that "Lorentz symmetry is spontaneously broken, so one should define a separate regime of energies and momenta for which the mixing can be neglected", and that for cosmological perturbations only the energy range matters [src: Sect. 3, footnote 4].

For energies well above the mixing scale the action becomes $\!\!S_{\rm\pi}=\int\!d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{1}{2}M_{\rm Pl}^{2}R-M^{2}_{\rm Pl}\dot{H}\left(\dot{\pi}^{2}-\frac{(\partial_{i}\pi)^{2}}{a^{2}}\right)+2M^{4}_{2}\left(\dot{\pi}^{2}+\dot{\pi}^{3}-\dot{\pi}\frac{(\partial_{i}\pi)^{2}}{a^{2}}\right)-\frac{4}{3}M^{4}_{3}\dot{\pi}^{3}+...\right].$ (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Eq. 28]. Its use for the late-time observables rests on the conservation of ζ outside the horizon "at any order in perturbation theory", so "the problem is reduced to calculating correlation functions just after horizon crossing"; "If the decoupling scale $E_{\rm mix}$ is smaller than H, the Lagrangian for π (28) will give the correct predictions up to terms suppressed by $E_{\rm mix}/H$" [src: Sect. 3, text after Eq. 28]. The cubic and other relevant operators from the Taylor expansion of the coefficients in $t+\pi$ are suppressed by "generalized slow roll parameters" and "have therefore been neglected" [src: Sect. 3, text after Eq. 28]. The paper adds that the approach "does not lose its validity when the mixing with gravity is important": then "The action (10) contains all the information about the model" [src: Sect. 3, last paragraph].

With a sound speed, the condition reads "H ≫ $M_{2}^{2}/M_{\rm Pl}$, or equivalently for $\epsilon/c_{s}^{2}\ll 1$" (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Sect. 4.2, inline, text after Eq. 39], as in `formalism.md#effective-field-theory-of-inflation`.

### Step 4 — speed of sound and the tied cubic operator
<!-- key: step-4-speed-of-sound-and-the-tied-cubic -->

The time kinetic term of Eq. 28 is $\left(-M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\dot{H}+2M_{2}^{4}\right)\dot{\pi}^{2}\;.$ (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Eq. 37], so "To avoid instabilities we must have $-M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\dot{H}+2M_{2}^{4}>0$" [src: Sect. 4.2, text after Eq. 37], while the spatial gradient term is fixed by $\dot H$ alone and has the "healthy" sign "only for $\dot{H}<0$" [src: Sect. 4.2, first paragraph]. The two coefficients differ, which defines $c_s$ (Eq. 38, `formalism.md`); "$c_{s}=1$ is not protected by any symmetry" and deviations are induced "at the very least by graviton loops" [src: Sect. 4.2, text after Eq. 37]. Rewriting $M_2$ through $c_s$ gives Eq. 39 (`formalism.md`), where "the same operator giving a reduced speed of sound induces cubic couplings of the Goldstones of the form $\dot{\pi}(\nabla\pi)^{2}$ and $\dot{\pi}^{3}$": "The non-linear realization of time diffeomorphisms forces a relation between a reduced speed of sound and an enhanced level of the 3-point function" (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Sect. 4.2, text after Eq. 41]. `[der]` In Eq. 28 one coefficient multiplies the quadratic time-derivative term and the two cubic terms of the same bracket: once the quadratic coefficient is fixed by the sound speed, those cubic coefficients are not free, which is the content of that sentence; the remaining free cubic coefficient is that of $M_3$.

Senatore et al. write the same action with $\tilde c_3$ and the higher-derivative terms of the near-de Sitter limit, $S_{\pi}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\left[-\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\dot{H}}{c_{s}^{2}}\left(\dot{\pi}^{2}-c_{s}^{2}\frac{1}{a^{2}}(\partial_{i}\pi)^{2}\right)\right.\!\!\!\!\!\!\!+\left.\frac{\dot{H}M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{c_{s}^{2}}(1-c_{s}^{2})\dot{\pi}\frac{1}{a^{2}}(\partial_{i}\pi)^{2}-\frac{\dot{H}M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{c_{s}^{2}}(1-c_{s}^{2})\left(1+\frac{2}{3}\frac{\tilde{c}_{3}}{c_{s}^{2}}\right)\dot{\pi}^{3}\right.\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\left.-\frac{d_{1}}{4}HM^{3}\left(6\,\dot{\pi}^{2}+\frac{1}{a^{2}}({\partial}_{i}\pi)^{2}\right)-\frac{(d_{2}+d_{3})}{2}M^{2}\frac{1}{a^{4}}({\partial}_{i}^{2}\pi)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}d_{1}M^{3}\frac{1}{a^{4}}({\partial}_{j}^{2}\pi)({\partial}_{i}\pi)^{2}\right.\!\!\!\!\!\!\!+\ \ldots\ \Big]\ ,$ (arXiv:0905.3746) [src: Eq. 8], rows joined, "assumed that the Goldstone boson is protected by an approximate shift symmetry" and with the metric perturbations neglected [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 8]. The third-line terms matter only "very close to de Sitter space", when Eq. 10 of that paper holds; "away from the near-de-Sitter limit", the cubic Lagrangian "contains, at leading order in derivatives, just two interaction operators" [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 10]. Planck 2018 IX prints the leading part as $S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\thinspace-\thinspace\frac{M^{2}_{\rm Pl}\dot{H}}{c_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}}\left(\dot{\pi}^{2}-c_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}\frac{(\partial_{i}\pi)^{2}}{a^{2}}\right)\right.\left.-M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\dot{H}(1-c_{\mathrm{s}}^{-2})\dot{\pi}\frac{(\partial_{i}\pi)^{2}}{a^{2}}+\left(M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\dot{H}(1-c_{\mathrm{s}}^{-2})-\frac{4}{3}M_{3}^{4}\right)\dot{\pi}^{3}\right]\thinspace.$ (arXiv:1905.05697) [src: Eq. 60], with $M_3$ kept.

### Step 5 — from $c_s$ and $M_3$ to the two amplitudes
<!-- key: step-5-from-cs-and-m3-to-the-two-amplitudes -->

The in-in calculation of the two operators, far from de Sitter and with the shift symmetry, gives the shapes $F_{\dot\pi(\partial_i\pi)^2}$ and $F_{\dot\pi^3}$ of Eq. 16 of arXiv:0905.3746 [src: Sect. 2, text before Eq. 16]; Senatore et al. take them from their Ref. [10] and note that "In a different and somewhat less general formalism, this expression was obtained also in [26]" [src: Sect. 2, footnote 5]. With Eq. 20 they define $f_{NL}^{\dot{\pi}({\partial}_{i}\pi)^{2}}=\frac{85}{324}\left(1-\frac{1}{c_{s}^{2}}\right)\ , f_{NL}^{\dot{\pi}^{3}}=\frac{10}{243}\left(1-\frac{1}{c_{s}^{2}}\right)\left(\tilde{c}_{3}+\frac{3}{2}c_{s}^{2}\right)\ ,$ (arXiv:0905.3746) [src: Eq. 21], rows joined. Their reading: "if we wish to have a speed of sound smaller than one (a necessary condition for the existence of a Lorentz invariant UV completion [27]), we need to have $f_{NL}^{\dot{\pi}({\partial}_{i}\pi)^{2}}<0$"; "the size and the shape of the three-point function is controlled by two parameters: the speed of sound $c_{s}$ and the parameter $\tilde{c}_{3}$", so that at fixed overall size "there is a one-parameter family of shapes"; "for $\tilde{c}_{3}$ of ${\cal{O}}(1)$, a necessary condition for the 3-point function to be large is that the speed of sound is small" (arXiv:0905.3746) [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 22]. <!-- num: not a value -->

Planck 2018 IX writes the same two amplitudes as $f_{\rm NL}^{\rm EFT1}=-(85/324)(c_{\mathrm{s}}^{-2}-1)$ and $f_{\rm NL}^{\rm EFT2}=-(10/243)(c_{\mathrm{s}}^{-2}-1)\left[\tilde{c}_{3}+(3/2)c_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}\right]$, and states the map to $M_3$, ${\tilde{c}}_{3}(c_{\mathrm{s}}^{-2}-1)=2M_{3}^{4}c_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}/(\dot{H}M_{\rm Pl}^{2})$ (arXiv:1905.05697) [src: Sect. 8.1, inline, text after Eq. 60], citing Senatore et al. 2010 for it. It then projects the two EFT shapes on the templates, $f^{\mathrm{equil}}_{\mathrm{NL}}=\frac{1-c_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}}{c_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}}\left[-0.275-0.0780c_{\rm s}^{2}-(2/3)\times 0.780\tilde{c}_{3}\right]\thinspace, f^{\mathrm{ortho}}_{\mathrm{NL}}=\frac{1-c_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}}{c_{\mathrm{s}}^{2}}\left[0.0159-0.0167c_{\rm s}^{2}-(2/3)\times 0.0167\tilde{c}_{3}\right]\thinspace,$ (arXiv:1905.05697) [src: Eq. 61], rows joined, "where the coefficients come from the Fisher matrix between the theoretical bispectra predicted by the two operators ... and the equilateral and orthogonal templates" [src: Sect. 8.1, text after Eq. 61]. DBI is the line $\tilde{c}_{3}=3(1-c_{\mathrm{s}}^{2})/2$, and "the non-interacting (vanishing NG) case corresponds to $c_{\mathrm{s}}=1$ and $M_{3}=0$" (arXiv:1905.05697) [src: Sect. 8.1, inline, text after Eq. 61]. Planck 2015 XVII prints the same coefficients (arXiv:1502.01592) [src: Eq. 99].

### Step 6 — from the two amplitudes to a bound
<!-- key: step-6-from-the-two-amplitudes-to-a-bound -->

Planck 2018 IX fits Eq. 61 to the joint equilateral and orthogonal estimates with a $\chi^2$ on $(\tilde c_3,c_s)$ and, "Marginalizing over $\tilde{c}_{3}$", finds $c_{\mathrm{s}}\geq\ 0.021\quad\quad\text{(95\thinspace\%, {T}\ only)}\thinspace,$ (arXiv:1905.05697) [src: Eq. 62] and $c_{\mathrm{s}}\geq\ 0.021\quad\quad\text{(95\thinspace\%, {T+E})}\thinspace.$ [src: Eq. 63]; on the DBI line the bound is Eq. 59 of the same paper (`results.md`).

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **ζ gauge with $P(X,\phi)$.** Chen 2010 (arXiv:1002.1416) computes the leading bispectrum of $P(X,\phi)$ in ζ gauge with full gravity and splits it into $S_\lambda$ and $S_c$, of sizes $f_{NL}^{\lambda}=\frac{5}{81}\left(\frac{1}{c_{s}^{2}}-1-\frac{2\lambda}{\Sigma}\right)~, f_{NL}^{c}=-\frac{35}{108}\left(\frac{1}{c_{s}^{2}}-1\right)~.$ (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Eqs. 6.19–6.20], rows joined; the subleading parts are of order $\epsilon/c_s^2$, $\epsilon\lambda/\Sigma$ and $\epsilon$, $\eta$, $s$ [src: Eq. 6.21]. In the limit "$c_{s}\ll 1$ or $\lambda/\Sigma\gg 1$" the same two shapes follow from "considering only the fluctuations in scalar field while neglecting those in gravity", with ${\cal L}_{3}=a^{3}\left(\frac{1}{2}P_{,XX}\dot{\phi}_{0}+\frac{1}{6}P_{,XXX}\dot{\phi}_{0}^{3}\right)\dot{\delta\phi}^{3}-\frac{a}{2}P_{,XX}\dot{\phi}_{0}\dot{\delta\phi}(\nabla\delta\phi)^{2}~.$ (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Eq. 6.22]; "The approach that we present here gives a rigorous justification to such an method" [src: Sect. 6.1, text after Eq. 6.22]. Cost: the split $(\lambda,c)$ is not the split $(\dot\pi^3,\dot\pi(\partial\pi)^2)$ of the EFT, because the ζ-gauge operators $\zeta\dot\zeta^2$ and $\zeta(\partial\zeta)^2$ both feed $S_c$ (Eq. 6.10 of arXiv:1002.1416). Cheung et al. take their Eq. 45 from this ζ-gauge calculation, "setting $P_{,XXX}=0$ in the notation of [21]" (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Sect. 4.2, footnote 8]; no paper read gives the map between (λ, Σ) and $M_3$ or $\tilde c_3$.
- **Full mixing with gravity.** Cheung et al. count the parameters of the three-point function from Eq. 10 without decoupling: "At leading order in slow-roll and in derivatives there are 2 coefficients ... $M_{2}$ and $M_{3}$. At first order in slow-roll, there will be 4 new parameters", the ε and η parameters and the slow variations of the two operators, which "is what one finds in the explicit calculation [21]" (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Sect. 4.2, text after Eq. 48]; "one of the coefficients does not give rise to an independent momentum dependence of the 3-point function" [src: Sect. 4.2, footnote 9]. <!-- num: not a value --> Chen et al. 2006 (arXiv:hep-th/0605045) find that "under reasonable assumptions, the non-Gaussianity is completely determined by 5 parameters" [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Near-de Sitter (ghost-like) limit.** When $M_{\rm Pl}^{2}|\dot{H}|\lesssim{\rm Max}(\;d_{1}HM^{3}/4,\,(d_{2}+d_{3})M^{2}H^{2}/(2\,c_{s}^{2})\;)\ .$ (arXiv:0905.3746) [src: Eq. 10], the higher-derivative terms of Eq. 8 dominate and "the system approaches Ghost Inflation" [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 10]; $c_s$ is then the speed of sound only "in the limit in which we neglect the term proportional to $d_{1}$" [src: Sect. 2, footnote 3]. Cheung et al. treat this limit in their Sect. 4.3 (not read here).
- **Heavy fields integrated out.** Achúcarro et al. 2012 (arXiv:1201.6342) derive the single-field EFT of a multi-field parent theory with heavy fields, "valid to arbitrary order in perturbations, provided certain generalized adiabaticity conditions are respected"; its couplings "can be written entirely in terms of the reduced speed of sound", and certain operators "can become transiently strongly coupled along the inflaton trajectory" [src: abstract].
- **Dissipative extension.** The Open EFT of inflation is in `formalism.md#effective-field-theory-of-inflation`; its bound $c_s\geq 0.38$ is in `results.md` (arXiv:2603.13473). <!-- num: not a value -->

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **The sign of the $c_s$ amplitude.** Cheung et al. print $f_{\rm NL}^{\rm equil.}=\frac{85}{324}\cdot\frac{1}{c_{s}^{2}}\;$ "at leading in order in $c_{s}^{-1}$" (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Eq. 45], positive; Senatore et al. print $\frac{85}{324}\left(1-\frac{1}{c_{s}^{2}}\right)$, negative for $c_s<1$ as their text says (arXiv:0905.3746) [src: Eq. 21, Sect. 2]; Planck 2018 IX prints $-(85/324)(c_{\mathrm{s}}^{-2}-1)$ (arXiv:1905.05697) [src: Sect. 8.1, inline, text after Eq. 60]. No paper read states the sign convention of Eq. 45 of arXiv:0709.0293; take the sign together with the definition of $f_{\rm NL}$ (Eqs. 14 and 20 of arXiv:0905.3746) from one paper. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Decoupling assumed outside its range.** "If this is not the case one is not assured that the Goldstone action contains the leading effects" (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Sect. 4.2, text after Eq. 39]. `[der]` For canonical slow roll the mixing scale of Eq. 26 lies below H by the square root of ε, so the Goldstone action is enough there only for observables not dominated by the mixing; Cheung et al. write that Eq. 28 computes "all the observables which are not dominated by the mixing with gravity, like for example the non-Gaussianities in standard slow-roll inflation [13, 14]" and that "the tilt of the spectrum can be calculated, at leading order" with it (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Sect. 3, last paragraph]. The slow-roll bispectrum with mixing is `deep/in-in-bispectrum.md`.
- **Two operators, one measured combination.** In 2007 "this difference is not experimentally appreciable at present, so that the constraint (46) is on the joint effect of the two operators", and "The constraint on the speed of sound will hold barring a cancellation between the two operators" (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Sect. 4.2, text after Eq. 47]. With equilateral and orthogonal templates the degeneracy is lifted only through Eq. 61 of arXiv:1905.05697, a projection whose coefficients come from a Fisher matrix, not from the shapes themselves.
- **Naturalness of $\tilde c_3$.** For $\tilde c_3$ of order one the strong-coupling scales of the two operators, $\Lambda^{4}_{\dot{\pi}({\partial}_{i}\pi)^{2}}\sim 16\pi^{2}M_{\rm Pl}^{2}|\dot{H}|\frac{c_{s}^{5}}{(1-c_{s}^{2})^{2}}\ ,$ (arXiv:0905.3746) [src: Eq. 11] and $\Lambda^{4}_{\dot{\pi}^{3}}\sim\Lambda^{4}_{\dot{\pi}({\partial}_{i}\pi)^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{\left(c_{s}^{2}+2\tilde{c}_{3}/3\right)^{2}}\ ,$ [src: Eq. 12], "are indeed of the same order", and "$\tilde{c}_{3}$ of order one, and a small speed of sound $c_{s}$, are technically natural"; this "has assumed that the operator proportional to $(d_{2}+d_{3})$ is negligible up the cutoff", which holds "only if $(d_{2}+d_{3})\lesssim c_{s}$" (arXiv:0905.3746) [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 12, footnote 4].
  - `[der]` The power of the factor in the sound speed in the denominator of Eq. 11 of arXiv:0905.3746 differs from the one in the second form of Eq. 50 of arXiv:0709.0293 (`formalism.md`); both are estimates, and the papers read do not compare them.
- **Weak coupling up to H is not guaranteed in the allowed region.** Grall & Melville bound the cutoff and the boost-breaking strength by radiative stability; for the Planck best fit "the strength of the symmetry breaking is at least $g_{n}^{2}\sim 1.4$", and "one should not conclude from this that radiative stability has been violated—however it does motivate a more careful analysis", "since observational constraints currently include regions of parameter space in which $g_{n}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)$ and $\Lambda_{\rm max}\sim\mathcal{O}(H)$" (arXiv:2005.02366) [src: Sect. 2.3, text after Eq. 2.42]. Their abstract proposes the weakly coupled single-field region as "a theoretical prior" [src: abstract].
- **Positivity.** Baumann, Green, Lee & Porto 2015 (arXiv:1502.07304), assuming a theory "Lorentz invariant at short distances" and forward scattering, derive a sum rule; when positivity applies, which they show holds for "all known UV completions of single-field inflation", it "constrains the size and the sign of the equilateral four-point function in terms of the amplitude of the three-point function" and "rules out about half of the parameter space that is still allowed by current observations" [src: abstract].
- **Printed slips** (copied as printed, not corrected):
  - Grall & Melville print the illustrative best-fit sound speed as "$c_{s}\approx-0.031$", a negative speed, and the maximal cutoff as $\Lambda_{\rm max}^{4}=16\pi^{2}c_{s}^{4}f_{\pi}^{4}/g_{n}^{2}$ in the text but $\Lambda_{\rm max}^{4}=16\pi^{2}c_{s}^{4}f_{\pi}^{2}/g_{n}^{2}$ in the caption of Fig. 2 (arXiv:2005.02366) [src: Sect. 2.3, text after Eq. 2.42, Fig. 2 caption]; none published.
  - In Eq. 61 of arXiv:1905.05697 the equilateral row prints $0.0780$ for the $c_s^2$ coefficient and $(2/3)\times 0.780$ for the $\tilde c_3$ one, while the orthogonal row repeats $0.0167$ in both places; Planck 2015 XVII prints the same (arXiv:1502.01592) [src: Eq. 99]. `[der]` Since the second amplitude of Eq. 21 of arXiv:0905.3746 carries the two parameters only through one combination, one pattern would be expected in both rows; the coefficients were not recomputed (see "Not verified").
