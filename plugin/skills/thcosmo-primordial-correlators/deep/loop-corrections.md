## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- **Senatore & Zaldarriaga 2009** work in the EFT of inflation with a large $\dot\pi^3$ self-interaction of coefficients $c_3$ and $M$ (their Sect. 3; definitions not transcribed), conformal time η, and μ for the renormalisation scale (arXiv:0912.2734) [src: Sect. 3.2.1]. In dimensional regularisation the spatial dimension is $3+\delta$ [src: Eq. 62]. <!-- num: not a value --> <!-- eq: notation -->
- **Kristiano & Yokoyama 2022** (v3 read) use conformal time τ with primes for $d/d\tau$, η for the second slow-roll parameter, $\tau_e$ for the end of the USR period, and dimensionless spectra written $\Delta^{2}_{s(0)}$ (tree level) and $\Delta^{2}_{s(1)}$ (one loop) (arXiv:2211.03395) [src: Eqs. 27, 34]. Their cubic vertex is written for the redefined field of Maldacena, "Strictly speaking, this interaction Hamiltonian is a function of redefined field $\bm{\zeta}$" [src: footnote 1]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Tada, Terada & Tokuda 2023** "consider the genuine curvature perturbation $\zeta$ rather than quantities like $\zeta_{n}$ related to the original $\zeta$ by field redefinitions" (arXiv:2308.04732) [src: Sect. 2, footnote 2], with $P$ the dimensionful and $\mathcal{P}=k^{3}P/(2\pi^{2})$ the dimensionless spectrum [src: Eq. 2]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- The gauge differs between replies: comoving (ζ) gauge in Fumagalli 2024 (arXiv:2408.08296) [src: Sect. 1], the δφ gauge in Ballesteros & Gambín Egea (arXiv:2404.07196) [src: abstract], comoving and spatially flat gauges in Artigas et al. (arXiv:2608.03725) [src: abstract], and the separate-universe and δN frameworks in Iacconi (arXiv:2605.04815) and Firouzjahi & Riotto (arXiv:2304.07801) [src: abstracts]. No paper read gives the map between their one-loop expressions.

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — the one-loop two-point function in the in-in formalism
<!-- key: step-1-the-one-loop-two-point-function-in-the -->

At one loop a cubic Hamiltonian enters twice; Kristiano & Yokoyama split the second-order in-in expectation value into a term with one insertion on each side of the operator, $\langle\mathcal{O}\rangle_{(1,1)}$, and a term with both insertions on one side, $\langle\mathcal{O}\rangle_{(0,2)}$, and sum them as $\langle\!\langle\zeta_{\mathbf{p}}(\tau_{0})\zeta_{-\mathbf{p}}(\tau_{0})\rangle\!\rangle_{(1)}=\langle\!\langle\zeta_{\mathbf{p}}(\tau_{0})\zeta_{-\mathbf{p}}(\tau_{0})\rangle\!\rangle_{(1,1)}+2\mathrm{Re}\langle\!\langle\zeta_{\mathbf{p}}(\tau_{0})\zeta_{-\mathbf{p}}(\tau_{0})\rangle\!\rangle_{(0,2)}=\frac{1}{4}M_{\mathrm{pl}}^{4}\epsilon^{2}(\tau_{e})a^{4}(\tau_{e})(\Delta\eta)^{2}$ (arXiv:2211.03395) [src: Eq. 31], the first part of a display that continues in their Eq. 32. Tada et al. classify the same contributions in the Schwinger–Keldysh language into a "cut-in-the-middle" diagram, "suppressed in the soft limit $p\to 0$", and a "cut-in-the-side" diagram that "is understood as the effective mass correction" and is "proportional to $p^{-3}$ and hence this diagram may cause a scale-invariant correction on the soft propagator" (arXiv:2308.04732) [src: Sect. 2, Fig. 1 and text after it]. <!-- eq: notation -->

### Step 2 — regularisation, and why the logarithm is log(H/μ)
<!-- key: step-2-regularisation-and-why-the-logarithm-is -->

Senatore & Zaldarriaga compute the one-loop two-point function for a large $\dot\pi^3$ interaction with "a sharp cutoff in momentum and frequency space, and the other in dimensional regularization", at finite time, and "by simply making dimensionless the loop integrals", then renormalise "by explicitly finding and solving for the relevant counterterms"; "all the calculations agree" (arXiv:0912.2734) [src: Sect. 3.4]. The dimensionless argument: assuming the ζ two-point function is time independent (shown in their Sect. 5), the loop integral of their Eq. 62 is made dimensionless with the external momentum k, "possible because the scale factor is unchanged in dim. reg:" $a(\eta_{1})=-\frac{1}{H\eta_{1}}=-\left(\frac{k}{H}\right)\frac{1}{k\eta_{1}}\ ,$ [src: Eq. 63], and because the wavefunctions "are just functions of $k\eta$ apart for multiplicative factors" [src: Sect. 3.2.1, text after Eq. 63]. The prefactor is then $(H/\mu)^\delta$ times a function $I(\delta)$, and $\left(\frac{H}{\mu}\right)^{\delta}\hskip 18.49988pt\rightarrow\hskip 18.49988pt1+\delta\log\left(\frac{H}{\mu}\right)\ .$ [src: Eq. 66], which "will multiply any pole coming from the integral $I(\delta)$ as $\delta\rightarrow 0$ , giving rise to a logarithm of the form" $\log\left(\frac{H}{\mu}\right)\ .$ [src: Eq. 67] (arXiv:0912.2734). <!-- num: not a value -->

The result: $\langle\zeta_{\vec{k}}(t)\zeta_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}(t)\rangle_{\rm{1-loop,\,t\rightarrow+\infty}}=-(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\vec{k}+\vec{k}^{\prime})\frac{2}{15\pi^{2}}\frac{c_{3}^{2}H^{8}M^{8}}{\dot{H}^{4}M_{\rm Pl}^{8}}\frac{1}{k^{3}}\times\log\left(\frac{H}{\mu}\right)\,.$ (arXiv:0912.2734) [src: Eq. 73]. "contrary to the $\log(k/\mu)$ found in the former literature, the real space version of (73) is symmetric under the rescaling" $a\rightarrow\lambda\,a\ ,\hskip 18.49988ptx\rightarrow x/\lambda\ ,\hskip 18.49988ptk\rightarrow\lambda\,k\ ,$ [src: Eq. 74], "a symmetry of the problem"; physically, "the Hubble scale is cutting off the infrared behavior that would otherwise be there in Minkowky space" [src: Sect. 3.4, text after Eq. 74] (typo "Minkowky" as printed).

### Step 3 — the USR vertex and the localisation of the time integral
<!-- key: step-3-the-usr-vertex-and-the-localisation-of -->

Kristiano & Yokoyama keep "The leading cubic self-interaction", the last term of their cubic action (Eq. 24), $H_{\mathrm{int}}=-\frac{1}{2}M_{\mathrm{pl}}^{2}\int\mathrm{d}^{3}x~a^{2}\epsilon\eta^{\prime}\zeta^{\prime}\zeta^{2}.$ (arXiv:2211.03395) [src: Eq. 27]. Their footnote 1 states what this choice leaves aside: the relation to the redefined field, "It is shown that the field redefinition method is equivalent to considering boundary interaction", and "quartic self-interaction ... with first-order perturbation theory is expected to generate independent contributions to the one-loop correction because it involves a higher-order SR parameter" [src: footnote 1]. With a sharp change of η at $\tau_e$, the time integrals localise, $\int_{-\infty}^{0}\mathrm{d}\tau~\eta^{\prime}(\tau)f(\tau)=\Delta\eta~f(\tau_{e}),$ [src: Eq. 30], and the commutator of the mode functions at the end of USR is $[\mathrm{Im}(\zeta_{q}\zeta_{q}^{\prime*})]_{\tau=\tau_{e}}=\frac{1}{4M_{\mathrm{pl}}^{2}\epsilon(\tau_{e})a^{2}(\tau_{e})},$ [src: Eq. 33] (arXiv:2211.03395), "independent of wavenumber $q$", which "leads to the commutation relation $[\zeta,\dot{\zeta}]\propto a^{3}$ , which is in line with large quantum loop correction" [src: text after Eq. 33].

### Step 4 — the one-loop correction to the large-scale spectrum
<!-- key: step-4-the-one-loop-correction-to-the-large -->

Substituting into Eq. 32 gives $\Delta_{s(1)}^{2}(p)=\frac{1}{4}(\Delta\eta)^{2}\Delta_{s(0)}^{2}(p)\int_{k_{\mathrm{IR}}}^{k_{\mathrm{UV}}}\frac{\mathrm{d}k}{2\pi^{2}}k^{2}\absolutevalue{\zeta_k(\tau_e)}^{2}.$ (arXiv:2211.03395) [src: Eq. 34]. The authors "conservatively restrict the wavenumber integration domain from $k_{\mathrm{IR}}=k_{s}$ to $k_{\mathrm{UV}}=k_{e}$", the UV part being "discussed later" [src: text after Eq. 34]; for $k_e/k_s\gg1$, $\Delta_{s(1)}^{2}(p)=\frac{1}{4}(\Delta\eta)^{2}\Delta_{s(0)}^{2}(p)\Delta_{s(\mathrm{PBH})}^{2}\left(1.1+\log\frac{k_{e}}{k_{s}}\right).$ [src: Eq. 35]. Requiring the loop to be much smaller than the tree level, $\frac{1}{4}(\Delta\eta)^{2}\Delta_{s(\mathrm{PBH})}^{2}\left(1.1+\log\frac{k_{e}}{k_{s}}\right)\ll 1.$ [src: Eq. 36]; with "$\Delta\eta=6$ as a transition from the USR to SR period", "Solving inequality (36) numerically leads to an upper bound $k_{e}/k_{s}\ll 15$ , which is equivalent to" $\Delta_{s(\mathrm{PBH})}^{2}\ll\frac{1}{(\Delta\eta)^{2}}\simeq 0.03.$ [src: Eq. 37] (arXiv:2211.03395).

### Step 5 — the UV divergence and its counterterm in Kristiano & Yokoyama
<!-- key: step-5-the-uv-divergence-and-its-counterterm-in -->

Modes still inside the horizon at $\tau_e$ are added with a physical cutoff Λ, $\int_{k_{\mathrm{IR}}}^{k_{\mathrm{UV}}}\frac{\mathrm{d}k}{k}\Delta^{2}_{s(0)}(k)=\left(\int_{k_{s}}^{k_{e}}+\int_{k_{e}}^{\Lambda a(\tau_{e})}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}k}{k}\Delta^{2}_{s(0)}(k).$ (arXiv:2211.03395) [src: Eq. 38]. The result "diverges as we take the $\Lambda\rightarrow\infty$ limit"; "To make it finite, we need to renormalize order by order the coefficient $\Delta_{s(0)}^{2}(p_{*})$ so it depends on $\tilde{\Lambda}$", "equivalent to introducing a counterterm $\delta Z$" [src: text after Eq. 38]. In dimensional regularisation "$\log\tilde{\Lambda}$ is changed to $1/\delta$", and "the time integral in our case is dominated by $\tau=\tau_{e}$ , not the CMB horizon crossing time $\tau=-1/p$" [src: footnote 3]. "Such order by order renormalization is justified if higher-order loop correction is suppressed compared to the lower-order one. In this context, this is possible only if $(\Delta\eta)^{2}\Delta_{s(\mathrm{PBH})}^{2}\ll 1$ , which is the same condition as (37). Therefore, condition (37) is a necessary condition for the theory to be perturbative, or a requirement to avoid strong coupling" [src: text after Eq. 38]. <!-- eq: notation -->

### Step 6 — the consistency relation turns the loop into a total derivative
<!-- key: step-6-the-consistency-relation-turns-the-loop -->

Tada, Terada & Tokuda write the squeezed bispectrum of ζ with any operator $\mathcal{O}$ as the Fourier-space dilatation of its spectrum (their Eq. 1, the soft theorem of `deep/soft-limits-and-consistency.md`). "the cut-in-the-side diagram includes the bispectrum one shown in Fig. 2 as a subdiagram. Therefore, if the squeezed bispectrum satisfies Maldacena’s consistency relation (1), the diagram contribution should be summarised as" proportional to the logarithmic derivative of $\mathcal{P}_\mathcal{O}$, and the loop is proportional to $P_{\zeta}(p)\int_{\ln k_{\mathrm{min}}}^{\ln k_{\mathrm{max}}}\frac{k^{3}}{2\pi^{2}}P_{\mathcal{O}}(k)\derivative{\ln\calP_\calO(k)}{\ln k}\differential{\ln k}=P_{\zeta}(p)\pqty{\calP_\calO(k_\umax)-\calP_\calO(k_\umin)}.$ (arXiv:2308.04732) [src: Eq. 3], with the macros as printed. The lower bound is taken with "$\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{O}}(k_{\mathrm{min}})\ll\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{O}}(k_{\mathrm{enh}})$", and "The UV contribution $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{O}}(k_{\mathrm{max}}\to\infty)$ can be dropped by the $i\varepsilon$ prescription", which "is necessary to correctly evaluate the correlation functions in the vacuum state of the interacting theory in the infinite past" [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 3]. `[der]` This is the step that separates the two sides: Step 4 integrates the vertex of Eq. 27 alone between two finite bounds, Step 6 integrates the full squeezed bispectrum over all momenta.

### Step 7 — background renormalisation (Kristiano & Yokoyama 2025, v1)
<!-- key: step-7-background-renormalisation-kristiano -->

"If the comoving curvature perturbation $\zeta$ has a nonvanishing one-point function in nonlinear order, $\langle\zeta\rangle\neq 0$ , then the scale factor of the background universe, $a(t)$ , would be shifted to $a(t)e^{\langle\zeta(t)\rangle}$"; "we have to renormalize it by imposing $\langle\zeta\rangle=0$ as a renormalization condition, which is done by introducing linear counterterms. Then, these linear counterterms can induce quadratic counterterms by a nonlinear effect" (arXiv:2504.18514) [src: introductory paragraphs]. Their conclusion: these counterterms "subtract the divergent part of one-loop two-point functions, resulting in a UV finite term that can be large and highly time-dependent. We have found that such a finite term depends on the regularization scheme and must be taken as it is because the quadratic counterterms are completely fixed by the one-point function renormalization condition" [src: conclusion paragraph]. In the same paper: "we need counterterms beyond background renormalization to completely remove the UV finite term of one-loop two-point functions with a renormalization condition on the two-point functions themselves", for example "the speed of sound [54] and higher-order derivatives [76, 77] counterterms" [src: discussion of the regularisation schemes]. <!-- eq: notation -->

## What the PBH loop debate turns on
<!-- key: what-the-pbh-loop-debate-turns-on -->

Each side in its own words, with the version read.

- **The claim — Kristiano & Yokoyama 2022 (arXiv:2211.03395, v3, body read).** Steps 3–5: with the vertex of Eq. 27, perturbativity requires the condition of Eq. 37, "a necessary condition for the theory to be perturbative, or a requirement to avoid strong coupling"; "such coupling also affect the small-scale power spectrum and the same constraint is needed to avoid strong coupling" [src: text after Eq. 38]. The v3 title is "Constraining Primordial Black Hole Formation from Single-Field Inflation" (`results.md#loop-corrections`).
- **First replies — Riotto 2023 (arXiv:2301.00599, v1; arXiv:2303.01727, v1; abstracts).** "Based on general arguments, we show that the loop corrections to the large-scale linear power spectrum from the short modes are small and conclude that the scenario is not ruled out" (arXiv:2301.00599) [src: abstract]; in reply to arXiv:2303.00341, "we argue that the standard scenario ... is not ruled out" (arXiv:2303.01727) [src: abstract].
- **The claimants' note — Kristiano & Yokoyama 2023 (arXiv:2303.00341, v3, abstract).** "We explicitly confirm that bispectrum satisfies Maldacena's theorem"; the one-loop correction from the bispectrum ("the source method") is "consistent with the calculation of the one-loop correction from the second-order expansion of in-in perturbation theory" [src: abstract].
- **Sharpness of the transition — Firouzjahi 2023 (arXiv:2303.12025, v4, abstract).** With cubic and quartic Hamiltonians, "the amplitude of one-loop corrections depends on the sharpness of the transition"; "For an arbitrarily sharp transition, the one-loop correction becomes arbitrarily large, invalidating the perturbative treatment", and "We speculate that for a mild transition, the large one-loop corrections are washed out" [src: abstract]. With δN, Firouzjahi & Riotto (arXiv:2304.07801, v1) "explicitly show that one-loop corrections are negligible when the transition from the ultra-slow-roll to the slow-roll phase is smooth" [src: abstract]. Ballesteros & Gambín Egea (arXiv:2404.07196, v2) find the ratio of loop to tree "controlled by the duration of the ultra-slow-roll phase and of the transitions", and that "perturbation theory does not necessarily break in well-known models" [src: abstract].
- **Boundary (total-derivative) terms — Fumagalli 2023 and 2024.** Fumagalli 2023 (arXiv:2305.19263, v3, abstract): "we show the relevance of seemingly irrelevant interactions terms, such as the one provided by total derivative terms (boundary terms), and how they equally lead to non-volume suppressed contributions and exact cancellations" [src: abstract]. Fumagalli 2024 (arXiv:2408.08296, v2) states that the 2023 total-derivative contribution "alone is not sufficient to grant the entire cancellation of all relative scale-invariant corrections", having "neglected terms proportional to the Green’s function associated with the linear modes" [src: Sect. 1, text and footnote 2], and with the quartic Hamiltonians and consistency relations expresses "the final result as a total derivative term over comoving momenta", concluding that "one-loop corrections to long-wavelength modes are unaffected by the physics of short and enhanced modes" [src: abstract]. Braglia & Pinol (arXiv:2403.14558, v2) show that total-derivative interactions "can lead to artificially large contributions cancelling at different orders of the in-in operator formalism", and avoid them through canonical transformations [src: abstract].
- **Consistency relation — Tada, Terada & Tokuda 2023 (arXiv:2308.04732, v2, Sect. 2 read).** Step 6; "As a corollary, primordial black hole production in single-field inflation scenarios is not excluded by perturbativity breakdown even for the sharp transition case in contrast to some recent claims in the literature", and "our argument is not directly generalisable to the tensor perturbations" [src: abstract]. Kawaguchi, Tsujikawa & Yamada (arXiv:2407.19742, v2) prove the same in the path-integral formalism, where "the consistency relations and the effective constancy of tree-level mode functions of $ζ$ for super-Hubble modes play essential roles" [src: abstract].
- **Gauge and symmetry — Artigas, Pi, Tanaka & Urakawa 2026 (arXiv:2608.03725, v1, abstract).** Dropping gradients, dilatation invariance suppresses loops on CMB scales "for any inflaton potential, both with smooth and sharp transitions, and at any loop order, as long as the CMB scales cross the horizon during an SR phase"; "in the spatially-flat gauge, the cubic interaction contains a term that diverges in the sharp transition limit. However, this contribution is exactly cancelled by the quartic interaction" [src: abstract].
- **Separate universe — Iacconi 2026 (arXiv:2605.04815, v1, abstract).** Back-reaction at one loop "is due to either (i) non-linear super-horizon evolution, or (ii) 1-loop-corrected initial conditions", and "By assuming separation of scales and adiabaticity of the long mode, we show that the 1-loop back-reaction is not observable" [src: abstract].
- **The finite term — Kristiano & Yokoyama 2025 against Fang & Guo 2026.** Step 7 for the first. Fang & Guo (arXiv:2605.13325, v1, Sect. V read): "loop corrections to the correlation functions depend strongly on the choice of regularization scheme and the finite coefficients in the counterterms"; the Ward identity of the background–perturbation split "can simultaneously constrain both the regularization scheme and the counterterm coefficients" and "directly leads to an exact cancellation among loop diagrams" [src: Sect. V]. On the total-derivative route: "the handling of the quadratic divergences originating from such boundary integrals is precisely the key issue leading to renormalization ambiguities" [src: Sect. V]. Their limits: the symmetry "intrinsically serves as an additional assumption about the UV theory", and "our proof essentially considers loop diagrams with zero external momentum"; "For modes with finite observable momentum, however, the presence of growing modes can generate non-trivial loop corrections at superhorizon scales" [src: Sect. V].
- `[der]` The debate turns on four choices, each visible in the sources above: which interactions are kept (the single vertex of Eq. 27 of arXiv:2211.03395, or the boundary and quartic terms of arXiv:2408.08296); whether the momentum integral is cut at finite bounds or taken over all momenta with the iε prescription (Step 4 against Step 6); how sharp the USR-to-slow-roll transition is (arXiv:2303.12025, arXiv:2304.07801, arXiv:2608.03725); and which renormalisation condition fixes the finite part (arXiv:2504.18514 against arXiv:2605.13325). No paper read reports a calculation that makes all four choices and that both sides accept.

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **Cutoff in momentum and frequency, versus dimensional regularisation.** Senatore & Zaldarriaga find that both, with finite-time dim. reg. and the dimensionless argument, agree on Eq. 73 (arXiv:0912.2734) [src: Sect. 3.4]. For the USR loop, Kristiano & Yokoyama 2025 find that the finite term differs between schemes, "which also happens in flat space QFT", where it "can be absorbed by the finite part of quadratic counterterms"; "Here, we cannot do that because the quadratic counterterms are induced from linear counterterms that are completely fixed by the renormalization condition of the one-point function" (arXiv:2504.18514) [src: discussion of the regularisation schemes]. In dim. reg., "In 3+δ-dimension, $z=a^{1+\delta}\sqrt{2\epsilon}$ , so $z^{\prime\prime}/z$ is a function of $\delta$", which must be expanded to obtain the finite part [src: footnote 3]; in the cutoff scheme "one has to regularize both the time and wavenumber integral" [src: discussion of the regularisation schemes]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- **Source method.** The one-loop spectrum from the bispectrum agrees with the second-order in-in expansion (arXiv:2303.00341) [src: abstract].
- **Hamiltonian phase-space variables.** Canonical transformations instead of integrations by parts avoid total time derivatives (arXiv:2403.14558) [src: abstract].
- **Separate universe and δN.** Valid with separation of scales and adiabaticity of the long mode (arXiv:2605.04815) [src: abstract]; Firouzjahi & Riotto use δN for a smooth transition (arXiv:2304.07801) [src: abstract].
- **Non-perturbative super-Hubble framework.** Artigas et al. drop spatial gradients and work "at any loop order" (arXiv:2608.03725) [src: abstract].
- **Ward identities in the path integral.** Fang & Guo derive them in coordinate space, "thereby imposing strict extra constraints on the regularization procedure itself" (arXiv:2605.13325) [src: Sect. V].

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Field redefinition without its boundary term.** The vertex of Eq. 27 of arXiv:2211.03395 is written for the redefined field, and "the field redefinition method is equivalent to considering boundary interaction" [src: footnote 1]; a calculation that changes variable without the boundary terms, or drops total derivatives as trivial, misses contributions that cancel "at different orders of the in-in operator formalism" (arXiv:2403.14558) [src: abstract]. Check: compare with arXiv:2305.19263 and arXiv:2408.08296, which keep them.
- **Partial cancellations taken for complete ones.** The total-derivative term of Fumagalli 2023 "alone is not sufficient to grant the entire cancellation of all relative scale-invariant corrections" (arXiv:2408.08296) [src: Sect. 1, text and footnote 2]; quartic vertices are needed, also in the flat gauge (arXiv:2608.03725) [src: abstract].
- **Finite momentum bounds.** Eq. 35 of arXiv:2211.03395 depends on the bounds $k_s$, $k_e$ chosen "conservatively" [src: text after Eq. 34]; Tada et al. integrate the total derivative and drop the UV end by the iε prescription (arXiv:2308.04732) [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 3]. A referee asks which bounds are used and why.
- **Scheme-dependent finite parts quoted as physical.** Kristiano & Yokoyama 2025 state that the finite term "depends on the regularization scheme" (arXiv:2504.18514) [src: abstract]; Fang & Guo call this the ambiguity their Ward identities remove, under an assumption on the UV theory and for zero external momentum (arXiv:2605.13325) [src: Sect. V].
- **Logarithms of k.** A one-loop result running as $\log(k/\mu)$ breaks the rescaling symmetry of Eq. 74 of arXiv:0912.2734 [src: Sect. 3.4, text after Eq. 74]; the dimensionless argument of Sect. 3.2.1 assumes that the two-point function does not depend on time [src: Sect. 3.2.1].
- **Tensors.** The consistency-relation argument of arXiv:2308.04732 "is not directly generalisable to the tensor perturbations" [src: abstract]; the Ward-identity result for tensors is in `formalism.md#loop-corrections` (arXiv:2509.00420).
- **Slips copied as printed.** "Minkowky" in Sect. 3.4 of arXiv:0912.2734; "such coupling also affect" in arXiv:2211.03395 [src: text after Eq. 38]. Neither changes an equation. None published as of the snapshot.
