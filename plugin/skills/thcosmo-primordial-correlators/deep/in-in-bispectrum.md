# The single-field bispectrum from the in-in formalism — thcosmo-primordial-correlators dossier

Scope: how the tree-level scalar bispectrum of canonical single-field slow roll is computed step by step, from the cubic action in comoving gauge to the Maldacena shape, with the term-by-term time integrals, the field redefinition against the boundary terms, and the three forms of the in-in expansion.

## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- Maldacena 2002 (arXiv:astro-ph/0210603) is not read at source: its arXiv rendering carries no equation numbers (`formalism.md#not-verified`). This dossier follows three readable sources that redo the calculation and cite it: Chen 2010 (arXiv:1002.1416), Wang 2013 (arXiv:1303.1523) and Chen, Huang, Kachru & Shiu 2006 (arXiv:hep-th/0605045).
- Planck mass. Chen 2010 sets the reduced Planck mass to one [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 4.1]; Wang 2013 takes "the Planck unit: $M_{p}=1$" (arXiv:1303.1523) [src: Sect. III.2, inline, text before Eq. 170]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Slow-roll parameters. Chen 2010 uses the Hubble-flow parameters ε and η of `formalism.md#conventions`. Chen et al. 2006 use slow-variation parameters ε, η, s and, for the non-canonical terms, λ and Σ (arXiv:hep-th/0605045) [src: Sect. 4.2], defined in their Sect. 2 (not read here).
- Normalisation of the shape. Chen 2010 writes the bispectrum with $P_{\zeta}^{2}/\prod_{i}k_{i}^{2}$ and a shape $S$ (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Eq. 4.22]; Wang 2013 with $P_{\zeta}^{2}/(k_{1}^{2}k_{2}^{2}k_{3}^{2})$ and a shape $\mathcal{F}$ (arXiv:1303.1523) [src: Eq. 194]; Chen et al. 2006 with $(P_{k}^{\zeta})^{2}/\prod_{i}k_{i}^{3}$ and an amplitude ${\cal A}$ (arXiv:hep-th/0605045) [src: Eq. 4.42]. Chen et al. 2006 state the factors between their ${\cal A}$ and the earlier papers: "${\cal A}_{\rm SL}=4{\cal A}_{\rm ours}$" for Seery and Lidsey and "${\cal A}_{\rm Maldacena}=8{\cal A}_{\rm ours}$" for Maldacena (arXiv:hep-th/0605045) [src: Sect. 5.1, text after Eq. 5.7]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Time. Chen et al. 2006 use conformal time with $\tau=-1/(aH)$ in the integrals and take the integral "from $-\infty$ to $0$" (arXiv:hep-th/0605045) [src: Sect. 4.2, text after Eq. 4.36].

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — the constraints need solving only to first order
<!-- key: step-1-the-constraints-need-solving-only-to -->

Chen 2010 inserts the comoving-gauge metric into the ADM action, solves the constraints for N and $N^i$, and expands to cubic order; "it is enough to solve N and $N^{i}$ to the first order in $\zeta$", because "their third order perturbations will multiply the zeroth order constraint equation which vanishes, and their second order perturbations will multiply the first order constraint equation which again vanishes" (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 4.7]. Wang 2013 spells out the argument with the lapse: the terms of the n-th order action containing $N_{n-1}$ are $S_{n}\supset\int d^{4}x~N_{n-1}\left(\left.\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial N}\right|_{0,\zeta^{1}}+N_{1}\left.\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}}{\partial N^{2}}\right|_{0,\zeta^{0}}\right)=0~.$ (arXiv:1303.1523) [src: Eq. 174], which vanishes by the first-order constraint, so that "To derive the n th order action ( $n\geq 3$ ), one needs to solve the (n-2) th order constraint equations" [src: Sect. III.2.1]. <!-- num: not a value -->

### Step 2 — the raw cubic action and its integrations by parts
<!-- key: step-2-the-raw-cubic-action-and-its -->

The cubic action before any integration by parts looks unsuppressed: in Chen et al. 2006, "The cubic effective action in (4.22) looks like order ${\cal O}(\epsilon^{0})$ in the slow variation parameters", and "as emphasized and demonstrated in Ref. [7], one can perform a lot of integrations by parts and cancel terms of order ${\cal O}(\epsilon^{0})$ and ${\cal O}(\epsilon)$" (arXiv:hep-th/0605045) [src: Sect. 4.2, first paragraph]; Ref. [7] there is Maldacena 2002. <!-- num: not a value --> Wang 2013 prints the raw action in $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $b_i$ (his Eqs. 175–176, not transcribed: a display of five rows) and warns that "Doing the integration by parts is quite difficult"; the boundary terms of his Eq. 180 record which integrations were done (arXiv:1303.1523) [src: Sect. III.2.3, footnote 42].

The result is Eq. 4.9 of Chen 2010, in `formalism.md#quadratic-and-cubic-actions-in-comoving-gauge`. Wang 2013 prints the same action with the last term written $2f(\zeta)\left.\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta\zeta}\right|_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{b}$, adding the collection $\mathcal{L}_{b}$ of boundary terms (arXiv:1303.1523) [src: Sect. III.2.3, Eq. 177]; his $f(\zeta)$ has, besides $\frac{\eta}{4}\zeta^{2}$, three terms with derivatives (arXiv:1303.1523) [src: Eq. 179]. The two papers normalise $\delta L/\delta\zeta|_{1}$ differently: Chen 2010 writes $\frac{\delta L}{\delta\zeta}\bigg|_{1}=2a\left(\frac{d\partial^{2}\chi}{dt}+H\partial^{2}\chi-\epsilon\partial^{2}\zeta\right)~$ (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Eq. 4.11], Chen et al. 2006 write it without the factor 2 and put a 2 in front of $f$ (arXiv:hep-th/0605045) [src: Eqs. 4.30–4.31].

The slow-roll order of each line follows from $\chi\propto\epsilon$: "the first line of (4.9) is proportional to $\epsilon^{2}$"; for "the featureless potential, $\dot{\eta}={\cal O}(\epsilon^{2})$", so the second line "is proportional to $\epsilon^{3}$, and negligible" (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 4.18]. <!-- num: not a value --> Wang 2013 adds that the inflaton self-coupling $V'''\delta\phi^3$, once gauge-transformed to $\zeta$ gauge, is $\mathcal{L}_{3}\supset-V^{\prime\prime\prime}\frac{\dot{\phi}^{3}}{H^{3}}\zeta^{3}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})\times\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{H^{2}}\zeta^{3}=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3})\zeta^{3}~.$ (arXiv:1303.1523) [src: Eq. 185], so that the dominant cubic interactions "come from gravitational interactions, instead of the inflaton’s direct coupling" [src: Sect. III.2.3, text before Eq. 183].

### Step 3 — from the Lagrangian to the interaction Hamiltonian
<!-- key: step-3-from-the-lagrangian-to-the-interaction -->

For single-field models $H_{I,3}=-L_{3}$ (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 4.18]. Wang 2013 shows that this holds even with derivative couplings: by a Legendre transform of a general Lagrangian to fourth order, "at the third order, we indeed have $\mathcal{H}_{3}=-\mathcal{L}_{3}$ even when there are derivative couplings. On the other hand, at the 4th order non-trivial terms arises in the last two lines" of his Eq. 193 (arXiv:1303.1523) [src: Sect. III.3, text after Eq. 193]; the cubic statement is $H_{3}=-\int d^{3}x\mathcal{L}_{3}~.$ (arXiv:1303.1523) [src: Eq. 186]. `[der]` The quartic Hamiltonian is therefore not minus the quartic Lagrangian; this matters for trispectra and loops, not for the tree-level bispectrum.

### Step 4 — removing the term proportional to the equation of motion
<!-- key: step-4-removing-the-term-proportional-to-the -->

The last term of Eq. 4.9 is "absorbed by a field redefinition $\zeta\to\zeta_{n}+f(\zeta_{n})$"; only $\frac{\eta}{4}\zeta^2$ survives outside the horizon, the others "involve derivatives of $\zeta$ so vanish outside the horizon" (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 4.18]. The redefinition gives the extra term $\langle\zeta({\bf k}_{1})\zeta({\bf k}_{2})\zeta({\bf k}_{3})\rangle=\langle\zeta_{n}({\bf k}_{1})\zeta_{n}({\bf k}_{2})\zeta_{n}({\bf k}_{3})\rangle+\frac{\eta}{4}(\langle\zeta_{n}^{2}({\bf k}_{1})\zeta_{n}({\bf k}_{2})\zeta_{n}({\bf k}_{3})\rangle+{\rm 2~perm.})+{\cal O}(\eta^{2}(P^{\zeta}_{k})^{3})~.$ (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Eq. 4.19]. In position space and with a sound speed, Chen et al. 2006 write the same step as $\langle\zeta(\textbf{x}_{1})\zeta(\textbf{x}_{2})\zeta(\textbf{x}_{3})\rangle=\langle\zeta_{n}(\textbf{x}_{1})\zeta_{n}(\textbf{x}_{2})\zeta_{n}(\textbf{x}_{3})\rangle+\frac{\eta}{2c_{s}^{2}}(\langle\zeta_{n}(\textbf{x}_{1})\zeta_{n}(\textbf{x}_{2})\rangle\langle\zeta_{n}(\textbf{x}_{1})\zeta_{n}(\textbf{x}_{3})\rangle+\textrm{sym})+{\cal O}(\eta^{2}(P^{\zeta}_{k})^{3})~.$ (arXiv:hep-th/0605045) [src: Eq. 4.34], after the Wick contraction of the quadratic term.

Wang 2013 gives the alternative: the term proportional to $\delta\mathcal{L}/\delta\zeta|_1$ "does not contribute to the three point function" if the linear equation of motion is used, "it is allowed to use the linear equation of motion, when calculating the third or higher order actions"; then the boundary terms matter, and "In the $a\rightarrow\infty$ limit, only one term in the $\partial_{t}[\cdots]$ survives" (arXiv:1303.1523) [src: Sect. III.2.3, footnote 43, text before Eq. 181], namely $\mathcal{L}_{b}\simeq\partial_{t}\left(-\frac{\epsilon\eta}{2}a^{3}\zeta^{2}\dot{\zeta}\right)~.$ (arXiv:1303.1523) [src: Eq. 181]. The equivalence of the two routes is Step 7 below.

### Step 5 — the mode function and the time integrals
<!-- key: step-5-the-mode-function-and-the-time-integrals -->

The free mode function with a sound speed is $u_{k}=u(\tau,{\bf k})=\frac{iH}{\sqrt{4\epsilon c_{s}k^{3}}}(1+ikc_{s}\tau)e^{-ikc_{s}\tau}~.$ (arXiv:hep-th/0605045) [src: Eq. 4.26], valid when "the sound speed changes slowly" (arXiv:hep-th/0605045) [src: Sect. 4.1, text after Eq. 4.26]; for $c_s=1$ it is Eq. 4.14 of arXiv:1002.1416 (`formalism.md#mode-function-and-power-spectrum`). The first-order in-in commutator, $\langle\zeta(t,\textbf{k}_{1})\zeta(t,\textbf{k}_{2})\zeta(t,\textbf{k}_{3})\rangle=-i\int_{t_{0}}^{t}dt^{\prime}\langle[\zeta(t,\textbf{k}_{1})\zeta(t,\textbf{k}_{2})\zeta(t,\textbf{k}_{3}),H_{int}(t^{\prime})]\rangle~,$ (arXiv:hep-th/0605045) [src: Eq. 4.36], is evaluated with $t_0$ "some very early time when the vacuum fluctuation of the inflaton is well within the horizon" and t "a time about several e-foldings after the horizon exit", and "we can in a good approximation take the integral over conformal time $\tau$ from $-\infty$ to $0$" [src: Sect. 4.2, text after Eq. 4.36].

The contributions of the leading terms, as Chen et al. 2006 print them for general $c_s$ (arXiv:hep-th/0605045):

- $\zeta\dot\zeta^2$: $i\frac{\epsilon}{c_{s}^{4}}(\epsilon-3+3c_{s}^{2})u(0,\textbf{k}_{1})u(0,\textbf{k}_{2})u(0,\textbf{k}_{3})\int_{-\infty}^{0}a^{2}d\tau\times 2(u^{*}(\tau,\textbf{k}_{1})\frac{du^{*}(\tau,\textbf{k}_{2})}{d\tau}\frac{du^{*}(\tau,\textbf{k}_{3})}{d\tau}+\textrm{sym})(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\sum_{i}{\bf k}_{i})+{\rm c.c.}=\frac{H^{4}}{16\epsilon^{2}c_{s}^{4}}(\epsilon-3+3c_{s}^{2})(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\textbf{k}_{1}+\textbf{k}_{2}+\textbf{k}_{3})(\prod_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{k_{i}^{3}})\times(\frac{k_{2}^{2}k_{3}^{2}}{K}+\frac{k_{1}k_{2}^{2}k_{3}^{2}}{K^{2}}+\textrm{sym})~.$ (arXiv:hep-th/0605045) [src: Eq. 4.38], rows joined in reading order.
- $\zeta(\partial\zeta)^2$: $\frac{H^{4}}{16\epsilon^{2}c_{s}^{4}}(\epsilon-2s+1-c_{s}^{2})(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\textbf{k}_{1}+\textbf{k}_{2}+\textbf{k}_{3})(\prod_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{k_{i}^{3}})\times((\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{k}_{2})(-K+\frac{k_{1}k_{2}+k_{1}k_{3}+k_{2}k_{3}}{K}+\frac{k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}}{K^{2}})+\textrm{sym})~.$ (arXiv:hep-th/0605045) [src: Eq. 4.39], rows joined.
- $\dot\zeta(\partial\zeta)(\partial\chi)$: $-\frac{H^{4}}{16\epsilon c_{s}^{4}}(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\textbf{k}_{1}+\textbf{k}_{2}+\textbf{k}_{3})(\prod_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{k_{i}^{3}})\times(\frac{(\textbf{k}_{1}\cdot\textbf{k}_{2})k_{3}^{2}}{K}(2+\frac{k_{1}+k_{2}}{K})+\textrm{sym})~.$ (arXiv:hep-th/0605045) [src: Eq. 4.40], rows joined.
- Field redefinition: $\frac{\eta}{2}\frac{H^{4}}{16\epsilon^{2}c_{s}^{4}}(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\textbf{k}_{1}+\textbf{k}_{2}+\textbf{k}_{3})(\frac{1}{k_{1}^{3}k_{2}^{3}}+\textrm{sym})~.$ (arXiv:hep-th/0605045) [src: Eq. 4.41].
- The cubic time-derivative term, Eq. 4.37 of arXiv:hep-th/0605045, carries a prefactor built from the deviation of the sound speed from one and from λ/Σ. `[der]` It is absent from canonical slow roll, with unit sound speed and vanishing λ, and is treated in `deep/eft-of-inflation.md`.

`[der]` In the first two lines the sound-speed parts of the coefficients cancel at unit sound speed and vanishing s, leaving a coefficient linear in ε; with the prefactor of these lines the contributions are then of first order in slow roll times the squared power spectrum, the estimate of Step 6.

### Step 6 — order of magnitude, then the sum
<!-- key: step-6-order-of-magnitude-then-the-sum -->

Chen 2010 estimates before integrating: "we only need to keep track of the factors of H and $\epsilon$"; with $a\propto H^{-1}$ and $\zeta\propto\zeta^{\prime}\propto H/\sqrt{\epsilon}$, the first vertex "contributes $\propto H\sqrt{\epsilon}$", so that $\langle\zeta^{3}\rangle=-i\int dt\langle[\zeta^{3},H_{I,3}(t)]\rangle\propto\frac{H^{4}}{\epsilon}\propto{\cal O}(\epsilon)P_{\zeta}^{2}~,$ (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Eq. 4.21]; the first line of Eq. 4.9 gives $f_{NL}={\cal O}(\epsilon)$ and the redefinition $f_{NL}={\cal O}(\eta)$ [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 4.21]. <!-- num: not a value -->

Adding the contributions, Chen et al. 2006 write $\langle\zeta(\textbf{k}_{1})\zeta(\textbf{k}_{2})\zeta(\textbf{k}_{3})\rangle=(2\pi)^{7}\delta^{3}(\textbf{k}_{1}+\textbf{k}_{2}+\textbf{k}_{3})(P_{k}^{\zeta})^{2}\frac{1}{\prod_{i}k_{i}^{3}}~{\cal A}~,$ (arXiv:hep-th/0605045) [src: Eq. 4.42] with ${\cal A}\supset\left(\frac{1}{c_{s}^{2}}-1-\frac{2\lambda}{\Sigma}\right)\frac{3k_{1}^{2}k_{2}^{2}k_{3}^{2}}{2K^{3}}+\left(\frac{1}{c_{s}^{2}}-1\right)\left(-\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i>j}k_{i}^{2}k_{j}^{2}+\frac{1}{2K^{2}}\sum_{i\neq j}k_{i}^{2}k_{j}^{3}+\frac{1}{8}\sum_{i}k_{i}^{3}\right)+\frac{\epsilon}{c_{s}^{2}}\left(-\frac{1}{8}\sum_{i}k_{i}^{3}+\frac{1}{8}\sum_{i\neq j}k_{i}k_{j}^{2}+\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i>j}k_{i}^{2}k_{j}^{2}\right)+\frac{\eta}{c_{s}^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{8}\sum_{i}k_{i}^{3}\right)+\frac{s}{c_{s}^{2}}\left(-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i}k_{i}^{3}-\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i>j}k_{i}^{2}k_{j}^{2}+\frac{1}{2K^{2}}\sum_{i\neq j}k_{i}^{2}k_{j}^{3}\right)~.$ (arXiv:hep-th/0605045) [src: Eq. 4.43], rows joined in reading order.

### Step 7 — the slow-roll limit is Maldacena's result
<!-- key: step-7-the-slow-roll-limit-is-maldacena-s-result -->

Chen et al. 2006 reduce this to slow roll, where the deviation u of the sound speed from one is assumed of first order in slow roll and, "As in Ref. [19]", the mixed derivative of P in X and φ is set to zero (arXiv:hep-th/0605045) [src: Sect. 5.1, first paragraph], and obtain ${\cal A}=-\left(\frac{\epsilon}{3\epsilon_{X}}s+u\right)\frac{k_{1}^{2}k_{2}^{2}k_{3}^{2}}{K^{3}}- u\left(-\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i>j}k_{i}^{2}k_{j}^{2}+\frac{1}{2K^{2}}\sum_{i\neq j}k_{i}^{2}k_{j}^{3}+\frac{1}{8}\sum_{i}k_{i}^{3}\right)+\epsilon\left(-\frac{1}{8}\sum_{i}k_{i}^{3}+\frac{1}{8}\sum_{i\neq j}k_{i}k_{j}^{2}+\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i>j}k_{i}^{2}k_{j}^{2}\right)+\eta\left(\frac{1}{8}\sum_{i}k_{i}^{3}\right)~.$ (arXiv:hep-th/0605045) [src: Eq. 5.7], rows joined, which recovers Eq. 83 of Seery and Lidsey (arXiv:astro-ph/0503692) with "${\cal A}_{\rm SL}=4{\cal A}_{\rm ours}$" (arXiv:hep-th/0605045) [src: Sect. 5.1, text after Eq. 5.7]. "Further setting the sound speed to be 1 (therefore u=0 and s=0 ), using the relation (3.5) and taking into account of the convention difference ${\cal A}_{\rm Maldacena}=8{\cal A}_{\rm ours}$, we recover the result Eq.(4.6) of Maldacena" (arXiv:hep-th/0605045) [sec: Sect. 5.1, text after Eq. 5.7]. <!-- num: not a value --> Maldacena's own Eq. 4.6 (arXiv:astro-ph/0210603) is therefore reached here only through this secondary source.

In Chen 2010's normalisation the same result is Eqs. 4.22–4.23 of arXiv:1002.1416 (`formalism.md#the-slow-roll-bispectrum-maldacena-s-result`). Wang 2013 writes it as $\mathcal{F}(k_{1}/k_{3},k_{2}/k_{3})\equiv\frac{3(\eta-\epsilon)}{8}\frac{(k_{1}^{3}+k_{2}^{3}+k_{3}^{3})}{3k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}}+\frac{3\epsilon}{4}\frac{(k_{1}k_{2}^{2}+k_{1}k_{3}^{2}+k_{2}k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}k_{3}^{2}+k_{3}k_{1}^{2}+k_{3}k_{2}^{2})}{6k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}}+\epsilon\frac{(k_{1}^{2}k_{2}^{2}+k_{1}^{2}k_{3}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}k_{3}^{2})}{k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3})}~.$ (arXiv:1303.1523) [src: Eq. 195], "Here the slow roll approximation is assumed" [src: Sect. III.4, footnote 46], and calls the first structure the "local shape" [src: Sect. III.4, Fig. 14 caption]. `[der]` With the prefactors of Eq. 4.22 of arXiv:1002.1416 and Eq. 194 of arXiv:1303.1523, which are the same, Wang's three structures carry the same coefficients as Chen's S: the η term and the $-\epsilon$ part of the first bracket combine into $(\eta-\epsilon)$ times the local structure.

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **Field redefinition against boundary terms.** Arroja & Tanaka 2011 (arXiv:1103.1102) redo the calculation for general k-inflation keeping all total time derivatives; the only one that contributes at leading order is $\int dt\,d^{3}x\frac{d}{dt}\left(-\frac{\eta\epsilon a^{3}}{2c_{s}^{4}}\zeta^{2}\dot{\zeta}\right),$ (arXiv:1103.1102) [src: Eq. 29], whose in-in value is $\langle\Omega|\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\hat{\zeta}(\tau_{e},\mathbf{k}_{1})\hat{\zeta}(\tau_{e},\mathbf{k}_{2})\hat{\zeta}(\tau_{e},\mathbf{k}_{3})|\Omega\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{K})\Im\bigg[\eta\epsilon c_{s}^{-4}a^{2}\zeta(\tau_{e},\mathbf{k}_{1})\zeta(\tau_{e},\mathbf{k}_{2})\zeta(\tau_{e},\mathbf{k}_{3})\zeta^{*}(\tau_{e},\mathbf{k}_{1})\zeta^{*}(\tau_{e},\mathbf{k}_{2})\zeta^{{}^{\prime}*}(\tau_{e},\mathbf{k}_{3})\bigg]\bigg|_{\tau_{e}\rightarrow 0}+5\,\mathrm{perms.}=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{K})\frac{H^{4}\eta}{32c_{s}^{4}\epsilon^{2}}\frac{k_{1}^{3}+k_{2}^{3}+k_{3}^{3}}{(k_{1}k_{2}k_{3})^{3}},$ (arXiv:1103.1102) [src: Eq. 30], rows joined; it "is of the so-called local type and exactly agrees" with the field-redefinition term [src: Sect. IV.2, text after Eq. 30]. Their conclusion: one can "ignore all the boundary terms that appear when one simplifies the action but then one has to perform a field redefinition", or "keep all the boundary terms and calculate the bispectrum using the usual method without the need to do the field redefinition"; "in the end the bispectrum of the curvature perturbation is the same in both procedures" (arXiv:1103.1102) [src: Sect. V]. Cost: the boundary route needs the full list of total derivatives (Eq. 28 of arXiv:1103.1102, Eq. 180 of arXiv:1303.1523); the redefinition route needs only the non-derivative part of $f$.
- **Factorised, commutator and mixed forms.** The n-th order term of the in-in expansion can be written in a factorised form, two separately time-ordered integrals (Eq. 3.40 of arXiv:1002.1416), or in the commutator form $i^{n}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}dt_{1}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}dt_{2}\cdots\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{n-1}}dt_{n}\left\langle\left[H_{I}(t_{n}),\left[H_{I}(t_{n-1}),\cdots,\left[H_{I}(t_{1}),Q_{I}(t)\right]\cdots\right]\right]\right\rangle~.$ (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Eq. 3.41]. "The factorized form is most convenient to achieve the UV" convergence, at early times, by tilting the contour "into the positive imaginary plane for the left integral, and negative imaginary plane for the right integral"; "The commutator form is most convenient to get the correct leading order behavior in the IR", but "the explicit IR cancellation is incompatible with the explicit UV convergence" [src: Sect. 3.4]. The mixed form uses a cutoff $t_c$: $\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{t_{c}}^{t}dt_{1}\cdots\int_{t_{c}}^{t_{i-1}}dt_{i}~{\rm\{commutator~form\}}~\int_{-\infty}^{t_{c}}dt_{i+1}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{t_{n-1}}dt_{n}~{\rm\{factorized~form\}}~.$ (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Eq. 3.42], "particularly efficient in numerical computations when combined with the Wick-rotations in the UV" [src: Sect. 3.4, text after Eq. 3.42]. `[der]` For the tree-level bispectrum (n = 1) the two forms coincide; the choice matters from two vertices on.
- **Two or more vertices.** Adshead, Easther & Lim 2009 (arXiv:0904.4207) "show that diagrams with two or more vertices require careful handling" and give an operator-formalism implementation for them [src: abstract].
- **Next order in slow variation.** Burrage, Ribeiro & Seery 2011 (arXiv:1103.4126) compute the "next-order" slow-roll corrections in closed form for a Lagrangian that is an arbitrary Lorentz-invariant function of the field and its first derivatives; in DBI the corrections from the potential and the warp factor "can be of order several tens of percent", and the result "satisfies the next-order version of Maldacena's consistency condition" [src: abstract]. Chen et al. 2006 also give the first corrections in their Sect. 4.3 and App. B (arXiv:hep-th/0605045) [src: Sect. 4.3], not read here.
- **General $P(X,\phi)$.** Seery & Lidsey 2005 (arXiv:astro-ph/0503692) obtain the three-point function for a general function of the field and its first derivative "in a self-consistent approximation scheme where the expansion rate varies slowly", in terms of the slow-roll parameters and "three new parameters" [src: abstract]; their Eq. 83 is recovered by Eq. 5.7 of arXiv:hep-th/0605045 (Step 7).
- **Numerical integration.** Beyond slow roll the time integrals are done numerically; Wang 2013 points to codes "without slow roll approximation" in his Refs. 109–110 (arXiv:1303.1523) [src: Sect. III.4, footnote 46]; `approximations.md#numerical-issues` lists the regularisation of feature integrals.
- **Other representations.** Euclidean-sphere, frequency-space, in-out and dressing-rule formulations of in-in correlators are in `formalism.md#other-representations-of-in-in-correlators-2026`.

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Dropping the time boundary terms without the field redefinition.** The total time derivatives "cannot a priori be neglected" (arXiv:1103.1102) [src: Sect. IV.2, text after Eq. 30]: one of them gives the whole η contribution. `[der]` A calculation that discards $\mathcal{L}_b$ and also uses the linear equation of motion to drop the $\delta L/\delta\zeta|_1$ term misses the local η term of the shape. Check: either the boundary term of Eq. 181 of arXiv:1303.1523 or the redefinition term of Eq. 4.19 of arXiv:1002.1416 is present, not both and not neither.
- **Second time derivatives in the action.** Arroja & Tanaka: "we should basically use the reduced action written in terms of the physical variables that does not contain second order time derivatives in total"; the boundary terms "are necessary to erase from the action the terms with second order time derivative on $\zeta$", generated by the integrations by parts (arXiv:1103.1102) [src: Sects. IV.2, V]. Wang 2013 states that with field redefinitions instead of the equation of motion "all the $\ddot{\zeta}$ terms can be eliminated without using equation of motion, and there is no boundary term contributing to the action, in terms of $\zeta_{n}$" (arXiv:1303.1523) [src: Sect. III.2.3, footnote 43].
- **Expecting the $\epsilon^0$ terms to survive.** The raw cubic action "looks like order ${\cal O}(\epsilon^{0})$" (arXiv:hep-th/0605045) [src: Sect. 4.2, first paragraph]; an estimate made before the integrations by parts overstates the bispectrum. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Normalisation factors between papers.** Three normalisations of the shape are in use (Conventions); the stated factors 8 and 4 of arXiv:hep-th/0605045 [src: Sect. 5.1] are the only conversions read here; no paper read states the factor between ${\cal A}$ and Chen 2010's $S$. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **The η of the field redefinition.** In Chen et al. 2006 the redefinition is $\zeta\rightarrow\zeta_{n}+\frac{\eta}{4c_{s}^{2}}\zeta_{n}^{2}$ (arXiv:hep-th/0605045) [src: Sect. 4.2, inline, text after Eq. 4.33], with a $c_s^{-2}$ that disappears for canonical slow roll.
- **Featureless background assumed.** Step 2 uses $\dot\eta={\cal O}(\epsilon^2)$ "For the featureless potential" (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 4.18]; with a feature the second line of Eq. 4.9 is not negligible (`approximations.md#leading-order-in-slow-roll`). <!-- num: not a value -->
