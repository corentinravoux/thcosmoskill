# Formalism of primordial correlators

Scope: definitions, governing equations and conventions for the quantum fluctuations of inflation and their correlators: in-in formalism, EFT of inflation, shapes, soft limits, cosmological collider, bootstrap, features, loops, decoherence, QFT in de Sitter.

Snapshot: 2026-11-02

## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

The hub's atlas (`theoretical-cosmology/references/conventions.md`, sections "Slow-roll parameters: Hubble-flow or potential definitions", "Curvature perturbation: ζ or ℛ, and its sign", "Fourier convention and power-spectrum normalisation" and "Sign and normalisation of f_NL, and the shape template") collects the choices of other works; the list below gives those of the main sources of this skill.

The conventions below are those of the main source for each subtopic, as that source states them; they are not converted into one another.

- **Metric signature (in-in, slow roll):** Chen 2010 (arXiv:1002.1416) uses the signature $(-1,1,1,1)$ [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 4.1]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **ADM line element:** $ds^{2}=-N^{2}dt^{2}+h_{ij}(dx^{i}+N^{i}dt)(dx^{j}+N^{j}dt)~$ (Chen 2010, arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Eq. 4.3].
- **Planck mass:** Chen 2010 (arXiv:1002.1416) uses the reduced Planck mass $M_{\rm P}$, defined as $(8\pi G)^{-1/2}$, and then sets it to one [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 4.1]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Slow-roll parameters:** Chen 2010 (arXiv:1002.1416) uses the Hubble-flow parameters $\epsilon=-\frac{\dot{H}}{H^{2}}~,~~~\eta=\frac{\dot{\epsilon}}{\epsilon H}~$ [src: Eq. 4.2].
- **Gauge and curvature variable:** Chen 2010 (arXiv:1002.1416) works in comoving (uniform-inflaton) gauge, $h_{ij}=a^{2}e^{2\zeta}\delta_{ij}~$ [src: Eq. 4.7], in which the inflaton fluctuation vanishes.
- **Fourier convention:** Chen 2010 (arXiv:1002.1416) writes $u_{k}=\int d^{3}x\zeta(t,{\bf x})e^{-i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf x}}~$ [src: Eq. 4.13].
- **Time variable:** Chen 2010 (arXiv:1002.1416) uses cosmic time $t$ in the action and conformal time $\tau$, approximately minus the comoving Hubble radius, in the mode functions [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 4.14].
- **Power-spectrum normalisation:** Chen 2010 (arXiv:1002.1416) defines $P_{\zeta}$ through $\langle\zeta({\bf k}_{1})\zeta({\bf k}_{2})\rangle=\frac{P_{\zeta}}{2k_{1}^{3}}(2\pi)^{5}\delta^{3}({\bf k}_{1}+{\bf k}_{2})~$ [src: Eq. 4.17].
- **de Sitter conventions (QFT in dS):** see the subsection below; Penedones et al. work in $(d+1)$-dimensional de Sitter space with isometry group $SO(1,d+1)$ (arXiv:2301.04146) [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value -->

## Quantisation and the in-in formalism
<!-- key: quantisation-and-the-in-in-formalism -->

### The in-in expectation value
<!-- key: the-in-in-expectation-value -->

- Chen 2010 (arXiv:1002.1416), following Weinberg's presentation, writes the expectation value of an operator $Q$ at the end of inflation in the interacting vacuum as $\langle Q\rangle=\langle\Omega|F^{-1}(t,t_{0})Q^{I}(t)F(t,t_{0})|\Omega\rangle~$ [src: Eq. 3.23, first row]; the second row of the same display writes $F$ and $F^{-1}$ as anti-time-ordered and time-ordered exponentials of the interaction Hamiltonian.
- The interaction-picture evolution operator is $F(t,t_{0})=T\exp\left(-i\int_{t_{0}}^{t}H_{I}(t)dt\right)~$ (Chen 2010, arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Eq. 3.22].
- At first order in the cubic interaction Hamiltonian, the three-point function is the commutator $\langle\zeta_{n}^{3}\rangle=-i\langle 0|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}dt[\zeta_{n}({\bf k}_{1})\zeta_{n}({\bf k}_{2})\zeta_{n}({\bf k}_{3}),H_{I,3}]|0\rangle~$ (Chen 2010, arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Eq. 4.20].
- For single-field models the cubic interaction Hamiltonian is minus the cubic Lagrangian (Chen 2010, arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 4.18].

### Other representations of in-in correlators (2026)
<!-- key: other-representations-of-in-in-correlators-2026 -->

- **Euclidean sphere.** Katsevich & Sun 2026 (arXiv:2610.28516) compute late-time de Sitter correlators by analytic continuation from the Euclidean sphere, where correlators of massive scalars take the form of generalised Euler integrals; they reproduce Schwinger–Keldysh results for a range of tree-level and loop diagrams, and a Landau analysis of the integrals gives the momentum-space singularities [src: abstract].
- **Frequency space.** Belrhali, Poisson & Renaux-Petel 2026 (arXiv:2607.05327) trade the nested in-in time integrals for frequency integrals over rational propagators and vertex functions in the Kontorovich–Lebedev–Fourier space, and obtain the double-exchange diagram as at most a double series of hypergeometric functions [src: abstract]; the momentum space it rests on is described in the section "QFT in de Sitter and infrared effects".
- **In-in from in-out.** Basumatary, Sinha & Zhou 2026 (arXiv:2603.26090) find, with tensor networks for 1+1-dimensional $φ^4$ theory, controlled non-perturbative evidence that suitably defined in-in correlators follow from an in-out formalism gluing the expanding and contracting Poincaré patches; the in-in formulation is numerically more favourable because its entanglement stays modest [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Dressing rules.** Chowdhury et al. 2026 (arXiv:2602.03841) obtain discontinuities of in-in correlators from unitarity cuts of flat-space Feynman diagrams dressed with auxiliary propagators, and reconstruct correlators from them by dispersion relations, at tree level and one loop for conformally coupled scalars [src: abstract].

### Quadratic and cubic actions in comoving gauge
<!-- key: quadratic-and-cubic-actions-in-comoving-gauge -->

- The canonical single-field action of Chen 2010 (arXiv:1002.1416) is printed as $S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{M_{\rm P}}{2}R+X-V(\phi)\right]~$ [src: Eq. 4.1]. The rendering prints $M_{\rm P}$ without a square in front of $R$; the paper sets the Planck mass to one right after, so the slip has no effect on later equations; it is transcribed as printed (see "Not verified").
- In that action $X$ is the canonical kinetic term, $X=-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi$ (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Sect. 4, inline, text after Eq. 4.1]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- The quadratic action for $\zeta$ is $S_{2}=\int dtd^{3}x~\left[a^{3}\epsilon\dot{\zeta}^{2}-a\epsilon(\partial\zeta)^{2}\right]~$ (Chen 2010, arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Eq. 4.8].
- The cubic action, copied row by row as the three rows of the display, is $S_{3}=\int dtd^{3}x\bigg[a^{3}\epsilon^{2}\zeta\dot{\zeta}^{2}+a\epsilon^{2}\zeta(\partial\zeta)^{2}-2a\epsilon\dot{\zeta}(\partial\zeta)(\partial\chi)$ $+\frac{a^{3}\epsilon}{2}\dot{\eta}\zeta^{2}\dot{\zeta}+\frac{\epsilon}{2a}(\partial\zeta)(\partial\chi)\partial^{2}\chi+\frac{\epsilon}{4a}(\partial^{2}\zeta)(\partial\chi)^{2}$ $+f(\zeta)\frac{\delta L}{\delta\zeta}\bigg|_{1}\bigg]~$ (Chen 2010, arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Eq. 4.9].
- The auxiliary field is $\chi=a^{2}\epsilon\partial^{-2}\dot{\zeta}~$ (Chen 2010, arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Eq. 4.10].
- The last term of the cubic action is removed by a field redefinition from $\zeta$ to $\zeta_{n}$ (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 4.18].
- Of the redefinition function, only $f(\zeta)=\frac{\eta}{4}\zeta^{2}+{\rm terms~with~derivatives~on~\zeta}~$ contributes outside the horizon (Chen 2010, arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Eq. 4.12].

### Mode function and power spectrum
<!-- key: mode-function-and-power-spectrum -->

- With Bunch–Davies initial conditions the mode function is $u_{k}=u({\bf k},\tau)=\frac{iH}{\sqrt{4\epsilon k^{3}}}(1+ik\tau)e^{-ik\tau}~$ (Chen 2010, arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Eq. 4.14].
- The tree-level power spectrum, in units of the reduced Planck mass and evaluated at horizon exit, is $P_{\zeta}=\frac{H^{2}}{8\pi^{2}\epsilon}~$ (Chen 2010, arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Eq. 4.18].

### The slow-roll bispectrum (Maldacena's result)
<!-- key: the-slow-roll-bispectrum-maldacena-s-result -->

- Chen 2010 (arXiv:1002.1416) writes the full slow-roll result as $\langle\zeta(\textbf{k}_{1})\zeta(\textbf{k}_{2})\zeta(\textbf{k}_{3})\rangle=(2\pi)^{7}\delta^{3}(\textbf{k}_{1}+\textbf{k}_{2}+\textbf{k}_{3})P_{\zeta}^{2}\frac{1}{\prod_{i}k_{i}^{2}}~S~$ [src: Eq. 4.22].
- This is the review's rendering of the computation of Maldacena 2002 (arXiv:astro-ph/0210603), whose own equations could not be read with a place (see "Not verified").
- The shape function, copied as the two rows of the display, is $S=\frac{\epsilon}{8}\left[-\left(\frac{k_{1}^{2}}{k_{2}k_{3}}+{\rm 2~perm.}\right)+\left(\frac{k_{1}}{k_{2}}+{\rm 5~perm.}\right)+\frac{8}{K}\left(\frac{k_{1}k_{2}}{k_{3}}+{\rm 2~perm.}\right)\right]$ $+\frac{\eta}{8}\left(\frac{k_{1}^{2}}{k_{2}k_{3}}+{\rm 2~perm.}\right)~$ (Chen 2010, arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Eq. 4.23]; $K$ is the sum of the three wavenumbers and $\eta$ the Hubble-flow parameter of the conventions above.
- The $\eta$ term comes entirely from the field redefinition (arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 4.21].

## Effective field theory of inflation
<!-- key: effective-field-theory-of-inflation -->

- **Construction.** Cheung et al. 2007 (arXiv:0709.0293) build the most general theory of fluctuations around a quasi de Sitter background for single-field models in unitary gauge, where the scalar mode is eaten by the metric, from the lowest-dimension operators invariant under spatial diffeomorphisms, such as $g^{00}$ and the extrinsic curvature of constant-time surfaces [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value --> <!-- eq: notation -->
- **Unitary-gauge action.** The action of Eq. 10 of arXiv:0709.0293 starts with the Einstein–Hilbert term, a tadpole term in $g^{00}$ and a cosmological term whose coefficients are fixed by $H$ and $\dot{H}$, followed by the operators $(g^{00}+1)^{n}$ with coefficients $M_{n}(t)^{4}$ and extrinsic-curvature operators [src: Sect. 2, Eq. 10]; the display spans several rows and is not transcribed here. For canonical slow-roll inflation, all but the first three terms vanish, which cannot hold exactly since loops generate the others (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 10]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Speed of sound.** The operator $(g^{00}+1)^{2}$ gives the Goldstone a speed of sound $c_{s}^{-2}=1-\frac{2M_{2}^{4}}{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\dot{H}}\;$ (Cheung et al. 2007, arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Eq. 38]; avoiding superluminal propagation requires $M_{2}^{4}>0$ for $\dot{H}<0$ [src: Sect. 4.2, inline, text after Eq. 38]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Goldstone action at cubic order.** $S_{\rm\pi}=\int\!d^{4}x\>\sqrt{-g}\left[-\frac{M^{2}_{\rm Pl}\dot{H}}{c^{2}_{s}}\left(\dot{\pi}^{2}-c^{2}_{s}\frac{(\partial_{i}\pi)^{2}}{a^{2}}\right)+M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\dot{H}\left(1-\frac{1}{c^{2}_{s}}\right)\left(\dot{\pi}^{3}-\dot{\pi}\frac{(\partial_{i}\pi)^{2}}{a^{2}}\right)-\frac{4}{3}M_{3}^{4}\dot{\pi}^{3}...\right]$ (Cheung et al. 2007, arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Eq. 39]. The non-linear realisation of time diffeomorphisms ties the cubic operator $\dot{\pi}(\partial_{i}\pi)^{2}$ to the reduced sound speed [src: Sect. 4.2, text after Eq. 41]; the coefficient of $\dot{\pi}^{3}$ from $M_{3}$ is a free parameter [src: Sect. 4.2, text after Eq. 44]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Decoupling limit.** Mixing with gravity can be neglected at energies above $E_{\rm mix}\simeq M_{2}^{2}/M_{\rm Pl}$, so the Goldstone action captures the leading effects if $\epsilon/c_{s}^{2}\ll 1$ (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Sect. 4.2, inline, text after Eq. 39]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Power spectrum with $c_{s}$.** $\langle\zeta(\vec{k}_{1})\zeta(\vec{k}_{2})\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta(\vec{k}_{1}+\vec{k}_{2})\frac{1}{c_{s*}}\cdot\frac{H^{4}_{*}}{4M_{\rm Pl}^{2}|\dot{H}_{*}|}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{3}}=(2\pi)^{3}\delta(\vec{k}_{1}+\vec{k}_{2})\frac{1}{c_{s*}}\cdot\frac{H_{*}^{2}}{4\epsilon_{*}M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{3}}\;$ (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Eq. 40], quantities with a star evaluated at sound-horizon crossing; the time dependence of $c_{s}$ adds to the tilt, $n_{s}=\frac{d}{d\log k}\log\frac{H^{4}_{*}}{|\dot{H}_{*}|c_{s*}}=\frac{1}{H_{*}}\frac{d}{dt_{*}}\log\frac{H^{4}_{*}}{|\dot{H}_{*}|c_{s*}}=4\frac{\dot{H_{*}}}{H^{2}_{*}}-\frac{\ddot{H}_{*}}{\dot{H}_{*}H_{*}}-\frac{\dot{c}_{s*}}{c_{s*}H_{*}}\;$ (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Eq. 41]. As printed, Eq. 41 is the log-derivative of the spectrum, which the paper calls the tilt; it is not written as $n_{s}-1$.
- **Size of non-Gaussianity.** The ratio of the cubic to the quadratic Lagrangian at freezing is $\frac{{\cal L}_{\dot{\pi}(\nabla\pi)^{2}}}{{\cal L}_{2}}\sim\frac{H\pi\left(\frac{H}{c_{s}}\pi\right)^{2}}{H^{2}\pi^{2}}\sim\frac{H}{c^{2}_{s}}\pi\sim\frac{1}{c^{2}_{s}}\zeta\,$ (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Eq. 42], giving $f^{\rm equil.}_{\rm NL,\;\dot{\pi}(\nabla\pi)^{2}}\sim\frac{1}{c^{2}_{s}}\;$ [src: Eq. 43]; the explicit calculation gives, at leading order in $c_{s}^{-1}$, $f_{\rm NL}^{\rm equil.}=\frac{85}{324}\cdot\frac{1}{c_{s}^{2}}\;$ (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Eq. 45], obtained with $P_{,XXX}=0$ in the notation of the paper it cites [src: footnote 8]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **The $\dot{\pi}^{3}$ operator.** $f^{\rm equil.}_{\rm NL,\ \dot{\pi}^{3}}\sim 1-\frac{4}{3}\frac{M_{3}^{4}}{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}|\dot{H}|c_{s}^{-2}}\;$ (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Eq. 44].
- **Four-point function.** The operator fixed by the sound speed dominates the trispectrum through $(\nabla\pi)^{4}$, with $\frac{{\cal L}_{(\nabla\pi)^{4}}}{{\cal L}_{2}}\sim\frac{\left(\frac{H}{c_{s}}\pi\right)^{4}}{H^{2}\pi^{2}}\sim\frac{H^{2}}{c^{4}_{s}}\pi^{2}\sim\frac{1}{c^{4}_{s}}\zeta^{2}\;$ (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Eq. 49].
- **Strong-coupling scale.** Tree-level partial-wave unitarity of $\pi$ scattering gives the cutoff $\Lambda^{4}\simeq 16\pi^{2}M_{2}^{4}\frac{c_{s}^{7}}{(1-c_{s}^{2})^{2}}\simeq 16\pi^{2}M_{\rm Pl}^{2}|\dot{H}|\frac{c_{s}^{5}}{1-c_{s}^{2}}\;$ (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Sect. 4.2.1, Eq. 50].
- **Open (dissipative) extension.** The Open EFT of inflation adds dissipative and stochastic effects while preserving scale invariance; dissipation enhances specific Goldstone interaction channels and, at weak dissipation, gives folded bispectrum shapes (Agüí Salcedo et al. 2026, arXiv:2603.13473) [src: abstract].

## Shapes and templates
<!-- key: shapes-and-templates -->

The definitions of $f_{\rm NL}$ in the variables $\Phi$, $\zeta$ and $\mathcal{R}$, with their signs and the local template of several reference works, are collected in the hub's atlas (`theoretical-cosmology/references/conventions.md`, section "Sign and normalisation of f_NL, and the shape template"); they are not repeated here.

- **Two classes of shapes.** Babich, Creminelli & Zaldarriaga 2004 (arXiv:astro-ph/0405356) define a cosine between bispectrum shapes, with a measure based on the ability of experiments to distinguish them, and find two broad classes with fairly orthogonal distributions: non-Gaussianity created at horizon crossing, largest for equilateral triangles, and non-Gaussianity from evolution outside the horizon, dominated by the influence of long-wavelength modes on short ones [src: abstract]. Translating constraints between models through the normalisation on equilateral triangles can be very misleading [src: abstract].
- **Equilateral template.** Planck 2018 IX (arXiv:1905.05697) writes, in the Bardeen potential $\Phi$, $B_{\Phi}^{\rm equil}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})=6A^{2}f_{\rm NL}^{\rm equil}\times\left\{-\frac{1}{k^{4-n_{\rm s}}_{1}k^{4-n_{\rm s}}_{2}}-\frac{1}{k^{4-n_{\rm s}}_{2}k^{4-n_{\rm s}}_{3}}-\frac{1}{k^{4-n_{\rm s}}_{3}k^{4-n_{\rm s}}_{1}}-\frac{2}{(k_{1}k_{2}k_{3})^{2(4-n_{\rm s})/3}}\right.\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.+\left[\frac{1}{k^{(4-n_{\rm s})/3}_{1}k^{2(4-n_{\rm s})/3}_{2}k^{4-n_{\rm s}}_{3}}+\mbox{5 perms.}\right]\right\}$ [src: Sect. 2.1, Eq. 1], rows joined in reading order.
- **Orthogonal template.** $B^{\rm ortho}_{\Phi}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})=6A^{2}f_{\rm NL}^{\rm ortho}\times\left\{-\frac{3}{k^{4-n_{\rm s}}_{1}k^{4-n_{\rm s}}_{2}}-\frac{3}{k^{4-n_{\rm s}}_{2}k^{4-n_{\rm s}}_{3}}-\frac{3}{k^{4-n_{\rm s}}_{3}k^{4-n_{\rm s}}_{1}}\right.-\frac{8}{(k_{1}k_{2}k_{3})^{2(4-n_{\rm s})/3}}\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.+\left[\frac{3}{k^{(4-n_{\rm s})/3}_{1}k^{2(4-n_{\rm s})/3}_{2}k^{4-n_{\rm s}}_{3}}+\mbox{5 perms.}\right]\right\}$ (Planck 2018 IX, arXiv:1905.05697) [src: Sect. 2.1, Eq. 2], rows joined in reading order.
- **Variable of the templates.** Planck 2018 IX defines $\Phi\equiv(3/5)\zeta$ on superhorizon scales, Bardeen's potential in matter domination, with spectrum $P_{\Phi}(k)=A/k^{4-n_{\rm s}}$ of normalisation $A$ (arXiv:1905.05697) [src: Sect. 2.1, inline, text after Eq. 2].
- **Local template.** The local bispectrum of $\Phi$ is Eq. 3 of arXiv:1905.05697, transcribed in the hub's atlas; it peaks in squeezed configurations $k_{1}\ll k_{2}\approx k_{3}$ because the transfer of non-Gaussianity happens on superhorizon scales and is local in real space (arXiv:1905.05697) [src: Sect. 2.2, inline, text after Eq. 3].
- **Why two single-field templates.** Using the EFT of inflation, Senatore, Smith & Zaldarriaga 2010 (arXiv:0905.3746) argue that the size and shape of single-field non-Gaussianity are generically described by $f_{\rm NL}^{\rm equil}$ and $f_{\rm NL}^{\rm orthog}$, the latter peaked on equilateral and on flat-triangle configurations with opposite signs [src: abstract]. Planck 2018 IX states that the EFT approach yields shapes that can be mapped into the equilateral and orthogonal template basis, and also searches for the exact non-separable EFT, DBI and ghost shapes (arXiv:1905.05697) [src: Sect. 2.1, text after Eq. 2].
- **Factorisable approximation.** Creminelli et al. 2006 (arXiv:astro-ph/0509029) show that the equilateral shape of higher-derivative single-field models is very well approximated by a factorisable form, which makes the CMB analysis practical [src: abstract].
- **Folded shapes.** Folded non-Gaussianity is enhanced near $k_1+k_2-k_3\rightarrow0$; excited initial states, imaginary speeds of sound and dissipative inflation produce it, either with a power-law width of the enhanced region (cutoff-regulated) or an exponentially narrow one (dissipation-regulated) (Yang & Philcox 2026, arXiv:2607.15337) [src: abstract].
- **Collider templates.** Simplified templates for massive-particle exchange, accurate over a broad range of masses, spins and sound speeds, are orthogonalised against the equilateral and orthogonal shapes before a search (Suman et al. 2025, arXiv:2512.22085) [src: abstract]; the shape of the signal itself is in the section "Cosmological collider" below.

## Consistency relations and soft limits
<!-- key: consistency-relations-and-soft-limits -->

- **Non-linear curvature variable.** Creminelli & Zaldarriaga 2004 (arXiv:astro-ph/0407059) use the non-linear $\zeta$ for which, once a mode is frozen outside the horizon, the metric is $ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+e^{2\zeta(x)}a(t)^{2}dx_{i}dx_{i}\;$ [src: Eq. 2].
- **Power spectrum (Creminelli & Zaldarriaga normalisation).** The same paper defines $\langle\zeta_{\vec{k}_{i}}\zeta_{\vec{k}_{j}}\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\vec{k}_{i}+\vec{k}_{j})P_{k_{i}}\;$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0407059) [src: Eq. 3].
- This normalisation is not the one of arXiv:1002.1416 given in the conventions above [der]; no paper read for this skill states the relation, so none is given.
- **Local non-linearity parameter.** Creminelli & Zaldarriaga 2004 define $f_{\rm NL}$ through $\zeta(x)=\zeta_{g}(x)-\frac{3}{5}f_{\rm NL}(\zeta_{g}(x)^{2}-\left<\zeta_{g}^{2}\right>)\;$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0407059) [src: Eq. 5]; the factor $3/5$ comes from defining $f_{\rm NL}$ for the Newtonian potential in matter domination [src: footnote 3]. <!-- num: not a value -->

## Cosmological collider
<!-- key: cosmological-collider -->

- **Idea.** New particles with masses comparable to the Hubble scale produce, in the squeezed limit of the correlators, a power-law or oscillatory behaviour carrying their masses, an angular dependence carrying their spin, and a relative phase that depends on the quantum-mechanical nature of the fluctuations (Arkani-Hamed & Maldacena 2015, arXiv:1503.08043) [src: abstract]. In quasi-single-field inflation, massive isocurvature modes of mass of order H coupled to the inflaton through a turning trajectory give bispectra with a one-parameter family of shapes between local and equilateral (Chen & Wang 2009, arXiv:0911.3380) [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Mass parameter.** Achúcarro et al. 2022 (arXiv:2203.08128) use $\mu=\sqrt{m^{2}/H^{2}-9/4}$ [src: Sect. 3.1, inline, text after Eq. 3.7]; for $m/H>3/2$ the shape oscillates in the squeezed limit, the fingerprint of particle production [src: Sect. 3.1, text after Eq. 3.7]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Building block.** The four-point function of a conformally coupled scalar exchanging a massive scalar obeys $(\Delta_{u}-\Delta_{v})\,F^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\varphi}(u,v)=0\,,\qquad\big(\Delta_{u}+\mu^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4}\big)\hskip 1.0ptF^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\varphi}(u,v)=\frac{u\,v}{u+v}\,$ (Achúcarro et al. 2022, arXiv:2203.08128) [src: Sect. 3.1, Eq. 3.7], with $u\equiv k_{I}/(k_{1}+k_{2})$, $v\equiv k_{I}/(k_{3}+k_{4})$ and $\Delta_{u}$ a second-order differential operator [src: Sect. 3.1, inline, text after Eq. 3.7]; the solution is fixed by the absence of a folded singularity at $u,v=1$ and by the normalisation at the partial-energy singularity $u,v=-1$ [src: Sect. 3.1, text after Eq. 3.7]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Exchange bispectrum.** $S^{\mathrm{ex}}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})=\frac{k_{3}^{2}}{k_{1}k_{2}}\sum_{s}\left.\lambda_{s}\hskip 1.0ptU_{s,m}(u,\partial_{u},\alpha)\hskip 1.0ptF^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\varphi}(u,1)\hskip 1.0ptP_{s}(\alpha)\right|_{k_{4}\to 0}+\text{2 perms}\,$ (arXiv:2203.08128) [src: Sect. 3.1, Eq. 3.8], where $U_{s,m}$ maps the conformally coupled exchange to a particle of mass m and spin s. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Squeezed limit and amplitude.** From Eq. 3.8, $S\sim q^{\frac{1}{2}+\mathrm{i}\mu}P_{s}(\cos\theta)$ with θ the angle between soft and hard modes, and the amplitude is slow-roll and Boltzmann suppressed, $f_{\mathrm{NL}}^{\mathrm{ex}}\sim\varepsilon\mu^{n}\mathrm{e}^{-\pi\mu}$ (Achúcarro et al. 2022, arXiv:2203.08128) [src: Sect. 3.1, inline, text after Eq. 3.8]. The non-analytic dependence $S\propto\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mu\log q}$ encodes the time dependence of the scale factor (the quantum primordial standard clock) [src: Sect. 3.1, inline, text after Eq. 3.8].
- **Heavy limit.** At large μ the non-analytic part is Boltzmann suppressed and the exchange shape becomes degenerate with the equilateral shape, the EFT limit in which the heavy particle is integrated out (arXiv:2203.08128) [src: Sect. 3.1, text after Fig. 4]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Spinning particles.** Within the EFT of inflation, Lee, Baumann & Pimentel 2016 (arXiv:1607.03735) classify the couplings of massive spinning particles to the Goldstone and the graviton, and find observable non-Gaussianity within the EFT's validity when masses are close to H and interactions break the approximate conformal symmetry [src: abstract].
- **Strong mixing.** When the mixing of the curvature perturbation with a massive field is too strong for perturbation theory, the Laplace-space representation solves the bispectrum analytically; the collider signal then carries only half the usual Boltzmann suppression (Belrhali, Poisson & Renaux-Petel 2026, arXiv:2608.23243) [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Many fields.** With N_f ∼ 100 fields and generic boost-breaking mixings, the late-time scaling dimensions are driven toward the unitarity boundary by extreme-value statistics and random-matrix universality, making an almost-local squeezed limit a generic prediction (Werth 2026, arXiv:2609.37570) [src: abstract].

## Cosmological bootstrap
<!-- key: cosmological-bootstrap -->

- **Programme.** The cosmological bootstrap studies the statistics of primordial fluctuations from consistency with unitarity, locality and symmetry assumptions, with emphasis on de Sitter and quasi-de Sitter backgrounds and on the role of singularities and unitarity (Baumann et al. 2022, Snowmass white paper, arXiv:2203.08121) [src: abstract]. A 2026 review covers the scattering-amplitude tools carried to cosmology (spinor helicity, on-shell recursion, double copy, positive geometry, generalised unitarity) and their links to the optical theorem, the bootstrap and the collider (Albayrak & Kharel 2026, arXiv:2609.19252) [src: abstract].
- **De Sitter-invariant bootstrap.** Arkani-Hamed, Baumann, Lee & Pimentel 2018 (arXiv:1811.00024) classify inflationary three- and four-point functions from weakly broken conformal symmetry; the bispectrum from exchange of a particle of any spin is fixed by the soft limit of the scalar-exchange four-point function of conformally coupled scalars and contact terms, and the correlators contain flat-space amplitudes after analytic continuation of the external momenta [src: abstract]. The differential equations of that building block are transcribed in the section "Cosmological collider" (arXiv:2203.08128, Eq. 3.7).
- **Total-energy singularity.** The wavefunction coefficient diverges when the total energy $E\equiv\sum k_{n}\to 0$ because of the unbounded time integration, and its coefficient is the flat-space amplitude, $\lim_{E\to 0}\psi_{n}=\frac{iA_{n}}{E^{\alpha}}\,$ (Baumann et al. 2022, arXiv:2203.08121) [src: Sect. 2.1.3, Eq. 2.9]; the order α depends on the fields and interactions, and in cosmological backgrounds higher-order poles and branch points occur [src: Sect. 2.1.3, text after Eq. 2.9, footnote 11]. Partial-energy singularities, where the energy into a connected subdiagram vanishes, characterise particle exchange [src: Sect. 2.1.3]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Hermitian analyticity.** For a field obeying $\phi^{\prime\prime}+\dfrac{2a^{\prime}}{a}\phi^{\prime}+\left[c_{s}^{2}(\eta)k^{2}+a^{2}(\eta)m^{2}(\eta)\right]\phi=0\,$ (arXiv:2203.08121) [src: Sect. 3.1, Eq. 3.1], the Bunch–Davies solution satisfies $\phi^{+}(k,\eta)=\phi^{+}(-k^{\ast},\eta)^{\ast}\implies\text{Disc}[\phi]\equiv\phi(k)-\phi^{\ast}(-k^{\ast})=0\,$ (arXiv:2203.08121) [src: Sect. 3.1, Eq. 3.3], a property inherited by the bulk-to-bulk and bulk-to-boundary propagators [src: Sect. 3.1, Eq. 3.4].
- **Cosmological optical theorem and cutting rules.** With real couplings (unitarity) and local interactions, the propagator identities give one cutting rule per diagram to all orders; isolating the term with no cut, $i\,\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{internal}\\ \text{lines}\end{subarray}}{\text{Disc}}\left[i\,\psi^{(D)}\right]=\sum_{\rm cuts}\left[\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rm cut\\ \rm momenta\end{subarray}}\int P\right]\prod_{\rm subdiagrams}(-i)\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{internal }\&\\ \text{ cut lines}\end{subarray}}{\text{Disc}}\left[i\,\psi^{(\rm subdiagram)}\right]\,$ (arXiv:2203.08121) [src: Sect. 3.1, Eq. 3.5], with an integral and a power spectrum P per cut line. Goodhew, Jazayeri & Pajer 2020 (arXiv:2009.02898) derived the relations for fields of any mass in de Sitter with general local interactions, not necessarily de Sitter invariant; for contact diagrams any correlator with an odd number of conformally coupled scalars and any number of massless scalars vanishes, and for four-point exchange the theorem relates $ψ_{3}$ and $ψ_{4}$, that is bispectrum and trispectrum [src: abstract].
- **Boostless bootstrap.** Inflationary backgrounds break de Sitter boosts; Pajer 2020 (arXiv:2010.12818) gives Bootstrap Rules that fix three-point functions of gravitons and curvature perturbations at the boundary without reference to bulk time evolution, and derives the scalar bispectrum of the EFT of inflation to any order in derivatives [src: abstract]. Pimentel & Wang 2022 (arXiv:2205.00013) bootstrap the complete set of single-exchange collider bispectra with boost-breaking interactions from three-point seed functions, with oscillations around the equilateral configuration when the massive particle has a much lower sound speed than the inflaton [src: abstract].
- **Positivity (bispectrum islands).** For a hidden sector coupled to the inflaton with mildly broken de Sitter isometries and dominated by scalar-operator exchange, unitarity forces the bispectrum to be negative in a region around the equilateral configuration, 0.027 ≲ k3/k1 ≤ 2 for isosceles triangles with k2 = k3, and bounds the shape from above and below (de Rham, Jazayeri & Tolley 2025, arXiv:2506.19198) [src: abstract].
- **Non-perturbative bootstrap.** Hogervorst, Penedones & Salehi Vaziri 2021 (arXiv:2107.13871) is the starting point of the non-perturbative programme in de Sitter; its content is summarised in the section "QFT in de Sitter and infrared effects". <!-- num: not a value -->

## Primordial features
<!-- key: primordial-features -->

- **Definition.** Achúcarro et al. 2022 (arXiv:2203.08128) write a featured spectrum as $\Delta_{\mathcal{R}}^{2}(k)=\Delta_{\mathcal{R},0}^{2}(k)\,[1+\delta\Delta_{\mathcal{R}}^{2}(k)]\,$ [src: Sect. 4.1, Eq. 4.1], around a power-law spectrum of amplitude $A_{\mathrm{s}}$ and tilt $n_{\mathrm{s}}$ at a pivot $k_{*}$ [src: Sect. 4.1]. A strongly scale-dependent correction arises when a background quantity (slow-roll parameters, sound speed, or couplings to other fields) departs from slow-roll evolution [src: Sect. 4.1, text after Eq. 4.1].
- **Sharp features.** A momentary departure of a background parameter from the attractor gives a transient linear oscillation, $\delta\Delta_{\mathcal{R}}^{2}(k)=f(k)\hskip 1.0pt\cos(2k/k_{0}+\phi)$, with f(k) a model-dependent envelope and $k_{0}$ the wavenumber crossing the horizon at the departure (arXiv:2203.08128) [src: Sect. 4.1, inline, text after Eq. 4.1]. The review prints the condition for this departure as $|\dot{B}/BH|\ll 1$, the same inequality it uses for slow-roll evolution in the preceding paragraph; it is copied as printed and is read here as a slip of the sign of the inequality (none published) [src: Sect. 4.1, inline, text after Eq. 4.1]. Examples: non-smooth potentials, sudden turns of multi-field trajectories, sudden changes of the sound speed [src: Sect. 4.1]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Resonant features.** A periodic oscillation of a background quantity with super-Hubble frequency resonates with subhorizon modes and gives a constant-frequency oscillation in $\log k$, $\delta\Delta_{\mathcal{R}}^{2}(k)\propto\cos(\Omega\log(2k)+\phi)$ (arXiv:2203.08128) [src: Sect. 4.1, inline, text after Eq. 4.1]; axion monodromy is the main example [src: Sect. 4.1].
- **Bispectrum of a sharp feature.** A feature in the potential makes slow-roll parameters transiently large and gives scale- and shape-dependent non-Gaussianity far larger than in slow roll, correlated with the feature of the 2-point function (Chen, Easther & Lim 2006, arXiv:astro-ph/0611645) [src: abstract]; Chen, Easther & Lim 2008 (arXiv:0801.3295) distinguish this horizon-crossing mechanism from the resonance mechanism, in which small-scale structure in the potential acts while modes are deep inside the horizon, and give factorisable analytic approximations for both [src: abstract].
- **Resonant non-Gaussianity.** For a canonical inflaton with small sinusoidal modulations of the potential, the resonance between background and fluctuation oscillations produces an observably large bispectrum with essentially no overlap with the local, equilateral and orthogonal shapes, not captured by the simplest EFT of inflation (Flauger & Pajer 2010, arXiv:1002.0833) [src: abstract].
- **Bootstrap with features.** For heavy fields with time-dependent masses and sound speeds, bulk locality gives integro-differential boundary equations with a memory kernel; for sinusoidal masses the squeezed bispectrum shows a scale-breaking collider signal whose amplitude can be exponentially enhanced relative to the Boltzmann suppression (Jazayeri, Tong & Zhu 2025, arXiv:2511.00152) [src: abstract].
- **Classical collider.** Classical oscillations or sharp features can excite heavy fields with masses much larger than H without Boltzmann suppression, extending the energy reach of the collider (Chen, Ebadi & Kumar 2022, arXiv:2205.01107) [src: abstract].

## Loop corrections
<!-- key: loop-corrections -->

- **In-in loops.** Weinberg 2005 (arXiv:hep-th/0506236) reviews and extends the in-in formalism and applies it to loop graphs; for some but not all theories, loop as well as tree contributions depend only on the inflaton potential near horizon exit [src: abstract]. The in-in expectation value used for tree-level results is transcribed in the section "Quantisation and the in-in formalism" (arXiv:1002.1416, Eqs. 3.22–3.23).
- **Size of the late-time growth.** In perturbation theory, loop corrections cannot grow as positive powers of the scale factor, only at most as powers of its logarithm (Weinberg 2006, arXiv:hep-th/0605244) [src: abstract].
- **Running.** Senatore & Zaldarriaga 2009 (arXiv:0912.2734) find the one-loop running of the two-point function to be $\log(H/\mu)$, not $\log(k/\mu)$, with a sharp cutoff, in dimensional regularisation and by making the loop integral dimensionless; super-horizon correlators do not depend on time once tadpoles are included [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value --> <!-- eq: notation -->
- **Time independence at one loop.** In single-clock inflation the one-loop $\zeta$ correlator is time independent: the individual time dependences cancel, one subset through the renormalisation of the background stress tensor, the other through the squeezed-limit consistency condition (Pimentel, Senatore & Zaldarriaga 2012, arXiv:1203.6651) [src: abstract]. Long modes have no infrared projection effect on physical questions; short modes change the mapping between horizon-crossing time and scale by an amount enhanced by $N_e$, with a variance not so enhanced (Senatore & Zaldarriaga 2012, arXiv:1203.6354) [src: abstract].
- **Renormalisation with features.** Ultraviolet divergences and tadpoles of the EFT of inflationary fluctuations with arbitrary time-dependent Wilson coefficients cancel with a finite set of local counterterms, provided an initial adiabatic phase connects to the Bunch–Davies vacuum (Braglia, Céspedes & Pinol 2026, arXiv:2603.12216) [src: abstract].
- **Background renormalisation.** Imposing a vanishing one-point function of $\zeta$ as renormalisation condition induces, through the non-linear symmetry of $\zeta$, a finite and scheme-dependent renormalised one-loop correction to the two-point function, which can be large and time dependent; the cancellation of the divergence is a manifestation of Maldacena's consistency condition (Kristiano & Yokoyama 2025, arXiv:2504.18514) [src: abstract].
- **Ward identities.** Exact Ward identities of the large gauge symmetry of the background–perturbation split constrain the renormalisation of loops in non-attractor inflation and, if the UV completion respects the symmetry, govern the infrared evolution of the power spectrum non-perturbatively (Fang & Guo 2026, arXiv:2605.13325) [src: abstract]. The same identity forbids one-loop corrections to large-scale tensor modes from enhanced small-scale scalars (Fang, Hu & Guo 2025, arXiv:2509.00420) [src: abstract].

## Decoherence
<!-- key: decoherence -->

- **Squeezing and classicality.** Polarski & Starobinsky 1995 (arXiv:gr-qc/9504030) show in the Heisenberg and Schrödinger pictures that inflationary perturbations from the vacuum are equivalent to classical perturbations with stochastic Gaussian amplitudes in the quasi-isotropic mode once the decaying mode, exponentially small in the squeezing parameter $r_k$, is neglected; the standard predictions for the rms values are not affected by interaction with the environment [src: abstract]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- **Gravitational decoherence.** Tracing out mostly shorter modes of $\zeta$ coupled through minimal gravitational cubic interactions suppresses the off-diagonal elements of the reduced density matrix of a long mode, leaving a diagonal mixture of classical configurations; decoherence is delayed after horizon crossing and Hubble-scale modes act as the environment (Nelson 2016, arXiv:1601.03734) [src: abstract].
- **Open-system (Lindblad) description.** Martin & Vennin 2018 (arXiv:1801.09949) model the perturbations as an open quantum system obeying a Lindblad equation: observations bound the interaction strength from above, completion of decoherence by the end of inflation bounds it from below, and they identify the scenarios in which decoherence completes without altering the power spectrum [src: abstract]. With a Lindblad term quadratic in the scalar perturbations, interactions reduce the squeezing as well as the purity in most of the parameter space, so purity alone does not decide whether correlations are quantum (Micheli, Oshima & Takahashi 2025, arXiv:2512.17622) [src: abstract].
- **Tensor-catalysed decoherence.** Burgess et al. 2026 (arXiv:2607.22914) compute the leading decoherence of the scalar metric fluctuation in single-clock models from gravitational interactions with the unmeasured short scalar and tensor modes, at leading order in $(H/M_p)^2$ and in $ε_1$, and find it not slow-roll suppressed, of the same order as tensor decoherence, because the dominant interactions involve both environments at once; the result is UV finite, and the interactions become time-local and Gaussian deep in the super-Hubble regime, which allows a reliable late-time evolution of the purity [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Mixed-state landscape.** A mixed state compatible with adiabatic, nearly Gaussian, nearly scale-invariant data is parametrised by its purity and momentum variance; decoherence models that classicalise, with a regular positive Glauber–Sudarshan P-function, must inject momentum beyond the vacuum value and source the decaying mode (Haque & Underwood 2026, arXiv:2606.07663) [src: abstract].
- **Tests of quantum origin.** Maldacena 2015 (arXiv:1508.01082) gives, as a proof of principle, an inflationary model in which a cosmological observable violating a Bell inequality can be constructed [src: abstract]. Sanz 2026 (arXiv:2601.13053) instead defines a classical hypothesis (local stochastic fields with a positive probability distribution) and classicality inequalities testable with large-scale structure and 21 cm observables, without Bell tests [src: abstract].

## QFT in de Sitter and infrared effects
<!-- key: qft-in-de-sitter-and-infrared-effects -->

- **Setting and units.** Penedones et al. 2023 (arXiv:2301.04146) study QFT in $(d+1)$-dimensional de Sitter space, whose isometry group is $\text{SO}(1,d+1)$ [src: abstract], and take the de Sitter radius to be one [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 2.1]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Labels of a representation.** In that paper an infinite-dimensional representation of $\text{SO}(1,d+1)$ is fixed by a complex scaling dimension $\Delta$ and, for the symmetric traceless tensors considered, a spin $s$; its quadratic Casimir is $\Delta(d-\Delta)-s(d+s-2)$ (arXiv:2301.04146) [src: Sect. 2.1, inline, first paragraph]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **The four families for $d\geq 3$** (arXiv:2301.04146) [src: Sect. 2.1, inline list of the four series]: principal series ${\cal P}_{\Delta,s}$ with $\Delta\in\frac{d}{2}+i\mathbb{R}$; complementary series $\mathcal{C}_{\Delta,s}$ with $0<\Delta<d$ for $s=0$ and $1<\Delta<d-1$ for $s\geq 1$; type I exceptional series ${\cal V}_{p,0}$ with $\Delta=d+p-1$, $s=0$, $p\geq 1$; type II exceptional series ${\cal U}_{s,t}$ with $\Delta=d+t-1$, $s\geq 1$, $t=0,1,2\cdots,s-1$. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Field content.** ${\cal F}_{\Delta,s}$ (principal or complementary) describes a spin-$s$ massive field of mass $m^{2}=\Delta(d-\Delta)$ when $s=0$ (arXiv:2301.04146) [src: Sect. 2.1, inline, paragraph after the list]; ${\cal U}_{s,t}$ describes partially massless gauge fields of spin $s$ and depth $t$, with $t=s-1$ the massless gauge fields [src: Sect. 2.1, paragraph after the list]; ${\cal V}_{1,0}$ "is expected to describe" the scalar with gauged shift symmetry $\phi(x)\to\phi(x)+c$ [src: Sect. 2.1, paragraph after the list] (arXiv:2301.04146). <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Pedagogical source.** Sun 2021 (arXiv:2111.04591) is a pedagogical introduction to the unitary irreducible representations of $\text{SO}(1,d+1)$ from the de Sitter QFT viewpoint, including Harish-Chandra characters [src: abstract]; arXiv:2301.04146 cites it for the classification [src: Sect. 2.1, refs. 29, 36, 43]. <!-- num: not a value -->

### Infrared effects of light fields and stochastic resummation
<!-- key: infrared-effects-of-light-fields-and-stochastic -->

- **Leading-order stochastic equation.** For a light scalar in de Sitter, the probability distribution of the field obeys, at leading order, $\frac{\partial}{\partial t}P(\phi,t)=\frac{H^{3}}{8\pi^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\phi^{2}}P(\phi,t)+\frac{1}{3H}\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\big[V^{\prime}(\phi)P(\phi,t)\big]\,$ (Cohen et al. 2021, arXiv:2106.09728) [src: Sect. 2.1, Eq. 2.2], the first term the quantum noise and the second the classical drift; its equilibrium solution is $P_{\rm eq}(\phi)=Ce^{-8\pi^{2}V(\phi)/3H^{4}}\,$ (arXiv:2106.09728) [src: Sect. 2.1, Eq. 2.4].
- **Origin.** Starobinsky & Yokoyama 1994 (arXiv:astro-ph/9407016) show with the stochastic approach that a weakly self-interacting light scalar in de Sitter reaches a de Sitter-invariant equilibrium state for either sign of the mass term, and give the two-point function, correlation time and correlation radius in that state [src: abstract]. Its equations were not read at source (see "Not verified").
- **Infrared divergences resolved.** Gorbenko & Senatore 2019 (arXiv:1911.00022) resolve the infrared divergences of light scalar fields in de Sitter [src: abstract].
- **Soft de Sitter effective theory (SdSET).** Cohen & Green 2020 (arXiv:2007.03693) describe superhorizon modes by their growing and decaying solutions with a power counting: for massive fields in fixed de Sitter all superhorizon interactions are irrelevant; for very light fields the leading ones are at most marginal, and resumming their logarithms by the renormalisation group gives the equation of stochastic inflation; with dynamical gravity, diffeomorphism invariance makes all interactions irrelevant, implying the all-orders conservation of adiabatic fluctuations and gravitational waves [src: abstract].
- **Beyond leading order.** In SdSET, stochastic inflation is the renormalisation-group evolution of composite operators; non-Gaussian quantum fluctuations first enter at NNLO, from a two-loop anomalous dimension (Cohen et al. 2021, arXiv:2106.09728) [src: abstract]. In dimensional regularisation with an initial-condition functional, renormalisation and matching work as for flat-space EFTs, checked on the tree-level trispectrum, the six-point function and the one-loop power spectrum of massless $\kappa\phi^4$ theory (Beneke, Hager & Sanfilippo 2026, arXiv:2603.09438) [src: abstract]; composite-operator mixing gives the first two-loop correction to the diffusion term of the Fokker–Planck equation (Beneke, Hager & Sanfilippo 2026, arXiv:2604.14283) [src: abstract]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- **Momentum space for de Sitter.** Belrhali, Poisson, Renaux-Petel & Werth 2026 (arXiv:2601.15228) build a momentum space for QFT in $\text{dS}_{d+1}$ in Poincaré slicing in which the dS frequency is the unitary-representation label of $\text{SO}(1,d+1)$, reformulate in-in perturbation theory in it, and accommodate principal and complementary series in the Källén–Lehmann decomposition of composite operators [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value -->
