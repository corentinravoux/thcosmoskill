# Open questions on primordial correlators

Scope: live debates with both sides, unresolved calculations, what the next data or calculation will decide, and stale claims with the paper that corrects them.

Snapshot: 2026-11-02

## Live debates
<!-- key: live-debates -->

### Do enhanced small-scale fluctuations change large-scale correlators at one loop?
<!-- key: do-enhanced-small-scale-fluctuations-change -->
- For: in a single-field model with a sharp transition of the second slow-roll parameter, models producing an appreciable amount of primordial black holes induce non-perturbative coupling on CMB scales, so large-scale observations constrain the small-scale spectrum (Kristiano & Yokoyama 2022, arXiv:2211.03395) [src: abstract]. Imposing a vanishing one-point function as renormalisation condition gives a finite, scheme-dependent one-loop correction that can be large and time dependent (Kristiano & Yokoyama 2025, arXiv:2504.18514) [src: abstract].
- Against: boundary terms cancel the non-volume-suppressed contributions (Fumagalli 2023, arXiv:2305.19263) [src: abstract]; with the quartic interactions and the consistency relations of a transient non-slow-roll phase, the one-loop correction to long modes is a total derivative and long modes are unaffected (Fumagalli 2024, arXiv:2408.08296) [src: abstract]; with separation of scales and adiabaticity, the back-reaction is not observable (Iacconi 2026, arXiv:2605.04815) [src: abstract]; Ward identities of the background–perturbation split fix the renormalisation and resolve the discrepancies for ultra-slow-roll (Fang & Guo 2026, arXiv:2605.13325) [src: abstract], and forbid the tensor analogue (arXiv:2509.00420) [src: abstract].
- `[der]` The disagreement has moved from the size of the one-loop integral to the renormalisation condition and the treatment of boundary terms; the 2025 paper of Kristiano & Yokoyama and the 2026 Ward-identity paper both appeal to the non-linear symmetry of ζ and reach different conclusions on the finite term.
- What decides it: an agreed renormalisation condition for the background, applied in one calculation that includes boundary and quartic terms.

### Is the decoherence of scalar fluctuations slow-roll suppressed?
<!-- key: is-the-decoherence-of-scalar-fluctuations-slow -->
- New result: including interactions that involve the tensor and scalar environments simultaneously, the leading scalar decoherence is not slow-roll suppressed, of the same order as tensor decoherence, unlike all earlier calculations (Burgess et al. 2026, arXiv:2607.22914) [src: abstract].
- Earlier picture: minimal gravitational nonlinearities with Hubble-scale scalar modes as environment decohere long modes, with a delay after horizon crossing due to the weak coupling (Nelson 2016, arXiv:1601.03734) [src: abstract].
- Reply: none found as of the snapshot.
- What decides it: an independent calculation including mixed scalar–tensor environments.

### Weak or strong mixing in the cosmological collider
<!-- key: weak-or-strong-mixing-in-the-cosmological -->
- Weak mixing gives Boltzmann- and slow-roll-suppressed amplitudes (arXiv:2203.08128) [src: Sect. 3.1, inline, text after Eq. 3.8].
- Strong mixing: the weak-mixing assumption is incompatible with current data except at the smallest masses (Philcox 2026, arXiv:2607.18369) [src: abstract]; at strong mixing the signal carries half the usual Boltzmann suppression (arXiv:2608.23243) [src: abstract], while Philcox finds the oscillatory contributions heavily suppressed and Planck constraints much tighter than the theoretical bounds (arXiv:2607.18369) [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- With many fields, an almost-local squeezed limit is generic and an enhanced squeezed limit with few fields is fine-tuned (Werth 2026, arXiv:2609.37570) [src: abstract].
- What decides it: templates that span weak and strong mixing in the same search, and the next CMB and LSS bispectra.

### Are the feature hints in the data primordial?
<!-- key: are-the-feature-hints-in-the-data-primordial -->
- Planck PR3 and PR4: several frequencies improve the fit by Δχ² ≃ −10 to −15, not supported by Bayesian model comparison; global significance at most $2.6σ$ after the look-elsewhere effect; some earlier anomalies shrink with the PR4 CamSpec likelihood (Raffaelli et al. 2026, arXiv:2605.30236) [src: abstract].
- DESI DR1: a ≃ 3σ preference for a logarithmic feature with fixed Planck cosmology disappears when cosmology is varied, attributed to the DESI–Planck distance-scale tension (Chen et al. 2026, arXiv:2610.10530) [src: abstract].
- What decides it: next-generation polarisation measurements, which the Planck search names as decisive in distinguishing primordial oscillations from fluctuations and systematics (arXiv:2605.30236) [src: abstract] (forecast).

## Unresolved calculations
<!-- key: unresolved-calculations -->

- **Hilbert space of QFT in de Sitter for $d\geq 3$.** The decomposition into unitary irreducible representations is complete for $d=1$ and $d=2$ only (Penedones et al. 2023, arXiv:2301.04146) [src: abstract]; general-$d$ results for spinning complementary series are very incomplete [src: Table 1.3 caption]. <!-- num: not a value --> <!-- eq: notation -->
- **In-in from in-out.** Non-perturbative evidence for obtaining in-in correlators from an in-out formalism exists for 1+1-dimensional $φ^4$ theory; the extension to 3+1 dimensions is only discussed (Basumatary et al. 2026, arXiv:2603.26090) [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Stochastic inflation beyond two loops.** The NNLO equation (arXiv:2106.09728) and the two-loop diffusion term (arXiv:2604.14283) are computed [src: abstracts]; higher orders are open [der].
- **Bootstrap without de Sitter invariance.** Boostless bootstrap rules exist for three-point functions (arXiv:2010.12818) [src: abstract] and for single-exchange collider bispectra (arXiv:2205.00013) [src: abstract]; scale-breaking correlators lead to integro-differential boundary equations solved at leading order in the oscillation amplitude (arXiv:2511.00152) [src: abstract].
- **Positivity beyond scalar exchange.** The bispectrum island assumes scalar-operator exchange and mildly broken de Sitter isometries; strongly boost-breaking couplings are only contemplated (de Rham et al. 2025, arXiv:2506.19198) [src: abstract].

## What the next data or calculation will decide
<!-- key: what-the-next-data-or-calculation-will-decide -->

- **Single-field versus multi-field.** A violation of the scalar soft theorem would rule out attractor single-field models (Ullah 2026, arXiv:2604.09765) [src: abstract]; the observable is local non-Gaussianity beyond projection effects (arXiv:1305.0824) [src: abstract].
- **Collider detection.** Orthogonalised collider templates are proposed as the strategy to detect primordial non-Gaussianity and rule out single-field inflation with forthcoming observations (Suman et al. 2025, arXiv:2511.17500) [src: abstract] (forecast). A 21 cm survey of the dark ages could detect or constrain new particles of different masses and interactions (Meerburg et al. 2016, arXiv:1610.06559) [src: abstract] (forecast).
- **Folded shapes in galaxy surveys.** Most of the constraining power on folded non-Gaussianity comes from the galaxy bispectrum (Yang & Philcox 2026, arXiv:2607.15337) [src: abstract] (forecast).
- **Quantum origin.** Classicality inequalities can be tested with large-scale structure and 21 cm surveys (Sanz 2026, arXiv:2601.13053) [src: abstract] (forecast).
- **Floor from non-primordial effects.** Even for Gaussian primordial perturbations, non-linear CMB evolution generates $f_{NL}\sim{\cal O}(1)$, and LSS a similar number, so slow-roll values are unlikely to be disentangled soon (Chen 2010, arXiv:1002.1416) [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 4.23].

## Stale claims and misconceptions
<!-- key: stale-claims-and-misconceptions -->

- **"Single-field inflation gives an observable squeezed bispectrum of order $1-n_{s}$."** The primordial squeezed signal of single-field slow roll vanishes in the CMB bispectrum and in scale-dependent halo bias up to projection effects (Pajer et al. 2013, arXiv:1305.0824) [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **"Loops run as $\log(k/\mu)$ and spoil predictivity."** The running is $\log(H/\mu)$ (Senatore & Zaldarriaga 2009, arXiv:0912.2734) [src: abstract], and super-horizon $\zeta$ is time independent at one loop (arXiv:1203.6651) [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value --> <!-- eq: notation -->
- **"One-loop corrections exclude PBH formation in single-field inflation."** The paper's version read speaks of constraining the small-scale spectrum (arXiv:2211.03395) [src: abstract], and the replies find no observable large-scale effect (arXiv:2408.08296, arXiv:2605.04815) [src: abstracts].
- **"Collider signals are always suppressed by $\mathrm{e}^{-\pi\mu}$."** Strong mixing halves the suppression (arXiv:2608.23243) [src: abstract]; mass oscillations can enhance the signal exponentially relative to it (arXiv:2511.00152) [src: abstract]; classical features can excite fields much heavier than H without it (arXiv:2205.01107) [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **"Scalar decoherence is slow-roll suppressed."** Not when scalar and tensor environments act together (arXiv:2607.22914) [src: abstract].
- **"The sound-speed bound is $c_{s}>0.028$."** That bound of Cheung et al. (arXiv:0709.0293) [src: Eq. 47] used the WMAP equilateral window of that time; Planck 2018 gives $c_{\mathrm{s}}^{\rm DBI}\geq\ 0.086$ at 95% from T+E (arXiv:1905.05697) [src: Eq. 59].
- **"An equilateral-triangle normalisation transfers constraints between models."** It can be very misleading because the two shape classes are nearly orthogonal (arXiv:astro-ph/0405356) [src: abstract].
