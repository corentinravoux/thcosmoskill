# Open questions in inflation

Scope: live debates with both sides, unresolved calculations, what the next data will decide, and stale claims with the paper that corrects them.

Snapshot: 2026-11-02

## Live debates
<!-- key: live-debates -->

### Is the high n_s of ACT DR6 with DESI a property of the primordial spectrum?
<!-- key: is-the-high-ns-of-act-dr6-with-desi-a-property -->

- For a real upward shift: P-ACT with lensing and DESI DR1 BAO gives $n_s=0.974\pm0.003$ (Louis et al. 2025, arXiv:2503.14452) [src: abstract], putting the Starobinsky model on the 2σ boundary (arXiv:2503.14454) [src: Sect. IV.4]; the combination of Planck, ACT and SPT with DESI disfavours Higgs, Starobinsky and exponential α-attractors at more than 2σ (McDonough & Ferreira 2025, arXiv:2512.05108) [src: abstract].
- Against reading it as settled: the same paper ties the shift to the BAO–CMB tension between CMB experiments and DESI and to a commensurate shift of τ, the largest for ACT, which has the largest tension with DESI, and calls for further study of that tension and of the interplay of $n_s$ and τ (arXiv:2512.05108) [src: abstract; Sect. I]. Iacconi et al. explore large-$n_s$ T-models "pending a resolution of the tension between BAO parameters as determined by CMB datasets and those determined by DESI" (arXiv:2511.14673) [src: abstract].
- Model-building responses: a longer or stiffer reheating stage (arXiv:2511.14673) [src: abstract]; higher reheating temperatures for E-models and monomial behaviour V(φ) ∝ φ^k with k ≥ 6 near the minimum (Ellis et al. 2025, arXiv:2510.18656) [src: abstract]; inflaton couplings to matter that shift $n_s$ through radiative corrections (Ellis et al. 2025, arXiv:2510.15137) [src: abstract]; higher-order curvature corrections to Starobinsky inflation, favoured by Qiu & Huang (arXiv:2606.24474) [src: abstract].
- Cosmological-model dependence: a small negative curvature, Ω_k ≃ 3 × 10⁻³, lowers $n_s$ to n_s = 0.9692 ± 0.0035 with Planck, ACT, SPT and DESI, and time-evolving dark energy gives n_s = 0.9716 ± 0.0032; the authors conclude that the tension with Starobinsky, Higgs and the simplest α-attractors holds only for ΛCDM (Chudaykin et al. 2026, arXiv:2607.28445) [src: abstract].
- What decides it: the resolution of the CMB–DESI BAO tension and an independent measurement of τ `[der]`; for the models, $n_s$ at the precision of the next CMB experiments together with the reheating history.

### Does inflation have an initial-conditions problem?
<!-- key: does-inflation-have-an-initial-conditions -->

- Robust: numerical relativity finds that inflation begins from highly inhomogeneous, gradient-dominated initial data if the field starts on a flat part of the potential and the universe is flat or open on average (East et al. 2016, arXiv:1511.05143) [src: abstract]; large-field models withstand dominant gradient energies (Clough et al. 2017, arXiv:1608.04408) [src: abstract]; no-hair theorems cover broad classes of homogeneous and inhomogeneous cosmologies (Snowmass report, arXiv:2203.07629) [src: Sect. 3.3].
- Not robust: small-field inflation can fail with subdominant gradients (arXiv:1608.04408) [src: abstract]; plateau models are especially vulnerable to anti-ultralocality, with fine-tuning that grows as the energy scale decreases (Shterenberg et al. 2026, arXiv:2608.24997) [src: abstract].
- The 2013 exchange: Ijjas, Steinhardt & Loeb argued that the preference of Planck 2013 for plateau potentials worsens the initial-conditions and multiverse-unpredictability problems and creates an "unlikeliness problem" (arXiv:1304.2785) [src: abstract]; Guth, Kaiser & Nomura replied that these conclusions rest on several problematic assumptions and that inflation is on a stronger footing than before (arXiv:1312.7619) [src: abstract].
- What decides it: simulations that start from the same class of initial data for plateau and large-field potentials, and a measure over initial data, which neither side has fixed `[der]`.

## Unresolved calculations
<!-- key: unresolved-calculations -->

- The tail of the curvature distribution beyond stochastic inflation: quantum corrections are expected to matter there, and the exact-RG master equation gives the framework, but no complete calculation of the far tail was read (Green & Gupta 2025, arXiv:2512.13842) [src: abstract].
- Stochastic inflation through sharp transitions: gradient interactions are now included in toy cases, and the role of backreaction with gradients is discussed but not settled (Briaud et al. 2025, arXiv:2509.05124) [src: abstract].
- The definition of the background when no classical trajectory exists makes forward and time-reversed stochastic δN differ, notably for eternal inflation (Blachier & Ringeval 2025, arXiv:2511.21388) [src: abstract].
- The equation of state from the end of inflation to radiation domination, model by model: lattice results show non-monotonic histories that change both $N_*$ and preheating gravitational waves (arXiv:2507.13465) [src: abstract].
- Radiative stability of plateau predictions once the reheating couplings are included (arXiv:2510.15137) [src: abstract].

## What the next data will decide
<!-- key: what-the-next-data-will-decide -->

- LiteBIRD's full-success criterion is a total error $\delta r<0.001$ for a fiducial $r=0$, which would give more than 10σ detection for models predicting $r>0.01$ (LiteBIRD Collaboration, arXiv:2202.02773) [src: Sect. 3.1; Sect. 3.2] (forecast).
- $n_s$: whether the upward shift survives depends on the CMB–DESI BAO tension, τ and the assumed cosmological model (arXiv:2512.05108; arXiv:2607.28445) [src: abstracts]; SPT-3G reports a 2.8σ difference in ΛCDM between CMB and DESI DR2 BAO, relaxed in extended models (Camphuis et al. 2025, arXiv:2506.20707) [src: abstract].
- Monomial T-models predict at most n_s = 0.9682 under perturbative reheating, a benchmark to rule them out (arXiv:2511.14673) [src: abstract].

## Stale claims and misconceptions
<!-- key: stale-claims-and-misconceptions -->

- "$m^{2}\phi^{2}$ fits the data with r ≈ 0.1" (stated as a prospect in arXiv:0907.5424, Eq. 243): strongly disfavoured, ln B = −11.5 relative to $R^{2}$ (arXiv:1807.06211) [src: Table 5]; convex monomials strongly excluded by BK18 (arXiv:2503.14454) [src: Sect. IV.4].
- "Natural inflation is viable": strongly disfavoured, ln B = −6.6 (arXiv:1807.06211) [src: Sect. 4.2].
- "Starobinsky and Higgs inflation are the best-fitting benchmarks": true for Planck 2018 alone (arXiv:1807.06211) [src: Sect. 4.2], not for CMB-SPA with DESI DR2 in ΛCDM (arXiv:2512.05108) [src: abstract]; restored in part outside ΛCDM (arXiv:2607.28445) [src: abstract].
- "n_s = 1 − 2/N and r = 12α/N² hold for all α-attractors": only at small α; for large α the predictions move to those of chaotic inflation (arXiv:1311.0472) [src: abstract].
- "The horizon-crossing formula holds whenever the spectrum is scale invariant": false in ultra-slow roll (arXiv:gr-qc/0503017) [src: abstract].
- "The tails of the curvature distribution follow from $f_{\rm NL}$-type expansions": the tails are exponential and those expansions describe only the neighbourhood of the maximum (arXiv:1912.05399) [src: abstract].
