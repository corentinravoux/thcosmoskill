# Reheating and the e-folds at the pivot — thcosmo-inflation dossier

Scope: the chain from a reheating history (mean equation of state, end density or temperature, the reheating parameter) to $\Delta N_*$, then to the field value at horizon exit and to $n_s$ and $r$ for a given potential, with the three parametrisations in use and the ways the chain breaks.
Snapshot: 2026-11-03.
From: Baumann arXiv:0907.5424, Sect. 6.4; Martin et al. arXiv:1303.3787, Sect. 3.2; Amin et al. arXiv:1410.3808 — `references/subtopics.tsv`, row `reheating-preheating`.
Audit questions: (2) How does $N_*$ follow from the reheating history, and how do $w_{\rm reh}$, $T_{\rm reh}$ and the reheating parameter propagate into $n_s$ and $r$ for a given model?

The definitions of $\overline{w}_{\rm reh}$ and $R_{\rm rad}$, the Encyclopaedia's $\Delta N_*$ (Eq. 48) and Planck 2018 X's $N_*$ (Eq. 47) are in `formalism.md#reheating-and-the-number-of-e-folds`; measured reheating constraints and the 2025 model-building responses are in `results.md#reheating-constraints`; the referee's view of a fixed e-fold window is in `approximations.md#the-number-of-e-folds-at-the-pivot`; the slow-roll formulas that turn $\phi_*$ into $n_s$ and $r$ are in `deep/slow-roll-predictions.md`. Preheating dynamics (Floquet bands, lattices) are not treated here.

## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- All papers below use the reduced Planck mass except Liddle and Leach, who write the full $m_{\rm Pl}$, for example $H_{k}^{2}\simeq 8\pi V_{k}/3m_{{\rm Pl}}^{2}$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0305263) [src: Sect. II, inline, text after Eq. 5].
- e-folds. Martin and Ringeval count forward, $N\equiv\ln\left(a/a_{\rm ini}\right)$, and call $N_{\rm T}$ the total number of e-folds of inflation (arXiv:1004.5525) [src: Sect. II.1, inline, text after Eqs. 4 and 6]; the Encyclopaedia writes $N_{\rm end}$ for the same quantity and $\Delta N_{*}\equiv N_{\mathrm{end}}-N_{*}.$ (arXiv:1303.3787) [src: Eq. 40]. Liddle and Leach define the e-folds before the end directly, $e^{N(k)}\equiv\frac{a_{{\rm end}}}{a_{k}}\,,$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0305263) [src: Eq. 1]; Dai, Kamionkowski and Wang write $N_k$, $N_{\rm re}$ and $N_{\rm RD}$ for inflation after exit, reheating and radiation domination (arXiv:1404.6704) [src: text after Eq. 5]; Iacconi et al. write $\Delta N_{\rm CMB}$ and $\Delta N_{\rm rh}$ (arXiv:2511.14673) [src: Eqs. 11, 17].
- Mean equation of state. $\overline{w}_{\rm reh}$ (Encyclopaedia, Eq. 42), $\bar{w}_{\mathrm{reh}}$ (Martin and Ringeval, Eq. 12), $w_{\rm re}$ taken constant (Dai et al., text after Eq. 7), $\bar w$ (Iacconi et al., Eq. 17) and $w_{\rm int}$ (Planck 2018 X, Eq. 47; Ellis et al., Eq. 28) are the same e-fold average under different names. <!-- num: not a value -->
- End of reheating. The Encyclopaedia and Martin and Ringeval use the energy density $\rho_{\rm reh}$ at the start of radiation domination; Martin and Ringeval define the temperature through $g_{*}^{1/4}T_{\mathrm{reh}}\equiv\left(\dfrac{30}{\pi^{2}}\rho_{\mathrm{reh}}\right)^{1/4},$ (arXiv:1004.5525) [src: Eq. 29]. Ellis et al. identify the end of reheating with $\rho_\varphi=\rho_{\rm R}$, which differs from the start of radiation domination by "a sub-percent correction in $N_{*}$" (arXiv:2510.18656) [src: Sect. "CMB Observables and Reheating", text after Eq. 27].
- Pivot. Planck, Iacconi et al. and Ellis et al. use $k_*=0.05$ Mpc⁻¹ (arXiv:2511.14673) [src: Sect. 2.1, text before Eq. 11]; (arXiv:2510.18656) [src: Sect. "CMB Observables and Reheating", text after Eq. 18]. Liddle and Leach quote $N$ for the present Hubble scale k_hor = a₀H₀ (arXiv:astro-ph/0305263) [src: Sect. II].

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — why reheating enters at all
<!-- key: step-1-why-reheating-enters-at-all -->

The curvature perturbation is conserved on large scales "under very general conditions (including the assumption that inflation proceeds with only one field)", so the spectrum itself is not affected by reheating; what reheating changes is "the relation between the physical scales at present time and during inflation" (Martin & Ringeval 2010, arXiv:1004.5525) [src: Sect. II.1]. In the mode equation written in e-folds, every term but $k/\mathcal{H}$ is fixed by the inflationary background [src: Sect. II.1, text after Eq. 5], and $\frac{k}{{\cal H}}=\frac{\Upsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}}{H(N)}{\rm e}^{N_{{}_{\rm T}}-N},$ [src: Eq. 6] with $\Upsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}\equiv\frac{k}{a_{\rm now}}\left(1+z_{\mathrm{end}}\right),$ [src: Eq. 7] which they rewrite as $\Upsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\frac{k}{a_{\rm now}}\left(\frac{\rho_{\rm end}}{\Omega_{\gamma}\rho_{\rm cri}}\right)^{1/4}R_{\rm rad}^{-1},$ (arXiv:1004.5525) [src: Eq. 8]. The whole reheating uncertainty is then in one number: $\frac{k}{\cal H}=\frac{k}{a_{\rm now}}\left(\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{end}}}{\rho_{\gamma}}\right)^{1/4}\frac{1}{H(N)R_{\mathrm{rad}}}{\rm e}^{N_{{}_{\rm T}}-N},$ (arXiv:1004.5525) [src: Eq. 14], "the theoretical uncertainty in this expression solely comes from the parameter $R_{\mathrm{rad}}$" [src: Sect. II.1, text after Eq. 14].

### Step 2 — the reheating parameter from the fluid
<!-- key: step-2-the-reheating-parameter-from-the-fluid -->

For "a conserved effective fluid" — not a limitation, since for an inflaton coupled to radiation the sum of the two is conserved [src: Sect. II.1, text after Eq. 8] — energy conservation gives Eq. 9 of arXiv:1004.5525, the Eq. 41 of the Encyclopaedia, and then $\ln R_{\mathrm{rad}}=\frac{\Delta N}{4}\left(-1+3\bar{w}_{\mathrm{reh}}\right),$ [src: Eq. 10] and $\ln R_{\rm rad}=\frac{1-3\bar{w}_{\mathrm{reh}}}{12(1+\bar{w}_{\mathrm{reh}})}\ln\left(\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{reh}}}{\rho_{\mathrm{end}}}\right),$ (arXiv:1004.5525) [src: Eq. 13], the forms already in `formalism.md`. Martin, Ringeval and Vennin add that "the above parametrization is the most generic as it remains valid even in presence of any additional entropy production eras that could occur after reheating" (arXiv:1410.7958) [src: text after Eq. 3].

### Step 3 — the exact relation between $R_{\rm rad}$ and the e-folds at exit
<!-- key: step-3-the-exact-relation-between-r-rm-rad-and -->

Expressed at Hubble crossing of the pivot, $\ln R_{\rm rad}=N_{{}_{\rm T}}-N_{*}+N_{0}-\frac{1}{4}\ln\left(\frac{H_{*}^{2}}{M_{{}_{\mathrm{P}l}}^{2}\epsilon_{1*}}\right)+\frac{1}{4}\ln\left(\frac{3}{\epsilon_{1*}}\frac{V_{\rm end}}{V_{*}}\frac{3-\epsilon_{1*}}{3-\epsilon_{1\,\rm end}}\right),$ (arXiv:1004.5525) [src: Eq. 15], with $N_{0}\equiv\ln\left(\dfrac{k/a_{\rm now}}{\rho_{\gamma}^{1/4}}\right).$ [src: Eq. 16]. "Despite the appearance of the first Hubble flow function, this equation is exact", with $\epsilon_{1\rm end}=1$ [src: Sect. II.2, text after Eq. 16]. At leading order $\dfrac{H_{*}^{2}}{M_{{}_{\mathrm{P}l}}^{2}\epsilon_{1*}}=8\pi^{2}P_{*},$ (arXiv:1004.5525) [src: Eq. 17], which turns Eq. 15 into the Encyclopaedia's Eq. 48 (`formalism.md`). In the Encyclopaedia the present radiation density carries the change of relativistic degrees of freedom through $\tilde{\rho}_{\gamma}\equiv\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{reh}}\rho_{\gamma}$ in $1+z_{\mathrm{end}}=\dfrac{1}{R_{\mathrm{rad}}}\left(\dfrac{\rho_{\mathrm{end}}}{\tilde{\rho}_{\gamma}}\right)^{1/4},\qquad\tilde{\rho}_{\gamma}\equiv\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{reh}}\rho_{\gamma}.$ (arXiv:1303.3787) [src: Eq. 47], whose precise value "is unimportant", shifting $\ln R_{\mathrm{rad}}$ by at most "a $\mathcal{O}(1)$ number" [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 47].

### Step 4 — the allowed range of $\Delta N_*$
<!-- key: step-4-the-allowed-range-of-delta-n -->

Requiring $-1/3<\bar{w}_{\rm reh}<1$ (positivity of energy and "the very definition of reheating which is not inflation", conditions on the mean only) and reheating between inflation and BBN, $\rho_{\rm nuc}<\rho_{\rm reh}<\rho_{\rm end}$ with $\rho_{\mathrm{nuc}}\equiv\left(10\mbox{MeV}\right)^{4}.$ (arXiv:1004.5525) [src: Sect. II.2; Eq. 18], gives the two ends of the range:

- $\Delta N_{*}^{\rm nuc}=-N_{0}+\ln\left(\frac{H_{*}}{M_{{}_{\mathrm{P}l}}}\right)-\frac{1}{3(1+\bar{w}_{\mathrm{reh}})}\ln\frac{\rho_{\rm end}}{M_{{}_{\mathrm{P}l}}^{4}}+\frac{1-3\bar{w}_{\mathrm{reh}}}{12(1+\bar{w}_{\mathrm{reh}})}\ln\frac{\rho_{\rm nuc}}{M_{{}_{\mathrm{P}l}}^{4}}\,,$ (arXiv:1004.5525) [src: Eq. 19];
- for instantaneous reheating, $\Delta N_{*}^{\rm end}=-N_{0}+\ln\left(\frac{H_{*}}{M_{{}_{\mathrm{P}l}}}\right)-\frac{1}{4}\ln\frac{\rho_{\rm end}}{M_{{}_{\mathrm{P}l}}^{4}}.$ (arXiv:1004.5525) [src: Sect. II.2, unnumbered display after Eq. 19], which "no longer depends on $\bar{w}_{\mathrm{reh}}$".

"These equations are algebraic for $\Delta N_{*}^{\mathrm{nuc}}$ and $\Delta N_{*}^{\mathrm{end}}$ because $H_{*}$ and $\rho_{\mathrm{end}}$ are also functions of $\Delta N_{*}$" [src: Sect. II.2, text after Eq. 19]. The range is "model-dependent since $\rho_{\mathrm{end}}$ or $V_{\mathrm{end}}/V_{*}$ differ for different inflationary scenarios" (arXiv:1303.3787) [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 50].

### Step 5 — from $\Delta N_*$ to $\phi_*$, then to $\epsilon_{n*}$, $n_s$ and $r$
<!-- key: step-5-from-delta-n-to-phi-then-to-epsilon-n-ns -->

With the slow-roll trajectory written as the primitive $\mathcal{I}(\phi)=\int^{\phi}\dfrac{V(\psi)}{V_{\psi}(\psi)}\mathrm{d}\psi,$ (arXiv:1303.3787) [src: Eq. 37], the field at exit is $\phi_{*}=\mathcal{I}^{-1}\left[\mathcal{I}(\phi_{\mathrm{end}})+M_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{Pl}}}^{2}\Delta N_{*}\right],$ [src: Eq. 39], and Eq. 48 becomes, for each allowed value of $\ln R_{\rm rad}$, an algebraic equation for $\phi_*$: $\frac{1}{M_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{Pl}}}^{2}}\left[\mathcal{I}(\phi_{*})-\mathcal{I}(\phi_{\mathrm{end}})\right]=\ln R_{\mathrm{rad}}-N_{{}_{0}}-\dfrac{1}{4}\ln\left\{\dfrac{9}{\epsilon_{1}(\phi_{*})[3-\epsilon_{1}(\phi_{\mathrm{end}})]}\dfrac{V(\phi_{\mathrm{end}})}{V(\phi_{*})}\right\}+\dfrac{1}{4}\ln(8\pi^{2}P_{*})\,.$ (arXiv:1303.3787) [src: Eq. 51]. "In general, this equation cannot be solved explicitly (except for LFI models ...) and we have to rely on numerical calculations"; solving it over the allowed $\ln R_{\rm rad}$ gives $\phi_*\in[\phi_*^{\rm nuc},\phi_*^{\rm end}]$ "and, therefore, ... the corresponding dispersion in r and $n_{\rm S}$" [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 51]. Inserting $\phi_*$ in the Hubble-flow parameters gives $\epsilon_{n*}$ and so $r$ and $n_{\rm S}$ [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 40]; the second-order formulas are in `deep/slow-roll-predictions.md`. The parametrisation "is independent on the microphysics of reheating", and preheating effects on the background "are already taken into account" [src: Sect. 3.2, last paragraphs].

### Step 6 — a worked case: large-field models
<!-- key: step-6-a-worked-case-large-field-models -->

For $V(\phi)=M^{4}\left(\frac{\phi}{M_{{}_{\mathrm{P}l}}}\right)^{p},$ (arXiv:1004.5525) [src: Eq. 21], with $\phi_{\mathrm{end}}/M_{{}_{\mathrm{P}l}}=p/\sqrt{2}$ [src: Sect. II.3, inline, text after Eq. 21], the trajectory is explicit, $\phi_{*}^{2}=2pM_{{}_{\mathrm{P}l}}^{2}\Delta N_{*}+\phi_{\mathrm{end}}^{2}.$ [src: Eq. 22], and the oscillation phase fixes $\bar{w}_{\mathrm{reh}}=\dfrac{p-2}{p+2}\,.$ [src: Eq. 23], derived without the inflaton–radiation coupling but "a very good approximation" (App. A) [src: Sect. II.3, text after Eq. 23]. The two algebraic equations of Step 4 then have closed solutions in the Lambert function, $\Delta N_{*}^{\rm nuc}=-\frac{p}{4}-\frac{p^{2}-2p+4}{12p}\mathrm{W}_{0}\biggl\{-\frac{12p}{p^{2}-2p+4}\times\exp\left[-\frac{12p\left({\cal N}^{\rm nuc}+p/4\right)}{p^{2}-2p+4}\right]\biggr\},$ [src: Eq. 24] and $\Delta N_{*}^{\rm end}=-\frac{p}{4}-\frac{p-2}{8}\mathrm{W}_{0}\Biggl\{-\frac{8}{p-2}\times\exp\left[-\frac{8\left({\cal N}^{\rm end}+p/4\right)}{p-2}\right]\Biggr\},$ [src: Eq. 25] (arXiv:1004.5525), with $\mathcal{N}^{\mathrm{end}}=-N_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(2\pi\sqrt{120}\frac{Q_{\mathrm{rms-PS}}}{T}\right)-\frac{1}{4}\ln\left(9\frac{2^{1-p}}{p^{1-p/2}}\right).$ (arXiv:1004.5525) [src: Eq. 28] and $\mathcal{N}^{\rm nuc}$ of their Eq. 26, whose last term vanishes for p = 4, since a quartic oscillation phase is radiation-like [src: Sect. II.3, text after Eq. 27]. "The range of allowed values of $\Delta N_{*}$ strongly depends on p", and the WMAP7 contours then give "a lower bound on the reheating temperature" for these models [src: Sect. II.3, text after Eq. 29].

### Step 7 — the same chain with a constant $w_{\rm re}$ and $T_{\rm re}$ (Dai, Kamionkowski & Wang)
<!-- key: step-7-the-same-chain-with-a-constant-w-rm-re -->

Dai et al. match scales and energy densities separately. Scale matching: $\frac{k}{a_{0}H_{0}}=\frac{a_{k}}{a_{\rm end}}\frac{a_{\rm end}}{a_{\rm re}}\frac{a_{\rm re}}{a_{\rm eq}}\frac{a_{\rm eq}H_{\rm eq}}{a_{0}H_{0}}\frac{H_{k}}{H_{\rm eq}}.$ [src: Eq. 5], so that $\ln\frac{k}{a_{0}H_{0}}=-N_{k}-N_{\rm re}-N_{\rm RD}+\ln\frac{a_{\rm eq}H_{\rm eq}}{a_{0}H_{0}}+\ln\frac{H_{k}}{H_{\rm eq}}.$ (arXiv:1404.6704) [src: Eq. 6]. Energy matching, for constant $w_{\rm re}$: $N_{\rm re}=[3(1+w_{\rm re})]^{-1}\ln\left(\rho_{\rm end}/\rho_{\rm re}\right),$ [src: Eq. 7] with $\rho_{\rm re}=(\pi^{2}/30)g_{\rm re}T^{4}_{\rm re}$ [src: inline, text after Eq. 7] and entropy conservation after reheating, $\frac{T_{\rm re}}{T_{0}}=\left(\frac{43}{11g_{s,\rm re}}\right)^{1/3}\frac{a_{0}}{a_{\rm eq}}\frac{a_{\rm eq}}{a_{\rm re}}.$ (arXiv:1404.6704) [src: Eq. 9], "under this assumption" that "no immense entropy production take place after $T_{\rm re}$" [src: text after Eq. 7]. Eliminating $N_{\rm RD}$ gives the duration of reheating for $V\propto\phi^\alpha$, $N_{\rm re}=\frac{4}{1-3w_{\rm re}}\left[-N_{k}-\ln\frac{k}{a_{0}T_{0}}-\frac{1}{4}\ln\frac{30}{g_{\rm re}\pi^{2}}\right.\left.-\frac{1}{3}\ln\frac{11g_{s,\rm re}}{43}+\frac{1}{4}\ln\frac{\pi^{2}rA_{s}}{6}-\frac{\alpha}{8}\ln\frac{r}{16\epsilon_{0}}-\frac{\ln(1+\lambda)}{4}\right].$ (arXiv:1404.6704) [src: unnumbered display after Eq. 10], with $N_k$ and $r$ fixed by $n_s$ through $n_{s}-1=-(2+\alpha)/(2N),\quad r=4\alpha/N.$ [src: Eq. 4]. The expression "depends linearly on $n_{s}-1$ through $N_{k}$, and is sensitive to $w_{\rm re}$", while the dependence on $\epsilon_0$, $g_{\rm re}$, $g_{s,\rm re}$, $r$ and $A_s$ is only logarithmic [src: text after the unnumbered display].

`[der]` This route inverts the Encyclopaedia's: instead of propagating an assumed reheating into the spectral index, it reads the reheating duration and temperature off a measured index for a chosen potential and equation of state.

### Step 8 — the same chain for T-models (Iacconi et al. 2025)
<!-- key: step-8-the-same-chain-for-t-models-iacconi-et -->

Iacconi et al. write $\begin{split}\Delta N_{\text{CMB}}&\equiv N_{\text{end}}-N_{\text{CMB}}\\ &\simeq 60.9+\frac{1}{4}\ln{\left(\frac{V_{\text{CMB}}^{2}}{\rho_{\text{end}}M_{\text{Pl}}^{4}}\right)}-\frac{1-3\bar{w}}{4}\Delta N_{\text{rh}}\;.\end{split}$ (arXiv:2511.14673) [src: Eq. 11], with $g_{\star,T_{\rm rh}}=106.75$, the constant becoming 61.1 for $g_{\star}=10.75$ [src: Sect. 2.1, footnote 1], and $\Delta N_{\text{rh}}\equiv\frac{1}{3(1+\bar{w})}\log{\left(\frac{\rho_{\text{end}}}{\rho_{\text{rh}}}\right)}\;.$ [src: Eq. 17]. They tie the equation of state to the potential near its minimum, $\bar{w}\approx\frac{p-2}{p+2}\;.$ [src: Eq. 19], so that "the choice of inflationary potential determines the reheating equation-of-state parameter, and the two should not be treated as independent" [src: Sect. 2.2.1, text after Eq. 19]. The procedure: solve Eq. 11 iteratively with $\Delta N_{\rm rh}=0$ and the CMB-normalised $V_0$ to get the instantaneous-reheating value, then scan $0\leq\Delta N_{\text{rh}}\leq\Delta N_{\text{rh, max}}$, and insert each $\Delta N_{\rm CMB}$ into $n_{s}\approx 1-\frac{2\Delta N_{\text{CMB}}+\frac{1}{p}\sqrt{3\alpha(3\alpha+p^{2})}+\frac{3\alpha}{2}}{\Delta N_{\text{CMB}}^{2}+\frac{\Delta N_{\text{CMB}}}{p}\sqrt{3\alpha(3\alpha+p^{2})}+\frac{3\alpha}{4}}\;,$ [src: Eq. 29] and $r\approx\frac{12\alpha}{\Delta N_{\text{CMB}}^{2}+\frac{\Delta N_{\text{CMB}}}{p}\sqrt{3\alpha(3\alpha+p^{2})}+\frac{3\alpha}{4}}\;.$ [src: Eq. 30] (arXiv:2511.14673) [src: Sect. 3.1]. The upper bounds on $\Delta N_{\rm rh}$ come from $T_{\rm rh}\geq 1$ TeV (to allow baryogenesis) or reheating before BBN, and, for $\bar w>1/3$, from the $\Delta N_{\rm eff}$ bound on the blue-tilted gravitational waves that re-enter during reheating [src: Sect. 2.2.2]. In their words, T-models "yield predictions for $n_{s}$ closer to unity when reheating has a stiff equation of state, $\bar{w}>1/3$, and lasts sufficiently long", because "reheating increases the number of e-folds" from pivot exit to the end of inflation [src: Sect. 1].

### Step 9 — the Planck and Ellis et al. form with a temperature
<!-- key: step-9-the-planck-and-ellis-et-al-form-with-a -->

Planck 2018 X writes the same relation with $w_{\rm int}$, $\rho_{\rm th}$ and $g_{\rm th}$ (Eq. 47, `formalism.md`). Ellis et al. print it with the constants separated, $N_{*}=\ln\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{30}\right)^{1/4}\left(\frac{43}{11}\right)^{1/3}\frac{T_{0}}{H_{0}}\right]-\ln\left(\frac{k_{*}}{a_{0}H_{0}}\right)-\frac{1}{12}\ln g_{\mathrm{RH}}+\frac{1}{4}\ln\left(\frac{V_{*}^{2}}{M_{P}^{4}\rho_{\mathrm{end}}}\right)+\frac{1-3w_{\mathrm{int}}}{12(1+w_{\mathrm{int}})}\ln\left(\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{rad}}}{\rho_{\mathrm{end}}}\right)\,,$ (arXiv:2510.18656) [src: Eq. 27], "assuming no additional entropy production between the end of reheating and horizon re-entry"; for $k_*=0.05$ Mpc⁻¹ the first two terms give $N_{*}\;=\;61.49-\frac{1}{12}\ln g_{\mathrm{RH}}+\Delta N_{\mathrm{inf}}+\Delta N_{\mathrm{rad}}\,,$ [src: Eq. 29], and with MSSM degrees of freedom $N_{*}^{\mathrm{MSSM}}=61.04+\cdots\,.$ [src: Eq. 30]. The temperature enters through $\rho_{\rm RH}=(g_{\rm RH}\pi^{2}/30)T_{\rm RH}^{4}$ (arXiv:2510.18656) [src: Sect. "CMB Observables and Reheating", inline, text after Eq. 27].

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **Bayesian: $R_{\rm rad}$ or $R_{\rm reh}$ as a parameter.** Martin and Ringeval treat $R_{\mathrm{rad}}$ "as an observable model parameter ... as it should be from a Bayesian point of view" and find it constrained by WMAP7 (arXiv:1004.5525) [src: Sect. II.2, last paragraph]. Martin, Ringeval and Vennin prefer the rescaled $R_{\mathrm{reh}}\equiv R_{\mathrm{rad}}\dfrac{\rho_{\mathrm{end}}^{1/4}}{M_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{Pl}}}}$ (arXiv:1410.7958) [src: Eq. 4], in one-to-one correspondence with $R_{\rm rad}$ within a model, because it "minimizes degeneracies in parameter space", with a uniform prior on $\ln R_{\rm reh}$ in $\ln\left(\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{nuc}}^{1/4}}{M_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{Pl}}}}\right)<\ln R_{\mathrm{reh}}<-\frac{1}{3}\ln\left(\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{nuc}}^{1/4}}{M_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{Pl}}}}\right)+\frac{4}{3}\ln\left(\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{end}}^{1/4}}{M_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{Pl}}}}\right).$ (arXiv:1410.7958) [src: Eq. 5]. Cost: one nested-sampling analysis per model; they ran about 200 [src: abstract].
- **Fixed $w_{\rm re}$ scan.** Muñoz and Kamionkowski restrict $w_{\rm re}$ to a "finite range of reasonable values" and find, for natural and Higgs-like inflation, that the lower limit on r derived from $n_s$ "is about 20%-25% higher (depending on the model) with this procedure than with the usual approach" (arXiv:1412.0656) [src: abstract]. Dai et al. note that numerical studies of thermalisation "suggest a range of variation 0 ≲ w_re ≲ 0.25" and that w_re > 1/3 "is difficult to conceive since it requires a potential dominated by high-dimension operators (higher than φ⁶) near its minimum" (arXiv:1404.6704) [src: Introduction, second paragraph].
- **Potential-fixed $\bar w$.** Iacconi et al. take $\bar w$ from the shape of the minimum (Eq. 19 above) and leave only $\Delta N_{\rm rh}$ free (arXiv:2511.14673) [src: Sect. 2.2]; Martin and Ringeval do the same for large-field models (Eq. 23 of arXiv:1004.5525).
- **A fixed window.** The usual alternative, a fixed $N_*$ range, is what the Encyclopaedia and Martin and Ringeval argue against (see "What goes wrong"); Planck 2018 X uses a fixed range for its $(n_s,r)$ figure and marginalises over reheating for the model comparison (`approximations.md#the-number-of-e-folds-at-the-pivot`; `results.md#reheating-constraints`).
- **Lattice equation of state.** Where preheating is non-perturbative, the e-fold average must come from simulations; see `results.md#reheating-constraints` for the non-monotonic $\bar w$ found by Antusch et al.

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **A fixed window can be inconsistent.** Assuming "blindly say $\Delta N_{*}\in[40,60]$, would lead to inconsistent reheating energy densities, either larger than $\rho_{\mathrm{end}}$ or smaller than $\rho_{\mathrm{nuc}}$" (arXiv:1303.3787) [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 51]. Martin and Ringeval: "Without such an assumption, from one model to another, an assumed value of $N_{*}$ may inconsistently imply that the reheating occurs after nucleosynthesis, or even at energy densities higher than $\rho_{\mathrm{end}}$" (arXiv:1004.5525) [src: Sect. II.2, text after Eq. 17]. Check: solve Eq. 51 of arXiv:1303.3787 over the allowed $\ln R_{\rm rad}$.
- **The drop of energy before the end of inflation is not always small.** Liddle and Leach warn that setting V_k = ρ_end "is not the correct way to maximize" their Eq. 6 (arXiv:astro-ph/0305263) [src: Sect. II.1]; "reducing the energy density during inflation wins" [src: Sect. II.4], and counting $\tilde{N}(k)=N(k)+\ln\frac{H_{{\rm end}}}{H_{k}}=N(k)-\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{V_{k}}{\rho_{{\rm end}}}\,.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0305263) [src: Eq. 17] removes the issue.
- **The equation of state is not constant.** "Of course, w is not constant during reheating"; it grows from −1 to 1/3 with the oscillations imprinted, and the fitted $w_{\rm fit}(\Delta N_{\rm rh})$ approaches $\bar w$ of Eq. 19 only for $\Delta N_{\rm rh}\gg 1$ (arXiv:2511.14673) [src: Sect. 2.2.1]. Lattice histories can be non-monotonic (`results.md#reheating-constraints`). Iacconi et al. model perturbative reheating only, "as modelling non-perturbative dynamics is beyond the scope of this work" [src: Sect. 2.2].
- **Entropy production after reheating.** Dai et al. and Ellis et al. assume none (arXiv:1404.6704) [src: text after Eq. 7]; (arXiv:2510.18656) [src: Sect. "CMB Observables and Reheating", text before Eq. 27]. The Encyclopaedia notes that the range of $\overline{w}_{\rm reh}$ "might be extended to smaller values if one allows a phase of acceleration to take place at lower energy than $\rho_{\mathrm{end}}$, such as in thermal or multistage inflation" (arXiv:1303.3787) [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 50]; Liddle and Leach treat early matter domination and thermal inflation in their Sect. III (not read).
- **Multi-field.** The amplitude formula behind the e-fold estimate "assumes that there is only one dynamically-important field during inflation, and may be modified if multi-field effects are important" (arXiv:astro-ph/0305263) [src: Sect. II.1]; Step 1 also rests on the single-field conservation of ζ (arXiv:1004.5525) [src: Sect. II.1].
- **Frames.** For $R^2$ inflation the reheating parametrisation must be reconsidered in the Einstein frame (`approximations.md#effective-field-theory-and-frames`).
- **Printed slips** (copied as printed, not corrected):
  - Iacconi et al. state, after their Eq. 19, that "depending on whether $\bar{w}$ is stiff or not, reheating contributes negatively ($p\geq 6\to\bar{w}>1/3$) or positively ($p<4\to\bar{w}<1/3$) to $\Delta N_{\text{CMB}}$, thereby decreasing or increasing its value" (arXiv:2511.14673) [src: Sect. 2.2.1, text after Eq. 19]. The term $-\frac{1-3\bar w}{4}\Delta N_{\rm rh}$ of their Eq. 11 and their Introduction, which says stiff reheating "increases the number of e-folds" [src: Sect. 1], give the opposite assignment; the next sentence of Sect. 2.2.1 ("Stiff reheating will therefore bring the scalar spectral tilt closer to unity") agrees with Eq. 11.
  - Martin and Ringeval print the last term of $\mathcal{N}^{\rm nuc}$ as $-\frac{p-4}{3p}\ln\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{nuc}}}{M_{{}_{\mathrm{P}l}}}$ (arXiv:1004.5525) [src: Eq. 26], with the first power of the Planck mass, where their Eq. 19 writes $\ln\frac{\rho_{\rm nuc}}{M_{{}_{\mathrm{P}l}}^{4}}$.
