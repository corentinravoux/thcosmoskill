## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- Gordon, Wands, Bassett and Maartens keep Newton's constant explicit and write the scalar metric perturbations A, B, ψ, E; their comoving curvature perturbation is $\mathcal{R}$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0009131) [src: Sect. II.3, Eq. 37]. Wands, Bartolo, Matarrese and Riotto use the same $\mathcal{R}$ and the full Planck mass, ${\cal P}_{\cal R}|_{*}\simeq{\cal P}_{\cal S}|_{*}\simeq\left({H^{2}\over 2\pi\dot{\sigma}}\right)^{2}_{*}\simeq{8\over 3\epsilon}{V_{*}\over M_{P}^{4}}\,.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0205253) [src: Eq. 27], and their slow-roll parameters carry $1/16\pi G$ and $1/8\pi G$ (Eqs. 12–13).
- Normalisation of the entropy perturbation. During inflation Wands et al. define $\mathcal{S}$ so that curvature and isocurvature have equal power at horizon crossing; "A different choice for the normalisation of ${\cal S}$ would lead to a different overall factor multiplying the transfer functions" (arXiv:astro-ph/0205253) [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 4]. After inflation $\mathcal{S}$ is the matter–photon number-density perturbation of their Eq. 2.
- Sign of the curvature perturbation. Lyth, Malik and Sasaki define $-\zeta(x^{i})={\cal R}_{c}(x^{i})=\psi(t,x^{i})+\frac{\delta\rho(t,x^{i})}{3(\rho+P)}\,.$ in the linear limit (arXiv:astro-ph/0411220) [src: Eq. 38]: their ζ has the opposite sign of $\mathcal{R}_c$.
- Turn rate. Gordon et al. and Achúcarro et al. 2012 write the turn as the angular velocity $\dot\theta$ of the trajectory (arXiv:astro-ph/0009131, Eq. 46; arXiv:1201.6342, Eq. 2.17); Achúcarro et al. 2011 (`formalism.md`) and Gonzalez Quaglia et al. write the dimensionless $\eta_\perp$ (arXiv:2507.03610, Eq. 43). The sections read do not print the relation between the two; none is written here.
- Field space. Sasaki and Stewart allow a field-space metric $h_{ab}$ and its Riemann tensor (arXiv:astro-ph/9507001) [src: Eqs. 37, 40]; Gordon et al. and Wands et al. use two canonical fields.
- Correlation angle. Wands et al. define $\cos\Delta$ from the curvature–isocurvature cross-spectrum (Eq. 35 below); Planck 2018 X writes $\cos\Delta={\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{RI}}}/{\left(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{RR}}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{II}}\right)^{1/2}}$ for CDI, NDI or NVI modes (arXiv:1807.06211) [src: Sect. 9.1, inline]. The sections read do not relate their sign conventions for the isocurvature mode.

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — rotate to adiabatic and entropy fields
<!-- key: step-1-rotate-to-adiabatic-and-entropy-fields -->

For two fields φ and χ, Gordon et al. define the adiabatic field as the path length along the classical trajectory, $\dot{\sigma}=(\cos\theta)\dot{\phi}+(\sin\theta)\dot{\chi}\,,$ [src: Eq. 31] with $\cos\theta=\frac{\dot{\phi}}{\sqrt{\dot{\phi}^{2}+\dot{\chi}^{2}}},\quad\sin\theta=\frac{\dot{\chi}}{\sqrt{\dot{\phi}^{2}+\dot{\chi}^{2}}}\,.$ [src: Eq. 32], so that $\ddot{\sigma}+3H\dot{\sigma}+V_{\sigma}=0\,,$ [src: Eq. 33], and the entropy field orthogonal to it, $\delta s=(\cos\theta)\delta\chi-(\sin\theta)\delta\phi\,.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0009131) [src: Eq. 35]. Because s is constant along the classical trajectory, "entropy perturbations are automatically gauge-invariant" [src: Sect. II.3, text after Eq. 35]. The trajectory turns at the rate $\dot{\theta}=-\frac{V_{s}}{\dot{\sigma}}\,,$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0009131) [src: Eq. 46], with $V_{s}=(\cos\theta)V_{\chi}-(\sin\theta)V_{\phi}\,.$ [src: Eq. 41]. The analysis "should be straightforward to extend to include additional scalar fields, but we do not expect to see any qualitatively new features" [src: Sect. II.3, first paragraph].

### Step 2 — the curvature perturbation is the adiabatic projection
<!-- key: step-2-the-curvature-perturbation-is-the -->

The comoving curvature perturbation depends only on δσ: ${\cal R}=\psi+H\left(\frac{\dot{\phi}\delta\phi+\dot{\chi}\delta\chi}{\dot{\phi}^{2}+\dot{\chi}^{2}}\right)\,,=\psi+\frac{H}{\dot{\sigma}}\delta\sigma\,.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0009131) [src: Eq. 37], identical in form to the single-field expression [src: Sect. II.3, text after Eq. 37]. Written per field, ${\cal R}=(\cos^{2}\theta){\cal R}_{\phi}+(\sin^{2}\theta){\cal R}_{\chi}\,,$ [src: Eq. 38], but "even fields with no explicit interaction will in general have non-zero intrinsic entropy perturbations on large scales in a multi-field system due to their gravitational interaction, so that ${\cal R}_{I}$ for each field is not conserved" [src: Sect. II.3, text after Eq. 39].

### Step 3 — turns source the curvature perturbation
<!-- key: step-3-turns-source-the-curvature-perturbation -->

The evolution of $\mathcal{R}$ acquires a source proportional to the entropy perturbation, $\dot{{\cal R}}=\frac{H}{\dot{H}}\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}\Psi+\frac{2H}{\dot{\sigma}}\dot{\theta}\delta s\,,$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0009131) [src: Eq. 45]: "there can be significant changes to ${\cal R}$ on large scales if the entropy perturbation is not suppressed and if the background solution follows a curved trajectory, i.e., $\dot{\theta}\neq 0$", even as k → 0 [src: Sect. II.3, text after Eq. 46].

### Step 4 — the entropy perturbation evolves on its own on large scales
<!-- key: step-4-the-entropy-perturbation-evolves-on-its -->

Using the constraint equations, the entropy equation becomes $\ddot{\delta s}+3H\dot{\delta s}+\left(\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}+V_{ss}+3\dot{\theta}^{2}\right)\delta s={\dot{\theta}\over\dot{\sigma}}{k^{2}\over 2\pi Ga^{2}}\Psi\,.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0009131) [src: Eq. 52], with $V_{ss}=(\sin^{2}\theta)V_{\phi\phi}-(\sin 2\theta)V_{\phi\chi}+(\cos^{2}\theta)V_{\chi\chi}\,.$ [src: Eq. 50]. It "is automatically gauge-invariant and holds in any gauge. On large scales the inhomogeneous source term becomes negligible", so the entropy perturbation decouples from the adiabatic and metric perturbations, and "If the initial entropy perturbation is zero on large scales, it will remain so" [src: Sect. II.3, text after Eq. 52]. The abstract states the asymmetry: entropy perturbations can source adiabatic ones on a curved trajectory, while "adiabatic perturbations cannot source entropy perturbations in the long-wavelength limit", and "It is the effective mass of the entropy field that determines the amplitude of entropy perturbations during inflation" (arXiv:astro-ph/0009131) [src: abstract].

The adiabatic perturbation in the spatially flat gauge obeys $\ddot{Q}_{\sigma}+3H\dot{Q}_{\sigma}+\left[\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}+V_{\sigma\sigma}-\dot{\theta}^{2}-{8\pi G\over a^{3}}\left({a^{3}\dot{\sigma}^{2}\over H}\right)^{\displaystyle{\cdot}}\right]Q_{\sigma}{}=2(\dot{\theta}\delta s)^{\displaystyle{\cdot}}-2\left({V_{\sigma}\over\dot{\sigma}}+{\dot{H}\over H}\right)\dot{\theta}\delta s\,.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0009131) [src: Eq. 55]. When $\dot\theta=0$ the two decouple: δσ obeys the single-field equation and δs that of a scalar field in an unperturbed FRW spacetime [src: Sect. II.3, text after Eq. 50].

The same structure in the turn-rate notation: on super-horizon scales $\dot{{\cal R}}=2H\eta_{\perp}{\cal S}+\frac{C(k)}{a^{3}\epsilon}\,,$ [src: Eq. 43] and $\ddot{\cal S}+(3+\eta_{H})H\dot{\cal S}+m_{\mathcal{S},{\rm eff}}^{2}{\cal S}={\cal O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}\right)\,$ [src: Eq. 44] with $m_{\mathcal{S},{\rm eff}}^{2}\equiv m_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}+4H^{2}\eta_{\perp}^{2}\,.$ (Gonzalez Quaglia et al. 2025, arXiv:2507.03610) [src: Eq. 45]; "no assumption on ε is made", so the equations "are still valid for ε > 1", after the end of inflation [src: Sect. 3.2, last paragraph].

### Step 5 — transfer functions from Hubble exit to the radiation era
<!-- key: step-5-transfer-functions-from-hubble-exit-to -->

On large scales the general form of the evolution is $\dot{{\cal R}}=\alpha H{\cal S}\,,\,\,\,\,\quad\dot{{\cal S}}=\beta H{\cal S}\,,$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0205253) [src: Eq. 5], which integrates to the transfer matrix $\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{{\cal R}}\\ {{\cal S}}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&{T}_{{\cal R}{\cal S}}\\ 0&{T}_{{\cal S}{\cal S}}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\cal R}\\ {\cal S}\end{array}\right)_{*}\,,$ [src: Eq. 6] with $T_{{\cal R}{\cal S}}(t_{*},t)=\int^{t}_{t_{*}}\alpha(t^{\prime})T_{{\cal S}{\cal S}}(t_{*},t^{\prime})H(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\,,T_{{\cal S}{\cal S}}(t_{*},t)=\exp\left(\int^{t}_{t_{*}}\beta(t^{\prime})H(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\right)\,.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0205253) [src: Eq. 7]. The zero in the lower-left corner is the statement that adiabatic perturbations cannot generate entropy perturbations on large scales [src: Sect. II, text before Eq. 5]. At Hubble exit, in slow roll, $\alpha_{*}\simeq-2\eta_{\sigma s}\,,\beta_{*}\simeq-2\epsilon+\eta_{\sigma\sigma}-\eta_{ss}\,.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0205253) [src: Eq. 23], with the rotated mass-matrix parameters $\eta_{\sigma\sigma}=\eta_{\phi\phi}\cos^{2}\theta+2\eta_{\phi\chi}\cos\theta\sin\theta+\eta_{\chi\chi}\sin^{2}\theta\,,\eta_{\sigma s}=(\eta_{\chi\chi}-\eta_{\phi\phi})\sin\theta\cos\theta+\eta_{\phi\chi}(\cos^{2}\theta-\sin^{2}\theta)\,,\eta_{ss}=\eta_{\phi\phi}\sin^{2}\theta-2\eta_{\phi\chi}\sin\theta\cos\theta+\eta_{\chi\chi}\cos^{2}\theta\,.\quad$ [src: Eq. 19].

The final spectra are ${\cal P}_{{\cal R}}=\left(1+T_{{\cal R}{\cal S}}^{2}\right){\cal P}_{\cal R}|_{*}\,,{\cal P}_{{\cal S}}=T_{{\cal S}{\cal S}}^{2}{\cal P}_{\cal R}|_{*}\,,{\cal C}_{{\cal R}{\cal S}}=T_{{\cal R}{\cal S}}T_{{\cal S}{\cal S}}{\cal P}_{\cal R}|_{*}\,.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0205253) [src: Eqs. 32–34], with correlation angle $\cos\Delta\equiv{{\cal C}_{{\cal R}{\cal S}}\over{\cal P}_{{\cal R}}^{1/2}{\cal P}_{{\cal S}}^{1/2}}\simeq{T_{{\cal R}{\cal S}}\over\sqrt{1+T_{{\cal R}{\cal S}}^{2}}}\,.$ [src: Eq. 35]. The tilts follow from the slow-roll parameters at exit and Δ alone: $n_{{\cal R}}\simeq-(6-4\cos^{2}\Delta)\epsilon+2\left(\eta_{\sigma\sigma}\sin^{2}\Delta+2\eta_{\sigma s}\sin\Delta\cos\Delta+\eta_{ss}\cos^{2}\Delta\right)\,,n_{\cal S}\simeq-2\epsilon+2\eta_{ss}\,,n_{\cal C}\simeq-2\epsilon+2\eta_{ss}+2\eta_{\sigma s}\tan\Delta\,.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0205253) [src: Eq. 40], where $n_{\cal R}$ is $n_s-1$ in their notation [src: Sect. V, footnote 3]. "Although the overall amplitude of the transfer functions ... are dependent upon the evolution after Hubble-exit, through reheating and into the radiation dominated era, the spectral tilts ... can be expressed solely in terms of the slow-roll parameters at Hubble-crossing during inflation and the correlation angle Δ" [src: Sect. V, last paragraph]. Tensors stay frozen, so the single-field consistency relation becomes ${{\cal P}_{T}\over{\cal P}_{\cal R}}\simeq-8n_{T}\sin^{2}\Delta\,.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0205253) [src: Eq. 38], which "Any two-field model of slow-roll inflation predicts", "not modified by any linear processes the matter perturbations may undergo between the end of inflation and some later time" [src: Sect. V, text after Eq. 38].

### Step 6 — the δN route
<!-- key: step-6-the-deltan-route -->

Instead of following the perturbations, Sasaki and Stewart evaluate the curvature perturbation at late time as the perturbation of the e-fold number from an initial flat slice, ${\cal R}_{\bf k}(t_{2})=\frac{\partial N}{\partial\phi^{a}}\delta\phi_{\bf k}^{a}(t_{1})\,.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/9507001) [src: Eq. 26], so that, with field fluctuations of amplitude H/2π at exit, $P_{\cal R}=\left(\frac{H}{2\pi}\right)^{2}h^{ab}\frac{\partial N}{\partial\phi^{a}}\frac{\partial N}{\partial\phi^{b}}\,,$ [src: Eq. 40], and the spectral index, including the field-space curvature, is $n_{\cal R}-1=2\frac{\dot{H}}{H^{2}}-2\frac{1+\frac{\partial N}{\partial\phi^{a}}\left(\frac{1}{3}R^{abcd}\frac{V_{;b}V_{;c}}{V^{2}}-\frac{V^{;ad}}{V}\right)\frac{\partial N}{\partial\phi^{d}}}{h^{ef}\frac{\partial N}{\partial\phi^{e}}\frac{\partial N}{\partial\phi^{f}}}\,,=\frac{\left[2{(\ln V)^{;a}}_{;b}+\left(\frac{2}{3}{R^{a}}_{cbd}-{h^{a}}_{b}h_{cd}\right)(\ln V)^{;c}(\ln V)^{;d}\right]N_{;a}N^{;b}}{N_{;e}N^{;e}}\,.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/9507001) [src: Eqs. 42–43], under the slow-roll conditions of their Eqs. 20 and 24 [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 29]. Their abstract states the motivation: the standard calculation "assumes that there is only one real dynamical degree of freedom during inflation. However, there is no reason to believe that this is actually the case" [src: abstract].

Lyth, Malik and Sasaki give the non-linear version on large scales. With $N(t_{2},t_{1};x^{i})\equiv\frac{1}{3}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\theta\,{\cal{N}}dt=-\frac{1}{3}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}dt\left.\frac{\dot{\rho}}{\rho+P}\right|_{x^{i}}\,,$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0411220) [src: Eq. 32], a definition "purely geometrical, independent of the gravitational theory", the curvature perturbation on a uniform-density slice that starts flat is $\psi_{A}(t_{2},x^{i})=N_{A}(t_{2},t_{1};x^{i})-N_{0}(t_{2},t_{1})=\Delta N_{F}(t_{2},t_{1};x^{i})\,,$ [src: Eq. 35], "a non-linear version of the ΔN formula that generalises the first-order result of Sasaki and Stewart" [src: Sect. IV, text after Eq. 35]. For $P=P(\rho)$ the quantity $-\zeta(x^{i})\equiv\psi(t,x^{i})+\frac{1}{3}\int_{\rho(t)}^{\rho(t,x^{i})}\frac{d\rho}{\rho+P}\,.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0411220) [src: Eq. 37] is conserved, independent of the slicing: "the curvature perturbation is conserved provided that the pressure is a unique function of the energy density" [src: abstract].

`[der]` In δN, the isocurvature transfer of Step 5 is carried by the dependence of N on the field directions orthogonal to the trajectory, evaluated up to a final uniform-density slice after which the pressure is a function of the density alone.

### Step 7 — integrating out a heavy entropic direction
<!-- key: step-7-integrating-out-a-heavy-entropic -->

When the entropic direction is heavy, Achúcarro et al. integrate it out. With $M_{\rm eff}^{2}\equiv V_{NN}-\dot{\theta}^{2}\,,\qquad V_{NN}\equiv N^{a}N^{b}V_{ab}\,,\qquad V_{NNN}\equiv N^{a}N^{b}N^{c}V_{abc}\,.$ (arXiv:1201.6342) [src: Eq. 2.17], the adiabatic mode acquires a reduced speed of sound, $\frac{\Delta}{a^{2}}\chi=\left(1+\frac{4\dot{\theta}^{2}}{M_{\rm eff}^{2}}\right)\epsilon\dot{\cal R}\equiv\frac{\epsilon}{c_{s}^{2}}\dot{\cal R}\,,$ (arXiv:1201.6342) [src: Eq. 4.4], "where we have identified $c_{s}^{-2}=1+4\dot{\theta}^{2}/M_{\rm eff}^{2}$" [src: Sect. 4.1, inline, text after Eq. 4.4]. The effective theory needs the adiabaticity conditions $\big|\ddot{\cal F}_{\pi}\big|\ll M_{\rm eff}^{2}\left|{\cal F}_{\pi}\right|\,,\qquad\left|\Delta{\cal F}_{\pi}\right|\ll M_{\rm eff}^{2}\left|{\cal F}_{\pi}\right|\,.$ [src: Eq. 2.22] (on the Minkowski toy model), which restrict the rate of change of the turn, $\left|\frac{d}{dt}\log\dot{\theta}\right|\ll M_{\rm eff}\,,$ (arXiv:1201.6342) [src: Eq. 5.5]. For "strong" turns with a slowly varying reduced $c_s$, "the EFT remains valid and accurate as long as $M_{\rm eff}^{2}\gg H^{2}$"; "sudden" turns with a fast-changing $c_s$ can render it invalid in particular limits, though the generalised conditions "are in fact less restrictive than has been previously assumed" [src: Sect. 6]. All couplings of the resulting theory "can be written entirely in terms of the reduced speed of sound" [src: abstract].

### Step 8 — when a multi-field model gives single-field predictions
<!-- key: step-8-when-a-multi-field-model-gives-single -->

The sources read give these conditions, each with its limit:

- **No sourcing.** $\mathcal{R}$ is conserved on large scales if δs is suppressed or the trajectory does not turn (arXiv:astro-ph/0009131) [src: Sect. II.3, text after Eq. 46]. Slow roll alone does not ensure it: in slow roll $\dot{\theta}\simeq 0$ instantaneously, "But the integrated change in θ cannot in general be neglected. Even working within the slow-roll approximation, fields do not in general follow a straight line trajectory in field space" [src: Sect. II.3, footnote 2].
- **Heavy entropic mode.** The amplitude of the entropy perturbation is set by its effective mass (arXiv:astro-ph/0009131) [src: abstract]; with a heavy entropic mass the light physics is a single-field EFT, but with $c_{s}^{-2}=1+4\dot{\theta}^{2}/M_{\rm eff}^{2}$ (arXiv:1201.6342) [src: Sect. 4.1, inline, text after Eq. 4.4], not the canonical single-field theory [src: Sect. 6].
- **Decoupling at Hubble exit is not enough.** Models with $\eta_{\sigma s}\approx 0$ have curvature and isocurvature decoupled at exit; this "includes models in which only one scalar field evolves during inflation, but where both fields play a significant dynamical role during reheating or afterwards" (arXiv:astro-ph/0205253) [src: Sect. VI, text after Eq. 41]. Gonzalez Quaglia et al. show that "multi-field inflationary models with negligible turning in field space during inflation can lead to an effective sourcing of adiabatic from entropic perturbations after the end of inflation" (arXiv:2507.03610) [src: abstract]; in their α-attractor-plus-axion models $n_s$ "remains the same as in the single-field α-attractors, whereas the tensor-to-scalar ratio r decreases by a factor inversely proportional to the enhancement", and "including the post-inflationary evolution is not optional but mandatory" [src: Sect. 5].
- **Observational tests.** Planck 2018 X notes that correlation arises "typically" when "the trajectory in field space is curved between Hubble radius exit and the end of multi-field inflation" (arXiv:1807.06211) [src: Sect. 9.1]; a squeezed bispectrum violating the consistency relation would, "With the exception of models with certain non-Bunch-Davies vacua or non-attractor solutions", be "a smoking gun for additional light particles during inflation" (arXiv:2203.08128) [src: Sect. 3.1, "Multi-Field Inflation"].
  - `[der]` With tensors detected, Eq. 38 of arXiv:astro-ph/0205253 turns a measured curvature–isocurvature correlation into a test of the two-field consistency relation.

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **Field rotation (Gordon et al.) against δN (Sasaki–Stewart).** The rotation follows the perturbations through the turn and gives the transfer functions as integrals of α and β (arXiv:astro-ph/0205253) [src: Eqs. 5–7]; δN needs only the background e-fold number as a function of initial field values, and gives the spectrum and index in closed form (arXiv:astro-ph/9507001) [src: Eqs. 40–43], at the cost of solving the background for a family of initial conditions and of fixing the final slice. `[der]`
- **Covariant formalism on a curved field space.** The Riemann term of Sasaki and Stewart's Eq. 37 and the covariant turn rate of Achúcarro et al. 2011 (`formalism.md#multi-field-dynamics`) extend the canonical two-field equations; curvature can destabilise the trajectory (arXiv:1510.01281, in `formalism.md`).
- **Heavy-field EFT.** Replaces the two-field system by one field with reduced $c_s$, valid under the adiabaticity conditions of Step 7 (arXiv:1201.6342) [src: Eqs. 2.22, 5.5].
- **Final-hypersurface geometry in δN.** Pi and Xiong extend δN "to directions transverse to the inflationary trajectory" to "account for the geometry of the final hypersurface"; in hybrid inflation they find an enhancement of $\mathcal{R}$ driven by the tachyonic growth of the isocurvature perturbation during the turn, "qualitatively distinct from the non-attractor solution in single-field inflation models such as ultra-slow-roll", with $f_{\rm NL}$ "always positive" (arXiv:2606.29796) [src: abstract].
- **Ultra slow-turn.** For a class of models with "an exponentially decreasing turn rate", a negative effective isocurvature mass squared does not signal an instability; "the stability of a given model can be correctly inferred by the total entropy perturbation"; fibre inflation, SL(2,ℤ) attractors and modular inflation fall in this class (Achúcarro et al. 2026, arXiv:2603.03204) [src: abstract].

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Per-field curvature perturbations.** ${\cal R}_{I}$ of each field is not conserved even without explicit interaction (arXiv:astro-ph/0009131) [src: Sect. II.3, text after Eq. 39]; only the total $\mathcal{R}$ of Eq. 37 is the observable.
- **Straight-line assumption.** The integrated turn cannot in general be neglected in slow roll (arXiv:astro-ph/0009131) [src: Sect. II.3, footnote 2].
- **Stopping at the end of inflation.** Entropy modes frozen during inflation can be converted afterwards; predictions taken at the end of inflation then miss the enhancement and the lower r (arXiv:2507.03610) [src: abstract; Sect. 5]. Check: integrate Eqs. 43–44 of that paper through the oscillations, or carry δN to a slice after which the pressure is a function of density (arXiv:astro-ph/0411220) [src: abstract].
- **Initial conditions for the super-horizon system.** At horizon crossing "the term proportional to $k^{2}/a^{2}$ is not yet negligible", so initial conditions for Eqs. 43–44 "should be set instead a few e-folds later" (arXiv:2507.03610) [src: Sect. 3.2, footnote 5].
- **Tachyonic isocurvature mass read as instability.** Wrong for ultra slow-turn models (arXiv:2603.03204) [src: abstract]; the opposite case, a destabilising field-space curvature, is geometrical destabilisation (`formalism.md#multi-field-dynamics`).
- **Sudden turns.** The single-field EFT with reduced $c_s$ can fail for turns faster than Eq. 5.5 of arXiv:1201.6342 allows [src: Sect. 6].
- **Normalisation of $\mathcal{S}$.** The amplitudes $T_{\mathcal{RS}}$ and $T_{\mathcal{SS}}$ depend on the arbitrary normalisation of the entropy perturbation during inflation (arXiv:astro-ph/0205253) [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 4]; the observable combinations are the final spectra and Δ.
- **Single-field e-fold and reheating formulae.** The chain of `deep/reheating-and-efolds.md` assumes that ζ is conserved after inflation, which holds "under very general conditions (including the assumption that inflation proceeds with only one field)" (arXiv:1004.5525) [src: Sect. II.1]; in a multi-field model the late-time $\mathcal{R}$ depends on the processing of Step 5.
