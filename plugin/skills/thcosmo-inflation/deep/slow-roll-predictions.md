Audit questions: (1) How are $n_s$, $r$ and the running derived from the Hubble-flow parameters to second order, and how do the Hubble-flow and potential parameters convert?

The definitions of the flow hierarchy, the leading-order potential forms of $\epsilon_1$, $\epsilon_2$, $\epsilon_2\epsilon_3$, the first-order $n_s$, $n_t$, $r$ and the coefficients $a_1$, $a_2$ of the Encyclopaedia are in `formalism.md#slow-roll-and-the-flow-parameters`; where slow roll fails and which order is in use is in `approximations.md#slow-roll-expansion-order-and-range`; the direction of N and the meanings of η are in `approximations.md#convention-traps`; measured values of $n_s$, $r$ and the running are in `results.md#observational-bounds-on-the-primordial`. ## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- Planck mass. Leach, Liddle, Martin and Schwarz (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094) and Liddle, Parsons and Barrow (arXiv:astro-ph/9408015) write the full Planck mass $m_{\rm Pl}$, for example $H^{2}\simeq\frac{8\pi}{3m_{\rm Pl}^{2}}V,$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094) [src: Eq. 19]. Stewart and Gong set the reduced Planck mass to one, with action $S=\int\left[-\frac{1}{2}R+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\phi)^{2}-V(\phi)\right]\sqrt{-g}\ d^{4}x.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0101225) [src: Eq. 8] and signature (+,−,−,−) [src: Eq. 1]. The Planck papers use the reduced $M_{\rm pl}$ (arXiv:1502.02114) [src: Table 2], as do the skill's main sources (`formalism.md#conventions`).
- Flow parameters. Leach et al. define $\epsilon_{n+1}\equiv{{\rm d}\ln|\epsilon_{n}|\over{\rm d}N},\qquad n\geq 0.$ with N counted forward from an initial time (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094) [src: Eq. 15], the hierarchy of the Encyclopaedia. Stewart and Gong use instead $\epsilon_{1}=-\frac{\dot{H}}{H^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\dot{\phi}}{H}\right)^{2},\ \ \delta_{1}=\frac{\ddot{\phi}}{H\dot{\phi}}\ ,$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0101225) [src: Eq. 6], with higher $\delta_n$ for higher time derivatives of φ̇. Liddle, Parsons and Barrow use the Hubble slow-roll (HSR) hierarchy $\epsilon_H$, $\eta_H$, $\xi_H$, … of their Eqs. 4.2–4.7, built from derivatives of $H(\phi)$, which is a third parameter set (arXiv:astro-ph/9408015) [src: Sect. 4.1]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Leach et al. relate the flow parameters to the parameters ε, η and ξ of their Refs. 25–26: $\epsilon_{1}$ is ε, $\epsilon_{2}=2\epsilon-2\eta$ and $\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{3}=4\epsilon^{2}-6\epsilon\eta+2\xi^{2}$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094) [src: Sect. IV, inline, text after Eq. 15].
- The constant of the Green's-function expansion. Leach et al. and the Encyclopaedia write $C\equiv\gamma_{\rm E}+\ln 2-2\approx-0.7296$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094) [src: Sect. IV, inline, text after Eq. 28]; (arXiv:1303.3787) [src: Sect. 3.1, inline, text after Eq. 25]. Stewart and Gong write instead $\alpha\equiv 2-\ln 2-\gamma\simeq 0.729637$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0101225) [src: Eq. 7]: the same number with the opposite sign, so their coefficients carry α where the others carry C.
- Pivot time. Leach et al. evaluate H and $\epsilon_1$ "when aH=k_{*}" (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094) [src: Sect. IV, text after Eq. 25], as the Encyclopaedia does (`formalism.md#conventions`) and Stewart and Gong at $aH=k$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0101225) [src: Eq. 4]. Auclair and Ringeval choose a time close to Hubble crossing with $k_{*}\eta_{*}=-1.$ (arXiv:2205.12608) [src: Eq. 41]. The constant terms of the spectra depend on this choice (see "What goes wrong").
- Running. Leach et al. write $\alpha_{\rm S}$ for the running (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094) [src: Eq. 35]; Planck 2015 XX writes $\mathrm{d}n_{\mathrm{s}}/\mathrm{d}\ln k$ (arXiv:1502.02114) [src: Eq. 9]. The α of Stewart and Gong is the constant above, not a running.

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — the spectra as an expansion about the pivot
<!-- key: step-1-the-spectra-as-an-expansion-about-the -->

Leach et al. expand each spectrum in $\ln k$ about the pivot, $ {{\cal P}(k)\over{\cal P}_{0}(k_{*})}=a_{0}+a_{1}\ln\left(k\over k_{*}\right)+\frac{a_{2}}{2}\ln^{2}\left(k\over k_{*}\right)+\dots$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094) [src: Eq. 23], which they call "a double approximation": an expansion in $\ln k$ and the slow-roll expansion of each coefficient [src: Sect. IV, text before Eq. 23]. "First-order" means all terms up to order $\epsilon_m$ and "second-order" terms up to $\epsilon_m^2$ [src: Sect. IV, text after Eq. 23]. The normalisations are ${\cal P}_{{\cal R}0}(k_{*})={H^{2}\over\pi\epsilon_{1}m_{\rm Pl}^{2}},$ [src: Eq. 24] and ${\cal P}_{h0}(k_{*})={16H^{2}\over\pi m_{\rm Pl}^{2}},$ [src: Eq. 25] (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094), with the full Planck mass.

### Step 2 — the zeroth coefficient from the Green's-function solution
<!-- key: step-2-the-zeroth-coefficient-from-the-green-s -->

The scalar amplitude was computed to first order by Stewart and Lyth and to second order by Stewart and Gong (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094) [src: Sect. IV, text after Eq. 25]. In their own variables, Stewart and Gong obtain $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{c}}}(k)=\frac{H^{4}}{(2\pi)^{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}}\left\{1+\left(4\alpha-2\right)\epsilon_{1}+2\alpha\delta_{1}+\left(4\alpha^{2}-23+\frac{7\pi^{2}}{3}\right)\epsilon_{1}^{2}+\left(3\alpha^{2}+2\alpha-22+\frac{29\pi^{2}}{12}\right)\epsilon_{1}\delta_{1}+\left(3\alpha^{2}-4+\frac{5\pi^{2}}{12}\right)\delta_{1}^{2}+\left(-\alpha^{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}\right)\delta_{2}\right\},$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0101225) [src: Eq. 41], and check it against the exact power-law and near-maximum solutions [src: Sect. 2.5].

Re-expressed in the flow parameters, the scalar coefficients are $a_{{\rm S}0}=1-2\left(C+1\right)\epsilon_{1}-C\epsilon_{2}+\left(2C^{2}+2C+{\textstyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}}-5\right)\epsilon_{1}^{2}+\left(C^{2}-C+{\textstyle\frac{7\pi^{2}}{12}}-7\right)\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}+\left({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}C^{2}+{\textstyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}}-1\right)\epsilon_{2}^{2}+\left(-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}C^{2}+{\textstyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{24}}\right)\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{3},$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094) [src: Eq. 26]. For the tensors Leach et al. computed the second-order terms "along the lines of" Stewart and Gong and present them "for the first time" [src: Sect. IV, text after Eq. 31]: $a_{{\rm T}0}=1-2\left(C+1\right)\epsilon_{1}+\left(2C^{2}+2C+{\textstyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}}-5\right)\epsilon_{1}^{2}+\left(-C^{2}-2C+{\textstyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}}-2\right)\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2},$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094) [src: Eq. 29].

The Encyclopaedia writes the same scalar coefficient with two symbols in place of the numbers, $a_{0}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{(\mathrm{S})}}}=1-2\left(C+1\right)\epsilon_{1}-C\epsilon_{2}+\left(2C^{2}+2C+\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}-f\right)\epsilon_{1}^{2}+\left(C^{2}-C+\frac{7\pi^{2}}{12}-g\right)\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}+\left(\frac{1}{2}C^{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}-1\right)\epsilon_{2}^{2}+\left(-\frac{1}{2}C^{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{24}\right)\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{3}\,,$ (arXiv:1303.3787) [src: Eq. 23], and sets f = 5 and g = 7 [src: Sect. 3.1, inline, text after Eq. 25]; the tensor one is $a_{0}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{(\mathrm{T})}}}=1-2\left(C+1\right)\epsilon_{1}+\left(2C^{2}+2C+\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}-f\right)\epsilon_{1}^{2}+\left(-C^{2}-2C+\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}-2\right)\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}\,,$ (arXiv:1303.3787) [src: Eq. 26]. These are the constants listed under "Not verified" in `formalism.md`; with the values of f and g printed after its Eq. 25, the Encyclopaedia's Eqs. 23 and 26 coincide with Eqs. 26 and 29 of Leach et al. `[der]`

### Step 3 — the higher coefficients by differentiation
<!-- key: step-3-the-higher-coefficients-by -->

The coefficients beyond $a_0$ need no new mode calculation: $a_{n}\equiv\left.{{\rm d}^{n}[{\cal P}(k)/{\cal P}_{0}(k_{*})]\over{\rm d}\ln^{n}k}\right|_{k=k_{*}}=\frac{1}{{\cal P}_{0}(k_{*})}\left({1\over 1-\epsilon_{1}}{{\rm d}\over{\rm d}N}\right)^{n}{\cal P}_{0}(k_{*})a_{0}(k_{*}),$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094) [src: Eqs. 32–33], using the horizon-crossing condition $k_{*}=k=aH$ for the second line [src: Sect. IV, text after Eq. 33]. "If $a_0$ has been written to first order, differentiation yields $a_1$ to second order, $a_2$ to third order and so on"; convergence of the Taylor series is governed by the size of $\epsilon_{m}\ln k/k_{*}$, so it "can still be strongly convergent even if $\ln k/k_{*}$ exceeds one" [src: Sect. IV, text after Eq. 33]. This gives the $a_{{\rm S}1}$, $a_{{\rm S}2}$, $a_{{\rm T}1}$, $a_{{\rm T}2}$ of Eqs. 27–28 and 30–31 of arXiv:astro-ph/0202094, identical to Eqs. 24–25 and 27–28 of the Encyclopaedia quoted in `formalism.md#slow-roll-and-the-flow-parameters`. `[der]`

### Step 4 — spectral indices and runnings from the logarithm
<!-- key: step-4-spectral-indices-and-runnings-from-the -->

To read off the indices, Leach et al. expand the logarithm instead, $\ln{{\cal P}(k)\over{\cal P}_{0}(k_{*})}=b_{0}+b_{1}\ln\left(k\over k_{*}\right)+\frac{b_{2}}{2}\ln^{2}\left(k\over k_{*}\right)+\dots.$ [src: Eq. 34], which "automatically enforces the positive definiteness" of the spectrum, and identify $b_{{\rm S}1}=n_{\rm S}-1,\ b_{{\rm T}1}=n_{\rm T},\ b_{{\rm S}2}=\alpha_{\rm S},\ b_{{\rm T}2}=\alpha_{\rm T}.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094) [src: Eq. 35]. At second order:

- Scalar index: $b_{{\rm S}1}=-2\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}-2\epsilon_{1}^{2}-(2C+3)\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}-C\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{3},$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094) [src: Eq. 37].
- Scalar running: $b_{{\rm S}2}=-2\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{3},$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094) [src: Eq. 38].
- Tensor index: $b_{{\rm T}1}=-2\epsilon_{1}-2\epsilon_{1}^{2}-2(C+1)\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2},$ [src: Eq. 40], and tensor running $b_{{\rm T}2}=-2\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2},$ [src: Eq. 41] (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094).
- The constant terms: $b_{{\rm S}0}=-2\left(C+1\right)\epsilon_{1}-C\epsilon_{2}+\left(-2C+{\textstyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}}-7\right)\epsilon_{1}^{2}+\left(-C^{2}-3C+{\textstyle\frac{7\pi^{2}}{12}}-7\right)\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}+\left({\textstyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}}-1\right)\epsilon_{2}^{2}+\left(-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}C^{2}+{\textstyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{24}}\right)\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{3},$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094) [src: Eq. 36].

Planck 2015 XX prints the same four results in its own notation: $n_{\mathrm{s}}-1=-2\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}-2\epsilon_{1}^{2}-\left(2\thinspace C+3\right)\thinspace\epsilon_{1}\thinspace\epsilon_{2}-C\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{3},$ [src: Eq. 8], $\mathrm{d}n_{\mathrm{s}}/\mathrm{d}\ln k=-2\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{3},$ [src: Eq. 9], $n_{\mathrm{t}}=-2\epsilon_{1}-2\epsilon_{1}^{2}-2\thinspace\left(C+1\right)\thinspace\epsilon_{1}\thinspace\epsilon_{2},$ [src: Eq. 10] and $\mathrm{d}n_{\mathrm{t}}/\mathrm{d}\ln k=-2\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}\thinspace,$ [src: Eq. 11] (arXiv:1502.02114), citing the Green's-function calculations of Gong & Stewart and Leach et al., with "Habib et al. 2002, Martin & Schwarz 2003, and Casadio et al. 2006 for alternative derivations" [src: Sect. 3.2].

`[der]` The running starts at second order: it is a product of two flow parameters, so a measurement of it is the first access to $\epsilon_3$, while $n_s$ and $r$ at first order involve only $\epsilon_1$ and $\epsilon_2$.

### Step 5 — the tensor-to-scalar ratio
<!-- key: step-5-the-tensor-to-scalar-ratio -->

The ratio at the pivot is $R=16\epsilon_{1}\left[1+C\epsilon_{2}+\left(C-{\textstyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}}+5\right)\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}+\left({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}C^{2}-{\textstyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}}+1\right)\epsilon_{2}^{2}+\left({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}C^{2}-{\textstyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{24}}\right)\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{3}\right].$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094) [src: Eq. 42], which "becomes the well-known 'consistency condition of inflation' $R=-8n_{\rm T}$ at leading order, which holds for single-inflaton-field slow-roll models" (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094) [src: Sect. IV, inline, text after Eq. 42]. Planck 2015 XX fixes $r=-8n_t$ by default and, when running is varied, "$n_{\mathrm{t}}=-r(2-r/8-n_{\mathrm{s}})/8$ and $\mathrm{d}n_{\mathrm{t}}/\mathrm{d}\ln k=r(r/8+n_{\mathrm{s}}-1)/8$" (arXiv:1502.02114) [src: Sect. 3.2, footnote 2].

### Step 6 — the limit of negligible $\epsilon_1$
<!-- key: step-6-the-limit-of-negligible-epsilon1 -->

When $\epsilon_{1}\lesssim 0.001$ the tensors have no effect on the low-ℓ spectrum "at the 1% level", H and $\epsilon_1$ can no longer be measured separately, and the scalar spectrum depends only on the amplitude, $\epsilon_2$ and $\epsilon_2\epsilon_3$, with $n_{\rm S}-1=-\epsilon_{2}-C\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{3}$ and $\alpha_{\rm S}=-\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{3}$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094) [src: Sect. VI, inline, text after Eq. 54]. Auclair and Ringeval make the same point for low-scale inflation, where "at N2LO, the scalar power spectrum is driven by only $\epsilon_2$ and $\epsilon_3$, with $\epsilon_{2}\simeq 1-n_{{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{S}}}}\simeq 0.04$" (arXiv:2205.12608) [src: Sect. I].

### Step 7 — converting between Hubble-flow and potential parameters
<!-- key: step-7-converting-between-hubble-flow-and -->

Exact relations. From H and $\epsilon_1$, $\epsilon_2$, $\epsilon_3$ the potential and its first two derivatives follow exactly (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094) [src: Sect. IV, text before Eq. 16]: $V=\frac{3m_{\rm Pl}^{2}H^{2}}{8\pi}\left(1-\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{3}\right),$ [src: Eq. 16], $V^{\prime}=-\frac{3m_{\rm Pl}H^{2}}{(4\pi)^{1/2}}\,\epsilon_{1}^{1/2}\left(1-\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{3}+\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{6}\right),$ [src: Eq. 17] and $\frac{V^{\prime\prime}}{3H^{2}}=2\epsilon_{1}-\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{2}-\frac{2\epsilon_{1}^{2}}{3}+\frac{5\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}}{6}-\frac{\epsilon_{2}^{2}}{12}-\frac{\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{3}}{6}\,.$ [src: Eq. 18] (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094). Planck 2015 XX writes them as exact expressions for the potential slow-roll parameters (Leach et al. 2002; Finelli et al. 2010):

- $\epsilon_{V}=\thinspace\frac{V_{\phi}^{2}M_{\mathrm{pl}}^{2}}{2V^{2}}=\epsilon_{1}\frac{\left(1-\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{3}+\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{6}\right)^{2}}{\left(1-\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{3}\right)^{2}}\thinspace,$ (arXiv:1502.02114) [src: Eq. 41];
- $\eta_{V}=\thinspace\frac{V_{\phi\phi}M_{\mathrm{pl}}^{2}}{V}=\frac{2\epsilon_{1}-\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{2}-\frac{2\epsilon_{1}^{2}}{3}+\frac{5\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}}{6}-\frac{\epsilon_{2}^{2}}{12}-\frac{\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{3}}{6}}{1-\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{3}}\thinspace,$ (arXiv:1502.02114) [src: Eq. 42];
- $\xi_{V}^{2}=\frac{V_{\phi\phi\phi}V_{\phi}M_{\mathrm{pl}}^{4}}{V^{2}}=\frac{1-\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{3}+\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{6}}{\left(1-\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{3}\right)^{2}}\left(4\epsilon_{1}^{2}-3\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}+\frac{\epsilon_{2}^{\phantom{2}}\epsilon_{3}}{2}-\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}^{2}\right.+\left.3\epsilon_{1}^{2}\epsilon_{2}-\frac{4}{3}\epsilon_{1}^{3}-\frac{7}{6}\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{3}+\frac{\epsilon_{2}^{2}\epsilon_{3}}{6}+\frac{\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{3}^{2}}{6}+\frac{\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{3}\epsilon_{4}}{6}\right)\thinspace,$ (arXiv:1502.02114) [src: Eq. 43].

Planck 2018 X calls the step from Hubble-flow to potential constraints "an exact remapping" (arXiv:1807.06211) [src: Sect. 4.1]. In the HSR hierarchy, Liddle, Parsons and Barrow give the exact relations $\epsilon_{\scriptscriptstyle V}=\epsilon_{H}\left(\frac{3-\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle H}}{3-\epsilon_{\scriptscriptstyle H}}\right)^{2}\,,$ [src: Eq. 4.14] and $\eta_{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}=\frac{3\epsilon_{{\scriptscriptstyle H}}+3\eta_{{\scriptscriptstyle H}}-\eta^{2}_{{\scriptscriptstyle H}}-\xi^{2}_{{\scriptscriptstyle H}}}{3-\epsilon_{{\scriptscriptstyle H}}}\,.$ [src: Eq. 4.15] (arXiv:astro-ph/9408015); the latter shows that $\eta_{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}=\epsilon_{{\scriptscriptstyle H}}+\eta_{{\scriptscriptstyle H}}$ in the lowest-order approximation [src: Sect. 4.2, inline, text after Eq. 4.15].

The inverse direction is not exact: the Hubble parameters follow from the potential only as a series, because the PSR parameters "only classify the flatness of the potential, and so encode no information about initial conditions", which requires adding the attractor condition (arXiv:astro-ph/9408015) [src: Sect. 4.2]. Liddle, Parsons and Barrow carry the series to fourth order, $\epsilon_{{\scriptscriptstyle H}}=\epsilon_{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}-\frac{4}{3}\epsilon^{2}_{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}+\frac{2}{3}\epsilon_{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}\eta_{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}+\frac{32}{9}\epsilon^{3}_{V}+\frac{5}{9}\epsilon_{V}\eta^{2}_{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}-\frac{10}{3}\epsilon^{2}_{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}\eta_{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}+\frac{2}{9}\epsilon{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}\xi^{2}_{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}-\frac{44}{3}\epsilon^{4}_{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}+\frac{530}{27}\epsilon^{3}_{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}\eta_{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}\mbox{}-\frac{62}{9}\epsilon^{2}_{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}\eta^{2}_{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}+\frac{14}{27}\epsilon_{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}\eta^{3}_{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}-\frac{16}{9}\epsilon^{2}_{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}\xi^{2}_{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}+\frac{2}{3}\epsilon_{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}\eta_{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}\xi^{2}_{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}+\frac{2}{27}\epsilon_{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}\sigma^{3}_{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}+{\cal O}_{5}\,.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/9408015) [src: Eq. 5.3], obtained iteratively, order by order, at each φ [src: Sect. 5.1]. The printed "\epsilon{{\scriptscriptstyle V}}\xi^{2}" of the third-order term lacks the subscript braces of the other terms; it is copied as printed. The leading order of the inverse map, in the flow hierarchy, is Eqs. 20–22 of arXiv:astro-ph/0202094, which are the Eqs. 7–9 of the Encyclopaedia already in `formalism.md`.

### Step 8 — the potential route to second order
<!-- key: step-8-the-potential-route-to-second-order -->

Stewart and Gong express their result in the potential through $\mathcal{U}_{1}\equiv\left(\frac{V^{\prime}}{V}\right)^{2},\ \ \mathcal{V}_{1}\equiv\frac{V^{\prime\prime}}{V}\ ,\ \ \mathcal{V}_{2}\equiv\frac{V^{\prime}V^{\prime\prime\prime}}{V^{2}}\ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ \mathcal{V}_{3}\equiv\frac{(V^{\prime})^{2}V^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}}{V^{3}}\ ,$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0101225) [src: Eq. 52], after expanding $\epsilon_1$ and the $\delta_n$ in these quantities [src: Eqs. 55–58]. The amplitude is $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{c}}}(k)=\frac{V}{12\pi^{2}\mathcal{U}_{1}}\left[1+\left(3\alpha-\frac{1}{6}\right)\mathcal{U}_{1}+\left(-2\alpha-\frac{2}{3}\right)\mathcal{V}_{1}\right.\left.\mbox{}+\left(\frac{3}{2}\alpha^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\alpha-\frac{77}{6}+\frac{11\pi^{2}}{8}\right)\mathcal{U}_{1}^{2}+\left(-2\alpha^{2}-\frac{2}{3}\alpha+\frac{142}{9}-\frac{11\pi^{2}}{6}\right)\mathcal{U}_{1}\mathcal{V}_{1}\right.\left.\mbox{}+\left(2\alpha^{2}+\frac{2}{3}\alpha-\frac{37}{9}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}\right)\mathcal{V}_{1}^{2}+\left(-\alpha^{2}-\frac{2}{3}\alpha-\frac{2}{9}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}\right)\mathcal{V}_{2}\right].$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0101225) [src: Eq. 59], and the index $n_{\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{c}}}(k)=1-3\mathcal{U}_{1}+2\mathcal{V}_{1}+\left(6\alpha-\frac{5}{6}\right)\mathcal{U}_{1}^{2}+(-8\alpha-1)\mathcal{U}_{1}\mathcal{V}_{1}+\frac{2}{3}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{2}+\left(2\alpha+\frac{2}{3}\right)\mathcal{V}_{2}\mbox{}+\left(-12\alpha^{2}+\frac{13}{3}\alpha-\frac{1867}{36}+\frac{11\pi^{2}}{2}\right)\mathcal{U}_{1}^{3}+\left(24\alpha^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\alpha+\frac{595}{6}-11\pi^{2}\right)\mathcal{U}_{1}^{2}\mathcal{V}_{1}\mbox{}+\left(-8\alpha^{2}-6\alpha-\frac{371}{9}+\frac{14\pi^{2}}{3}\right)\mathcal{U}_{1}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{2}+\frac{4}{9}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{3}+\left(-6\alpha^{2}-2\alpha-\frac{49}{3}+2\pi^{2}\right)\mathcal{U}_{1}\mathcal{V}_{2}\mbox{}+\left(\alpha^{2}+\frac{8}{3}\alpha+\frac{28}{3}-\frac{13\pi^{2}}{12}\right)\mathcal{V}_{1}\mathcal{V}_{2}+\left(\alpha^{2}+\frac{2}{3}\alpha+\frac{2}{9}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}\right)\mathcal{V}_{3}\,.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0101225) [src: Eq. 61], whose numerical coefficients they list as 3.544490, −6.837097, 2.125941, −0.804970, 3.621120, −3.800854, −1.247621, 1.119331, 0.418550 [src: Sect. 2.6, text after Eq. 61]. The derivative along the trajectory used for it is $\frac{dX|_{aH=k}}{d\ln k}\simeq-\left.\left(1+\frac{1}{6}\mathcal{U}_{1}+\frac{1}{3}\mathcal{V}_{1}+\frac{5}{36}\mathcal{U}_{1}^{2}-\frac{2}{9}\mathcal{U}_{1}\mathcal{V}_{1}+\frac{2}{9}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{9}\mathcal{V}_{2}\right)\frac{V^{\prime}}{V}\frac{dX}{d\phi}\right|_{aH=k}$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0101225) [src: Eq. 60]. No paper read gives r or the running in potential parameters at second order (see "Not verified").

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **Third and fourth order (N3LO).** Auclair and Ringeval push the Green's-function method to N3LO, "fully expanded around an observable pivot wavenumber", for minimal and non-minimal kinetic terms (arXiv:2205.12608) [src: abstract]. Each successive integration by parts of the conformal time gives a perturbative expansion in the flow functions [src: Sect. II.1, text after Eq. 7]. At N3LO $\epsilon_{4}$ appears and corrects the lower terms [src: Sect. I]; to search for a running in the data "one should perform a data analysis involving $\epsilon_{4}$ and marginalize over it" [src: Sect. IV]. Their index at first and second order, $n_{{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{S}}}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{(1)}}}=-2\epsilon_{1{\scriptscriptstyle{\diamond}}}-\epsilon_{2{\scriptscriptstyle{\diamond}}}+\delta_{1{\scriptscriptstyle{\diamond}}},n_{{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{S}}}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{(2)}}}=-2\epsilon_{1{\scriptscriptstyle{\diamond}}}^{2}-\left(2C+3\right)\epsilon_{1{\scriptscriptstyle{\diamond}}}\epsilon_{2{\scriptscriptstyle{\diamond}}}-C\epsilon_{2{\scriptscriptstyle{\diamond}}}\epsilon_{3{\scriptscriptstyle{\diamond}}}+3\epsilon_{1{\scriptscriptstyle{\diamond}}}\delta_{1{\scriptscriptstyle{\diamond}}}+\epsilon_{2{\scriptscriptstyle{\diamond}}}\delta_{1{\scriptscriptstyle{\diamond}}}-\delta_{1{\scriptscriptstyle{\diamond}}}^{2}+\left(C+2\right)\delta_{1{\scriptscriptstyle{\diamond}}}\delta_{2{\scriptscriptstyle{\diamond}}},$ (arXiv:2205.12608) [src: Eq. 138]. The tensor-to-scalar ratio is given at fourth order, $r=16\epsilon_{1{\scriptscriptstyle{\diamond}}}c_{\mathrm{s}{\scriptscriptstyle{\diamond}}}\left[1+r^{{\scriptscriptstyle{(1)}}}+r^{{\scriptscriptstyle{(2)}}}+r^{{\scriptscriptstyle{(3)}}}\right]+\order{\eps{}^5},$ (arXiv:2205.12608) [src: Eq. 161]. Cost: the coefficients are longer, and the pivot is not $k_*=aH$: Eqs. 138 and 161 use $k_{{\scriptscriptstyle{\diamond}}}\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle{\diamond}}c_{\mathrm{s}}(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle{\diamond}})=-1.$ [src: Eq. 66], which reduces to the pivot $k_{*}\eta_{*}=-1.$ of the minimal case [src: Eq. 41] only for a unit sound speed.
  - `[der]` With the sound-flow functions set to zero, the $\epsilon$ terms of their second-order index coincide with Eq. 37 of Leach et al.
- **Third order with data.** Ballardini, Davoli and Sirletti extend the Green's-function spectra to third order, compare them with numerical solutions for a selection of models, and find ε₁ ≲ 0.002 at 95% CL (dominated by BICEP/Keck), ε₂ ≃ 0.031 ± 0.004 at first order growing to ε₂ ≃ 0.034 ± 0.007 at 68% CL with second- and third-order corrections, ε₃ ≃ 0.1 ± 0.4 at 95% CL "both at second and at third order", and $\epsilon_4$ "always unconstrained" (arXiv:2408.05210) [src: abstract].
- **Arbitrary order.** Giarè (2026) gives the spectra at any finite order; see `formalism.md#slow-roll-and-the-flow-parameters`.
- **Other approximation schemes.** Planck 2015 XX names the uniform approximation of Habib et al. 2002, Martin & Schwarz 2003 and Casadio et al. 2006 as alternative derivations, and states "the good agreement between the first and second method as well as with alternative approximations" in the appendix of its Ref. 205 (arXiv:1502.02114) [src: Sect. 3.2]. Not read here.
- **Exact numerical integration.** For models where the slow-roll conditions are not well satisfied, Planck 2015 XX uses the inflation module of CLASS and ModeCode (arXiv:1502.02114) [src: Sect. 3.2]; the Encyclopaedia uses FieldInf (`approximations.md#numerical-issues`). Leach et al. recommend a numerical check of any analytically reconstructed potential, "which will provide a further estimate of the magnitude of higher-order corrections" (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094) [src: Sect. VII].
- **Expanding P against ln P.** Eq. 23 and Eq. 34 of arXiv:astro-ph/0202094 are two truncations of the same spectrum; their difference at order n, $\sigma_n$ of Eq. 52, is the authors' estimator of the neglected orders, "of order $\epsilon_{m}^{n+1}$" [src: Sect. VI, text after Eq. 52].

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **The constants depend on the pivot time.** Another choice of pivot time changes the constants of Eqs. 23–28 of the Encyclopaedia (arXiv:1303.3787) [src: Sect. 3.1, text after Eq. 28]. With the pivot $k_{*}\eta_{*}=-1$ (arXiv:2205.12608) [src: Eq. 41], Auclair and Ringeval print the scalar spectrum as $\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}(k)=\dfrac{H_{*}^{2}}{8\pi^{2}\epsilon_{1*}}\bigg\{1-2(C+1)\epsilon_{1*}-C\epsilon_{2*}+\quantity(\frac{\pi^2}{2} + 2 C^2 + 2 C - 3)\epsilon_{1*}^{2}+\quantity(\frac{7 \pi^2}{12} + C^2 - C - 6)\epsilon_{1*}\epsilon_{2*}+\frac{1}{8}\quantity(\pi^2 + 4 C^2 - 8)\epsilon_{2*}^{2}+\frac{1}{24}(\pi^{2}-12C^{2})\epsilon_{2*}\epsilon_{3*}-\frac{1}{24}\quantity[4 C^3 + 3 \qty(\pi^2 - 8) C + 14 \zeta(3) - 16]\quantity(8 \epsast{1}^3 + \epsast{2}^3)$ (arXiv:2205.12608) [src: Eq. 54, first two rows]: to be set beside Eq. 26 of Leach et al. in Step 2.
  - `[der]` The Encyclopaedia attributes such changes of the constants to the choice of pivot time, and neither of the sections read says that the other expansion is in error; a fit or a referee check must therefore take the coefficients and the pivot from one paper together. The Encyclopaedia gives one instance: "setting $\eta_{*}$ such that $k_{*}\eta_{*}=-1$ would set f=3 and g=6" (arXiv:1303.3787) [src: Sect. 3.1, inline, text after Eq. 28]; the general transformation is not printed in the sections read.
- **Mixing parameter sets.** The flow hierarchy, Stewart and Gong's $\delta_n$, the HSR hierarchy of Liddle, Parsons and Barrow and Baumann's η are four different second parameters (see "Conventions"). `[der]` A second-order formula copied from one paper and evaluated with another's parameters is wrong at first order already.
- **An ill-defined higher parameter.** Liddle, Parsons and Barrow note that the third parameter of an earlier second-order paper, defined with $H'$ in the denominator, "is not guaranteed to tend to zero" as the potential flattens, which "has led to some confusion"; "the hierarchy of slow-roll parameters should be carefully defined, so as to tend to zero as the potential approaches flatness in arbitrary ways" (arXiv:astro-ph/9408015) [src: Sect. 4].
- **Truncation near the end of inflation.** For $m^2\phi^2/2$, if only the first-order corrections to $\epsilon_H(\phi)$ are kept, "they conspire so that $\epsilon_{H}(\phi)$ fails to reach unity for any φ"; the second-order corrections remove the problem; for $\phi^{\alpha}$ with α ≥ 4 the end of inflation "is well defined, even at first-order" (arXiv:astro-ph/9408015) [src: Sect. 5.1, text after Eq. 5.6].
- **Error estimator near a singular line.** The map from $(n_{\rm S}-1,\alpha_{\rm S})$ to $(\epsilon_2,\epsilon_3)$ "is nonlinear and singular at $\epsilon_{2}=0$"; near $\alpha_{\rm S}=(n_{\rm S}-1)/C$ the value of $\epsilon_3$ "can be huge" and the estimator $\sigma_2$ "is misleading, because it gives a small error even for models which violate the slow-roll condition $\epsilon_{3}\ll 1$" (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094) [src: Sect. VI, inline, text after Eq. 54].
- **Need for second order.** For the arctan model of Leach et al. the first-order expression "is not sufficient for MAP", and "For Planck the plot suggests that the third order is necessary" (arXiv:astro-ph/0202094) [src: Sect. VI, text after Fig. 8 caption]. For Planck data, Auclair and Ringeval report that inference "based on N2LO power spectra ends up being indistinguishable from an exact numerical treatment. Therefore, up to now, N2LO corrections are sufficient" (arXiv:2205.12608) [src: Sect. I].
- **Quantum diffusion.** The estimate that semi-classical corrections matter up to order six "assumes that quantum diffusion never dominates the field dynamics in the last 60, or so, e-folds"; with a diffusion phase just before the end, the map from wavenumbers to field values "become probabilistic, and the observed power spectra may even be severely modified at first order" (arXiv:2205.12608) [src: Sect. I]. Stochastic effects: `formalism.md#stochastic-inflation`.
- **Printed slips** (copied as printed, not corrected):
  - Planck 2018 X writes the running as "d ln n_s/d ln k" in its Eq. 4 and the sentence after it, $\ln\mathcal{P_{R}}(k)=\ln\mathcal{P}_{0}(k)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d\ln n_{\mathrm{s}}}{d\ln k}\ln(k/k_{*})^{2}+\frac{1}{6}\frac{d^{2}\ln n_{\mathrm{s}}}{d\ln k^{2}}\ln(k/k_{*})^{3}+\ldots,$ (arXiv:1807.06211) [src: Eq. 4], while its Table 1 lists the running as $dn_{\mathrm{s}}/d\ln k$ [src: Table 1] and Planck 2015 XX writes $\mathrm{d}n_{\mathrm{s}}/\mathrm{d}\ln k$ in the same expansion (arXiv:1502.02114) [src: Eq. 5].
  - Planck 2015 XX prints the first-order potential constraint as $\eta_{V}^{2}=\thinspace-0.010^{+0.005}_{-0.009}$ (arXiv:1502.02114) [src: Eq. 45]: a squared quantity with a negative value; the sentence before it announces the constraints "for the first two slow-roll potential parameters", $\epsilon_V$ and $\eta_V$ [src: Sect. 6.1, text before Eq. 44].

## Reading list
<!-- key: reading-list -->

- Stewart & Lyth 1993, arXiv:gr-qc/9302019 — foundation: first-order spectra from the exact power-law solution (abstract read only).
- Stewart & Gong 2001, arXiv:astro-ph/0101225 — derivation: the Green's-function formalism to arbitrary order and the second-order scalar spectrum and index, also in potential variables (Sects. 2.4, 2.6).
- Leach, Liddle, Martin & Schwarz 2002, arXiv:astro-ph/0202094 — derivation: the flow-parameter form of every second-order coefficient, the second-order tensors and r, the $b_n$ indices, and the accuracy map (Sects. IV, VI).
- Liddle, Parsons & Barrow 1994, arXiv:astro-ph/9408015 — foundation: HSR against PSR hierarchies, exact $\epsilon_V(\epsilon_H,\eta_H)$, $\eta_V$, and the fourth-order inverse series (Sects. 4, 5.1).
- Martin, Ringeval & Vennin 2013, arXiv:1303.3787 — review: the same coefficients with symbolic f, g, used for the Encyclopaedia's fits (Sect. 3.1).
- Planck 2015 XX, arXiv:1502.02114 — constraint: the second-order $n_s$, runnings and $n_t$ in one place, and the exact potential remapping (Sects. 3.2, 6.1).
- Planck 2018 X, arXiv:1807.06211 — constraint: second-order bounds on $\epsilon_{1,2,3}$ and on $\epsilon_V$, $\eta_V$, $\xi_V^2$ (Sect. 4.1).
- Auclair & Ringeval 2022, arXiv:2205.12608 — extension: N3LO spectra for canonical and k-inflation, the pivot $k_*\eta_*=-1$, the order at which quantum backreaction enters (Sects. I, II, App. C).
- Ballardini, Davoli & Sirletti 2024, arXiv:2408.05210 — extension: third order with Planck, ACT, SPT and BICEP/Keck (abstract read).
