## Electroweak baryogenesis and first-order electroweak transitions
<!-- key: electroweak-baryogenesis-and-first-order -->

### Electroweak baryogenesis (class)
<!-- key: electroweak-baryogenesis-class -->
- **Defining equations:** the semiclassical CP-violating force $F_{z}=-\frac{\left(|m|^{2}\right)^{\prime}}{2E}+s\left[\frac{\left(|m|^{2}\theta^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}}{2EE_{z}}-\frac{|m|^{2}\left(|m|^{2}\right)^{\prime}\theta^{\prime}}{4E^{3}E_{z}}\right]\ ,$ (Bodeker & Buchmuller 2020, arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Eq. 49], transport equations and the weak sphaleron rate; formalism in `formalism.md`. The idea goes back to Kuzmin, Rubakov & Shaposhnikov 1985 (doi:10.1016/0370-2693(85)91028-7), not read here.
- **Parameters:** strength of the transition $v_n/T_n$ (or $x(T_c)$ in the 3d EFT), wall velocity $v_w$ and width $\ell_w$, CP-violating phase in the wall.
- **Predictions:** an asymmetry "directly proportional to the weak-sphaleron rate in the symmetric phase", a lepton asymmetry "of the same order of magnitude as the baryon asymmetry" (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. III.2]; supersonic walls are not excluded: "contrary to common lore, that baryogenesis with $v_{\rm w}$ larger than the speed of sound is possible, and that the generated asymmetry smoothly decreases with increasing $v_{\rm w}$", quoted from its Ref. [111] [src: Sect. III.2, third paragraph after Fig. 5, beginning "For a quantitative description"]. The fluid-equation calculation of Cline & Kainulainen 2020, made "without making the small-wall-velocity approximation", finds "that the suppression of the baryon asymmetry is a smooth function of the wall speed and that there is no special behavior when crossing the sound speed barrier" (arXiv:2001.00568) [src: abstract]. A gravitational-wave background from the same transition: `thcosmo-cosmological-gravitational-waves`.
- **Consistency:** in the Standard Model the transition is a crossover, so "Sakharov's third condition is not satisfied" (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. III.1, text after Eq. 47]. The vev-insertion source used in part of the literature vanishes "at leading order in the gradient expansion (but to all orders in the VIA expansion)", as shown in the references followed by van de Vis, de Vries & Postma (their Refs. [255, 256]) (arXiv:2508.09989) [src: Sect. 3.4.1]; reply: none found in this run (see `open-questions.md`).
- **Observational status:** the electron EDM bounds "essentially exclude all models of electroweak baryogenesis that can be treated perturbatively", "it appears" (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. III.3, paragraph beginning "Studies of electroweak baryogenesis with two Higgs doublets"]; "scenarios of electroweak baryogenesis are increasingly challenged by results from contemporary experiments" (arXiv:2508.09989) [src: abstract]; "The current best limit on the electron EDM comes from the HfF+ JILA experiment", their Ref. [363] [src: Sect. 6.1.1]. Ways out named by Bodeker & Buchmuller: models in which "CP violation in baryogenesis and low-energy CP violation are decoupled, and the electroweak phase transition takes place at temperatures well above a TeV", and "strongly coupled composite Higgs models" [src: Sect. III.5].
- **Status date:** 2026-11-13

### SMEFT with a $(\varphi^\dagger\varphi)^3$ operator
<!-- key: smeft-with-a-varphi-dagger-varphi-3-operator -->
- **Defining equations:** ${\cal L}_{\rm SMEFT}={\cal L}_{\rm SM}+\sum_{i}\frac{c_{i}\mathcal{O}_{i}}{\Lambda^{d_{i}-4}}\,.$ (arXiv:2508.09989) [src: Eq. 2.24]; with only $\mathcal{O}_{\varphi}=(\varphi^{\dagger}\varphi)^{3}/\Lambda^{2}$, the dim-6 operators with Higgs derivatives, "generically subdominant during the EWPT", being neglected "for simplicity" [src: Sect. 2.3.5, text before Eq. 2.25], $V_{\rm SMEFT}=\frac{1}{2}a_{2}\phi^{2}+\frac{1}{4}a_{4}\phi^{4}+\frac{1}{6}a_{6}\phi^{6}=-\frac{1}{4}(m_{h}^{2}-2a_{6}v^{4})\phi^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{m_{h}^{2}}{2v^{2}}-2a_{6}v^{2}\right)\phi^{4}+\frac{1}{8}a_{6}\phi^{6}\,,$ [src: Eq. 2.25], $a_{6}=3/(4\Lambda^{2})$; the text after it prints "$m_{h}^{2}=125$ GeV" for the Higgs mass, as printed [src: Sect. 2.3.5, text after Eq. 2.25].
- **Parameters:** the cutoff $\Lambda$ only.
- **Predictions:** "a FOPT is possible for cutoffs in the 600 - 1000 GeV range", as found in their Ref. [210] (arXiv:2508.09989) [src: Sect. 2.3.5, text after Eq. 2.25].
- **Consistency:** "despite appearances, there is no hierarchy of scales", as pointed out in their Refs. [28, 209, 213, 214] (arXiv:2508.09989) [src: Sect. 2.3.5]; the uncertainty from "truncating the EFT at finite operator dimension" of the 3d EFT is shown, in the complex-singlet extension, in arXiv:2511.14831 [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Observational status:** not assessed in the sources read.
- **Status date:** 2026-11-13

## Leptogenesis
<!-- key: leptogenesis -->

### Thermal leptogenesis in the type-I seesaw (hierarchical $N_i$)
<!-- key: thermal-leptogenesis-in-the-type-i-seesaw -->
- **Defining equations:** kinetic equations $\begin{split}\frac{dN_{N}}{dz}&=-D(N_{N}-N_{N}^{\text{eq}})\ ,\\ \frac{dN_{B-L}}{dz}&=-\varepsilon D(N_{N}-N^{\text{eq}})-WN_{B-L}\ .\end{split}$ (Bodeker & Buchmuller 2020, arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Eq. 102]; proposed by Fukugita & Yanagida 1986 (doi:10.1016/0370-2693(86)91126-3), not read here.
- **Parameters:** $M_1$, $\varepsilon_1$, $\widetilde m_1$, $\overline m$; initial $N_1$ abundance.
- **Predictions:** $\eta_{B}\sim 10^{-2}\ \varepsilon_{1}\kappa_{f}\sim 10^{-10}\cdots 10^{-8}\ ,$ (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Eq. 113], introduced for "GUTs with hierarchical heavy right-handed neutrinos" by "a simple estimate yields the right order of magnitude for the baryon-to-photon ratio [88, 91]", for "$M_{1}/M_{3}\sim 10^{-5}\cdots 10^{-4}$" and "$\kappa_{f}\sim 10^{-2}\cdots 10^{-1}$" [src: Sect. IV.3.1, text before Eq. 113], with $\varepsilon_1$ estimated assuming "normal ordering" and keeping "the largest contribution proportional to the light-neutrino mass $m_{3}$" [src: Sect. IV.3.1, text before Eq. 112]; light-neutrino mass window and $M_1$ bounds in `results.md`. "The generated $B-L$ asymmetry strongly depends on neutrino parameters, and it is noteworthy that for masses and mixings consistent with neutrino oscillations the observed baryon-to-photon ratio is naturally obtained" [src: Sect. IV.3.1, second paragraph after Fig. 15].
- **Consistency:** with the reheating temperature implied by the estimates "as made by Buchmuller and Plumacher" for hierarchical $\nu_R$ with $m_{\nu_3}=\sqrt{\Delta m^2_{\rm atm}}$, $T_{reh} > 10^{10}$ GeV, "Implications for the gravitino problem are also discussed" (arXiv:hep-ph/0202239) [src: abstract, inline].
- **Observational status:** not directly testable at the seesaw scale `[der]`; the cosmological bound on $\sum m_\nu$ "is consistent with this mass window" (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. IV.3.1, text after Eq. 111]; the measured bound is in cosmoskill `cosmo-neutrinos-dark-matter`.
- **Status date:** 2026-11-13

### Flavoured leptogenesis
<!-- key: flavoured-leptogenesis -->
- **Defining equations:** the two-flavour equations of Eq. 117 of arXiv:2009.07294 [src: Eq. 117], valid below $T_\tau\sim 10^{12}$ GeV [src: Sect. IV.3.2, text after Eq. 115]; far below $T_\tau$ "an involved system of Boltzmann equations or, depending on the temperature regime, kinetic equations for the lepton density matrix" [src: Sect. IV.3.2, text after Eq. 118]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Parameters:** projections $p_{\tau}=|C_{1\tau}|^{2}\ ,\quad p_{\tau^{\perp}}=1-|C_{1\tau}|^{2}\ ,\quad\langle\tau|\tau^{\perp}\rangle=0\ ,$ (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Eq. 116] and flavoured CP asymmetries.
- **Predictions:** a $B-L$ asymmetry with the flavour structure $N_{B-L}\propto\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{\tau\tau}}{p_{\tau\tau}}+\frac{\varepsilon_{\tau^{\perp}\tau^{\perp}}}{p_{\tau^{\perp}\tau^{\perp}}}\right)\propto\varepsilon_{\tau\tau}\left(\frac{1}{p_{\tau\tau}}-\frac{1}{1-p_{\tau\tau}}\right)\ .$ (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Eq. 118]; a lower leptogenesis scale with tuning (`results.md`).
- **Consistency:** none found among the sources read.
- **Observational status:** not assessed in the sources read.
- **Status date:** 2026-11-13

### Resonant leptogenesis
<!-- key: resonant-leptogenesis -->
- **Defining equations:** nearly degenerate heavy Majorana neutrinos "that have mass differences comparable to their decay widths" (Pilaftsis & Underwood 2003, arXiv:hep-ph/0309342) [src: abstract]; equations not transcribed here.
- **Parameters:** heavy masses and their splitting, Yukawa couplings.
- **Predictions:** viable "with heavy Majorana neutrinos even as light as about 1 TeV" [src: abstract].
- **Consistency:** the subtraction of real intermediate states is addressed in the same paper [src: abstract]; the widely used prescription of Kolb–Wolfram and Harvey et al. "turned out to be incorrect, as observed by Giudice et al." (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. IV.2, footnote 18].
- **Observational status:** "In principle, searches for CP asymmetries in rare $W$-boson decays can test low-scale leptogenesis in the GeV-scale type-I seesaw with three quasi-degenerate-in-mass heavy right-handed neutrinos" (Fortin et al. 2026, arXiv:2610.24063) [src: abstract, first sentence]; the rest of that paper was not read. **Forecast.**
- **Status date:** 2026-11-13

### Leptogenesis from sterile-neutrino oscillations (ARS)
<!-- key: leptogenesis-from-sterile-neutrino-oscillations -->
- **Defining equations:** lepton asymmetries "produced through the CP-violating oscillations of 'sterile' (electroweak singlet) neutrinos", communicated to ordinary leptons through Yukawa couplings and reprocessed by sphalerons (Akhmedov, Rubakov & Smirnov 1998, arXiv:hep-ph/9803255) [src: abstract]; quantum kinetic equations from Hamiltonian evolution or the closed-time-path formalism (Drewes et al. 2017, arXiv:1711.02862) [src: abstract].
- **Parameters:** right-handed neutrinos "at the electroweak scale or below" (arXiv:1711.02862) [src: abstract], mixings, initial conditions.
- **Predictions:** "the observed value of baryon asymmetry can be generated in this way" with no physics beyond the singlet neutrinos (arXiv:hep-ph/9803255) [src: abstract]. In the $\nu$MSM, "The observed baryon asymmetry requires lepton chemical potentials $\mu_{\alpha}/T\sim 10^{-10}$ at $T\sim T_{\rm EW}$", the lightest sterile neutrino being dark matter "in the range $1<M_{1}\lesssim 50$ keV" and "the scenario predicts that the lightest neutrino mass effectively vanishes ($m_{1}\simeq 0$)" (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. IV.4, inline]. The $\nu$MSM proposal of Asaka & Shaposhnikov 2005 states that three right-handed neutrinos "with masses smaller than the electroweak scale" "can explain simultaneously dark matter and baryon asymmetry of the universe and be consistent with the experiments on neutrino oscillations" (arXiv:hep-ph/0505013) [src: abstract].
- **Consistency:** none found among the sources read.
- **Observational status:** parameter space charted for two right-handed neutrinos in arXiv:1711.02862 [src: abstract]; laboratory searches are out of scope.
- **Status date:** 2026-11-13

## Affleck–Dine and other mechanisms
<!-- key: affleck-dine-and-other-mechanisms -->

### Affleck–Dine baryogenesis
<!-- key: affleck-dine-baryogenesis -->
- **Defining equations:** a complex scalar carrying baryon number with $B$-violating interactions, CP-violating "For general complex $\epsilon$ and $\delta$", ${\cal L}_{I}=\lambda|\phi|^{4}+\epsilon\phi^{3}\phi^{*}+\delta\phi^{4}+c.c.$ (Dine & Kusenko 2003, arXiv:hep-ph/0303065) [src: Eq. 65]; in supersymmetry, the condensate is made of "linear combinations of the squark and slepton fields" [src: Sect. III.5]; original proposal Affleck & Dine 1985 (doi:10.1016/0550-3213(85)90021-5).
  - In the supersymmetric Standard Model with Hubble-induced soft terms, a flat direction lifted at order $n$ has, during inflation, $V(\phi)=-cH_{I}^{2}|\phi|^{2}+\left({a\lambda H_{I}\phi^{n}\over nM^{n-3}}+~h.c.~\right)+|\lambda|^{2}{|\phi|^{2n-2}\over M^{2n-6}}$ (Dine, Randall & Thomas 1995, arXiv:hep-ph/9507453) [src: Eq. 18]; the field must carry $B-L$ to survive the sphalerons [src: Sect. 2]. The full set of equations (lifting, Hubble-induced mass, onset, phase, $n_b/s$) is in `formalism.md`. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Parameters:** in the toy model, initial amplitude $\phi_o$ and mass $m$ ("With supersymmetry in mind, we will think of $m$ as of order $M_{W}$", arXiv:hep-ph/0303065) [src: Sect. III.2, inline]. In the supersymmetric version (arXiv:hep-ph/9507453, Sects. 2–4): the order $n$ of the lifting operator and its coefficient $\lambda/M^{n-3}$, the Hubble-induced coefficients $c$ and $a$, the low-energy soft mass "$m_{\phi}\sim m_{3/2}$" [src: Sect. 4.3, inline, text after Eq. 26] and $A$, the CP phase $\theta_{a}-\theta_{A}$, and the reheating temperature $T_R$. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Predictions:**
  - Toy model: the late-time $n_B$ of Eq. 73 of arXiv:hep-ph/0303065 [src: Sect. III.2, text before Eq. 71]; in the inflationary picture that review adopts in Sect. III.5, the baryon number "develops classically" and "is frozen once $H\sim m_{3/2}$" [src: Sect. III.5, inline].
  - Dine, Randall & Thomas 1995 (arXiv:hep-ph/9507453): for the trajectory of their Fig. 1, with "$m_{\phi}=c^{\prime}=-a=1$, $n=4$, and $n\theta_{i}=\frac{9}{10}\pi$", "$n_{b}/n_{\phi}\simeq.8$"; "Integrating over $\theta_{i}$, the rms $n_{b}/n_{\phi}$ is $0.3$. This is typical of the result for other $n$ and reasonable soft parameters" [src: Sect. 4.3, inline, text after Eq. 28]. "For $n>4$ with $M\sim M_{p}$ and a reasonable $T_{R}$, $n_{b}/s$ is generally too large, without additional entropy releases. For example, $n=6$ naturally gives the correct $n_{b}/s$ only when $T_{R}$ is of order the weak scale" [src: Sect. 4.4, inline, paragraph beginning "The total density in the AD condensate"]; for $n=4$, with parameters that "satisfy the constraint on $\phi/T$ to avoid the thermal scatterings", $\frac{n_{b}}{s}\sim 10^{-10}\left(\frac{T_{R}}{10^{6}~{\rm GeV}}\right)\left(\frac{10^{-3}M}{\lambda M_{p}}\right)$ [src: Eq. 32]. Along $LH_u$, the only $n=4$ directions carrying $B-L$, lifted by $W=\frac{\lambda}{M}(LH_{u})^{2}$ [src: Eq. 33], the operator of the neutrino masses, $\frac{n_{b}}{s}\sim 10^{-10}\left(\frac{T_{R}}{10^{6}~{\rm GeV}}\right)\left(\frac{10^{-8}~{\rm eV}}{m_{\nu}}\right)$ [src: Eq. 34], $m_\nu$ being the lightest neutrino mass "since the field moves out furthest along the eigenvector of $L_{i}L_{j}$ corresponding to the smallest eigenvalue of the neutrino mass matrix" [src: Sect. 4.4, inline, text after Eq. 33]; "$n_{b}/s$ is inversely proportional to the neutrino mass" [src: Sect. 4.4, inline, text after Eq. 34].
  - With thermal effects (Anisimov & Dine 2000, arXiv:hep-ph/0008058, $W={\phi^{n+3}\over M^{n}}$, who "usually take the reheating temperature to be $T_{R}=10^{10}$ GeV" [src: Sect. 3, inline, second paragraph]): for their $n=1$ "crude estimates" give a result "four order of magnitudes less then desired ratio", and "if the reheating temperature is somewhat lower one might be able to produce larger assymetry" [src: Sect. 3, text after Eq. 21]; with the thermal potential and again as a "crude estimate", for $n=2$ "the baryon to photon ratio is of order $10^{-10}\sin(\delta)$", for $n=3$ "roughly $10^{-4}\sin(\delta)$" [src: Sect. 3, inline, text after Eq. 27]; "Numerical study, however, indicates that these estimates are not always reliable. The baryon number is often significantly larger" [src: Sect. 3, last paragraph].
  - Q-balls: "Supersymmetric extensions of the standard model generically contain stable non-topological solitons, Q-balls", of which "large Q-balls can be copiously produced in the early universe, can survive until the present time, and can contribute to dark matter" (Kusenko & Shaposhnikov 1997, arXiv:hep-ph/9709492) [src: abstract]. Dine & Kusenko treat the fragmentation of the condensate into Q-balls and B-balls as dark matter in their Sects. III.6.2–III.6.5 (arXiv:hep-ph/0303065; section titles only were read). In the MSSM "with soft SUSY breaking terms of the minimal N=1 SUGRA type", "The L-ball always has a small charge, typically of the order of 1000, whilst the B-ball can have an arbitrarily large charge", "likely to be greater that 10^14" when formed by the collapse of an unstable condensate; "The B-balls typically decay at temperatures less than that of the electroweak phase transition", so that "the B asymmetry comes from Q-ball decay rather than condensate decay" (Enqvist & McDonald 1997, arXiv:hep-ph/9711514) [src: abstract]. Their properties "depend on how supersymmetry breaking is transmitted to the MSSM; if by gravity, then the Q-balls are semi-stable but long-lived and can be the source of all the baryons and LSP dark matter; if by gauge interactions, the Q-balls can be absolutely stable and form dark matter" (arXiv:hep-ph/0209244) [src: abstract].
  - Without supersymmetry, "by means of a complex Nambu-Goldstone boson carrying baryon number", or with "a charged Peccei-Quinn field containing the QCD axion as a phase" in the role of the AD field (Bodeker & Buchmuller 2020, arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. V]. Separately, in the supersymmetric version ("the framework is inherently supersymmetric"), because "In the Affleck-Dine mechanism, the baryon asymmetry is naturally proportional to the inflationary reheating temperature $T_{\rm rh}$", Chang, Shin & Unwin 2025 outline scenarios with UV freeze-in dark matter giving "a baryon-to-dark matter ratio of order unity that is largely insensitive to $T_{\rm rh}$" (arXiv:2506.03269) [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Consistency:**
  - Sign of the Hubble-induced mass: "With minimal Kahler terms only, $\phi=0$ is stable and the large expectation values required for baryogenesis do not result"; a negative sign needs general Kähler terms (arXiv:hep-ph/9507453) [src: Sect. 3, text after Eq. 13].
  - Gravitino bound and neutrino mass: "Assuming $T_{R}<10^{9}$ GeV in order to satisfy the gravitino bound [12], then requires that at least one neutrino be lighter than roughly $10^{-5}$ eV", and the thermal-scattering condition "would reduce the allowed $T_{R}$ to roughly $10^{6}$ Gev, and therefore the upper limit on the lightest neutrino to $10^{-8}$ eV" (arXiv:hep-ph/9507453) [src: Sect. 4.4, text after Eq. 34].
  - Thermal effects: "Thermal effects significantly alter the predictions for baryogenesis due to the coherent evolution of scalar fields"; for relatively flat potentials, after integrating out heavy particles, "This can alter by several orders of magnitude the predictions for the asymmetry", and "in some cases, the evaporation of the condensate tends to eliminate also any would-be $Q$-balls"; "For large enough $n$, thermal considerations are irrelevant" (Anisimov & Dine 2000, arXiv:hep-ph/0008058) [src: Sect. 5]. No later paper settling the size of these corrections was read.
  - Isocurvature: `[der]` a condensate present during inflation carries quantum fluctuations that become baryon isocurvature. Wu et al. 2026 "update the viable parameter space for baryogenesis using recent Cosmic Microwave Background constraints on baryon-density isocurvature perturbations, taking the quantum fluctuations of the scalar condensate generated during inflation as initial conditions" (arXiv:2602.08781) [src: abstract]. In the Affleck–Dine curvaton scenario, "in the framework of supersymmetry, taking Q-ball formation into account", "correlated baryon isocurvature perturbations are inevitably generated, resulting in excessively large correlated matter isocurvature perturbations"; reply, a proposed mechanism: cancellation "by anti-correlated dark matter isocurvature perturbations, thereby realizing compensated isocurvature perturbations" (Kawasaki & Neda 2026, arXiv:2607.21008) [src: abstract].
  - Swampland: Nakamura & Yamada 2026 find that "a large condensate compatible with the observed baryon asymmetry and isocurvature constraints readily satisfies the Festina-Lente bound", for flat directions that break all gauge symmetries during inflation "with gauge boson masses above the Hubble scale", and that "all single-direction monomials that admit complete breaking carry nonzero $B-L$" (arXiv:2609.37674) [src: abstract].
- **Observational status:** constrained through baryon isocurvature by the CMB (arXiv:2602.08781) [src: abstract]; the isocurvature bounds are in cosmoskill `cosmo-inflation-primordial`. A 2025 variant with Q-ball formation is proposed "to produce large lepton flavor asymmetries with vanishing total lepton asymmetry", "evading the current BBN and the CMB constraints by neutrino oscillations at MeV temperature", and is proposed as possibly "resolving the recently reported helium-4 anomaly" (Akita, Hamaguchi & Ovchynnikov 2025, arXiv:2509.08175) [src: abstract].
- **Status date:** 2026-11-18

### GUT baryogenesis
<!-- key: gut-baryogenesis -->
- **Defining equations:** "CP-violating decays of leptoquarks" (Bodeker & Buchmuller 2020, arXiv:2009.07294) [src: abstract], "scalar and vector leptoquarks, whose decays violate baryon and lepton number" [src: Sect. II.1]. Out-of-equilibrium decay with asymmetry per decay $\Delta B=\frac{1}{\Gamma_{X}}\sum_{n}B_{n}\>\left[\Gamma(X\rightarrow f_{n})-\Gamma(\overline{X}\rightarrow\overline{f}_{n})\right].$ (Riotto 1998, arXiv:hep-ph/9807454) [src: Eq. 73] and departure from equilibrium measured by $K\equiv\left.\frac{\Gamma_{X}}{H}\right|_{T=M_{X}}$ [src: Eq. 56]; details in `formalism.md`.
- **Parameters:** leptoquark masses $M_X$ and couplings $\alpha_X$, CP phases entering $\Delta B$, $g_*$, and the reheating temperature $T_{RH}$. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Predictions:** $B\equiv\frac{n_{B}}{s}\simeq\frac{\Delta Bn_{\gamma}}{g_{*}n_{\gamma}}\simeq\frac{\Delta B}{g_{*}}.$ (arXiv:hep-ph/9807454) [src: Eq. 75], assuming "the entropy realese in X decays is negligible" [src: Sect. 4.2, text after Eq. 75]; out of equilibrium requires $M_{X}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt$\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}}(10^{-4}-10^{-3})\>M_{\rm P}\simeq(10^{15}-10^{16})\>{\rm GeV}.$ for gauge bosons [src: Eq. 63]. In SU(5), which conserves $B-L$, "leptoquark decays can create only a $B+L$ asymmetry, with a vanishing asymmetry for $B-L$" (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. II.1].
- **Consistency:**
  - Sphalerons: "at temperatures above the electroweak phase transition ($B+L$)-violating sphaleron processes are in thermal equilibrium. Hence, any nonzero ($B+L$)-asymmetry is washed out", as emphasised by Kuzmin, Rubakov & Shaposhnikov (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. II.1]; the abstract says these GUT models SU(5) models "were excluded by the discovery of unsuppressed, $(B+L)$-violating sphaleron processes at high temperatures" [src: abstract]. In SO(10), "With $B-L$ broken at the GUT scale, right-handed neutrinos with masses below the GUT scale are ideal agents for generating a $B-L$ asymmetry" [src: Sect. II.1].
  - Reheating and gravitinos: with the gravitino bound $T_{RH}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt$\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$<$}}(10^{10}-10^{11})\>{\rm GeV}.$ (arXiv:hep-ph/9807454) [src: Eq. 125], "if the initial state after inflation was free from gravitinos, the reheating temperature seems to be too low to create superheavy X bosons" [src: Sect. 5.2.3, text after Eq. 125]. Reply: in preheating, "even particles with mass larger than that of the inflaton may be produced", and "X production will be possible if $M_{X}<g^{1/2}10^{15}$ GeV" [src: Sect. 5.4, inline, text after Eq. 136], for "$M_{\phi}\sim 10^{13}$ GeV" [src: Sect. 5.4, inline, text before Eq. 136]; the estimate assumes an interaction "of the type $g^{2}\phi^{2}|X|^{2}$" [src: Sect. 5.4, inline, first paragraph]. The resonance "is efficient only if the self-interaction couplings of the superheavy particles are not too large" [src: Sect. 5.4, inline]. Separately, it is "rendered less efficient when the X particles have a (large) decay width", and "Roughly speaking, one expects that the explosive production of particles takes place only if" the time in which the number of X bosons grows by a factor of $e$ is smaller than the X lifetime; for a decay width written $\Gamma_{X}=\alpha_{X}\>M_{X}$, "this requires $\alpha_{X}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt$\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$<$}}0.1M_{\phi}/M_{X}$" [src: Sect. 5.4, inline, paragraph beginning "The parametric resonance is also rendered less efficient"].
- **Observational status:** excluded in its SU(5) form by the sphaleron argument above `[der]`; no observational test of the SO(10) variant beyond leptogenesis was read.
- **Status date:** 2026-11-18

### Spontaneous baryogenesis and axiogenesis
<!-- key: spontaneous-baryogenesis-and-axiogenesis -->
- **Defining equations:** the arrow of time is set "by the motion of a light pseudo Goldstone boson of a spontaneously broken approximate global $\text{U(1)}_{B}$ baryon symmetry", where "Baryon-number-violating interactions can be in thermal equilibrium, and the observed baryon asymmetry can be generated for a sufficiently large $\text{U(1)}_{B}$ breaking scale" (Bodeker & Buchmuller 2020, arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. V]. The derivative coupling $-\sum_{i}c_{i}\frac{\partial_{\mu}\phi}{f}j_{i}^{\mu}$ acts in equilibrium as the chemical potential $\mu_{i}=c_{i}\frac{\dot{\phi}}{f},$ (De Simone & Kobayashi 2016, arXiv:1605.00670) [src: Eq. 2.9]; in axiogenesis the rotating Peccei–Quinn field gives, "before the electroweak phase transition", $n_{B}=c_{B}\dot{\theta}T^{2},\hskip 28.90755ptc_{B}\simeq 0.1-0.15\,c_{W}.$ (Co & Harigaya 2019, arXiv:1910.02080) [src: Eq. 3]. Full equations in `formalism.md`. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Parameters:** the breaking scale $f$, the couplings $c_i$, the decoupling temperature $T_{\rm dec}$ of $B$ violation, the mass of $\phi$, the inflation scale and the reheating temperature (arXiv:1605.00670) [src: abstract]; in axiogenesis $\dot\theta$ at $T_{\rm ws}$, $c_W$, $f_a$.
- **Predictions:**
  - $\left.\frac{n_{B}}{s}\right|_{\mathrm{dec}}=\left.\frac{15}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{\sum_{i}B_{i}c_{i}g_{i}}{g_{s*}}\frac{\dot{\phi}}{Tf}\right|_{\mathrm{dec}}.$ (arXiv:1605.00670) [src: Eq. 2.14]. For the minimal scenario, "for a given inflation scale, the other parameters including the reheat temperature, decoupling temperature of the baryon violating interactions, and the mass and decay constant of the scalar are restricted to lie within ranges of at most a few orders of magnitude" [src: abstract].
  - "spontaneous baryogenesis is a rather general phenomenon in the presence of axionlike particles", and "their coupling to gluons is already enough to generate a baryon asymmetry", quoted from its Ref. [142] (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. V]. A 2026 Froggatt–Nielsen realisation, with $\text{U(1)}_H$ "global and broken before the end of inflation" and $B-L$ violation "parametrized here model-independently via the dimension-five Weinberg operator", reproduces the asymmetry "for axion oscillation temperatures above $10^{11}$ GeV and axion decay constants ranging from $10^{13\cdots 14}$ GeV to above $10^{17}$ GeV", "Accounting for baryonic isocurvature and entropy dilution from axion decays" (Mojahed & Stanzione 2026, arXiv:2609.05605) [src: abstract].
  - Axiogenesis: $Y_{B}=\frac{n_{B}}{s}=\left.\frac{45c_{B}}{2g_{*}\pi^{2}}\frac{\dot{\theta}}{T}\right|_{T=T_{\rm ws}}\hskip-14.45377pt\simeq 2\times 10^{-3}\left(\frac{c_{B}}{0.1}\right)\frac{\dot{\theta}(T_{\rm ws})}{T_{\rm ws}},$ (arXiv:1910.02080) [src: Eq. 4]. With a rotating axion-like field, Co et al. 2026 claim that "both the hint of cosmic birefringence and the observed baryon asymmetry can be simultaneously explained", "The minimal setup" predicting "matter and kination domination ending around a temperature of $\mathcal O(3\text{-}50)$ MeV" (arXiv:2609.25202) [src: abstract].
- **Consistency:**
  - Cosmology of the scalar: the constraints come from "the backreaction of the produced baryons on the scalar field, the cosmological expansion history after baryogenesis, and the baryon isocurvature perturbations", and "cosmological considerations alone provide powerful constraints, especially for the minimal scenario with a quadratic scalar potential"; the paper proposes two ways of evading the isocurvature constraints, nonquadratic potentials or compensation "with cold dark matter isocurvature by making the scalar survive until the present" (arXiv:1605.00670) [src: abstract]. If $\phi$ is the PNGB, "the symmetry breaking should happen prior to inflation"; "Otherwise, unless with a specifically designed potential $V(\phi)$", the spatially averaged $\partial_{0}\phi$ "would be close to zero", "resulting only in an extremely tiny baryon asymmetry" [src: Sect. 2.1, inline, paragraph after the list beginning "Note that in the case"].
  - Assuming PQ charge conservation, $\dot{\theta}\propto a^{-3}$ once the PQ breaking field has reached its minimum [src: inline, text before Eq. 5]; Eq. 5 is introduced with "Assuming the latter case at the weak scale". Under that premise, QCD-axion axiogenesis overproduces dark matter: $\frac{\Omega_{a}h^{2}}{\Omega_{\rm DM}h^{2}}\simeq 140\ \left(\frac{f_{a}}{10^{8}~{\rm GeV}}\right)\left(\frac{130~{\rm GeV}}{T_{\rm ws}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{0.1}{c_{B}}\right),$ (arXiv:1910.02080) [src: Eq. 5], "much larger than the observed DM abundance" "for $f_{a}$ satisfying the astrophysical constraints", "the SM prediction $T_{\rm ws}\simeq 130$ GeV", and "$c_{B}=\mathcal{O}(0.1\mathchar 451)$"; the authors "require either 1) the axion rotation is damped after the electroweak phase transition, 2) the electroweak phase transition occurs earlier than the SM prediction, or 3) $c_{B}\gg\mathcal{O}(1)$ because of a large coefficient of the weak anomaly" [src: text after Eq. 5].
- **Observational status:** the birefringence hint is "$2\text{-}4\sigma$" in "Recent analyses of Planck and ACT CMB data", as quoted in arXiv:2609.25202 [src: abstract]; the measurement is in cosmoskill `cosmo-inflation-primordial`. Baryon isocurvature constrains the minimal spontaneous scenario (arXiv:1605.00670) [src: abstract].
- **Status date:** 2026-11-18

### Gravitational baryogenesis
<!-- key: gravitational-baryogenesis -->
- **Defining equations:** $\frac{1}{M_{*}^{2}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}(\partial_{\mu}{\cal R})J^{\mu},$ (Davoudiasl et al. 2004, arXiv:hep-ph/0403019) [src: Eq. 1], with $B$-violating processes in equilibrium until $T_D$ [src: text before Eq. 2]; the Ricci scalar "can play the role of the axion in spontaneous baryogenesis" (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. V].
- **Parameters:** the cutoff $M_*$; the operator is "natural to expect" in the low-energy effective theory of quantum gravity "if $M_{*}$ is of order the reduced Planck scale" (arXiv:hep-ph/0403019) [src: text after Eq. 1]; $T_D$; the equation of state $w$.
- **Predictions:** $\frac{n_{B}}{s}\approx\left.\frac{\dot{{\cal R}}}{M_{*}^{2}T}\right|_{T_{D}}.$ (arXiv:hep-ph/0403019) [src: Eq. 2], where "the scalar curvature is proportional to (1-3w)" [src: text before Eq. 3] and $\dot{\cal R}=-(1-3w)\frac{\dot{\rho}}{M_{P}^{2}}=\sqrt{3}\,(1-3w)(1+w)\frac{\rho^{3/2}}{M_{P}^{3}}.$ [src: Eq. 3]; the abstract states that the mechanism "can drive the universe towards an equilibrium baryon asymmetry that is observationally acceptable" [src: abstract]. A related mechanism is leptogenesis from inflationary gravitational waves "via the gravitational anomaly of the lepton current" (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. V]; in Alexander, Peskin & Sheikh-Jabbari 2004 the source "comes from elliptically polarizated gravity waves that are produced during inflation if the inflaton field contains a CP-odd component", and "In simple inflationary scenarios, the generated matter asymmetry is very small" (Alexander, Peskin & Sheikh-Jabbari 2004, arXiv:hep-th/0403069) [src: abstract].
- **Consistency:** the operator "is usually treated as a spectator interaction that generates an effective chemical potential in a prescribed background"; included in the gravitational action, "the metric equations remain open unless the metric dependence of $J^μ$ is specified", and Pereira et al. 2026 give "dimensionless diagnostics that quantify when the spectator approximation is controlled" (arXiv:2603.11837) [src: abstract]. No reply read.
- **Observational status:** not assessed in the sources read.
- **Status date:** 2026-11-18

### Low-scale and late-time baryogenesis (class)
<!-- key: other-low-scale-mechanisms-pointers -->
- **Defining equations:** cold electroweak transition with tachyonic preheating generating Chern–Simons number; decays of a singlet scalar "coupled to higher-dimensional $B$-violating operators" below the electroweak transition; $B$-meson oscillations "in an extension of the standard model with exotic $B$-meson decays" (Bodeker & Buchmuller 2020, arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. V]; equations of the first two not transcribed here. <!-- eq: notation -->
  - $B$-mesogenesis (Elor, Escudero & Nelson 2018, arXiv:1810.00880): "decays of a long lived particle produce $B$ mesons and anti-mesons out of thermal equilibrium", which "undergo CP violating oscillations before quickly decaying into visible and dark sector particles"; "Dark matter will be charged under Baryon number so that the visible sector baryon asymmetry is produced without violating the total baryon number of the Universe" [src: abstract]. Schematically, the size is set by $Y_{B}\,\,\propto\,\,\sum_{q=s,d}A_{\ell\ell}^{q}\times\text{Br}(B_{q}^{0}\to\phi\,\xi+\text{Baryon}+X)\,,$ (arXiv:1810.00880) [src: Eq. 1], with $A_{\ell\ell}^{q}$ the leptonic charge asymmetry of neutral $B_q$ decays.
- **Parameters:** model-dependent; in $B$-mesogenesis the mass $m_\Phi$ and width $\Gamma_\Phi$ of the decaying scalar, the branching fraction into a baryon and dark states, $A_{\ell\ell}^{s,d}$, dark masses (arXiv:1810.00880) [src: Sect. III.2, Fig. 3 caption].
- **Predictions:** the singlet-decay mechanism "can be probed by neutron-antineutron oscillations and the neutron EDM"; the $B$-meson mechanism operates "At even lower temperatures of around 10 MeV" (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. V]. In $B$-mesogenesis the benchmark points of Fig. 3, e.g. "$\{m_{\Phi},\,\Gamma_{\Phi},\,\text{Br}({B\to\xi\phi}+\text{Baryon}),\,m_{\Psi},\,y_{d}\}=\{25\,\text{GeV},\,10^{-22}\,\text{GeV},\,5.6\times 10^{-3},\,3.3\,\text{GeV},\,0.3\}$" with "$A_{\ell\ell}^{s}=10^{-4}=A_{\ell\ell}^{d}$" in the left panel (other values in the right panel), reproduce "$\Omega_{\rm DM}h^{2}=0.12$, and baryon asymmetry $Y_{B}=8.7\times 10^{-11}$" (arXiv:1810.00880) [src: Sect. III.2, inline, Fig. 3 caption]; the mechanism "would have unique experimental signals -- a positive leptonic asymmetry in $B$ meson decays, a new decay of $B$ mesons into a baryon and missing energy, and a new decay of $b$-flavored baryons into mesons and missing energy" [src: abstract]. The Snowmass white paper of Elor et al. 2022 surveys such ideas "with an emphasis on experimental testability" (arXiv:2203.05010) [src: abstract].
- **Consistency:** in mesogenesis, "Experimental probes of these decays place strong constraints on the amount of $CP$ violation required, placing it well above observed limits in meson mixing", and "strong lower bounds on the proton lifetime seemingly rule out Mesogenesis mechanisms which use $D$ mesons"; reply, a proposal: "'morphing' the mass of the dark sector particles using a late-time phase transition" (Baruch et al. 2026, arXiv:2603.28330) [src: abstract].
- **Observational status:** $B$-mesogenesis "requires a late period of matter domination by a scalar field $Φ$ that decays into $b\bar b$ quarks, reheating the Universe below the QCD phase transition"; from BBN, "For $M_Φ\lesssim 100\,$GeV, $T_{rh}$ must be $\gtrsim 10\,$MeV" (Cline et al. 2026, arXiv:2610.03455) [src: abstract]. Collider limits on the decays were not read.
- **Status date:** 2026-11-18

### Magnetogenesis at the electroweak transition
<!-- key: magnetogenesis-at-the-electroweak-transition -->
- **Defining equations:** field from Higgs gradients, $({\bf B}_{V})_{i}\sim-i\frac{2\sin\theta_{w}}{g\eta^{2}}\frac{1}{V}\int_{V}d^{3}x\,\epsilon_{ijk}\partial_{j}\Phi^{\dagger}\partial_{k}\Phi\sim-i\frac{2\sin\theta_{w}}{g\eta^{2}}\frac{1}{V}\int_{\partial V}dS^{j}\,\epsilon_{ijk}\Phi^{\dagger}\partial_{k}\Phi$ (Vachaspati 2020, arXiv:2010.10525) [src: Eq. 43].
- **Parameters:** domain size $\xi$ at the transition, helicity from CP violation.
- **Predictions:** a random-walk estimate, "under the assumption that $|\Phi|\sim\eta$ on the surface of integration" and "assuming that the Hopf angles are approximately constant in domains of size $\xi$" [src: Sect. III.1.2, text after Eq. 43], gives $E_{M}(k)\sim\frac{1}{k}B^{2}_{V,\lambda}\sim k^{3}.$ (arXiv:2010.10525) [src: Eq. 45]; "The generation of magnetic helicity requires new CP violating interactions that can be tested by accelerator experiments via decay channels of the Higgs particle" [src: abstract]. A 2026 review notes that "Near the electroweak scale, these phenomena are not necessarily independent" and that "The electroweak anomaly allows hypermagnetic helicity to exchange charge with chiral fermions" (Abu-Ajamieh 2026, arXiv:2611.02007) [src: abstract].
- **Consistency:** an earlier estimate of its Ref. [129] "used a different definition of the 'average magnetic field' that has been criticized in Refs. [26, 27]" (arXiv:2010.10525) [src: Sect. III.1.2, text after Eq. 45]; its Ref. [91] "argues that causality considerations imply" $n\geq 4$ for $E_M\propto k^n$, "in contradiction with the $E_{M}\propto k^{3}$ derived above", while the review finds "it is questionable if the premise of the argument applies to our situation" [src: Sect. III.1.2, text after Eq. 45]. <!-- eq: notation --> The Standard-Model electroweak transition is a crossover (`results.md`), so the domain picture applies to a first-order transition beyond the Standard Model or to the crossover as an estimate `[der]`.
- **Observational status:** relic strength depends on the decay law; under reconnection-controlled decay the electroweak relic is consistent with the blazar constraints (arXiv:2203.03573) [src: abstract]; under earlier decay models it was "too low" [src: abstract].
- **Status date:** 2026-11-13

### Inflationary magnetogenesis with a kinetic coupling $f(\varphi)^2F^2$
<!-- key: inflationary-magnetogenesis-with-a-kinetic -->
- **Defining equations:** $\ddot{A}_{i}+2\frac{\dot{f}}{f}\dot{A}_{i}-\Delta A_{i}=0~,$ (Durrer & Neronov 2013, arXiv:1303.7121) [src: Eq. 34] with, "at least for a short time", "a simple power law in conformal time" [src: Sect. 3.1.1, text before Eq. 35], $f(t)=f_{1}\left(\frac{t}{t_{1}}\right)^{\gamma}\;.$ [src: Eq. 35], following Martin & Yokoyama 2008 and Subramanian 2010 as cited there.
- **Parameters:** $\gamma$ (spectral slope), $f_1$, $t_1$, $H_{\rm inf}$.
- **Predictions:** scale invariance for $\gamma=-2$; "Normalizing $e^{2}$ such that $f=f_{1}=1$ after inflation", $\frac{\rho_{B}}{\rho_{\rm rad}}\simeq\frac{1}{(t_{1}a_{1}M_{P})^{2}}\simeq\left(\frac{H_{\rm inf}}{M_{P}}\right)^{2}\,.$ (arXiv:1303.7121) [src: Eq. 50]; "it is then easy to obtain magnetic fields of the order of e.g. $10^{-9}$ Gauss $/a^{2}$ on all cosmologically relevant scales in the scale invariant case" "With a suitable choice of $f_{1}^{2}t_{1}^{4}$", "while maintaining the condition $f_{1}t_{1}a<M_{P}^{-1}$ during all of inflation, in order to prevent back reaction" [src: Sect. 3.1.1, inline, text after Eq. 50]; for $\gamma>0$ a "very blue" spectrum [src: Sect. 3.1.1, text before Eq. 51].
- **Consistency:** strong-coupling problem for $\gamma\sim-2$: "To arrive at $e^{2}=1/137$ at the end of inflation, $e^{2}/f$ must have been much larger than 1 during most of inflation" (arXiv:1303.7121) [src: Sect. 3.1.1, text after Eq. 50]; backreaction bound of arXiv:0907.1030 [src: abstract] (`results.md`). The same review adds: "This is actually the only way to save this model, to violate gauge invariance", whose consequences "have not yet been studied", and "This problem is avoided if $\gamma>0$", at the price of a "very blue" spectrum (arXiv:1303.7121) [src: Sect. 3.1.1, inline, text after Eq. 50]. No reply restoring the $\gamma\sim-2$ case was read.
- **Observational status:** not assessed in the sources read beyond the bounds above.
- **Status date:** 2026-11-13

### Magnetogenesis at a first-order transition beyond the Standard Model
<!-- key: magnetogenesis-at-a-first-order-transition -->
- **Defining equations:** "Sufficiently strong first-order phase transitions (FOPTs) in the early Universe can simultaneously produce an observable stochastic gravitational wave background (SGWB) and a large-scale primordial magnetic field (PMF)"; example: an ALP sector with a radiatively broken global $U(1)$ coupled through a Higgs portal (Borah, Dev & Ghoshal 2026, arXiv:2604.20768) [src: abstract].
- **Parameters:** transition parameters ($\alpha$, $\beta/H$, $T_*$), helicity fraction; ALP decay constant $f_a$ in the example.
- **Predictions:** "For maximally helical configurations, we find peak field strengths up to $B_0 \sim 10^{-9}$ G at coherence length $\lambda_0 \sim 10^{-3}-10^{-1}$ Mpc", computed "accounting for inverse cascade effects", with correlated GW signals for "$10^3$ GeV $\lesssim f_a \lesssim 10^5$ GeV" (arXiv:2604.20768) [src: abstract, inline]. The GW spectrum belongs to `thcosmo-cosmological-gravitational-waves`.
- **Consistency:** none found among the sources read.
- **Observational status:** "consistent with lower bounds on the IGMF inferred from blazar observations", assuming maximally helical fields (arXiv:2604.20768) [src: abstract].
- **Status date:** 2026-11-13
