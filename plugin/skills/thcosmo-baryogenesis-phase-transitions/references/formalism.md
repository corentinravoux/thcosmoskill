## Sakharov conditions and sphaleron processes
<!-- key: sakharov-conditions-and-sphaleron-processes -->

Sakharov's three conditions, as stated by Bodeker & Buchmuller 2020 (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. II.1]: baryon-number violation; C and CP violation; departure from thermal equilibrium. The review adds that "after an inflationary phase one cannot have $B\neq 0$ as an initial condition of the hot early Universe", and that in a nonthermal system the arrow of time "can be provided by the time evolution of the scalar fields, as in Affleck-Dine baryogenesis" [src: Sect. II.1]. Sakharov's paper itself (1967) was not read; it is cited through this review.

All equations below are from Bodeker & Buchmuller 2020 (arXiv:2009.07294), Sects. II.2–II.3, natural units.

- Anomaly of the baryon current, with the U(1) hypercharge contribution neglected: $\partial_{\mu}J_{B}^{\mu}=\frac{n_{f}}{32\pi^{2}}g^{2}F^{a}_{\mu\nu}\widetilde{F}^{a\mu\nu},$ (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Eq. 3], $n_f=3$ families; "The same relation holds for the lepton-number current $J_{L}^{\mu}$, so that $B-L$ is conserved in the standard model" [src: Sect. II.2, text after Eq. 3].
- Change of baryon number and Chern–Simons number: $B(t)-B(0)=n_{f}Q(t)\ ,$ (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Eq. 4], and, when $\int d^{3}x\nabla\cdot{\bf K}$ can be neglected, $Q(t)=N_{\rm CS}(t)-N_{\rm CS}(0)\ ,$ [src: Eq. 7] with $N_{\rm CS}=\int d^{3}xK^{0}.$ [src: Eq. 8].
- The sphaleron is "a static solution to the equations of motion" at the top of the barrier, "which has half integer $N_{\rm CS}$ and an energy of the order of $m_{W}/\alpha_{W}$"; at low energies the tunnelling amplitude is "proportional to $\exp(-16\pi^{2}/g^{2})$, which is small and has no observable consequences" [src: Sect. II.2, text after Eq. 8]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Relaxation of baryon number for a small deviation from equilibrium, without Hubble expansion: $\frac{d}{dt}B=-\gamma(B-B_{\rm eq})\ .$ (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Eq. 9]; "When the dissipation rate $\gamma$ is larger than the Hubble parameter, the baryon number is in equilibrium" [src: Sect. II.2, text after Eq. 9].
- Sphaleron (Chern–Simons diffusion) rate, "the number of transitions per unit time and unit volume", through $\left\langle Q^{2}(t)\right\rangle=\Gamma_{\rm sph}V|t|.$ (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Eq. 15], which $Q$ "must satisfy" for Eqs. 12 and 14 "to be consistent" [src: Sect. II.2, text before and after Eq. 15]; "If there is a time window in which Eqs. (12) and (15) are both valid, which will be checked a posteriori" [src: Sect. II.2, text before Eq. 17], it is related to the dissipation rate by $\gamma=\frac{n_{f}^{2}\Gamma_{\rm sph}V}{2\langle[B-B_{\rm eq}]^{2}\rangle}\ .$ [src: Eq. 17]; "the window exists if the size of the relevant field configurations is large compared to $T^{-1}$" [src: Sect. II.2, text after Eq. 17]. Here, "In the temperature range between the electroweak transition and the equilibration temperature of the right-handed electron Yukawa interaction", $\langle[B-B_{\rm eq}]^{2}\rangle=VT^{3}\frac{2n_{f}(5n_{f}+3N_{s})}{3(22n_{f}+13N_{s})}\ ,$ [src: Eq. 13], $N_s$ Higgs doublets.
- In the symmetric phase the relevant length is $\ell\sim\frac{1}{g^{2}T}\ .$ (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Eq. 20], and the effective theory there is the Langevin equation ${\bf D}\times{\bf B}=\sigma{\bf E}+\bm{\zeta}\ .$ [src: Eq. 23] with the "conductivity of SU(2) charges" $\sigma=\frac{4\pi}{3}\frac{m^{2}_{\rm D}}{Ng^{2}T}\frac{1}{\log(1/g)}\sim\frac{T}{\log(1/g)}\ ,$ [src: Eq. 21], giving $\Gamma_{\rm sph}\sim g^{10}\log(1/g)T^{4}\ .$ [src: Eq. 25] and, from a lattice solution, $\Gamma_{\rm sph}=\kappa\frac{2\pi T}{3\sigma}\alpha^{5}T^{4},$ [src: Eq. 26] with $\kappa=10.8\pm 0.7$ quoted from their Ref. [251] [src: Sect. II.2, text after Eq. 26].
- Chemical equilibrium: the weak sphaleron relation $\sum_{i}\left(3\mu_{qi}+\mu_{li}\right)=0\;.$ (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Eq. 29], the strong sphaleron relation $\sum_{i}\left(2\mu_{qi}-\mu_{ui}-\mu_{di}\right)=0\;.$ [src: Eq. 30], and, for massless particles and $\beta\mu_{i}\ll 1$, $n_{i}-n_{\bar{i}}={g_{i}T^{3}\over 6}\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\beta\mu_{i}+{\cal O}\big(\left(\beta\mu_{i}\right)^{3}\big)&(\text{fermions})\ ,\\ 2\beta\mu_{i}+{\cal O}\left(\big(\beta\mu_{i}\right)^{3}\big)&(\text{bosons})\ ,\end{array}\right.$ [src: Eq. 33].
- Conversion of $B-L$ into $B$ when all Yukawa interactions are in equilibrium ("the case for $T<85$ TeV" [src: Sect. II.3, text after Eq. 36]), above the electroweak transition, with family-independent lepton chemical potentials assumed "For simplicity": $B=c_{s}(B-L)\ ,\quad L=(c_{s}-1)(B-L)\ ,$ (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Eq. 39], "where $c_{s}=(8n_{f}+4)/(22n_{f}+13)$"; "Near the electroweak transition the ratio $B/(B-L)$ is a function of $\langle\phi\rangle/T$" [src: Sect. II.3, text after Eq. 39]. For leptogenesis, $B(t_{0})=c_{s}(B-L)(t_{f})\;.$ [src: Eq. 40].

## The electroweak and QCD transitions
<!-- key: the-electroweak-and-qcd-transitions -->

From Bodeker & Buchmuller 2020 (arXiv:2009.07294), Sect. III.1, in their notation; for the signature see the note below the conventions table. <!-- num: not a value -->

- The effective potential "can be defined as $V_{\rm eff}(\phi)\equiv-P(\phi)$, where $P(\phi)$ is the pressure in the presence of a constant classical value $\phi$ of the Higgs field"; for $T$ much larger than the mass $M$, $P_{\rm ideal}=T^{4}\left(a-b\frac{M^{2}}{T^{2}}+c\frac{M^{3}}{T^{3}}+O\left(M^{4}/T^{4}\right)\right)\ ,$ (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Eq. 41], "with positive constants $a$ and $b$". The review notes that the effective potential "is gauge fixing dependent", while "the value of $V_{\rm eff}$ at the minima" is not [src: Sect. III.1, footnote 8]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- For one real scalar with $V_{\text{tree}}(\varphi)=\frac{\mu^{2}}{2}\varphi^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{4}\varphi^{4}\ ,$ (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Eq. 45], $V_{\rm eff}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mu^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{4}T^{2}\right)\varphi^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{4}\varphi^{4}+O(M^{3}/T)\ ,$ [src: Eq. 46]; "it turns out that the loop-expansion parameter is $\lambda T/M$", quoted from their Ref. [24], so perturbation theory "is thus not reliable for determining the details of the phase transition", which "is, in fact, second order" [src: Sect. III.1, text after Fig. 4].
- With gauge bosons, the $M^3/T$ term produces a cubic term, $V_{\rm eff}(\varphi)=\frac{A}{2}\left(T^{2}-T^{2}_{b}\right)\varphi^{2}-\frac{B}{3}\varphi^{3}+\frac{\lambda}{4}\varphi^{4}+\cdots.$ (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Eq. 47], with $T_{b}\equiv\sqrt{-\mu^{2}/A}$ the temperature below which the barrier vanishes; the longitudinal gauge bosons, which get a Debye mass, "do not contribute to the cubic term", and "For simplicity, the resulting contribution is not shown in Eq. (47)" [src: Sect. III.1, footnote 9]. "In the SM $B$ is small", the loop-expansion parameter $g^{2}T/M$ "is again large, so perturbation theory cannot be trusted" [src: Sect. III.1, text after Eq. 47].
- Nucleation in this language: the probability of forming a bubble per time and volume "is $A\exp(-\beta S_{\rm eff}[\phi_{\rm bubble}])$"; "Nonperturbative lattice computations of the nucleation rate indicate that perturbation theory slightly underestimates the strength of the phase transition while overestimating the amount of supercooling", quoted from their Ref. [254]; bubbles nucleate "roughly when the nucleation rate equals $H^{4}$" (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. III.1, text after Eq. 47]. The general formalism is in the nucleation section below. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Dimensionally reduced description: in many extensions of the Standard Model, high-temperature electroweak transitions "can be described in a minimal dimensionally reduced effective theory with SU(2) gauge field and fundamental Higgs scalar", in which "all thermodynamic information is governed by two dimensionless ratios $x \equiv \lambda_3/g^2_3$ and $y\equiv m^2_3/g^4_3$" (Annala, Rummukainen & Tenkanen 2025, arXiv:2506.04939) [src: abstract, inline].

## Finite-temperature effective potential
<!-- key: finite-temperature-effective-potential -->

From Athron et al. 2023 (arXiv:2305.02357), Sect. 2, in their notation (natural units; $\overline{\text{MS}}$ renormalisation scale $\mu$). <!-- num: not a value -->

- One-loop zero-temperature correction from a real scalar in $\overline{\text{MS}}$, in Landau gauge: $\Delta V^{\overline{\text{MS}}}_{1}(\phi)=\frac{1}{4(4\pi)^{2}}\,m^{4}_{s}(\phi)\left[\log\left(\frac{m^{2}_{s}(\phi)}{\mu^{2}}\right)-\frac{3}{2}\right].$ (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Eq. 2.23].
- One-loop thermal part from a real scalar: $V^{S}_{1T}(\phi)=T\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\log\left(1-e^{-\omega/T}\right)=\frac{T^{4}}{2\pi^{2}}J_{B}\left(\frac{m^{2}_{s}(\phi)}{T^{2}}\right),$ (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Eq. 2.45], with the bosonic thermal function $J_{B}(y^{2})=\int_{0}^{\infty}dk\ k^{2}\log\left[1-e^{-\sqrt{k^{2}+y^{2}}}\right].$ [src: Eq. 2.46].
- High-temperature expansion ($y^{2}\ll 1$): $\begin{split}J^{HT}_{B}(y^{2})={}&-\frac{\pi^{4}}{45}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}y^{2}-\frac{\pi}{6}y^{3}-\frac{1}{32}y^{4}\log\frac{y^{2}}{a_{B}}\\ &-2\pi^{7/2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{\ell}\frac{\zeta(2\ell+1)}{(\ell+1)!}\Gamma\!\left(\ell+\frac{1}{2}\right)\!\left(\frac{y^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\right)^{\!\!\ell+2},\end{split}$ (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Eq. 2.47]. Low-temperature form: $J^{LT}_{B}(y^{2})=-\sqrt{2}\,\Gamma(3/2)\,y^{\frac{3}{2}}e^{-y}\left[1+{\cal O}(1/y)+{\cal O}(e^{-y})\right],$ [src: Eq. 2.48], and the Bessel-sum form $J^{LT}_{B}(y^{2})\approx-\sum^{m}_{n=1}\frac{1}{n^{2}}y^{2}K_{2}(yn),$ [src: Eq. 2.50].
- `[der]` The $y^{3}$ term of Eq. 2.47 of arXiv:2305.02357 is the origin of the cubic term of Eq. 47 of arXiv:2009.07294, as the latter's text on the $M^3/T$ term says in words. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Daisy resummation, Parwani method: $\text{Parwani:}\;\;\;\;V^{\text{1L,Par}}_{\text{eff}}(\{\phi_{i}\},T)=\left.[V_{0}(\{\phi_{i}\})+V_{1,T=0}(\{\phi_{i}\})+V_{1T}(\{\phi_{i}\},T)]\right|_{m_{\tilde{B}}^{2}\rightarrow m_{\tilde{B}}^{2}+\Delta_{T,\tilde{B}}},$ (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Eq. 2.70], the masses being "all scalars and longitudinal vector boson masses appearing in the one-loop corrections", with thermal mass corrections of the form $c_{i}T^{2}$ at lowest order in $m/T$; fermions get no correction at this order and transverse vector bosons none of that form [src: Sect. 2.6.1, inline, text before Eq. 2.70; text after Eq. 2.70 for the list of masses]. Arnold–Espinosa method: the resummation is applied to the high-temperature-expanded bosonic potential, written in Landau gauge as $V^{\text{HTB}}_{\text{1T,Landau}}(\{\phi_{i}\},T)=-\frac{T^{4}\pi^{2}}{90}N+\frac{T^{2}}{24}\sum_{\tilde{B}}m_{\tilde{B}}^{2}(\{\phi_{i}\})-\frac{T}{12\pi}\sum_{\tilde{B}}m^{3}_{\tilde{B}}(\{\phi_{i}\})+{\cal O}(m_{\tilde{B}}^{4}),$ (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Eq. 2.71]. In the Parwani method "one must take care to consistently cancel the higher corrections that are already included by the resummation procedure to avoid a double counting" [src: Sect. 2.6.1, text after Eq. 2.70].
- Three-dimensional EFT (dimensional reduction): with the Matsubara decomposition $\Phi(x,\tau)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x)\exp{i \omega_n \tau},$ (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Eq. 2.80], integrating out the non-zero modes gives a three-dimensional theory of the bosonic zero modes; it "resums the leading infrared-divergent contributions captured by daisy resummation, while additionally correctly resumming other temperature contributions", including large logarithms [src: Sect. 2.6.2]. The matching scale "is taken to be about $\pi T$", and the review quotes from its Ref. [150] that $\mu=4\pi Te^{-\gamma}$ "is the scale that removes all logarithmic contributions from the matching"; the 3d theory "is super-renormalizable": "The beta functions for the masses are exact at the two-loop level, while the dimensionless couplings are renormalization group invariants", with their Ref. [150] [src: Sect. 2.6.2]. <!-- num: not a value -->

## Bubble nucleation and the progress of a transition
<!-- key: bubble-nucleation-and-the-progress-of-a -->

From Athron et al. 2023 (arXiv:2305.02357), Sects. 3–5.4, in their notation; the foundations are Coleman 1977 (doi:10.1103/PhysRevD.15.2929) for the bounce and Linde 1983 (doi:10.1016/0550-3213(83)90072-X) for finite temperature, neither read here. <!-- num: not a value -->

- At finite temperature the Euclidean action of an O(3)-symmetric bounce is $S_{\text{E}}=\beta S_{3}=\frac{S_{3}}{T},$ (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Eq. 3.30]; "At low temperatures the periodicity becomes relevant and this breaks down" [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 3.30].
- The nucleation rate is written $\Gamma=A\exp(-S)$ [src: Sect. 5.4, inline, text before Eq. 5.46]. For thermal fluctuations over the barrier the prefactor, given with their Ref. [257] [src: Sect. 3.5, text before Eq. 3.72], is $A(T)=\frac{\sqrt{-\lambda_{-}}}{2\pi}\left(\frac{S_{3}[\bar{\phi}(T)]}{2\pi T}\right)^{\!\!\frac{3}{2}}\!\!\left(\frac{\detprime[-\laplacian+V^{\prime\prime}(\bar{\phi})]}{\det[-\laplacian+V^{\prime\prime}(\phi_{f})]}\right)^{\!\!-\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\frac{S_{3}[\bar{\phi}(T)]}{2\pi T}\right)^{\!\!\frac{3}{2}}\!\!\left(\frac{\detplus[-\laplacian+V^{\prime\prime}(\bar{\phi})]}{\det[-\laplacian+V^{\prime\prime}(\phi_{f})]}\right)^{\!\!-\frac{1}{2}},$ (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Eqs. 3.72–3.73], "where $\detplus$ omits negative and zero eigenvalues"; the extra factor $\sqrt{-\lambda_{-}}\beta/(2\pi)$, obtained "by Eq. 3.27", "may be interpreted as a dynamical factor describing the growth of supercritical bubbles" [src: Sect. 3.5, text after Eq. 3.73].
- False-vacuum fraction (JMAK form): $P_{f}(t)=\exp(-\Vext(t)),$ (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Eq. 4.7], with $\Vext$ printed as a macro for the extended volume $\mathcal{V}_{t}^{\text{ext}}$ of the review's Sect. 4.2.2, whose definition was not transcribed.
- Bubble number density, quoted from their Ref. [320] and "evidently diluted by the expansion of space": $n(t)=\int_{t_{c}}^{t}dt^{\prime}\Gamma(t^{\prime})P_{f}(t^{\prime})\frac{a^{3}(t^{\prime})}{a^{3}(t)},$ (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Eq. 5.42], and mean bubble separation $R_{\text{sep}}(t)=n(t)^{-\frac{1}{3}}.$ [src: Eq. 5.41].
- **Milestones.** Nucleation, "on average one bubble nucleated per Hubble volume": $N(t_{n})=\frac{4\pi}{3}\int_{t_{c}}^{t_{n}}dt\,\frac{\Gamma(T(t))P_{f}(t)}{H^{3}(t)}=1,$ (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Eq. 4.58]; in a fast transition with $P_{f}(t\leq t_{n})\approx 1$, $N(t_{n})\approx\frac{4\pi}{3}\int_{t_{c}}^{t_{n}}dt\,\frac{\Gamma(T(t))}{H^{3}(t)}=1.$ [src: Eq. 4.59]; approximated further, $\frac{\Gamma(T(t_{n}))}{H^{4}(t_{n})}=1.$ [src: Eq. 4.60], and the action criterion $S(T(t_{n}))\sim 140,$ [src: Eq. 4.61], "valid only at the electroweak scale and during the radiation dominated era" [src: Sect. 4.7.1, text after Eq. 4.61].
- Percolation: in a fast transition "where the expansion of space can be neglected" [src: Sect. 4.7.2, first paragraph], with the percolation threshold $P_{t}\approx 0.29$ from "Percolation studies of uniformly nucleated spherical bubbles", $P_{f}(t_{p})\approx 0.71.$ (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Eq. 4.62]; an alternative is $P_{f}(t_{e})=e^{-1},$ [src: Eq. 4.63], which "does not represent the onset of percolation" but "is a conservative indication for whether percolation has occurred" [src: Sect. 4.7.2, text after Eq. 4.63]. In strong supercooling, with $\mathcal{V}_{\text{phys}}(t)=a^{3}(t)P_{f}(t),$ [src: Eq. 4.64], "Percolation is questionable if" $\derivative{t}\ln(P_f(t))\geq-3H(t)$ [src: Eq. 4.66] at $t_p$; "To our knowledge, a percolation study has not been performed in expanding spacetime" [src: Sect. 4.7.2, text after Eq. 4.66].
- Completion: $P_{f}(t_{f})=\epsilon,$ (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Eq. 4.67] with $\epsilon\ll 1$; taking $t_p$ or $t_e$ as the completion time "is equivalent to taking $\epsilon=0.71$ and $\epsilon=0.37$, respectively"; "The completion time is very weakly dependent on the completion threshold $\epsilon$, except in strongly supercooled transitions where the asymptotic value of $P_{f}$ may be non-zero" [src: Sect. 4.7.3, text after Eq. 4.67]. Their Ref. [322] "found that Eq. 4.66 at the percolation temperature is more constraining than requiring $P_{f}<0.01$ and several other conditions", shown "analytically for a simple model of simultaneous nucleation, and numerically for various scenarios from the scalar singlet model and a toy model of supercooling" [src: Sect. 4.7.3]. "it is also possible that if pockets of the false vacuum persist to low energies they can lead to the formation of primordial black holes" [src: Sect. 4.7.3, last paragraph]; that formation belongs to `thcosmo-pbh-defects` (planned).
- **Inverse duration.** For a fast transition the action is linearised about $t_*$, $S(t)\approx S(t_{*})-\beta_{*}(t-t_{*}),$ (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Eq. 5.46], with $\beta_{*}=-\left.\derivative{S}{t}\right|_{t_{*}}.$ [src: Eq. 5.47], so that $\Gamma(t)=\Gamma(t_{*})\exp(\beta_* (t - t_*)).$ [src: Eq. 5.48]. For this exponential rate and "additionally neglecting effects from the expansion of space", $R_{\text{sep}}\approx(8\pi)^{\frac{1}{3}}\frac{v_{w}}{\beta_{*}},$ [src: Eq. 5.49], as found in their Refs. [483, 385] [src: Sect. 5.4, text before Eq. 5.49], "Strictly speaking" obtained "at the so-called $e$-folding time/temperature, where the false vacuum fraction $P_{f}=1/e$, but it is often applied at the percolation or nucleation temperature"; their Ref. [10], the LISA report of Caprini et al. 2019 (arXiv:1910.13125), instead uses, as printed by Athron et al., $R_{\text{sep}}\approx(8\pi)^{\frac{1}{3}}\frac{\max(v_{w},c_{s,f})}{\beta_{*}}.$ (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Eq. 5.50], which "accounts for the collision of shocks preceding the bubble walls for bubbles expanding as subsonic deflagrations" [src: Sect. 5.4, text after Eq. 5.50]. The authors "currently recommend using $L_{*}=R_{\text{sep}}$ because the hydrodynamic simulations that provide GW fits calculate $R_{\text{sep}}$" [src: Sect. 5.4, text before Eq. 5.46]. The spectra built on these parameters are in `thcosmo-cosmological-gravitational-waves` (`deep/phase-transition-spectra.md`).
- The energy density of a field configuration, with $V$ the temperature-dependent effective potential: $\rho(\boldsymbol{\phi},T)=V(\boldsymbol{\phi},T)-T\partialderivative{V}{T}.$ (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Eq. 4.34].

## Bubble walls and hydrodynamics
<!-- key: bubble-walls-and-hydrodynamics -->

From Athron et al. 2023 (arXiv:2305.02357), Sects. 5.5.2–5.5.3, Minkowski metric assumed in the field equation [src: Sect. 5.5.3, text after Eq. 5.55]. The definitions of $\alpha$, $\kappa$ and $K$ used in the spectra are collected in `thcosmo-cosmological-gravitational-waves` (`references/formalism.md`, first-order phase transitions). <!-- num: not a value -->

- **Expansion modes.** If the wall velocity is below $c_{s,-}$, "a subsonic deflagration is the only possible expansion mode"; in the bag equation of state the Chapman–Jouguet velocity is $v_{\text{CJ}}=\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{+}(2+3\alpha_{+})}+1}{\sqrt{3}(1+\alpha_{+})}$ (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Eq. 5.52], "where $\alpha_{+}$ is the ratio of the vacuum energy density to the radiation energy density"; "Chapman-Jouguet detonations are not hydrodynamically favored over weak detonations, so fixing $v_{w}=v_{\text{CJ}}$ is an arbitrary choice", and "$v_{\text{CJ}}$ is the smallest wall velocity corresponding to a weak supersonic detonation" [src: Sect. 5.5.2, text after Eq. 5.52].
- **Pressure balance.** $p_{\text{tot}}(t,v)=p_{\text{dr}}(t)-p_{\text{fr}}(t,v),$ (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Eq. 5.53], with driving pressure $p_{\text{dr}}(t)=V(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{f}(t),T_{+}(t))-V(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{t}(t),T_{-}(t)).$ [src: Eq. 5.54]; "the terminal bubble wall velocity is usually defined such that $p_{\text{tot}}(t_{*},v_{w})=0$" [src: Sect. 5.5.3, footnote 52].
- **Scalar equation of motion in the plasma** (WKB, wall thicker than $T^{-1}$ [src: Sect. 5.5.3, footnote 53]): $\partial_{\rho}\partial^{\rho}\phi_{i}+\partialderivative{V_0}{\phi_i}+\sum_{j}\derivative{m_j^2}{z}\!\int\!\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{j}}f_{j}(k,z)=0,$ (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Eq. 5.55]. With $f_j=f_j^{\rm eq}+\delta f_j$, the friction is identified, following their Ref. [510], as $p_{\text{fr}}(t,v)=\int\!dz\derivative{T}{z}\partialderivative{V}{T}-\int\!dz\derivative{\phi_i}{z}\sum_{a}\derivative{m_j^2}{\phi_i}\!\int\!\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{j}}\delta f_{j}(k,z).$ [src: Eq. 5.61] (the sum index is printed as $a$ with summand index $j$, as printed); "Usually only the second term in Eq. 5.61 is identified as friction", and the first "sometimes is mistakenly ignored by assuming $\text{d}T/\text{d}z=0$" [src: Sect. 5.5.3, text after Eq. 5.61].
- **Local thermal equilibrium.** With $\delta f_j=0$, "provided the bubble wall is not ultra-relativistic", entropy conservation gives $s_{+}\gamma_{+}v_{+}=s_{-}\gamma_{-}v_{-},$ (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Eq. 5.62], and, quoted from their Ref. [512], $p_{\text{fr}}(t,v)=\absolutevalue{\Delta\! \left\{(\gamma^2 - 1) T s \right\}},$ [src: Eq. 5.63], "contrary to the long-held expectation of vanishing friction in local thermal equilibrium" [src: Sect. 5.5.3, text after Eq. 5.63]. A fit for $c_{s,f}=c_{s,t}=1/\sqrt{3}$ from Ai et al. 2023 (arXiv:2303.10171) is $v_{w}=\left(\absolutevalue{\frac{3\alpha+ \Psi- 1}{2(2 - 3\Psi+ \Psi^3)}}^{\frac{p}{2}}+\absolutevalue{v_{\text{CJ}} \! \left(1 - a \frac{(1 - \Psi)^b}{\alpha} \right)}^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)^{\!\!\frac{1}{p}},$ (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Eq. 5.64], with $\Psi=w_{t}/w_{f}$, $a=0.2233$, $b=1.704$, $p=-3.433$ [src: Sect. 5.5.3, text after Eq. 5.64]; "The predicted value of $v_{w}$ from (5.64) is a rough upper bound because out-of-equilibrium effects would act to reduce $v_{w}$" [src: Sect. 5.5.3, paragraph beginning "The predicted value of $v_{w}$ from (5.64)"]. In LTE, under "the reasonable assumption that the sound speeds in the plasma are approximately uniform", the hydrodynamics "can be fully characterized by four quantities": $\alpha_n$, $\Psi_n$, $c_s$ and $c_b$ (arXiv:2303.10171) [src: abstract].
- **Out of equilibrium.** The heavy species obey $d_{t}f_{j}\equiv\partial_{t}f_{j}+\dot{z}\partial_{z}f_{j}+\dot{k}_{z}\partial_{k_{z}}f_{j}=-C[f_{j}],$ (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Eq. 5.65]; collision integrals "are often computed in the leading-log approximation", and two improved treatments, their Refs. [517, 518], "found a reduction in the predicted terminal bubble wall velocity", so "the typical treatment in the leading-log approximation underestimates the friction" [src: Sect. 5.5.3, text after Eq. 5.65].
- **Ultra-relativistic limit.** Leading-order friction, quoted from their Refs. [502, 523] (the first is arXiv:0903.4099) and "approximately given by" $p_{\text{fr}}^{\text{LO}}(t)=\sum_{j}c_{j}g_{j}\frac{\Delta m_{j}^{2}T^{2}}{24},$ (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Eq. 5.67], "where $c_{j}=1$ for bosons and $c_{j}=1/2$ for fermions", independent of the wall velocity [src: Sect. 5.5.3, text after Eq. 5.67]. Bodeker & Moore 2009 show that, "In extensions of the Standard Model with SU(2) singlet scalar fields", in "regions of parameter space" where the transition is first order "already at the mean-field level", "the relativistic gamma factor gamma = (1-v_{wall}^2)^{-1/2}" can grow "linearly with the wall's propagation distance" (arXiv:0903.4099) [src: abstract]; Bodeker & Moore 2017 find, "In extensions of the Standard Model with extra scalars", that transition radiation, "Wall-frame soft processes, though suppressed by a power of the coupling $\alpha$", limits "wall velocities to $\gamma\sim 1/\alpha$" (arXiv:1703.08215) [src: abstract].
- **Phenomenological friction.** $\mathcal{K}(\phi_{i})=\eta u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\phi_{i},$ (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Eq. 5.68], with $\eta=\tilde{\eta}T$, entering $\partial_{\rho}\partial^{\rho}\phi_{i}+\partialderivative{V_0}{\phi_i}-\mathcal{K}(\phi_{i})=0,$ [src: Eq. 5.69]; it "models only the out-of-equilibrium contribution to the friction" [src: Sect. 5.5.3, text after Eq. 5.69].

## Electroweak baryogenesis
<!-- key: electroweak-baryogenesis -->

Mechanism, from Bodeker & Buchmuller 2020 (arXiv:2009.07294), Sect. III.2: CP-violating interactions with the wall separate particles of different spin; "In the symmetric phase electroweak sphalerons are unsuppressed" and act on the left-chiral baryon number $B_L$; "If the weak-sphaleron rate is sufficiently suppressed on the other side of the wall, a net baryon number is generated" [src: Sect. III.2]. One distinguishes "the thin-wall limit $\ell_{\rm w}\sim T^{-1}$ from the thick-wall limit $\ell_{\rm w}\gg T^{-1}$" [src: Sect. III.2, inline]. <!-- num: not a value -->

- Thick-wall, semiclassical (WKB) force for a fermion with ${\cal L}_{\rm mass}=-\overline{\psi_{L}}m\psi_{R}-\overline{\psi_{R}}m^{\ast}\psi_{L}\ ,$ (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Eq. 48], $m=|m|\exp(i\theta)$, in the wall rest frame, keeping "the first two terms" of the expansion in derivatives of $m$, "corresponding to an expansion in $(\ell_{\rm w}T)^{-1}$": $F_{z}=-\frac{\left(|m|^{2}\right)^{\prime}}{2E}+s\left[\frac{\left(|m|^{2}\theta^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}}{2EE_{z}}-\frac{|m|^{2}\left(|m|^{2}\right)^{\prime}\theta^{\prime}}{4E^{3}E_{z}}\right]\ ,$ [src: Eq. 49], with $s=\pm 1$ the spin "(as defined in the frame where the momentum transverse to the wall vanishes)"; "The leading-order term is independent of spin", and the spin dependence "first appears at second order" [src: Sect. III.2, text after Eq. 49].
- Transport: the force enters Boltzmann equations; "for small wall velocity one can make a fluid ansatz" with moments of weights 1 and $p_z/E$, giving "a network of coupled differential equations for $\delta v_{i}$ and $\mu_{i}$"; "The resulting asymmetry is directly proportional to the weak-sphaleron rate in the symmetric phase" (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. III.2, third paragraph after Fig. 5, beginning "For a quantitative description"]. A self-contained implementation of the WKB approach with fluid-like moment equations, including updated diffusion constants, helicity-flip and sphaleron rates, is the code BARYONET (Barni 2025, arXiv:2510.21915) [src: abstract].
- Because $B-L$ is conserved, "the produced lepton asymmetry is of the same order of magnitude as the baryon asymmetry. If a larger lepton asymmetry would be observed, this would rule out electroweak baryogenesis as the sole origin of the baryon asymmetry of the Universe" (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. III.2, last paragraph].
- **Washout criterion.** "A rough estimate for the non-washout criterion (see e.g. [42, 33])", assuming the broken-phase rate "does not vary significantly over the time scale of the transition" [src: Sect. 5.4, text before Eq. 5.16], van de Vis, de Vries & Postma 2025 write $\rho_{b}(z)\sim\rho_{b}(0){\rm e}^{-\frac{{\mathcal{R}}}{2v_{w}}z}=\rho_{b}(0){\rm e}^{-\frac{n_{f}A\Gamma_{\rm sph,b}}{2v_{w}T^{3}}z},$ (arXiv:2508.09989) [src: Eq. 5.16]; with $\Gamma_{\rm sph,b}\sim T^{4}e^{-E_{\rm sph}/T}$ [src: Sect. 5.4, inline, text after Eq. 5.16] at nucleation and "demanding that the exponent is larger than e.g. $-10$, one obtains the well-known criterion" $\frac{v_{n}}{T}\gtrsim 1.$ [src: Eq. 5.17]. Caveats in the same place: a washout factor $e^{-10}$ "requires a very large $\rho_{b}(0)$"; demanding at most $1/e$ "results in order 10% corrections to the right-hand-side of eq. 5.17" (their Ref. [76]); and the sphaleron energy is gauge-dependent, "making the washout criterion eq. 5.17 gauge-dependent as well" [src: Sect. 5.4, text after Eq. 5.17]. The lattice bound of their Ref. [325] is $x(T_{c})\lesssim 0.025.$ (arXiv:2508.09989) [src: Eq. 5.18], the value also given in the abstract of arXiv:2506.04939 (see `results.md`); the perturbative, gauge-independent computation of their Ref. [320] is "remarkably close" to it [src: Sect. 5.4, text before Eq. 5.18].

## Magnetogenesis
<!-- key: magnetogenesis -->

Statistics of a stochastic, statistically isotropic field, from Vachaspati 2020 (arXiv:2010.10525), Sect. I.1; Fourier convention ${\bf b}({\bf k})=\int d^{3}x\,{\bf B}({\bf x})e^{+i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf x}},\ {\bf B}({\bf x})=\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}{\bf b}({\bf k})e^{-i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf x}}$ [src: Eq. 4]. "Attention should be paid to the order of the points on the left-hand side of (2) and the signs and factors on the right-hand side since different conventions are prevalent in the literature" [src: Sect. - Real-space correlator: $\langle B_{i}({\bf x}+{\bf r})B_{j}({\bf x})\rangle=M_{N}(r)P_{ij}+M_{L}(r){\hat{r}}_{i}{\hat{r}}_{j}+\epsilon_{ijk}r_{k}M_{H}(r)$ (arXiv:2010.10525) [src: Eq. 2]; $k$-space: $\langle b_{i}({\bf k})b_{j}^{*}({\bf k}^{\prime})\rangle=\left[\frac{E_{M}(k)}{4\pi k^{2}}p_{ij}+i\epsilon_{ijl}k^{l}\frac{H_{M}(k)}{8\pi k^{2}}\right]\times(2\pi)^{6}\delta^{(3)}({\bf k}-{\bf k}^{\prime})$ [src: Eq. 5], with $E_M$ the power spectrum and $H_M$ the helicity spectrum; "Batchelor spectrum with $E_{M}(k)\propto k^{4}$" and "scale invariant" spectrum with $E_{M}(k)\propto k^{-1}$ [src: Sect. I.1, text after Eq. 5]. Energy density: $\rho_{B}=\frac{1}{2}\langle{\bf B}^{2}\rangle=M_{N}(0)+\frac{M_{L}(0)}{2}=\int dk\,E_{M}(k)$ [src: Eq. 19]. Magnetic helicity "$\equiv\int d^{3}x\,{\bf A}\cdot{\bf B}$" [src: Eq. 9] "is conserved in MHD evolution of plasmas with high electrical conductivity" [src: Sect. I.1, text after Eq. 9].
- **At the electroweak transition** (the review's estimate): the Higgs vacuum value "will be different in different spatial domains", $\Phi=\eta\begin{pmatrix}\sin\alpha\,e^{i\beta}\\ \cos\alpha\,e^{i\gamma}\end{pmatrix}$ (arXiv:2010.10525) [src: Eq. 42], and gradients source a field estimated by $({\bf B}_{V})_{i}\sim-i\frac{2\sin\theta_{w}}{g\eta^{2}}\frac{1}{V}\int_{V}d^{3}x\,\epsilon_{ijk}\partial_{j}\Phi^{\dagger}\partial_{k}\Phi\sim-i\frac{2\sin\theta_{w}}{g\eta^{2}}\frac{1}{V}\int_{\partial V}dS^{j}\,\epsilon_{ijk}\Phi^{\dagger}\partial_{k}\Phi$ [src: Eq. 43]; assuming $|\Phi|\sim\eta$ on the integration surface and Hopf angles "approximately constant in domains of size $\xi$", a random-walk estimate gives $E_{M}(k)\sim\frac{1}{k}B^{2}_{V,\lambda}\sim k^{3}.$ [src: Eq. 45]. III.1.2, text after Eq. 42].

- **During inflation** (Durrer & Neronov 2013, arXiv:1303.7121, Sect. 3.1.1, conformal time $t$, notation of Subramanian 2010): with the coupling $f^{2}F^2$, in Coulomb gauge, $\ddot{A}_{i}+2\frac{\dot{f}}{f}\dot{A}_{i}-\Delta A_{i}=0~,$ (arXiv:1303.7121) [src: Eq. 34], and the power law, adopted "at least for a short time" following Martin & Yokoyama 2008, $f(t)=f_{1}\left(\frac{t}{t_{1}}\right)^{\gamma}\;.$ [src: Eq. 35]; then, after Martin & Yokoyama 2008 and Subramanian 2010 as cited there, $P_{B}=4\pi\frac{k^{2}}{f^{2}}|{\tilde{A}}(k,t)|^{2}\;,\qquad P_{E}~=~4\pi\left|\left(\frac{{\tilde{A}}(k,t)}{f}\right)^{\prime}\right|^{2}$ [src: Eq. 37]. ## Magnetohydrodynamic evolution
<!-- key: magnetohydrodynamic-evolution -->

From Vachaspati 2020 (arXiv:2010.10525), Sect. IV, comoving quantities, conformal time $\eta$ [src: Sect. IV.1, footnote 17]. <!-- num: not a value -->

- Induction equation: $\partial_{\eta}{\bf B}_{c}=\nabla\times({\bf v}_{c}\times{\bf B}_{c})+\frac{1}{\sigma_{c}}\nabla^{2}{\bf B}_{c}$ (arXiv:2010.10525) [src: Eq. 64]; the Navier–Stokes equation (Eq. 65) carries the Lorentz force $\propto{\bf B}\times(\nabla\times{\bf B})$ [src: Sect. II.2.2].
- Non-helical decay, for "vanishing velocity field at the initial time" ("freely decaying MHD turbulence"), whose "detailed evolution" "depends on the initial conditions": the peak moves as $k_{\rm in}(\eta)\propto\eta^{-1/2},\ \ {\rm nonhelical\ case}.$ (arXiv:2010.10525) [src: Eq. 72]. Helical decay: the field approaches maximal helicity, $E_{M}(k,\eta)\to\frac{k}{2}|H_{M}(k,\eta)|$ [src: Eq. 73], and, "Once the field achieves maximal helicity" [src: Sect. IV.1, text after Eq. 73], $k_{\rm in}(\eta)\sim k_{\rm in}(\eta_{0})\left(\frac{\eta_{0}}{\eta}\right)^{2/3},\ \ {\rm helical\ case}$ [src: Eq. 77], "determined by simulations", quoted from its Refs. [179, 186] [src: Sect. IV.1, text before Eq. 77].
- Reconnection-controlled decay (Hosking & Schekochihin 2021, arXiv:2012.01393) [src: abstract]: decaying magnetically dominated turbulence "in the absence of a mean magnetic field" "is governed by the reconnection of magnetic structures, and not by ideal dynamics"; for non-helical fields they propose a new invariant that "expresses the conservation of the random (scaling as $\mathrm{volume}^{1/2}$) magnetic helicity contained in any sufficiently large volume"; they "expect" "the canonical $t^{-2/3}$ power law for helical turbulence when reconnection is fast", "a shallower $t^{-4/7}$ decay in the slow 'Sweet-Parker' reconnection regime", and, for non-helical fields, "for which there currently exists no definitive theory", they predict "$t^{-10/9}$ and $t^{-20/17}$ in the fast- and slow-reconnection regimes, respectively".
- Alfvén-time scaling: "The size of the largest processed eddy scales with the Alfvén speed and grows with time"; in a diagram of Alfvén speed against length scale, "all possible solutions must lie on a line through the origin with a slope proportional to the inverse of the present time" (Brandenburg et al. 2026, arXiv:2606.10863) [src: abstract].
