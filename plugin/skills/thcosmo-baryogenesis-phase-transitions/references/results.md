# Results on baryogenesis, phase transitions and primordial magnetic fields

Scope: results that hold independently of one model: Standard-Model values (asymmetry, sphaleron rate and freeze-out, nature of the electroweak and QCD transitions), theorems and bounds (Davidson–Ibarra, washout), results on nucleation and wall velocities, and on the evolution of magnetic fields; then the history of key results.

Snapshot: 2026-11-13

## The baryon asymmetry and the Standard-Model sphaleron
<!-- key: the-baryon-asymmetry-and-the-standard-model -->

- Observed asymmetry as quoted by Bodeker & Buchmuller 2020: $\eta_{B}\equiv\frac{n_{B}}{n_{\gamma}}=(6.12\pm 0.04)\times 10^{-10}\ ,$ (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Eq. 1]. The measured value and its dataset belong to cosmoskill (`cosmo-cmb`, `cosmo-bbn`).
- Standard-Model electroweak crossover and sphaleron rate from lattice simulations (D'Onofrio, Rummukainen & Tranberg 2014, arXiv:1404.3565) [src: abstract, inline]: "there is no true phase transition between the high-temperature symmetric phase and the low-temperature broken phase", and "the cross-over is sharply defined at $T_c = (159\pm 1)$ GeV"; in the symmetric phase ($T>T_c$) $\Gamma/T^4 = (18\pm 3)\alpha_W^5$; in the broken phase, "in the physically interesting temperature range $130\,{\rm GeV} < T < T_c$", $\log(\Gamma/T^4) = (0.83\pm 0.01)T/{\rm GeV} - (147.7\pm 1.9)$; the freeze-out temperature, "where the Hubble rate wins over the baryon number violation rate", is $T_* = (131.7\pm 2.3)$ GeV.
- The same broken-phase fit is printed by Bodeker & Buchmuller as $\log(\Gamma_{\rm sph}/T^{4})=(0.83\pm 0.01)\frac{T}{\rm GeV}-(147.7\pm 1.9),$ (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Eq. 27], for "the temperature range $130<T<159$ GeV", with "The rate computed on the lattice is larger than the perturbative results [98] but consistent within errors" [src: Sect. II.2, text after Eq. 27]. The review also quotes from its Ref. [144] the symmetric-phase value $\Gamma_{\rm sph}/T^{4}=(8.0\pm 1.3)\times 10^{-7}\approx(18\pm 3)\alpha_{W}^{5}$, "just above the crossover temperature", "factors of $\ln\alpha_{W}$" being "absorbed in the numerical constant", and "Without the Higgs field the rate is $\Gamma\approx(25\,\pm\,2)\,\alpha_{W}^{5}T^{4}$", quoted from its Refs. [252, 248] [src: Sect. II.2, inline, paragraph beginning "Close to the electroweak phase transition", after Eq. 26].
- Sphaleron conversion factors including the variation of the Higgs expectation value and of the sphaleron rate across the crossover (Benso et al. 2026, arXiv:2609.07467) [src: abstract, inline]: "Standard equilibrium calculations in the symmetric and broken phases are performed at constant temperature and hence neglect the fact that both the Higgs expectation value and the sphaleron rate vary as functions of temperature across the electroweak crossover"; from a Boltzmann equation across the crossover, "including higher-order corrections to both the grand canonical partition function and the perturbative Higgs expectation value", they obtain "$C_\text{sph} = 0.3328(5)$ for $B-L$ and $\mathcal{F}_\text{sph} = 0.0279(19)$ for the flavored charges weighted by the charged-lepton Yukawa couplings".
- `[der]` Read against Eq. 39 of arXiv:2009.07294, $C_{\rm sph}$ plays the role of the symmetric-phase coefficient $c_s$ of `formalism.md` for an asymmetry frozen out across the crossover; the paper's own comparison with $c_s$ was not read.
- A baryon asymmetry "can thus also arise from flavored lepton asymmetries with vanishing $B-L$" (arXiv:2609.07467) [src: abstract].

## The electroweak and QCD transitions in the Standard Model
<!-- key: the-electroweak-and-qcd-transitions-in-the -->

- Endpoint of the electroweak first-order line (Kajantie et al. 1996): "non-perturbative evidence for the fact that there is no hot electroweak phase transition at large Higgs masses, $m_H = 95$, 120 and 180 GeV"; "In the minimal standard electroweak theory 70 GeV $<m_{H,c}<$ 95 GeV and most likely $m_{H,c} \approx 80$ GeV" (arXiv:hep-ph/9605288) [src: abstract, inline]. Bodeker & Buchmuller summarise: "for Higgs masses larger than about 70-80 GeV, and thus in the SM, there is no electroweak phase transition but a smooth crossover", so that "Sakharov's third condition is not satisfied" (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. III.1, text after Eq. 47].
- The crossover temperature and sphaleron freeze-out are in the previous section (arXiv:1404.3565) [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- QCD (Aoki et al. 2006, arXiv:hep-lat/0611014) [src: abstract]: from lattice calculations "for physical quark masses", with susceptibilities extrapolated to vanishing lattice spacing for three volumes, "the finite-temperature QCD transition in the hot early Universe was not a real phase transition, but an analytic crossover (involving a rapid change, as opposed to a jump, as the temperature varied)".
- QCD chiral crossover temperature at zero chemical potentials, with 2+1 flavours at physical pion and kaon masses in the continuum limit: $T_c(0)=(156.5\pm1.5)$ MeV (Bazavov et al. 2018, arXiv:1812.08235) [src: abstract, inline].
- Criterion for preserving the asymmetry after a first-order electroweak transition, in the minimal SU(2)+Higgs 3d effective theory, from lattice sphaleron rates over the Higgs phase "by applying previous lattice results for the bubble nucleation": $x(T_c) \lesssim 0.025$, which "translates to $v/T_c \equiv \sqrt{2 \Delta\langle \phi^\dagger \phi\rangle}/T_c \gtrsim 1.33$ for the (gauge-invariant) discontinuity in Higgs condensate"; this "indicates that viability of the electroweak baryogenesis requires the phase transition to be slightly stronger than previously anticipated" (Annala, Rummukainen & Tenkanen 2025, arXiv:2506.04939) [src: abstract, inline]. Their template for such analyses covers "a wide class of beyond the Standard Model theories, in which new fields are heavy enough to be integrated out at high temperature" [src: abstract].

## Nucleation and wall dynamics
<!-- key: nucleation-and-wall-dynamics -->

- **Typical wall velocities.** In the real scalar singlet model, quoted by Athron et al. from their Ref. [514], "the most likely terminal bubble wall velocities" "are either near the speed of sound or are ultra-relativistic with $\gamma\gtrsim 10$"; this "is expected to hold for other models because the friction is strongly peaked near $v_{\text{CJ}}$"; "very low values $v_{w}\lesssim 0.1$ are unlikely unless the driving pressure is weak", although a fluid-approximation scan of the singlet model "found bubble wall velocities as low as $v_{w}=0.1$" and a study of mildly first-order transitions found "a wide range of values for $v_{w}$ including below $0.1$" (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Sect. 5.5.3, paragraph beginning "We now remark on the typical values"].
- In the Standard Model with "a small Higgs mass $m_{H}<90$ GeV", $v_{\rm w}\sim 0.4$ and $\ell_{\rm w}T\sim 25$ were found, and in the MSSM $v_{\rm w}\lesssim 0.1$, as quoted by Bodeker & Buchmuller from their Refs. [253] and [197]; "The wall velocity is quite model dependent: it can vary from $v_{\rm w}\ll 1$ to $v_{\rm w}\sim 1$ in the plasma rest frame" (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. III.1, inline, footnote 11 and text before it].
- **Ultra-relativistic friction.** Höche et al. 2020, resumming soft gauge-boson emission at leading-log accuracy, find "for fast-moving walls" "the scaling of the thermal pressure exerted against the wall to be $P\sim \gamma^2T^4$, independent of the particle masses" (arXiv:2007.10343) [src: abstract, inline]. Gouttenoire et al. 2021 resum "the real and virtual emissions at all leading-log orders" and find the retarding pressure "linear both in the Lorentz boost and in the order parameter, up to a log" (arXiv:2112.07686) [src: abstract]. The debate is in `open-questions.md`.
- **Runaway criterion.** Athron et al. report that their Ref. [511] "argued that the condition $p_{\text{dr}}>p_{\text{fr}}$ for the runaway regime is necessary but not sufficient, because there can be cases where there is both a runaway and steady state solution", and "if a detonation solution is possible, then it would be favored over the runaway" solution (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Sect. 5.5.3, inline, text after Eq. 5.67].
- **Uncertainty budget.** With WallGo for two extensions of the Standard Model, van de Vis et al. 2025 find "several $O(1)$ uncertainties arising from the number of particles taken out of equilibrium, the logarithmically and power enhanced collision integrals, the treatment of thermal masses, the nucleation temperature, the $\tanh$ ansatz, and the perturbative order of the effective potential", while "the linearisation of the Boltzmann equations is generally a good approximation with much smaller associated errors" (arXiv:2510.27691) [src: abstract].
- **Thermal masses in the collision terms.** Including thermal masses "consistently in both the Liouville operator and the collision integrals" gives "only percent-level changes" for top quarks but "strongly suppress[es] the infrared gauge-boson contribution", so that "gauge bosons become subleading and wall velocities are close to those obtained from top-quark friction alone", illustrated "in the singlet-extended Standard Model" (Branchina et al. 2026, arXiv:2607.14867) [src: abstract].

## Leptogenesis bounds
<!-- key: leptogenesis-bounds -->

- **Davidson–Ibarra.** "For a hierarchical spectrum of right-handed neutrinos", a "model independent upper bound on the CP asymmetry" holds; "If $m_{\nu_3} = \sqrt{\Delta m^2_{atm}}$ and the $\nu_R$ are produced thermally, then leptogenesis requires a reheat temperature of the Universe $T_{reh} > M_{\nu_R} > 10^8$ GeV", and "Reasonable estimates of $\nu_R$ production and the subsequent washout", "as made by Buchmuller and Plumacher, imply $M_{\nu_R} > 10^9$ GeV, and $T_{reh} > 10^{10}$ GeV" (Davidson & Ibarra 2002, arXiv:hep-ph/0202239) [src: abstract, inline].
- In Bodeker & Buchmuller's form, with $\kappa^{\text{max}}\sim 1$ and $\kappa^{\text{max}}\sim 0.1$ for thermal and zero initial abundance, "A baryon-to-photon ratio $\eta_{B}\sim 10^{-9}$ then requires a heavy-neutrino mass $M\gtrsim 10^{8}$ GeV and $10^{9}$ GeV for the two different initial conditions, respectively" (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. IV.3.1, inline, text after Eq. 109].
- **Efficiency in strong washout** ("universal, with an uncertainty of about 50%"): $\kappa_{\text{f}}=(2\pm 1)\times 10^{-2}\left(\frac{0.01~\text{eV}}{\widetilde{m}_{1}}\right)^{1.1\pm 0.1}\ .$ (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Eq. 108]; in weak washout $\kappa_{\rm f}$ "strongly depends on the initial conditions" and on the $\Delta L=1$ scattering rate [src: Sect. IV.3.1, text before Eq. 108].
- **Light-neutrino masses.** With $\Delta L=2$ washout, "for the light-neutrino masses one finds $m_{i}<0.12$ eV"; "Assuming $\widetilde{m}_{1}=\mathcal{O}(m_{i})$" [src: Sect. IV.3.1, inline, text before Eq. 111], successful leptogenesis implies $10^{-3}\lesssim m_{i}\lesssim 0.1~\text{eV}\ .$ (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Eq. 111]; "the upper bound on the light-neutrino masses holds only in type-I seesaw models" [src: Sect. IV.3.1, inline, text after Eq. 111]. Without using $m_{\rm atm}$, the bounds are "$m_{3}\lesssim 250$ eV and $M_{1}\gtrsim 2\times 10^{6}$ GeV" [src: Sect. IV.3.1, second paragraph after Eq. 111].
- **Lower scales.** "Flavor effects, together with tuning of the parameters of the seesaw mass matrix, can be used to lower the leptogenesis temperature significantly below $T\sim 10^{10}$ GeV" (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. IV.3.2, inline]; resonant leptogenesis "can be realized with heavy Majorana neutrinos even as light as about 1 TeV" (Pilaftsis & Underwood 2003, arXiv:hep-ph/0309342) [src: abstract].

## Primordial magnetic fields
<!-- key: primordial-magnetic-fields -->

- **Observational window** as summarised by Vachaspati 2020: "A variety of observations impose upper limits at the nano Gauss level on magnetic fields that are coherent on inter-galactic scales while blazar observations indicate a lower bound $\sim 10^{-16}$ Gauss" (arXiv:2010.10525) [src: abstract, inline]. The measurements and their assumptions are outside this skill (cosmoskill `cosmo-cmb` for CMB limits; the blazar halo analyses are astrophysical).
- **Inflationary seeds with backreaction.** "Assuming that back reaction does not spoil inflation", the amplitude of seeds generated during inflation "recalculated to the present epoch cannot exceed $10^{-32}G$ in $Mpc$ scales", which "seems to be too small to be amplified to the observable values by galactic dynamo mechanism" (Demozzi, Mukhanov & Rubinstein 2009, arXiv:0907.1030) [src: abstract]. In the $f^2F^2$ model with $\gamma\sim-2$, "The electron field becomes strongly coupled and we cannot trust our perturbative quantum field theory calculation anymore", a problem "noted first by Demozzi et al (2009)" (arXiv:1303.7121) [src: Sect. 3.1.1, text after Eq. 50].
- **Electroweak relics and the decay law.** "accepted models of turbulent magnetohydrodynamic decay predict that the present-day strength of fields originally generated at the electroweak phase transition (EWPT) without parity violation would be too low to explain the observed scattering of $\gamma$-rays from TeV blazars"; if the decay "is mediated by magnetic reconnection and conserves the mean square fluctuation level of magnetic helicity", "the relic fields would be stronger by several orders of magnitude", which "restores the consistency of the EWPT-relic hypothesis with the observational constraints" (Hosking & Schekochihin 2022, arXiv:2203.03573) [src: abstract].
- **Inverse cascade without helicity.** In the critical examination of its Ref. [197], "It is found that the inverse cascade depends on the Prandtl number" and "decreases in efficiency as the Prandtl number increases"; Ref. [197] "suggests that the non-helical inverse cascade may be completely suppressed at the Prandtl numbers relevant to cosmology ($\sim 10^{8}(T/{\rm keV})^{-3/2}$)" (arXiv:2010.10525) [src: Sect. IV.1]. For a "non-helical white noise spectrum" ($p=2$), "the simulations in [196] do not find an inverse cascade" [src: Sect. IV.1, text after Eq. 72].
- **Attractor spectrum.** "With $E_{M}\propto k^{p}$ at the initial time for small $k$ where $p\geq 4$, the spectrum evolves to the Batchelor spectrum, $E_{M}\propto k^{4}$", in both the non-helical and helical cases; in helical white-noise runs the spectrum "evolved to $E_{M}\propto k^{4}$ once the field became maximally helical, suggesting that the Batchelor spectrum is an attractor" (arXiv:2010.10525) [src: Sect. IV.1, inline, text after Eq. 72; Sect. IV.1, inline, text after Eq. 77].
