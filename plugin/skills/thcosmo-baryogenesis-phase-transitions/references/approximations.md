# Approximations in baryogenesis, phase transitions and magnetic-field evolution

Scope: the approximations the domain relies on, the regime in which a paper validates each and the error it quotes, how each fails, the convention traps, and the list a referee checks.

Snapshot: 2026-11-13

## Perturbative effective potential and its resummation
<!-- key: perturbative-effective-potential-and-its -->

- **What it assumes.** $V_{\rm eff}$ is obtained by integrating out the non-zero-momentum modes around a constant field $\phi$ (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. III.1, text before Eq. 42], usually in a loop expansion with the high-temperature expansion of the thermal functions where used (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Sect. 2.3] `[der]`. Its breakdown: the loop-expansion parameter is $\lambda T/M$ for a scalar and $g^{2}T/M$ with gauge bosons, so "perturbation theory breaks down when $M$ becomes too small" and in the Standard Model "perturbation theory cannot be trusted" (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. III.1, inline, text after Fig. 4 and after Eq. 47]; in the Standard Model the transition is a crossover, established non-perturbatively (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. III.1, text after Eq. 47].
- **Daisy resummation against the 3d EFT.** In the daisy-resummed approach applied to the SMEFT, Croon et al. 2020 find "large uncertainties due to renormalisation scale dependence, which amount to two to three orders-of-magnitude uncertainty in the peak gravitational wave amplitude"; "utilising dimensional reduction in a more sophisticated perturbative approach drastically reduces this scale dependence, pushing it to higher orders", is gauge invariant ("explicitly demonstrated for the Standard Model"), and "avoids an uncontrolled derivative expansion in the bubble nucleation rate" (arXiv:2009.10080) [src: abstract]. Athron et al. say the 3d EFT "appears to be a formally more precise solution when the temperature is large compared to the field" but "is considerably more work to implement, especially in models with many new states beyond the SM" (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Sect. 2.6.2].
- **Limit of the 3d EFT.** In the complex-singlet extension, the 3d EFT with the $\hbar$-expansion gives "gauge-independent results in excellent agreement with those obtained from a direct minimization of the loop-corrected potential", while "large discrepancies between the two methods persist in the $4D$ approaches"; "across most of the parameter space" the 3d EFT reduces the theoretical uncertainty in the GW peak amplitude "by more than an order of magnitude compared to the $4D$ calculations"; but truncating the 3d EFT "at finite operator dimension" adds an uncertainty, higher-dimensional operators "can substantially modify the predicted transition strength and GW signals", which "indicates a potential breakdown of the high-temperature expansion precisely in the region with the lowest transition temperatures, where the strongest GW signals are expected" (Biekötter et al. 2025, arXiv:2511.14831) [src: abstract].
- **Gauge dependence.** The effective potential "is gauge fixing dependent", the pressure at the minima is not (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. III.1, footnote 8]. Within the 3d EFT "with a consistent power-counting scheme", Liu, Qin & Bian 2025 state that they "rigorously establish the gauge invariance of the physical nucleation rate up to two-loop order" (arXiv:2512.05565) [src: abstract].
- **Paper a referee expects cited.** The comparison of methods in arXiv:2009.10080 and the review arXiv:2305.02357, Sect. 2. `[der]`

## Nucleation rate and transition milestones
<!-- key: nucleation-rate-and-transition-milestones -->

- **Linear action and $\beta$.** The linearisation of Eq. 5.46 of arXiv:2305.02357 "is only valid for fast transitions"; "caution must be taken regarding the use of $\beta_{*}$ because this picture is not always valid", for example "if a large barrier persists at low energies" (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Sect. 5.4, text after Eq. 5.49]. The action criterion $S(T(t_{n}))\sim 140,$ [src: Eq. 4.61] holds "only at the electroweak scale and during the radiation dominated era"; for potentials "with a zero-temperature potential barrier between the phases" the action has a finite-temperature minimum that "can be above 140", where the criterion "would suggest there is no nucleation time", yet "it was recently found that it is possible for a transition to complete without having one bubble per Hubble volume", quoted from their Ref. [322], a scenario that "could arise for very strongly supercooled transitions" [src: Sect. 4.7.1, inline, text after Eq. 4.61].
- **Which temperature.** "the nucleation time provides a reasonable approximation for the time at which GWs are generated during a fast transition" but "is a poor indication of the time at which GWs are generated during a slow transition"; the review looks for "an event that corresponds directly to when GWs are generated, in both fast and slow transitions", which "brings us to the event of bubble percolation" (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Sect. 4.7.1, last paragraph]. "To our knowledge, a percolation study has not been performed in expanding spacetime", and "The duration of the phase transition and the time at which GWs are produced could change significantly from the predictions in Minkowski spacetime" [src: Sect. 4.7.2, text after Eq. 4.66].
- **The prefactor and the derivative expansion.** For the classically conformal Abelian Higgs model, Puchades-Ibáñez & Schwaller 2026 compute the nucleation rate "at NLO in the dimensionally reduced theory" and "test the gradient expansion against explicit fluctuation determinants"; a calibrated correction "reproduces the determinant exponent with a mean absolute relative difference of $0.8\%$"; the corrected rate "shifts the preferred gauge coupling by $9\%$ and $13\%$" for NANOGrav 15-year and IPTA DR2, "and the input scale by $18\%$ for NANOGrav"; "After the determinant calibration, the largest theoretical uncertainties arise from uncertainties in the gravitational-wave spectra and the relation between $R_\star$ and $\beta/H$" (arXiv:2609.12098) [src: abstract]. The daisy-resummed approach involves "an uncontrolled derivative expansion in the bubble nucleation rate", avoided by dimensional reduction (arXiv:2009.10080) [src: abstract].
- **Gauge dependence of the rate.** See the effective-potential subsection; the two-loop gauge-invariance result is arXiv:2512.05565 [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Paper a referee expects cited.** Athron et al. 2023 (arXiv:2305.02357), Sects. 4.7 and 5.4, for the milestones and the $\beta$–$R_{\rm sep}$ mapping. `[der]`

## Wall velocity and hydrodynamics
<!-- key: wall-velocity-and-hydrodynamics -->

- **Local thermal equilibrium (LTE).** Assumes $\delta f_j=0$; valid only "provided the bubble wall is not ultra-relativistic"; "the assumption of local thermal equilibrium is not reasonable when $\gamma\gg 1$, so it should not be used to analyze the runaway behavior of bubble walls"; as "a general expectation", LTE predictions of $v_w$ "should be larger than when out-of-equilibrium effects are taken into account", so "If the friction arising from local thermal equilibrium predicts a subsonic deflagration, then a more rigorous analysis should also predict a subsonic deflagration" (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Sect. 5.5.3, text before Eq. 5.62; Sect. 5.5.3, text after Eq. 5.64].
- **Fluid ansatz and its singularity.** The fluid approximation truncates the Boltzmann equation "to first order in these perturbations" and takes "three moments"; the original ansatz has a singularity in the friction "when $v_{w}=c_{s,-}$", which one study adjusted the ansatz to avoid while another "argued that the singularity in friction for $v_{w}=c_{s,-}$, the so-called 'sonic boom', has a physical interpretation stemming from hydrodynamics"; studies going beyond the fluid approximation, or using no ansatz, found friction "a smooth function of the bubble wall velocity" and "no peaks in the friction" (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Sect. 5.5.3, inline, paragraph beginning "Rather than solving the Boltzmann equation directly"]. A 2026 benchmark compares the fluid ansatz with WallGo (Dorsch, Lewicki & Pinto, arXiv:2607.18207) [src: abstract]; its results were not read beyond the abstract. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Collision integrals.** "the typical treatment in the leading-log approximation underestimates the friction" (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Sect. 5.5.3, text after Eq. 5.65]; thermal masses shift the infrared gauge-boson contribution (arXiv:2607.14867) [src: abstract]; the full uncertainty budget is in arXiv:2510.27691 [src: abstract].
- **Bag model.** Eq. 5.52 of arXiv:2305.02357 for $v_{\rm CJ}$ holds "in the bag equation of state"; analytic forms for more general equations of state exist (their Refs. [399, 400]) [src: Sect. 5.5.2, footnote 51]. The LTE fit of Eq. 5.64 holds for $c_{s,f}=c_{s,t}=1/\sqrt{3}$ [src: Sect. 5.5.3, inline, text before Eq. 5.64].
- **Phenomenological friction** (Eq. 5.68 of arXiv:2305.02357) scales as $v_w\gamma$ and "do[es] not match the current expected scaling in the ultra-relativistic regime", "either $\gamma^{1}$ or $\gamma^{2}$" [src: Sect. 5.5.3, text after Eq. 5.69].
- **Paper a referee expects cited.** Bodeker & Moore 2009 and 2017 (arXiv:0903.4099; arXiv:1703.08215) for runaway and its limit; arXiv:2510.27691 for the uncertainty of state-of-the-art calculations. `[der]`

## Transport in electroweak baryogenesis
<!-- key: transport-in-electroweak-baryogenesis -->

- **WKB / semiclassical force.** An expansion in derivatives of the wall mass, "corresponding to an expansion in $(\ell_{\rm w}T)^{-1}$", keeping "the first two terms" (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. III.2, text before Eq. 49]; it describes the thick-wall case, while in the thin-wall limit "the particle-wall interaction is described by quantum reflection and transmission" [src: Sect. III.2].
- **Small wall velocity.** The fluid ansatz is motivated because "For vanishing wall velocity the plasma is in local thermal equilibrium"; "most works have assumed small wall velocity and expanded in $v_{\rm w}$" (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. III.2, paragraph after Fig. 5].
- **Vev-insertion approximation (VIA).** An expansion "in the small (compared to the temperature) off-diagonal masses"; the original literature assumes that the bath "can be regarded as a collection of particles in flavor eigenstates", and the thermal self-energies "are not included in the kinetic equation"; included consistently, the leading-order VIA source vanishes, as shown in their Refs. [255, 256], a result that "does not depend on the specific structure of the self-energies" (arXiv:2508.09989) [src: Sect. 3.4.1].
- **Washout criterion.** $v_n/T\gtrsim 1$ is "a rough estimate" with a gauge-dependent sphaleron energy (arXiv:2508.09989) [src: Sect. 5.4]; the lattice criterion $x(T_c)\lesssim 0.025$ applies "to any BSM theory which maps into the effective theory of eq. 2.19" [src: Sect. 5.4, text after Eq. 5.18].

## Kinetic equations of leptogenesis
<!-- key: kinetic-equations-of-leptogenesis -->

- **Boltzmann equations with integrated rates.** All rates "have to be evaluated as functions of $z$ by performing a thermal average over the corresponding matrix elements" (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. IV.3.1, text after Eq. 100]; "The robustness of the leptogenesis mechanism is largly due to the fact that for neutrino masses below 0.1 eV the $B-L$ asymmetry is essentially determined only by decays and inverse decays", and "relativistic corrections are small" [src: Sect. IV.3.1, text after Fig. 15].
- **Real-intermediate-state (RIS) subtraction.** "The RIS subtraction is a delicate issue. The original, widely used prescription given by Kolb and Wolfram [213] and Harvey et al. [187] turned out to be incorrect, as observed by Giudice et al. [175]" (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. IV.2, footnote 18].
- **One-flavour approximation.** Valid above $T_\tau\sim 10^{12}$ GeV; below it the coherence of the lepton state is destroyed and flavoured equations are needed (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. IV.3.2, text after Eq. 115]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Beyond Boltzmann.** Effective kinetic equations and Kadanoff–Baym equations are treated in arXiv:2009.07294, Sect. IV.6 (not read in this run); for ARS, Hamiltonian and closed-time-path derivations of quantum kinetic equations and "their relations" are reviewed in arXiv:1711.02862 [src: abstract].

## Magnetohydrodynamic evolution
<!-- key: magnetohydrodynamic-evolution -->

- **Decay laws.** The non-helical law of the peak scale (Eq. 72 of arXiv:2010.10525) follows from small-scale dissipation and "a weak inverse cascade", with its Refs. [199, 190], and the helical law (Eq. 77) "has been determined by simulations" [src: Sect. IV.1]; <!-- num: not a value --> the reconnection picture gives different laws in fast and slow (Sweet–Parker) reconnection regimes (arXiv:2012.01393) [src: abstract].
- `[der]` A decay law quoted for a relic field should state the reconnection regime assumed.
- **Prandtl number.** Its Ref. [197] "suggests that the non-helical inverse cascade may be completely suppressed at the Prandtl numbers relevant to cosmology ($\sim 10^{8}(T/{\rm keV})^{-3/2}$)" (arXiv:2010.10525) [src: Sect. IV.1, inline]; the Prandtl numbers of the simulations behind Eqs. 72 and 77 were not read.
- **Two-dimensional simulations.** The isochrone analysis uses "two-dimensional numerical simulations of decaying MHD turbulence", in which "the evolution is governed by the conservation of anastrophy" (arXiv:2606.10863) [src: abstract].
- **"Naive evolution"** of an inflationary spectrum, $P_B$ unchanged on large scales and $B\propto 1/a^{2}$, "We shall show later, in Section 4, that evolution is usually more complicated" (arXiv:1303.7121) [src: Sect. 3.1.1, text after Eq. 51].
- **Definitions of field strength.** "there are several ways to define the 'average magnetic field strength'", line-averaged, energy-averaged ($B_{\rm eff}$) and others (arXiv:2010.10525) [src: Sect. I.1, Eqs. 11–12]; an earlier electroweak estimate was criticised for its definition [src: Sect. III.1.2, text after Eq. 45].

## Convention traps
<!-- key: convention-traps -->

- **$R_\xi$ gauge parameter.** In one early calculation "the $\xi$ parameter is written such that the Landau gauge is $\xi\rightarrow\infty$ while the unitary gauge is in the limit $\xi\rightarrow 0$", opposite to "the standard way of writing the $R_{\xi}$ gauge", in which "the Landau gauge is obtained with $\xi=0$" (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Sect. 2.2.2, footnote 7].
- **Renormalisation scheme constants.** The one-loop potential carries scheme-dependent constants $k_{s,f,V}$ "for the cases of $\overline{\text{MS}}$ and $\overline{\text{DR}}$"; Eq. 2.33 of arXiv:2305.02357 is written for real scalars, Weyl fermions and massive vectors with $n_{\phi}=1,n_{V}=3,n_{f}=2$, without merging degenerate states ("one must also explicitly sum over the different colors for quarks") [src: Sect. 2.2.2, text after Eq. 2.33].
- **Mean bubble separation.** $R_*$ against $R_{\rm sep}$: Athron et al. avoid $R_*$ because "by our convention it could be confused with any radius evaluated at the transition temperature" (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Sect. 5.4, footnote 45]; the factor $(8\pi)^{1/3}$ "is sometimes omitted" and $v_w$ is replaced by $\max(v_w,c_{s,f})$ in the mapping of their Ref. [10] [src: Sect. 5.4, text after Eqs. 5.49–5.50].
- **Which temperature.** The linear approximation of the bounce action "is only valid for fast transitions, where the differences between various transition times/temperatures will be small; however, $\textrm{d}S/\textrm{d}t$ could vary significantly" (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Sect. 5.4, inline, text after Eq. 5.49].
- **Conversion factor.** $c_{s}=(8n_{f}+4)/(22n_{f}+13)$ holds above the electroweak transition with all Yukawas in equilibrium (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. II.3, text after Eq. 39]; across the crossover use the computed $C_{\rm sph}$ (arXiv:2609.07467) [src: abstract].
- **Planck masses.** Dine & Kusenko call $M\approx 10^{18}$ GeV "the so-called reduced Planck mass" while also writing $M_p$ (arXiv:hep-ph/0303065) [src: Sect. III.5, inline]; Bodeker & Buchmuller quote $M_{\rm P}\sim 10^{19}$ GeV (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sect. II.1, inline]. `[der]` Read each formula with its paper's symbol.
- **Magnetic spectra.** The order of points and factors in the correlator differ between papers (arXiv:2010.10525) [src: Sect. I.1, footnote 6]; $P_B$ of Durrer & Neronov (arXiv:1303.7121, Eq. 37) and $E_M$ of Vachaspati (arXiv:2010.10525, Eq. 5) are different normalisations, and no relation between them was read. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Printed slips copied as printed.** Eq. 104 of arXiv:2009.07294 repeats $\eta_B$ after the first $\simeq$; Eq. 5.61 of arXiv:2305.02357 sums over $a$ with summand index $j$; the text after Eq. 2.25 of arXiv:2508.09989 prints "$m_{h}^{2}=125$ GeV"; the text after Eq. 69 of arXiv:hep-ph/0303065 says the energy "decreases like $R^{3}$". No erratum was found for any of them.

## What a referee checks
<!-- key: what-a-referee-checks -->

1. Is the transition first order in a non-perturbative or 3d-EFT treatment, or only in a 4d daisy-resummed potential, and is the renormalisation-scale dependence shown (arXiv:2009.10080) [src: abstract]?
2. Is the gauge dependence of $V_{\rm eff}$, $T_n$ and the washout criterion handled (arXiv:2512.05565; arXiv:2508.09989) [src: abstract; Sect. 5.4]?
3. Which transition temperature is used, and is percolation checked with Eq. 4.66 of arXiv:2305.02357 in supercooled cases [src: Sect. 4.7.2]?
4. Is $\beta/H$ from the linearised action valid, or is $R_{\rm sep}$ computed from the bubble number density (arXiv:2305.02357) [src: Sect. 5.4]?
5. Is $v_w$ computed, and with which approximations (LTE, fluid ansatz, leading-log collisions, thermal masses), or set by hand, and is $v_w=v_{\rm CJ}$ avoided (arXiv:2510.27691; arXiv:2305.02357) [src: abstract; Sect. 5.5.2, inline]?
6. EWBG: is the washout criterion the lattice one ($x(T_c)\lesssim 0.025$), is the CP source semiclassical or VIA, and are EDM bounds applied (arXiv:2506.04939; arXiv:2508.09989) [src: abstract; Sects. 3.4.1, 6.1]?
7. Leptogenesis: flavour regime for the $M_1$ considered, initial $N_1$ abundance, $\Delta L=2$ washout, RIS subtraction (arXiv:2009.07294) [src: Sects. IV.2–IV.3]; type-I seesaw assumed for neutrino-mass bounds [src: Sect. IV.3.1, text after Eq. 111].
8. Affleck–Dine and other condensate mechanisms: baryon isocurvature (arXiv:2602.08781) [src: abstract].
9. Magnetogenesis: backreaction and strong coupling for inflationary models (arXiv:0907.1030; arXiv:1303.7121) [src: abstract; Sect. 3.1.1]; helicity and the decay law assumed for relic strengths (arXiv:2012.01393; arXiv:2203.03573) [src: abstract]; the definition of field strength (arXiv:2010.10525) [src: Sect. I.1].
10. GW predictions built on the transition parameters: templates and their validity are checked in `thcosmo-cosmological-gravitational-waves`.
