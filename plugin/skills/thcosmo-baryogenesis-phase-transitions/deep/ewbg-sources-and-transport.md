## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- Kainulainen, Prokopec, Schmidt & Weinstock 2001 (arXiv:hep-ph/0105295) work "in the collisionless limit in 1+1 dimensions", with spin $s$, kinetic momentum $k_z$, and the complex mass entering through its modulus squared $|m|^2$ and the derivative $\theta'$ of its phase; their kinetic equation "is correct up to second order in gradients (first order in $\hbar$ )" [src: Sect. 6, last paragraph; Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 35]. <!-- num: not a value --> <!-- eq: notation -->
- Barni 2025 (arXiv:2510.21915) fixes "broken phase to the left, symmetric to the right), wall motion to $+z$ in the plasma frame (and particle drift to $-z$ in the wall frame)", with which "the sign of the baryon asymmetry $\eta_{B}$ is physical and unambiguous" [src: Sect. 6.2, Note]. <!-- num: not a value --> <!-- eq: notation -->
- Postma, van de Vis & White 2022 (arXiv:2206.01120) label left- and right-handed (or flavour) states $L$, $R$ with off-diagonal mass $m_{LR}$; "In the case of fermions, the term ‘flavor’ is (ab)used to refer to the two different chiralities, which obtain distinct thermal corrections from the plasma" [src: Sect. 5, first paragraph]. <!-- num: not a value --> <!-- eq: notation -->
- `[der]` The papers place the broken phase on different sides of the wall and orient the wall motion differently; compare signs only after aligning these choices. The skill's conventions block is `formalism.md#conventions`.

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — the semiclassical force from a gradient expansion of the Kadanoff–Baym equations
<!-- key: step-1-the-semiclassical-force-from-a-gradient -->

Kainulainen et al. start from the equation of motion of the Wightman function $G^<$ in the collisionless limit and expand in gradients, an expansion that "corresponds to an expansion in the de Broglie wave length divided by the wall width", with "$\ell_{dB}/\ell_{w}\ll 1$" "typically" in electroweak baryogenesis (arXiv:hep-ph/0105295) [src: Sect. 6, first paragraph]. The constraint equations put the spin-$s$ quasiparticles on the shell $\Omega^{2}_{s}\;\equiv\;k_{0}^{2}-k_{z}^{2}-|m|^{2}+\frac{s}{k_{0}}|m|^{2}\theta^{\,\prime}\;=\;0,$ [src: Eq. 30]; its additional poles "correspond to unphysical, but harmless, tachyonic modes" from the expansion in inverse powers of $k_0$ [src: Sect. 2.1, text after Eq. 34]. To second order in gradients the kinetic equation becomes $k_{0}\partial_{t}g^{s}_{0}+k_{z}\partial_{z}g^{s}_{0}-\left(\frac{1}{2}{|m|^{2}}^{\,\prime}-\frac{s}{2k_{0}}(|m|^{2}\theta^{\,\prime})^{\,\prime}\right)\partial_{k_{z}}g^{s}_{0}=0.$ [src: Eq. 36], and, integrated over positive and negative frequencies, the Liouville equation $\partial_{t}f_{s\pm}+v_{s\pm}\partial_{z}f_{s\pm}+F_{s\pm}\partial_{k_{z}}f_{s\pm}=0,$ [src: Eq. 37] with $v_{s\pm}=\frac{k_{z}}{\omega_{s\pm}},$ [src: Eq. 39] and the spin-dependent, CP-violating force $F_{s\pm}=-\frac{{|m|^{2}}^{\,\prime}}{2\omega_{s\pm}}\pm\frac{s(|m|^{2}\theta^{\prime})^{\prime}}{2\omega_{0}^{2}}.$ [src: Eq. 40]. <!-- num: not a value -->

What the paper credits to earlier work: the form of the force "was already found by Joyce, Prokopec and Turok [29]"; the canonical-variable WKB equations of their Ref. [15] (Joyce, Prokopec & Turok 1996) "are not invariant under reparametrization of the wave functions"; and their Ref. [22] (Cline & Kainulainen 2000; Cline, Joyce & Kainulainen 2000, arXiv:hep-ph/0006119; and a proceedings by Kainulainen) "introduced the kinetic momentum as a physical variable" and "obtained the unique reparametrization invariant transport equations identical with (37) and (39-40)"; what is new is "a controlled first principle derivation of these equations without any a priori assumptions" (arXiv:hep-ph/0105295) [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 40]. They also "prove that there is no CP-violating force at first order in $\hbar$ for scalar fields", and, having "pointed out that the axial current equation corresponds to the first velocity moment of the kinetic equation", write that "This explains why the CP-violating source appears at first order in moment expansion", citing their Refs. [29, 15] for that order [src: Sect. 6, second and third paragraphs]. The wall-frame force to second order in derivatives, in the review's notation, is Eq. 49 of arXiv:2009.07294, in `formalism.md#electroweak-baryogenesis`. <!-- num: not a value -->

### Step 2 — the CP-odd Boltzmann equation, its source and its moments
<!-- key: step-2-the-cp-odd-boltzmann-equation-its-source -->

Cline & Kainulainen classify the perturbations by CP parity, each parity carrying its own chemical potential μ and out-of-equilibrium part δf, $\mu\equiv\mu_{e}+s_{k_{0}}\mu_{o}$ and $\delta\!f\equiv\delta\!f_{e}+s_{k_{0}}\delta\!f_{o}$ [src: Sect. III.1, Eq. 11]; the CP-even equation is $L[\mu_{e},\delta f_{e}]={\cal S}_{e}+\delta{\cal C}_{e},$ [src: Eq. 15] and the CP-odd one $L[\mu_{o},\delta f_{o}]={\cal S}_{o}+\delta{\cal C}_{o}\,,$ (arXiv:2001.00568) [src: Eq. 18], with the source, the two rows of one display, ${\cal S}_{oh}=-v_{w}\gamma_{w}hs_{\rm p}\frac{(m^{2}\theta^{\prime})^{\prime}}{2EE_{z}}f^{\prime}_{v_{w}}+v_{w}\gamma_{w}hs_{p}\frac{m^{2}(m^{2})^{\prime}\theta^{\prime}}{4E^{2}E_{z}}\left({f^{\prime}_{v_{w}}\over E}-\gamma_{w}f^{\prime\prime}_{v_{w}}\right)$ [src: Eq. 19]. In their text, FH06 is Fromme & Huber 2006 (Step 3): "A number of CP-odd source terms computed in FH06, proportional to $\mu_{e}$ and $\delta f_{e}$ , were dropped during evaluation, since they are higher order in gradients" [src: Sect. III.2, text after Eq. 19], and "Setting $h=-1$ and $s_{\rm p}={\rm sign}(p_{z})$ , eq. (19) agrees with FH06 up to an overall sign" [src: Sect. III.2, text after Eq. 19]. On helicity: one "often concentrates only on the negative helicity sector", which "is reasonable when masses vanish in front of the wall"; otherwise "one should compute the asymmetry in the positive helicity sector as well" [src: Sect. III.2, first paragraph and footnote 5]. <!-- num: not a value --> Moments are taken "by integrating over $p$ , weighted by $(p_{z}/E)^{l}$ , and dividing by a normalization factor" $N_1$ [src: Sect. III.3, text before Eq. 20], defining the velocity perturbations $u_{\ell}\equiv\Big\langle\!\left(\frac{p_{z}}{E}\right)^{\!\ell}\delta f\Big\rangle\,.$ [src: Eq. 22] and the moment equations $\Big\langle\!\left(\frac{p_{z}}{E}\right)^{\!\ell}L\Big\rangle=\Big\langle\!\left(\frac{p_{z}}{E}\right)^{\!\ell}({\cal S}+\delta{\cal C})\Big\rangle\,.$ [src: Eq. 23]. Keeping the full $v_w$ dependence, the condition for a singular fluid matrix gives $v_{c}=-\left({D_{2}\over D_{1}}\right)_{v_{w}=v_{c}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad v_{c}=1\,,$ [src: Eq. 47], whereas the small-velocity FH06 matrix gives $v_{c}^{\prime}=\left|{K_{4}^{\scriptscriptstyle\rm FH}\over K_{1}^{\scriptscriptstyle\rm FH}}\right|^{1/2}_{v_{w}=0}\!.$ [src: Eq. 48], close to the sound speed for light particles, "a mere coincidence due inappropriate use of the small $v_{w}$ -approximation" [src: Sect. III.6, text after Eq. 48]. They trace the "widespread notion" that "diffusion is inefficient for wall speeds exceeding the plasma sound speed" to their Ref. [44], Huet & Nelson 1995, "apparently", and call it "not true" [src: Sect. III.6, first paragraph]. <!-- num: not a value -->

### Step 3 — the truncated fluid network
<!-- key: step-3-the-truncated-fluid-network -->


The truncated network, written for one effective species by Barni, is $-\,D_{1}\,\mu^{\prime}(z)+u^{\prime}(z)+\alpha_{1}(z)\,\mu(z)+\beta_{1}(z)\,u(z)=S_{1}(z),$ [src: Eq. 113] and $-\,D_{2}\,\mu^{\prime}(z)+R\,u^{\prime}(z)+\alpha_{2}(z)\,\mu(z)+\beta_{2}(z)\,u(z)=S_{2}(z),$ (arXiv:2510.21915) [src: Eq. 114], the collision terms being absorbed into the coefficients of μ and u [src: Sect. 7.1, text after Eq. 114].

The older network of Fromme & Huber shows where the small-velocity truncation enters. Following their Ref. [7] (Cline, Joyce & Kainulainen 2000), "A crucial assumption made in that work is that it is the kinetic momentum that is conserved in the scatterings of WKB particles"; the fluid-type ansatz is $f_{i}({\bf x},{\bf p})=\frac{1}{e^{\beta[\gamma_{w}(E_{i}+v_{w}p_{z})-\mu_{i}]}\pm 1}+\delta f_{i}({\bf x},{\bf p}).$ (arXiv:hep-ph/0604159) [src: Eq. 14], with $\delta f_i$ carrying no density [src: Sect. 3, text after Eq. 14]. Averaging with weights 1 and $p_z/E_0$ and expanding "in the wall velocity, keeping only the linear order" [src: Sect. 3, text before Eq. 19], they obtain $v_{w}K_{1}\mu_{2}^{\prime}+v_{w}K_{2}(m^{2})^{\prime}\mu_{2}+u_{2}^{\prime}-\left\langle{\bf C}[f]\right\rangle=S_{\mu}$ and $-K_{4}\mu_{2}^{\prime}+v_{w}\tilde{K}_{5}u_{2}^{\prime}+v_{w}\tilde{K}_{6}(m^{2})^{\prime}u_{2}-\left\langle\frac{p_{z}}{E_{0}}{\bf C}[f]\right\rangle=S_{\theta}+S_{u}$ (arXiv:hep-ph/0604159) [src: Eq. 19], the two rows of one display, whose principal source is $S_{\theta}=-v_{w}K_{8}(m^{2}\theta^{\prime})^{\prime}+v_{w}K_{9}\theta^{\prime}m^{2}(m^{2})^{\prime}$ [src: Eq. 20, second row]; $S_{\mu,u}$ are "formally" one order higher in gradients and "contribute only a small fraction to the total source term" [src: Sect. 3, text after Eq. 20]. `[der]` The linear-in-$v_w$ truncation of this system is the one whose singular matrix Cline & Kainulainen discuss in their Eq. 48.

### Step 4 — solving the network: diffusion form and Green's function
<!-- key: step-4-solving-the-network-diffusion-form-and -->

Eliminating $u$ algebraically (subtracting R times Eq. 113 from Eq. 114, "Provided $\beta_{2}-R\beta_{1}\neq 0$") gives one second-order equation, $-\,D_{\rm eff}(z)\,\mu^{\prime\prime}(z)+V_{\rm eff}(z)\,\mu^{\prime}(z)+\Gamma_{\rm eff}(z)\,\mu(z)=S_{\rm eff}(z),$ (arXiv:2510.21915) [src: Eq. 117; Sect. 7.1, text before Eq. 116]. When the coefficients vary slowly over the source, $-\,D\,\mu^{\prime\prime}(z)+\gamma_{w}v_{w}\,\mu^{\prime}(z)+\Gamma\,\mu(z)=S(z),$ [src: Eq. 118], whose first-derivative coefficient "is the wall–frame drift term and therefore naturally appears as $\gamma_{w}v_{w}$ , rather than only in its low–velocity limit" [src: Sect. 7.1, text after Eq. 118]. For constant D and Γ and $\mu(\pm\infty)=0$, $\mu(z)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\!dz^{\prime}\,G(z-z^{\prime})\,S(z^{\prime}),$ [src: Eq. 119] with $\lambda_{\pm}=\frac{\gamma_{w}v_{w}\pm\sqrt{\gamma_{w}^{2}v_{w}^{2}+4D\Gamma}}{2D},\qquad\lambda_{+}>0,\quad\lambda_{-}<0.$ [src: Eq. 121] fixing the decay lengths into the two phases; "the symmetric–phase tail — the one most relevant for baryon production through weak sphalerons — is broader for small $\Gamma$ and large $D$" [src: Sect. 7.1, text after Eq. 122]. <!-- num: not a value -->

The multi-species version, "equivalent to the formalism introduced in refs. [47, 44], consisting of coupled second-order diffusion equations for the local particle densities" in which the VEV-insertion formalism is written, is $D\mu^{\prime\prime}+v_{w}\mu^{\prime}-\delta C[\mu]=S.$ (arXiv:2001.00568) [src: Eq. 62], with $\mu=(\mu_{t_{\rm L}},\mu_{b_{\rm L}},\mu_{t_{\rm R}},\mu_{h})^{T}$ and $S=(S_{t},0,-S_{t},0)^{T}$; "The unsourced equation for $\mu_{b_{\rm L}}$ is usually omitted in the literature", and baryon-number conservation then fixes $\mu_{b_L}$ in terms of the top potentials, "a reasonable approximation" for their fiducial case [src: Sect. VI, text after Eq. 62]. Mapping the semiclassical fluid equations onto it, in "The standard way to do this", their Ref. [28], "by eliminating $u$ from the WKB equations (35), neglecting all $(m^{2})^{\prime}\mu$ and $(m^{2})^{\prime}u$ terms", gives, as the first row of their Eq. 63, $D_{\rm WKB}={D_{2}-v_{w}^{2}D_{0}\over D_{0}\Gamma_{\rm tot}},$ with $S_{\rm WKB}$ and $\delta C_{\rm WKB}$ in the other two rows [src: Eq. 63; Sect. VI, text before Eq. 63]; the numerator of $D_{\rm WKB}$ "is just the determinant of the matrix A", so the critical-speed condition "corresponds to zero diffusion length" [src: Sect. VI, footnote 11]. <!-- num: not a value -->

The supersymmetric route, summarised by Lee, Cirigliano & Ramsey-Musolf "for pedagogical purposes" from their Ref. [10], Huet & Nelson 1995 (arXiv:hep-ph/9506477), writes for each density $\partial_{\mu}J_{i}^{\mu}=S_{i}^{CP}+S_{i}^{CP\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\raisebox{0.0pt}{\small$\diagup$}}+S_{i}^{\rm sph}\ ,$ (arXiv:hep-ph/0412354) [src: Eq. 69], relates densities to chemical potentials in local thermal equilibrium (Eq. 70) and, "Dropping terms of ${\cal O}(\mu_{i}^{3})$" [src: Sect. IV.1, text before Eq. 71], $n_{i}={k_{i}(m_{i}/T)\,T^{2}\over 6}\mu_{i}\ ,$ [src: Eq. 71], and assumes "approximate chemical equilibrium between SM particles and their superpartners" and between members of left-handed doublets [src: Sect. IV.1, first paragraph]. Fast Yukawa and strong-sphaleron rates, $\Gamma_{Y}\gg\Gamma_{M}^{-}$ (estimated by the authors of their Ref. [10]) and $\Gamma_{ss}\gg\Gamma_{M}^{\pm}$ for $\kappa'\sim\mathcal{O}(1)$, tie Q and T to the Higgs density, and the diffusion approximation $\mathbf{H}=-D_{h}\nabla H$ leads to ${\dot{H}}-{\bar{D}}\nabla^{2}H+{\bar{\Gamma}}H-{\bar{S}}=\mathcal{O}(\delta_{ss},\delta_{Y})\ \ \ ,$ [src: Eq. 76; Sect. IV.1, text before Eq. 76]. Ignoring wall curvature and taking $\bar\Gamma$ "nonzero and constant for $\bar{z}>0$" (the broken phase in their orientation), the solution in the unbroken phase is $H(\bar{z})={\cal A}\,e^{v_{w}\bar{z}/\bar{D}}\,$ (arXiv:hep-ph/0412354) [src: Eq. 78; Sect. IV.1, text before Eq. 78]. Two corrections recorded there: the strong-sphaleron expressions of their Refs. [27, 10] "have erroneously omitted a factor of $1/N_{C}$ [28]", [27] being Giudice & Shaposhnikov 1994 and [28] Moore 1997 [src: Sect. IV.1, text after Eq. 69]; and their Eq. 77 differs from Ref. [10], differences "we believe result from an algebraic error", whose "numerical impact … is not significant" [src: Sect. IV.1, footnote 6]. <!-- num: not a value -->

### Step 5 — from the chiral asymmetry to the baryon asymmetry
<!-- key: step-5-from-the-chiral-asymmetry-to-the-baryon -->

The baryon density obeys a linear first-order equation driven by the weak sphalerons acting on $\mu_{B_L}$; with $n_B(+\infty)=0$, "no asymmetry far ahead of the wall", $n_{B}(z)=-\int_{z}^{+\infty}dz^{\prime}\,\frac{n_{f}N_{c}}{2v_{w}\gamma_{w}}\Gamma_{ws}(z^{\prime})\mu_{B_{L}}(z^{\prime})T^{2}\exp\left[-\frac{An_{f}}{2v_{w}\gamma_{w}}\int_{z}^{z^{\prime}}\Gamma_{ws}(z^{\prime\prime})dz^{\prime\prime}\right].$ (arXiv:2510.21915) [src: Eq. 98]; of the entropy-normalised result (his Eq. 99) he writes that "the kernel multiplying $\mu_{B_{L}}$ is the source that converts chiral charge into baryon number where sphalerons are active, while the exponential is the washout that erases $n_{B}$ when the wall is too slow" [src: Sect. 6.2, text after Eq. 98]. Barni states that "the overall minus sign in front of Eq. (99), which is not found in general in the literature, follows from the correct solution" [src: Sect. 6.2, Note]. <!-- num: not a value -->

In Cline & Kainulainen's form, "Following for example ref. [46] (but including the full Lorentz-covariant relations)" [src: Sect. IV.1, text before Eq. 60], $\eta_{B}={405\,\Gamma_{\rm sph}\over 4\pi^{2}v_{w}\gamma_{w}g_{*}T}\int dz\,\mu_{\!B_{\rm L}}f_{\rm sph}\,e^{-45\Gamma_{\rm sph}|z|/4v_{w}\!\gamma_{w}}.$ (arXiv:2001.00568) [src: Eq. 60], with $\mu_{B_{\rm L}}={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{q}\mu_{q_{\rm L}}$ [src: Sect. IV.1, text after Eq. 60]; "To our knowledge, the $1/\gamma_{w}$ factor in front has been omitted in previous literature. It arises from the change of variable $dt\to dz/(v_{w}\gamma_{w})$ with $z$ in the rest frame of the wall" [src: Sect. IV.1, footnote 8]. <!-- num: not a value -->

### Step 6 — the vev-insertion source, and why it vanishes at leading order
<!-- key: step-6-the-vev-insertion-source-and-why-it -->

**The usual derivation.** Following the literature it compares with (their Refs. [31, 40]), Postma, van de Vis & White expand the self-energy to second order in the mass insertion, $\Pi^{\lambda}_{II,(2)}=-(\delta M^{2})_{IJ}G^{\lambda}_{JJ}(\delta M^{2})_{JI},$ (arXiv:2206.01120) [src: Eq. 37], take the Wightman functions at leading order, expand the masses about $x$, $m_{IJ}^{2}(y)=m_{IJ}^{2}(x)+(x-y)^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}m^{2}_{IJ}(x)+{\cal O}(\partial^{2}).$ [src: Eq. 40], an expansion that "also assumes a slowly varying bubble wall background" but "is different from the derivative expansion, i.e. the expansion in terms of the diamond operator, of the KB equations" [src: Sect. 3.5, text after Eq. 40], and recover the usual CP-violating source from the next-to-leading term, $S^{(2)}_{\rm CPV}=2{\rm Im}\left[m_{LR}^{2}\partial_{\mu}m^{2}_{RL}\right]\int d^{4}y\,(y-x)^{\mu}\Big(G_{RR}^{<}(x,y)G_{LL}^{>}(y,x)-G^{>}_{RR}(x,y)G_{LL}^{<}(y,x)\Big).$ [src: Eq. 41], the two rows of one display.

**Why it cancels.** Postma et al. "believe that this erroneous result", the non-zero source, "is a consequence of the non-1PI approach, which is not a systematic perturbative expansion, and obscures the correct counting of diagrams"; with 1PI self-energies, "the VIA source cancels to all orders in the vev-insertion expansion" [src: Sect. 3.5, last two paragraphs]. In their abstract: the VIA source "corresponds to the leading order contribution in the gradient expansion of the Kadanoff-Baym (KB) equations", and "it vanishes both for bosons and fermions, both in the perturbative and in the resummed approach", "a result of a cancellation between different terms in the KB equations, and not of a pathology in the vev-insertion approximation itself" (arXiv:2206.01120) [src: abstract]. The reason given "in hindsight": "the VIA expansion source arises at leading order in the derivative expansion, at which order no distinction between a constant and an evolving background is made – and certainly no source term is expected in the former case" [src: Sect. 5, second paragraph]. The cancellation requires the collision term, "non-zero because of the non-trivial flavor dynamics", under the assumption that "the interactions with the thermal bath are fast, and to a good approximation the bath can be regarded as a collection of particles in flavor eigenstates" [src: Sect. 5, fourth paragraph]. What they leave open: "We do expect that the VIA approximation gives a non-zero source term at the next (first) order in the derivative expansion, and that the inclusion of thermal effects may lead to resonant enhancement. But it remains to be seen whether this will be enough to save large classes of models from the ever tightening constraints imposed by the non-observation of EDMs" [src: Sect. 5, last paragraph]. <!-- num: not a value -->

**The same conclusion by another route.** Kainulainen 2021, deriving both formalisms from the closed-time-path formalism with one-loop thermal corrections, states that "the VIA-method is based on an assumption [emphasised in the original] that leads to an ill-defined source term containing a pinch singularity, whose regularisation by thermal effects leads to ambiguities including spurious ultraviolet and infrared divergences", and that "the VIA-source term is not just ambiguous, but that it does not exist" (arXiv:2108.08336) [src: abstract]. `[der]` The two papers agree on the verdict but not on the cause. Postma et al. write that the problem does not stem "from the existence of so-called pinch singularities (which are absent when the fully resummed propagator solutions are used)" (arXiv:2206.01120) [src: Sect. 5, third paragraph]. Postma 2021 had located the VIA source in "the same term in the kinetic equation as the semi-classical source, but at leading order in the derivative expansion (the expansion in diamond operators)", with a second derivative expansion "which we estimate to be valid for a bubble width larger than the inverse thermal width" (arXiv:2107.05971) [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value -->

The 2025 review by two of the same authors and de Vries draws the practical rule: "The VIA source terms should not be used for the calculation of the CP-asymmetry, and conclusions drawn in the literature on the viability of baryogenesis models based on the VIA source, including work [28, 214] by the authors, should be reconsidered"; "Whether thermal corrections can lead to non-trivial flavor dynamics at higher order has not been fully worked out yet"; "for a more degenerate spectrum the classical source term gives a lower bound on the asymmetry (barring accidental cancellations)" (arXiv:2508.09989) [src: Sect. 3.5]. <!-- num: not a value -->

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **One network, three comparisons (Cline & Kainulainen 2020, fiducial model).** VIA against WKB: "The VEV-insertion method predicts 10-50 times larger asymmetry than does the semiclassical method"; for small $v_w$ the difference "is dominated by the source terms", and for $v_{w}\gtrsim 0.4$ "a further deviation is caused by the $v_{w}$ -dependence of the evolution equations"; with the wall width, "the semiclassical BAU decreases rapidly", the VIA "much slower" [src: Sect. VI, text after Eq. 66]. Fluid network against diffusion equation: the two semiclassical approximations "agree remarkably well, in particular for large $v_{w}$"; "For $L_{w}T\gtrsim 2$ the two semiclassical approximations agree very well" at $v_{w}=0.5$, the difference at small $L_w$ being expected "because the diffusion approximation neglects several $m^{2\prime}$ -corrections included in the fluid equations" [src: Sect. VI, text after Eq. 66]. Small-velocity FH06 equations against full $v_w$ dependence: "all approximations agree very well for $v_{w}\ll 1$"; "For larger $v_{w}$ the predictions differ significantly", the FH06 prediction plummeting "as $v_{w}$ approaches the sound speed", while the improved treatment "does nothing special near $v_{w}=v_{s}$" and decays smoothly as $v_w\to 1$ (arXiv:2001.00568) [src: Sect. V, text after Fig. 3 caption]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Why the VIA yield was larger.** Postma et al. write that "The larger yield was mainly due to the CP-violating VIA source containing only one spacetime derivative", so that "there is no partial cancellation in particle asymmetries when integrated over the bubble wall, in sharp contrast to the semi-classical and flavor oscillation sources" (arXiv:2206.01120) [src: Sect. 5, last paragraph].
- **FH06 refinements.** Fromme & Huber had resolved "certain discrepancies between computations starting from the Dirac equation and the Schwinger--Keldysh formalism" and kept "the W-scatterings at finite rate"; in a model "with one Higgs doublet, augmented by dimension-6 operators", these refinements "lead to an increase in the baryon asymmetry by a factor of up to about 5" (arXiv:hep-ph/0604159) [src: abstract].
- **Historical VIA claims.** Riotto 1998 found that "non-Markovian features enhance the final baryon asymmetry by at least two orders of magnitude" compared with previous estimates, so that "$|\sin φ_μ|$ as small as $10^{-3}$ (or even smaller) is enough" in the MSSM (arXiv:hep-ph/9803357) [src: abstract]; Lee, Cirigliano & Ramsey-Musolf 2004 showed that "the relaxation transport coefficients may be resonantly enhanced under appropriate conditions on electroweak model parameters" and that such enhancements "can mitigate the impact of similar enhancements in the CP-violating source terms" (arXiv:hep-ph/0412354) [src: abstract]. `[der]` Both are worked out in the vev-insertion framework, whose leading-order source Step 6 shows to vanish.
- **Thermal corrections in the semiclassical source.** Kainulainen 2021 gives "semiclassical Boltzmann equations for thermal WKB-quasiparticles with source terms up to the second order in gradients that contain both dispersive and finite width corrections", and shows "that the collisional source terms reported earlier in the semiclassical literature are also spurious, and vanishes in a consistent calculation" (arXiv:2108.08336) [src: abstract].
- **Code.** BARYONET implements the WKB force with the fluid network and is described as "a comprehensive, self-contained pedagogical computation" from "the derivation of the semiclassical, CP-dependent force to the formulation and solution of the transport equations" (arXiv:2510.21915) [src: abstract].

## EDM bounds: how far they constrain the source
<!-- key: edm-bounds-how-far-they-constrain-the-source -->

- **Two operator examples, from one review.** For a CP-violating top Yukawa (dimension six), through two-loop Barr–Zee diagrams the electron EDM is $\frac{d_{e}}{e}=-m_{e}\frac{8n_{c}Q_{t}^{2}\alpha_{\rm em}}{(4\pi)^{3}}g(x_{t})\times{\rm Im}(Y_{t})\,,\qquad g(x_{t})=\frac{x_{t}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}{\rm d}x\frac{1}{x(1-x)-x_{t}}\ln\left(\frac{x(1-x)}{x_{t}}\right)\,,$ (van de Vis, de Vries & Postma 2025, arXiv:2508.09989) [src: Eq. 6.17]; for couplings to top quarks, the HfF+ limit "gives a limit $|v^{2}{\rm Im}(Y_{t})|<4\cdot 10^{-4}$ which essentially kills this source for EWBG", citing their Refs. [25, 26, 28], the electron-EDM limits having been "roughly 250 times weaker at the time of earlier reviews" [src: Sect. 6.1.3, text after Eq. 6.18]. The neutron EDM through the Weinberg operator gives $|v^{2}{\rm Im}(Y_{t})|<0.1$, "far weaker than the electron EDM limit" [src: Sect. 6.1.3, text after Eq. 6.21]. <!-- num: not a value --> For a CP-violating Higgs–gauge operator, "The HfF+ limit leads to $v^{2}C_{\varphi\tilde{W}}<10^{-6}$ while the neutron and diamagnetic EDMs are roughly three orders of magnitude weaker", citing their Ref. [410] [src: Sect. 6.1.3, inline, text after Eq. 6.22]. Scope: "these are far from complete and … every explicit EWBG model requires its own analysis"; the "most simple EWBG scenarios with BSM physics around the TeV scale" are "stringently constrained", "even" in two-Higgs-doublet models where EDMs arise at two loops [src: Sect. 6.1.3, first paragraph; Sect. 6.1.4, first paragraph]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Ways out listed by that review.** Cancellations ("rather ad hoc"); flavour-changing CP violation such as a top–charm interaction, for which "A rough estimate" of the induced down-quark EDM "renders EDM limits irrelevant", while "More likely, other flavor observables should be considered"; decoupling the transition from the CP source; two-step transitions in which the CP-violating scalar vev disappears; spontaneous CP breaking by a CP-odd field (arXiv:2508.09989) [src: Sect. 6.1.4, items 1–5].
- **How the verdict moved.** In 2000, Cline, Joyce & Kainulainen, with the chargino sector as "the dominant source" in the MSSM, found that "a large enough baryon asymmetry can be created for the phase of the complex mu parameter as small as ~ 0.001, which is consistent with bounds from the neutron electric dipole moment" (arXiv:hep-ph/0006119) [src: abstract]; in 2017 Cline described EWBG as "severely challenged in its traditional settings: the Minimal Supersymmetric Standard Model, and in more general two Higgs doublet models", pointing to a singlet with its own CP-violating couplings, in a model where "dark matter plays the leading role in creating a CP asymmetry" (arXiv:1704.08911) [src: abstract]. The 2020 review's verdict on perturbative models is in `open-questions.md#is-perturbative-electroweak-baryogenesis-still`. <!-- num: not a value -->

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Using the leading-order VIA source.** It vanishes (Step 6); the review asks that conclusions based on it "be reconsidered" (arXiv:2508.09989) [src: Sect. 3.5]. Check: which source a paper uses, and whether its comparison with data rests on it.
- **Three slips of the small-velocity literature.** `[der]` The spurious quench of diffusion at the sound speed (Step 2), the missing inverse Lorentz factor in front of the η_B integral (Step 5) and the diffusion form used for thin walls (first item of Variants) are each caught by Cline & Kainulainen 2020; the places are given there. <!-- eq: notation -->
- **Signs.** The Cline–Kainulainen source differs from FH06 by an overall sign (Step 2); Barni's overall minus sign "is not found in general in the literature" (arXiv:2510.21915) [src: Sect. 6.2, Note]. Check the phase orientation before comparing signs (Conventions).
- **Spurious collisional sources.** Collisional source terms of the earlier semiclassical literature "are also spurious, and vanishes in a consistent calculation" (arXiv:2108.08336) [src: abstract].
- **Resonant flavour sources.** The flavour source "has only been calculated in toy models, for fermions only away from the resonance, and the resonance regime is not fully understood yet (it may require including higher-order gradient corrections for an accurate description)" (arXiv:2508.09989) [src: Sect. 3.5, item 3].
