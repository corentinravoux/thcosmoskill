## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- WallGo (Ekstedt et al. 2024, arXiv:2411.04970) works with the distance from the wall $\xi=-\bar{u}^{\mu}_{w}x_{\mu}\,,$ [src: Eq. 3.5], with $\bar{u}_{w}^{\mu}=\gamma_{w}(v_{w},0,0,1)$ perpendicular to the wall four-velocity $u_{w}^{\mu}=\gamma_{w}(1,0,0,v_{w})$ [src: Eq. 3.6], the measure $\int_{{\bf p}}=\int\frac{{\rm d}^{d}p}{(2\pi)^{d}}$ with $d=3$ [src: Sect. 3.2, inline, text after Eq. 3.4], and absorbs the equilibrium contributions of all plasma particles into $V^{\rm eff}$ [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 3.4]. <!-- num: not a value --> <!-- eq: notation -->
- Bodeker & Moore 2009 (arXiv:0903.4099) work in the wall rest frame with the plasma moving "along the $-z$ direction" and label the two phases $h_1$ and $h_2$, with momenta and occupancies "defined based on their symmetric phase ( $z>0$ ) values" [src: Sect. III; footnote 7]. <!-- num: not a value --> <!-- eq: notation -->
- Ai, Garbrecht & Tamarit 2021 (arXiv:2109.13710) "use the metric signature (+,-,-,-)" [src: Sect. 1, last paragraph]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- `[der]` The papers use $v_w$, $\gamma_w$ or $\gamma$ for the wall speed and Lorentz factor, $T_{\rm n}$ or $T_{\rm nuc}$ for the nucleation temperature, and $p_{\rm fr}$, $F/A$ or $\mathcal{P}$ for the friction pressure; the skill's own conventions block is `formalism.md#conventions`.

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — the scalar equation of motion with plasma friction, and the hydrodynamic closure
<!-- key: step-1-the-scalar-equation-of-motion-with -->

In WallGo, the scalar fields obey $\partial^{2}\phi_{i}+\frac{\partial V^{{\mbox{\scriptsize eff}}}(\bm{\phi},T)}{\partial\phi_{i}}+\sum_{a}\frac{\partial m_{a}^{2}}{\partial\phi_{i}}\int_{{\bf p}}\frac{1}{2E}\delta f^{a}(p^{\mu},\xi)=0\,,$ (arXiv:2411.04970) [src: Eq. 3.4], the last term being "the friction caused by out-of-equilibrium particles", dominated "in a SM-like plasma" by "the top quark, the electroweak gauge bosons and possibly the scalar fields" [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 3.6]. The classical treatment of the scalar field rests on the premise that "over sufficiently large distances and times $t,L\gg T^{-1}$ , scalar fields behave classically" [src: Sect. 3.2, footnote 3]. Because the equation depends on the temperature profile, two more equations come from energy-momentum conservation in the wall frame, the wall "assumed to be planar and moving in the $z$ -direction": $T^{30}=w\gamma_{\rm pl}^{2}v_{\rm pl}+T^{30}_{\rm out}=c_{1}\,,$ and $T^{33}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{z}\phi_{i})^{2}-V^{{\mbox{\scriptsize eff}}}(\bm{\phi},T)+w\gamma_{\rm pl}^{2}v_{\rm pl}^{2}+T^{33}_{\rm out}=c_{2}\,,$ [src: Eq. 3.7], whose constants $c_1$, $c_2$ come from solving the hydrodynamic equations "for the given $T_{\rm n}$ and $v_{w}$" [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 3.8]. <!-- num: not a value --> The wall shape is a tanh ansatz with a width $L_i$ and offset $\delta_i$ per field [src: Eq. 3.9], which "does not solve the scalar EOM eq. (3.4) exactly, but in most known cases the impact of this approximation on the value of the wall velocity is small" [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 3.9]. The shape parameters minimise the action of their Eq. 3.10; since $\delta_1=0$ is imposed "to fix the center of the wall", the minimisation "is not sufficient to ensure that the total pressure on the wall" vanishes [src: Sect. 3.2, text before Eq. 3.13], and the wall velocity is the root of $P_{\rm tot}(v_{w})=0\,,$ [src: Eq. 3.13]. What the hydrodynamics alone does, citing their Refs. [96, 97, 53, 54]: "even for vanishing $\delta f^{a}$ , the wall still feels a backreaction force from the plasma that gets heated"; for deflagration and hybrid solutions it "is an increasing function of $v_{w}$ , but for detonation solutions, it decreases with $v_{w}$" ("see e.g. [54, 52]"), and "In many cases, this hydrodynamic backreaction effect is already sufficient to obtain a static deflagration or hybrid solution" [src: Sect. 3.2, last paragraph]. <!-- num: not a value -->


### Step 2 — the friction in local equilibrium: an entropy jump, not a $\gamma^2$ law
<!-- key: step-2-the-friction-in-local-equilibrium-an -->

Ai, Garbrecht & Tamarit show that for a perfect fluid in local equilibrium, with $\omega=\rho_{f}+p_{f}=Ts,\quad s=\frac{\partial p_{f}}{\partial T}\,.$ [src: Eq. 20], the backreaction force is $\displaystyle\frac{F_{\rm back}}{A}=\Delta\{(\gamma^{2}-1)Ts\}\,\quad{\rm in\ local\ equilibrium}\,.$ (arXiv:2109.13710) [src: Eq. 21]. Their Ref. [39] is Barroso Mancha, Prokopec & Swiezewska 2020, who state that "When local thermal equilibrium is attained, we find a strong friction force which grows as the Lorentz factor squared, such that the bubbles quickly reach stationary state and cannot run away" (arXiv:2005.10875) [src: abstract]. Ai et al. resolve that claim of an effective friction "scaling as $\gamma_{w}^{2}$" in local equilibrium: the result "was derived assuming constant plasma temperature and velocity throughout the wall", whereas the friction "originates from inhomogeneous temperature distributions, such that the $\gamma_{w}^{2}$ -scaling of the effective force is violated" (arXiv:2109.13710) [src: abstract]. In their words, "a non-constant fluid temperature across the bubble wall is a necessary condition for the backreaction force to exist in local equilibrium" [src: Sect. 1, paragraph beginning "The result in Ref. [39]"]. <!-- num: not a value -->

The closure they add is "a new matching condition for the hydrodynamic quantities in the plasma valid in local equilibrium and tied to local entropy conservation"; with it, "bubble velocities in local equilibrium can be determined once the parameters in the equation of state are fixed", "where we use the bag equation in order to illustrate this point", and, using the bag equation of state, "there is a critical value of the transition strength $\alpha_{\rm crit}$ such that bubble walls run away for $\alpha>\alpha_{\rm crit}$" (arXiv:2109.13710) [src: abstract, inline]. For deflagrations the force "grows with velocity", "but remains below the quantitative estimates" of their Ref. [39], "as was noted before in Ref. [43]", while "for detonations the force actually decreases with the wall velocity" (arXiv:2109.13710) [src: Sect. 1, paragraph beginning "In this work"], their Ref. [43] being Balaji, Spannowsky & Tamarit 2020. Balaji et al. themselves argue that hydrodynamic effects in deflagrations and the entropy change across the wall "are related and can be understood from the conservation of the entropy of the degrees of freedom in local equilibrium, leading to subluminal speeds for both deflagrations and detonations" (arXiv:2010.08013) [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value -->

The model-independent LTE fit built on this condition is in `formalism.md#bubble-walls-and-hydrodynamics` (Eq. 5.64 of the review, from arXiv:2303.10171).

### Step 3 — the Boltzmann equation with the wall force, linearised
<!-- key: step-3-the-boltzmann-equation-with-the-wall -->

WallGo writes $f^{a}({\bf p},\xi)=f^{a}_{{\rm eq}}({\bf p},\xi)+\delta f^{a}({\bf p},\xi)\,,$ with $f^{a}_{{\rm eq}}=\frac{1}{\exp{[p_{\mu}u^{\mu}_{\rm pl}(\xi)/T(\xi)]\pm 1}}\biggr|_{E_{a}={\bf p^{2}}+m_{a}^{2}}\,,$ (arXiv:2411.04970) [src: Eq. 3.14], where $f_{\rm eq}$ includes "all such thermal masses to one-loop" [src: Sect. 3.3, footnote 5]. The distribution evolves by $\Bigl(p^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}{\bf\nabla}m_{a}^{2}\cdot\nabla_{{\bf p}}\Bigr)f^{a}({\bf p},x^{\mu})=-\mathcal{C}_{a}[\bm{f}]\,,$ [src: Eq. 3.15], the second term being "the classical force term that the particles experience due to the mass change caused by the passing wall" [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 3.15]. Assuming $|\delta f^{a}|\ll f^{a}_{\rm eq}$ ("the validity of this assumption can be checked in WallGo") [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 3.14], the equation is linearised: $\Bigl(-p_{\mu}\bar{u}^{\mu}_{w}\partial_{\xi}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\xi}(m_{a}^{2})\bar{u}^{\mu}_{w}\partial_{p^{\mu}}\Bigr)\delta f^{a}=-\mathcal{C}_{ab}^{\rm lin}[\delta f^{b}]+\mathcal{S}_{a}\,,$ with source $\mathcal{S}_{a}=\Bigl(p_{\mu}\bar{u}^{\mu}_{w}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\xi}(m_{a}^{2})\bar{u}_{w}^{\mu}\partial_{p^{\mu}}\Bigr)f^{a}_{{\rm eq}}\,,$ [src: Eqs. 3.16–3.17]. The linearised collision term "contains mixing", which "is usually not considered in computations of $v_{w}$" [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 3.17]. <!-- num: not a value -->

The same equation in the parametrisation of earlier literature (their Refs. [22, 18, 17]), as recapitulated by Laurent & Cline 2020 under "Old formalism", who write the equilibrium distribution "in the rest frame of the bubble wall, taken to be planar and moving to the left" with $\beta=1/T$ [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 1], with the wall-frame distribution perturbed by a chemical potential, a temperature and a velocity perturbation, is $f={1\over e^{X}\pm 1}={1\over e^{\beta\gamma(E-vp_{z})-\delta X}\pm 1},$ and $\delta X(z)=\mu+\beta\gamma[\delta\tau(E-vp_{z})+u(p_{z}-vE)]$ (arXiv:2007.10935) [src: Eq. 1], inserted into ${\mathbf{L}}[f]=\left({p_{z}\over E}\partial_{z}-{(m^{2})^{\prime}\over 2E}\partial_{p_{z}}\right)(f_{v}+\delta\!f)\cong-{\cal C}[f]$ [src: Eq. 2].

### Step 4 — solving it: three moments (fluid ansatz) or a spectral expansion
<!-- key: step-4-solving-it-three-moments-fluid-ansatz-or -->

**Fluid ansatz.** "three moments are taken, by integrating over momenta $\int d^{\,3}p$ with the respective weight factors" 1, $\gamma(p_{z}-vE)$ and $\gamma(E-vp_{z})$, giving three coupled ordinary differential equations for $q\equiv(\mu,u,\delta\tau)^{\intercal}$, $A_{v}q^{\prime}+\Gamma q=S$ (arXiv:2007.10935) [src: Eq. 3], "with a rate matrix $\Gamma$ from the moments of the collision term" and a source "$S\sim v\beta^{2}(m^{2})^{\prime}$" [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 3]. The ansatz goes back to the "seminal work" of Moore & Prokopec 1995 (arXiv:hep-ph/9506475), which WallGo describes as using "the so-called fluid ansatz" and taking "three moments of the Boltzmann equations" (arXiv:2411.04970) [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 3.17]; it "was generalised to a larger number of moments" in their Ref. [51] [src: Sect. 3.3, footnote 7]. <!-- num: not a value -->

The singularity: "The $A_{v}$ matrix depends on $v$ in such a way that $A_{v}^{-1}\Gamma$ becomes singular at $v=c_{s}$", so that "the perturbations $q$ must strictly vanish in front of the wall for $v>c_{s}$"; Ref. [16] of that paper "has argued that this kind of behavior is unphysical", because "There is no reason why diffusion should be suddenly quenched in the vicinity of a supersonic wall" (arXiv:2007.10935) [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 3]. Laurent & Cline 2022 then rederive the equations "without linearizing in the background temperature and fluid velocity", and "The resulting equations have a finite solution for any wall velocity" (arXiv:2204.13120) [src: abstract].

**Spectral method.** Following Laurent & Cline 2022, WallGo expands $\delta f^{a}(\chi,\rho_{z},\rho_{\parallel})=\sum_{i=2}^{M}\sum_{j=2}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\delta f^{a}_{ijk}\bar{T}_{i}(\chi)\bar{T}_{j}(\rho_{z})\tilde{T}_{k}(\rho_{\parallel})\,,$ (arXiv:2411.04970) [src: Eq. 3.18] on restricted Chebyshev polynomials that vanish at the boundaries [src: Eqs. 3.19–3.20], with compact momentum coordinates $\rho_{z}(p_{z})=\tanh\Bigl(\frac{p_{z}}{2T}\Bigr)\,,$ and $\rho_{\parallel}(p_{\parallel})=1-2\exp{\Bigl(-\frac{p_{\parallel}}{T}\Bigr)}\,.$ [src: Eq. 3.29]. On the grid of their Eqs. 3.22–3.24, the Boltzmann equation becomes "a (dense) matrix equation" [src: Sect. 3.3, text before Eq. 3.25]; "The convergence of the approximation by expanding in polynomials is exponential in the number of basis polynomials" [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 3.20]. <!-- num: not a value -->

### Step 5 — the collision terms: leading log, and what lies beyond
<!-- key: step-5-the-collision-terms-leading-log-and-what -->

"at leading logarithmic order, only $2\to 2$ scatterings contribute", citing their Refs. [101, 102, 103] (Arnold, Moore & Yaffe) (arXiv:2411.04970) [src: Sect. 3.4, first paragraph; References]. The linearised collision term is a nine-dimensional momentum integral over $|M_{ac\rightarrow de}|^{2}$ with the factor 1/4 explained in the text [src: Eq. 3.30; Sect. 3.4, text after Eq. 3.32], of which four integrations are removed by the δ-function and the rest done "using a Monte Carlo method" [src: Sect. 3.4, last paragraph before Sect. 3.4.1]. Without vacuum masses, $\mathcal{C}_{ab}^{\rm lin}[\delta f^{b}]=T^{2}\hat{\mathcal{C}}^{\rm lin}_{ab}[\delta f^{b}]\,.$ [src: Eq. 3.34], a rescaling WallGo uses, leaving "the inclusion of vacuum masses to future work" [src: Sect. 3.4, text after Eq. 3.34]. <!-- num: not a value --> The leading log: small-angle $2\to 2$ scatterings with $d\sigma\sim\frac{d\theta}{\theta}$, softened by screening, give "a multiplicative $O(|\log g|)$ logarithmic enhancement" [src: Sect. 3.4.1, first paragraph]. The implementation adopted neglects external masses and regulates internal lines with asymptotic thermal masses, at LO $m_{{\mbox{\tiny\rm{$V$}}},\infty}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}m_{\mbox{\tiny\rm{D}}}^{2}\,,$ and $m_{q,\infty}^{2}=2m_{q}^{2}\,,$ [src: Eq. 3.35]; "This approximation was found to lead to an overestimate of the quark diffusion constant by 50% [102], consistent with the expected $O(|\log g|^{-1})$ relative errors" [src: Sect. 3.4.1, second paragraph]. Beyond leading log, "Collision integrals for naively forbidden $1\to 2$ scatterings must be included, momentum dependence and scalar field background dependence must be resummed in the particle self-energies, and gauge and fermion fields experience non-local Landau damping"; this is "beyond the scope of the current work" [src: Sect. 3.4.1, last paragraph]. <!-- num: not a value -->

How large the residual errors are was assessed with WallGo for two benchmarks, the singlet extension (xSM) and the inert doublet model (IDM): "linearising the Boltzmann equation is almost always a valid approximation, as the associated error vanishes both near and far from equilibrium" (van de Vis et al. 2025, arXiv:2510.27691) [src: Sect. 5, second paragraph]; a relative $\mathcal{O}(1)$ error in $\delta f_1$ "induces a relative $\mathcal{O}(\delta f_{1}/f_{\text{eq}})$ error in the pressure", and "In the limit of large collisions, deviations from equilibrium vanish as $\delta f/f_{\text{eq}}\propto 1/\mathcal{C}$ , while in the opposite limit of small collisions, $\delta f/f_{\text{eq}}=\mathcal{O}(1)$" [src: Sect. 5, text after Eq. 5.1]. "Using a standard one-loop approximation", the parametric size of the errors in the scalar equation of motion is "somewhat smaller than those in the Boltzmann equation", but they "more directly affect the wall speed": "even though the errors are parametrically suppressed by a single power of the coupling", "this offers little benefit at realistic coupling values" [src: Sect. 5, second paragraph after Eq. 5.1], and the overall verdict is that "substantial theoretical progress is required to achieve predictions more reliable than order-of-magnitude estimates" [src: Sect. 5, last paragraph]. <!-- num: not a value -->

`[der]` "Full" collision terms in the sense of the audit question therefore mean, in the papers read, the full leading-log two-body scattering integrals solved without an ansatz on $\delta f$ (Laurent & Cline 2022, WallGo, Branchina et al. 2025), not collision terms beyond leading log, for which the papers read give estimates only (the next-to-leading-log row of Table 3 of arXiv:2510.27691). Branchina, Conaci, De Curtis & Delle Rose use "a spectral algorithm that allows a first-principle treatment of the collision integral" (arXiv:2510.21942) [src: abstract]; Branchina et al. 2026 add thermal masses "consistently in both the Liouville operator and the collision integrals" (arXiv:2607.14867) [src: abstract], with the result given in `results.md#nucleation-and-wall-dynamics`. <!-- num: not a value --> <!-- eq: notation -->

### Step 6 — ultra-relativistic walls, leading order: the runaway criterion
<!-- key: step-6-ultra-relativistic-walls-leading-order -->

Bodeker & Moore 2009 start from the WKB force on a particle, $\frac{dp_{z}}{dt}=-\frac{1}{2E}\frac{dm^{2}}{dh}\frac{dh}{dz}\,.$ [src: Eq. 8], whose total over the plasma per unit wall area is $\frac{F}{A}=-\int dz\frac{dh}{dz}\sum_{a}\frac{dm^{2}_{a}}{dh}\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}\,2E_{a}}f_{a}(\vec{p},z)$ (arXiv:0903.4099) [src: Eq. 9], particles following classical (WKB) trajectories "If the interface is not too sharp" [src: Sect. III, first paragraph]. For $\gamma\gg 1$, in the wall frame the reflection coefficients "are exponentially suppressed", scatterings occur "at a time-dilated rate" "and can be neglected", and the incoming particles "have received no signal that the wall is approaching, and are in equilibrium" [src: Sect. III, inline, text after Eq. 10]. Summing the momentum change of each particle crossing the wall, $-\frac{F}{A}=\sum_{a}\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}f_{a}(p,{\rm in})\Big(p_{z,\rm in}-p_{z,\rm out}\Big)\,.$ [src: Eq. 11], and energy and $p_\perp$ conservation give $\frac{F}{A}=\sum_{a}(m^{2}_{a}(h_{2})-m^{2}_{a}(h_{1}))\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{p,h_{1},a}}f_{a}(p,{\rm in})+{\cal O}(1/\gamma^{2})\,.$ [src: Eq. 13], "identical" to the mean-field potential difference of their Eq. 5 [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 13]. The mean-field approximation is $V_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(h_{2})-V_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(h_{1})\simeq\sum_{a}\Big(m^{2}_{a}(h_{2})-m^{2}_{a}(h_{1})\Big)\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{f_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm B}(E_{p,h_{1},a})}{2E_{p,h_{1},a}}\,.$ [src: Eq. 5], "the same as determining $V_{\scriptscriptstyle T}(h_{2})$ by performing a 2’nd order Taylor series expansion of $V_{\scriptscriptstyle T}$ about $h_{1}$ – provided that the potential is at most quartic, and particle squared masses quadratic, in the condensate values of scalar fields" [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 5]. <!-- num: not a value -->

The criterion, as their conclusions state it, is "valid at the 1-loop level of analysis": with $\tilde V$ the vacuum potential plus "the quadratic-order Taylor expansion of the thermal part $V_{\scriptscriptstyle T}$ of the potential about the high-temperature phase", "If the low-temperature phase is preferred at $T_{\rm nuc}$ by $\tilde{V}$ , then the wall runs away; otherwise it has a finite terminal velocity" (arXiv:0903.4099) [src: Sect. V]. Its scope there: "such strong mean-field type transitions generally – but not always – involve interfaces which propagate at virtually the speed of light", while "in the MSSM or other theories where the first order nature of the transition is fluctuation induced, the bubble wall can never run away" [src: Sect. V]. The pressure is finite at large γ because "The density of particles increases as $\gamma$ , due to Lorentz contraction. But the mean momentum that each particle induces, in climbing the wall, goes as $1/\gamma$" [src: Sect. III, paragraph after footnote 8]. <!-- num: not a value --> The later qualification, that this condition is "necessary but not sufficient", is in `results.md#nucleation-and-wall-dynamics`.

### Step 7 — next order: transition radiation and the $\gamma^1$ against $\gamma^2$ pressure
<!-- key: step-7-next-order-transition-radiation-and-the -->

**$\gamma^1$ (Bodeker & Moore 2017).** For the emission of a vector boson with broken-phase mass $m_{V,\rm h}$ (their Eq. 27), the momentum integral "counts the wall-frame density of incoming particles, and scales as $\gamma T^{3}$", with a small-$k^0$ divergence that "should be cut off by the mass scale", so that $\mathcal{P}_{1\to 2}\sim g^{2}C_{2}[R]n_{p}m\propto\gamma g^{2}mT^{3}\,.$ (arXiv:1703.08215) [src: Eq. 28]. Their conclusions: "Since this term rises linearly with $\gamma$ , it will limit the $\gamma$ -factor of the wall and prevent a true “runaway.” However, under criteria where the leading-order calculation predicts runaway, we still expect a parametrically large $\gamma$ -factor, $\gamma\sim m/g^{2}T$" [src: Sect. VI]. They also note that the occupancy of emitted bosons, $f(k)\sim\frac{n}{\Delta k^{3}}\sim\frac{g^{2}\gamma T^{3}}{m^{3}}\,.$ [src: Eq. 40], "can exceed the “saturation” occupancy $1/g^{2}$" for "large $\gamma>m^{3}/g^{4}T^{3}$" [src: Sect. V, text after Eq. 40]. <!-- num: not a value -->

**$\gamma^2$ (Höche et al. 2020).** At fixed order, $\mathbb{P}_{1\to 2}\approx\mathbb{P}_{1\to 1}\times\sum_{i}C_{i}\,\frac{\alpha_{i}}{2\pi}\,\log\frac{x\left(\gamma T\right)^{2}}{\vec{k}_{\perp}^{\,2}}\,,$ (arXiv:2007.10343) [src: Eq. 2], a logarithm "in principle unbounded, which makes it necessary to resum Eq. (2) to all orders in perturbation theory" [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 2]. Performing "this resummation both analytically and numerically at leading logarithmic accuracy", the numerical one "by means of a particle shower in the broken phase of the electroweak theory" ("The two approaches agree to the 10% level") [src: abstract], they approximate $P\simeq P_{1\rightarrow 1}+\gamma^{2}\times\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{P_{\rm FC}}{\gamma^{2}}\right)\,,$ [src: Eq. 78]; "in the limit of large wall velocities, the net thermal pressure experienced by the bubble wall in its rest frame is simply a constant fraction ( $\sim 0.5\%$ ) of the total pressure of the gas of symmetric-phase particles that couple directly to the wall" [src: Sect. VI, inline, text after Eq. 77], a statement made "up to $\log\gamma$ effects stemming from thresholds and the running of the couplings" in the sentence before it [src: Sect. VI, inline, text after Eq. 77]. <!-- num: not a value -->

**$\gamma^1$ again (Gouttenoire, Jinno & Sala 2021).** For non-confining transitions, "where particles simply acquire a mass in the broken phase" [src: Sect. 6.1, first paragraph], having resummed "the real and virtual emissions at all leading-log orders" [src: abstract] with "a leading-log Sudakov resummation" [src: Sect. 6.1, text after Eq. 98], they obtain $\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{{{\text{LO}}}}+\mathcal{P}_{{{\text{LL}}}},$ (arXiv:2112.07686) [src: Eq. 95], with $\mathcal{P}_{{{\text{LO}}}}=\sum_{a}g_{a}c_{a}\,\frac{\Delta m^{2}\,T_{\rm nuc}^{2}}{24},\qquad c_{a}=1~(1/2)~\textrm{for bosons (fermions)},$ [src: Eq. 96] and, as the last row of the display for $\mathcal{P}_{\rm LL}$, $\simeq\frac{\kappa\,\zeta(3)}{\pi^{3}}\left[\sum_{a,b,c}\nu_{a}g_{a}C_{abc}\right]\alpha\,\text{ln}\dfrac{m_{c,h}}{\mu}\,\gamma\,m_{c,h}\,T_{\rm nuc}^{3},$ [src: Eq. 98], with $\kappa\approx 4$ [src: Sect. 6.1, text after Eq. 98]. "The regime of validity of Eq. (98) is $\mu\ll m_{c,h}\ll E_{a}$ and thin walls", and it "neglects additional effects due to the presence of the thin layer of reflected vector bosons in front of the bubble wall" [src: Sect. 6.1, text after Eq. 98]. They "have recovered the linear $\gamma$ increase found in [57], in contrast to [1] in which $\mathcal{P}_{{\text{LL}}}\propto\gamma^{2}$ has been found (see App. F for more details on this discrepancy)", [57] being Bodeker & Moore 2017 and [1] Höche et al. 2020 [src: Sect. 6.1, text after Eq. 98; References]. The terminal Lorentz factor follows from $\Delta V=\mathcal{P}_{{{\text{LO}}}}+\mathcal{P}_{{{\text{LL}}}}(\gamma_{{{\text{LL}}}}),$ [src: Eq. 103], and the factor at collision is $\gamma_{\rm coll}\simeq\textrm{Min}\big[\gamma_{{{\text{LL}}}},\,\gamma_{\rm run}\big].$ [src: Eq. 106]. <!-- num: not a value -->

`[der]` The two families differ in what is resummed and in which momentum transfer is kept (the review's account of the Ward-identity objection is in `open-questions.md#friction-on-ultra-relativistic-walls-gamma-1-or`); App. F of arXiv:2112.07686, where the discrepancy is discussed, was not read.

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **LTE only (hydrodynamic backreaction).** Cheapest; WallGo notes that "For the xSM, it was even shown that the local thermal equilibrium (LTE) approximation gives a reasonable estimate of the wall velocity [52]", while "as seen for the Standard Model with a light Higgs mass in section 6.2 and the Inert Doublet Model in section 6.3, the wall velocity is significantly overestimated in LTE" (arXiv:2411.04970) [src: Sect. 3.2, last paragraph], [52] being Laurent & Cline 2022 [src: References]. Laurent & Cline find that the departure from equilibrium "only gives a small correction, which changes the wall velocity by 2% on average", except "in some borderline situations", where "it can generate a small additional pressure that transforms a would-be detonation into a deflagration" (arXiv:2204.13120) [src: Sect. VI, third paragraph], in the singlet scalar extension. In the same singlet benchmark, Branchina et al. find instead that "out-of-equilibrium corrections substantially enhance the effective friction on the expanding front, leading to slower wall velocities and broader wall profiles compared to the equilibrium case" (arXiv:2510.21942) [src: abstract]. `[der]` The two singlet-model results disagree on the size of the out-of-equilibrium correction; the parameter points compared were not checked.
- **Fluid ansatz against the spectral solution.** Dorsch, Lewicki & Pinto "show that the two approaches yield essentially the same wall velocity in the regime of reasonably mild phase transitions, $α\lesssim 0.01$"; "the agreement is excellent when only top-quark annihilation is considered, but a noticeable discrepancy appears once scattering processes are included"; investigating "the limitations of linearizing the Boltzmann equation when the fluid Ansatz is applied to stronger phase transitions", they find that "non-linear contributions induce significant shifts in the predicted terminal velocity as $α\to 1$" (arXiv:2607.18207) [src: abstract, inline].
- **Phenomenological friction calibrated on Boltzmann solutions.** Krajewski et al. write the matching with a friction parameter, $\frac{T_{+}}{T_{-}}=\frac{\gamma_{-}}{\gamma_{+}}\,\left(1+\tilde{\eta}\,\gamma_{+}\,v_{+}\right),$ with $\tilde{\eta}\equiv\frac{T_{+}\Delta S}{w_{+}\gamma_{+}^{2}v_{+}^{2}},$ (arXiv:2603.24583) [src: Eqs. 12–13], and find that $\tilde\eta$ "follows a simple power-law dependence on the transition strength ( $\propto v_{n}^{4}/T_{n}^{4}$ )", which "accurately reproduces the terminal wall velocities obtained from the full microscopic analysis performed using WallGo" [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Approximation schemes of Laurent & Cline.** Fixed wall velocity, LTE, and "a numerical fit of the out-of-equilibrium pressure and pressure gradient", which "allows future practitioners to quantitatively estimate the effects of friction, without having to solve the Boltzmann equations" (arXiv:2204.13120) [src: Sect. VI, fourth paragraph; Sects. V.1–V.3, titles]; "the methods discussed here do not address the question of how large $\gamma_{w}$ is in the case of highly relativistic walls" [src: Sect. VI, fourth paragraph]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Which method when** `[der]`, from the statements above: LTE first, as an upper bound on $v_w$ (`approximations.md#wall-velocity-and-hydrodynamics`); a linearised Boltzmann solution (the fluid ansatz in the mild regime where Dorsch et al. find agreement, the spectral method otherwise) when the LTE answer is a deflagration or near a detonation boundary; for runaway candidates, the Bodeker–Moore mean-field test, then the next-order pressure of Step 7, whose $\gamma$ scaling is disputed; the ultra-relativistic regime is outside the scope of the Boltzmann solvers (Laurent & Cline's caveat above).

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **The fluid ansatz at the speed of sound.** In the old fluid equations $A_{v}^{-1}\Gamma$ "becomes singular at $v=c_{s}$", and the perturbations "must strictly vanish in front of the wall for $v>c_{s}$" (arXiv:2007.10935) [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 3], "an artifact that can be overcome by more carefully truncating the full Boltzmann equation" [src: abstract]. Laurent & Cline 2022 hold that the singularity "can be induced by an overly restrictive ansatz", "while recognizing that the linearization of the background perturbations is the root of the problem, as was argued in Ref. [54]" (arXiv:2204.13120) [src: Sect. I, paragraph beginning "In this work"], [54] being Dorsch, Huber & Konstandin, "A sonic boom in bubble wall friction" [src: References]; in their Sect. II.1, "Ref. [54] has argued that this linearization leads to a singularity of the fluid equations" [src: Sect. II.1, paragraph beginning "In previous studies"]. The check is the non-linearised background of Laurent & Cline 2022 (Step 4). The review's account of the "sonic boom" interpretation is in `approximations.md#wall-velocity-and-hydrodynamics`.
- **Constant temperature across the wall.** A non-constant temperature is "a necessary condition for the backreaction force to exist in local equilibrium", and the $\gamma_{w}^{2}$ scaling derived with a constant one "is an artificial result of the inappropriate assumption" (arXiv:2109.13710) [src: abstract; Sect. 1, paragraph beginning "The result in Ref. [39]"]; the check is the entropy-conservation matching of the same paper.
- **A static solution that is not reached.** "Simulations of [98] suggest that this static solution does not always get reached in a dynamical simulation, and the wall would in fact run away without additional friction effects" (arXiv:2411.04970) [src: Sect. 3.2, footnote 4], [98] being Krajewski, Lewicki & Zych 2024 [src: References].
- **Linearising the fluid-ansatz equations at strong transitions.** Linearising the Boltzmann equation "when the fluid Ansatz is applied to stronger phase transitions", "non-linear contributions induce significant shifts in the predicted terminal velocity as $α\to 1$, even though the non-linear contribution to the wall pressure remain quantitatively small"; the authors also highlight "the possible limitations of the WKB approach itself when applied to the regime of strong transitions", so that "a treatment beyond the WKB approximation may be needed" (arXiv:2607.18207) [src: abstract, inline].
- **Infrared gauge bosons.** Their contribution "is artificially enhanced in the massless approximation" (arXiv:2607.14867) [src: abstract]; the check is to include thermal masses in both the Liouville operator and the collision integrals (`results.md#nucleation-and-wall-dynamics`).
- **Leading-log truncation and infrared power enhancements.** In the xSM and IDM benchmarks, "infrared power enhancements can dominate individual channels of the collision integrals", and capturing them "correctly at leading order requires including background field dependence and the full momentum dependence of the hard-thermal-loop self-energies" (van de Vis et al. 2025, arXiv:2510.27691) [src: Sect. 5, second paragraph]; their Table 3 gives the size (see Numbers).
- **Negative mass squared inside the wall.** The "superluminal propagation is a spurious artefact of the quasiparticle approximation" (arXiv:2510.27691) [src: Sect. 5, second paragraph].
- **Phase-space saturation at large γ.** For $\gamma>m^{3}/g^{4}T^{3}$ "we expect that it is no longer safe to consider different radiations as independent processes; there are nonperturbatively large interactions between emitted quanta" (arXiv:1703.08215) [src: Sect. V, text after Eq. 40]; Gouttenoire et al. use the saturation scale as the infrared cut-off in the non-abelian case (arXiv:2112.07686) [src: Sect. 6.1, text after Eq. 98]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Mean-field criterion used beyond one loop.** It is "valid at the 1-loop level of analysis"; "For cases where couplings are relatively large or where two-loop thermal effects are important, it would also be necessary to understand how to incorporate these effects" (arXiv:0903.4099) [src: Sect. V]. <!-- num: not a value -->
