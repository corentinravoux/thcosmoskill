## FLRW background and the Friedmann equations
<!-- key: flrw-background-and-the-friedmann-equations -->

Laine & Procacci 2026 (arXiv:2607.06983), with curvature κ, a perfect fluid of energy density $e$ and pressure $p$, conformal time τ:

- Perfect-fluid energy-momentum tensor of the background: $\mathbin{\bar{T}^{\mu}}_{\nu}\;=\;(\bar{e}+\bar{p})\,\bar{u}^{\mu}\bar{u}_{\nu}+\bar{p}\,\mathbin{\delta}^{\mu}_{\nu}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\bar{T}_{\mu\nu}\;=\;(\bar{e}+\bar{p})\,\bar{u}_{\mu}\bar{u}_{\nu}+\bar{p}\,\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}\;.$ (arXiv:2607.06983) [src: Sect. 1.3, Eq. 1.34].
- Sound speed, defined when $e$ and $p$ depend on a single quantity: $\bar{p}\;=\;\bar{p}(\bar{e})\,,\hskip 20.00003ptc_{s}^{2}\;\equiv\;\frac{\partial\bar{p}}{\partial\bar{e}}\,,$ (arXiv:2607.06983) [src: Sect. 1.3, Eq. 1.35].
- First Friedmann equation in conformal time: $\mathcal{H}^{2}+\kappa\overset{{\mbox{\tiny\rm{(\ref{eq_ee-b-00})}}}}{=}\;\frac{8\pi G}{3}\bar{e}a^{2}\,,$ (arXiv:2607.06983) [src: Sect. 1.3, Eq. 1.39].
- Second (acceleration) equation: $\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\underset{{\mbox{\tiny\rm{(\ref{eq_ee-b-ij})}}}}{\overset{{\mbox{\tiny\rm{(\ref{eq_ee-b-00})}}}\vphantom{|^{b}_{q}}}{=}}\;-\frac{4\pi G}{3}(\bar{e}+3\bar{p})\,a^{2}\,.$ (arXiv:2607.06983) [src: Sect. 1.3, Eq. 1.40].
- Continuity equation from $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu}{}_{0}=0$: $\mathbin{\bar{T}^{\mu}}_{0;\mu}\;\underset{{\mbox{\tiny\rm{(\ref{eq_chsy-a1})}}}}{\overset{{\mbox{\tiny\rm{(\ref{eq_continuity})}}}\vphantom{|^{b}_{q}}}{=}}\;\mathbin{\bar{T}^{0}}^{\prime}_{0}+\underbrace{4\mathcal{H}_{\vphantom{j}}}_{\Gamma^{\mu}_{\mu\tau}}\mathbin{\bar{T}^{0}}_{0}-\underbrace{\mathcal{H}_{\vphantom{j}}}_{\Gamma^{\tau\vphantom{i}}_{\tau\tau\vphantom{j}}}\mathbin{\bar{T}^{0}}_{0}-\underbrace{\mathcal{H}\delta^{i}_{j}}_{\Gamma^{i}_{j\tau}}\,\mathbin{\bar{T}^{j}}_{i}=0\hskip 10.00002pt\Rightarrow\hskip 10.00002pt\bar{e}\hskip 0.85358pt^{\prime}=-3\mathcal{H}(\bar{e}+\bar{p})\ .$ (arXiv:2607.06983) [src: Sect. 1.3, Eq. 1.42].
- The three equations "are not independent of each other": Eq. 1.40 follows from differentiating Eq. 1.39 and using Eq. 1.42, and the authors take Eqs. 1.39 and 1.42 as the basic relations and call the system obtained by converting them to physical time (Eqs. 1.43–1.44) the Friedmann equations (arXiv:2607.06983) [src: Sect. 1.3, text after Eqs. 1.42 and 1.44]. In physical time the first reads $H^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{a^{2}}\;=\;\frac{8\pi G}{3}\bar{e}$ (arXiv:2607.06983) [src: Sect. 1.3, Eq. 1.43, right part].
- The two Hubble rates are related by $\mathcal{H}\;\underset{{\mbox{\tiny\rm{(\ref{eq_hubble})}}}}{\overset{{\mbox{\tiny\rm{(\ref{t_tau})}}}\vphantom{|^{b}_{q}}}{=}}\;aH\,,\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\;=\;a^{2}(H^{2}+\dot{H})\;=\;a\ddot{a}\,,$ (arXiv:2607.06983) [src: Sect. 1.2, Eq. 1.6], the labels over the first equality being the rendering's references to Eqs. 1.3 and 1.4.
- κ "parametrizes the Ricci scalar of the constant-τ submanifolds": $\bar{R}_{\tau}\;\underset{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\,\to\,0}{\overset{{\mbox{\tiny\rm{(\ref{bg_ricci_scalar})}}}\vphantom{|^{b}_{q}}}{=}}\;\frac{6\kappa}{a^{2}}\;.$ (arXiv:2607.06983) [src: Sect. 1.4, "More on spatial curvature", Eq. 1.91], obtained from the four-dimensional Ricci scalar by setting $\mathcal{H}$ and $\mathcal{H}'$ to zero. The authors call κ neither a Lagrangian parameter nor, "as long as we have no theory of quantum gravity", a derived quantity, and say that from a general-relativistic point of view "it may be viewed as an initial condition" [src: same subsection].

Malik & Wands 2009 (arXiv:0809.4944), flat background, conformal time η, total density ρ and pressure P, several fluids α and fields $\varphi_I$:

- Friedmann constraint and evolution equation: ${\cal H}^{2}=\frac{8\pi G}{3}a^{2}\rho\,,$ ${\cal H}^{\prime}=-\frac{4\pi G}{3}a^{2}\left(\rho+3P\right)\,,$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 8.1, Eqs. 8.4–8.5].
- Continuity equation of each fluid with energy transfer: $\rho_{\alpha}^{\prime}=-3{\cal H}\left(\rho_{\alpha}+P_{\alpha}\right)+aQ_{\alpha}\,,$ with $Q_{\alpha}\equiv-u_{\mu}Q^{\mu}_{(\alpha)}\,.$ and the constraint $\sum_{\alpha}Q_{\alpha}=0\,.$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 8.1, Eqs. 8.8–8.10]; the authors credit Eq. 8.8 to their Ref. 66.
- Klein–Gordon equation of homogeneous fields: $\varphi_{I}^{\prime\prime}+2{\cal H}\varphi_{I}^{\prime}+a^{2}\frac{dU}{d\varphi_{I}}=0\,.$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 8.1, Eq. 8.11]; it "is sometimes useful" to identify the kinetic energy density and pressure of each field, equal to each other, in which case the Klein–Gordon equation implies an energy transfer to the potential energy, $aQ_{I}=-\varphi^{\prime}\frac{dU}{d\varphi_{I}}\,.$ [src: Sect. 8.1, Eq. 8.13, text before Eq. 8.12].

## Distances and horizons
<!-- key: distances-and-horizons -->

Hogg 1999 (arXiv:astro-ph/9905116, v4), for a universe of matter, Λ and curvature (his E(z) of Eq. 14 has no radiation term):

- Density parameters: $\Omega_{\rm M}\equiv\frac{8\pi\,G\,\rho_{0}}{3\,H_{0}^{2}}$ and $\Omega_{\Lambda}\equiv\frac{\Lambda\,c^{2}}{3\,H_{0}^{2}}$ (arXiv:astro-ph/9905116) [src: Sect. 2, Eqs. 5–6], with $\Omega_k$, which "can be defined by" the sum rule of Eq. 7 [src: Sect. 2].
- Hubble function: $E(z)\equiv\sqrt{\Omega_{\rm M}\,(1+z)^{3}+\Omega_{k}\,(1+z)^{2}+\Omega_{\Lambda}}$ (arXiv:astro-ph/9905116) [src: Sect. 4, Eq. 14], so that $H(z)=H_0E(z)$ [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 14]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Line-of-sight comoving distance: $D_{\rm C}=D_{\rm H}\,\int_{0}^{z}\frac{dz^{\prime}}{E(z^{\prime})}$ with the Hubble distance $D_{\rm H}=c/H_0$ (arXiv:astro-ph/9905116) [src: Sect. 4, Eq. 15; Sect. 2, Eq. 4].
- Transverse comoving distance: $D_{\rm M}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}D_{\rm H}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Omega_{k}}}\,\sinh\left[\sqrt{\Omega_{k}}\,D_{\rm C}/D_{\rm H}\right]&{\rm for}~\Omega_{k}>0\\ D_{\rm C}&{\rm for}~\Omega_{k}=0\\ D_{\rm H}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\Omega_{k}|}}\,\sin\left[\sqrt{|\Omega_{k}|}\,D_{\rm C}/D_{\rm H}\right]&{\rm for}~\Omega_{k}<0\end{array}\right.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/9905116) [src: Sect. 5, Eq. 16]; Hogg notes that it equals the proper-motion distance [src: Sect. 5] and is "not a proper distance" [src: Sect. 5, footnote 2].
- Angular-diameter and luminosity distances: $D_{\rm A}=\frac{D_{\rm M}}{1+z}$ (arXiv:astro-ph/9905116) [src: Sect. 6, Eq. 18] and $D_{\rm L}=(1+z)\,D_{\rm M}=(1+z)^{2}\,D_{\rm A}$ [src: Sect. 7, Eq. 21]. The angular-diameter distance between two redshifts is "not found by subtracting the two individual angular diameter distances"; Hogg's Eq. 19 holds for $\Omega_{k}\geq 0$ and "is not correct for $\Omega_{k}<0$", a point Hogg credits to a 1998 private communication from P. Helbig [src: Sect. 6, text around Eq. 19].
- Comoving horizon (Baumann 2009, arXiv:0907.5424): the conformal time as a logarithmic integral of the comoving Hubble radius, $\tau\equiv\int_{0}^{t}\frac{{\rm d}t^{\prime}}{a(t^{\prime})}=\int_{0}^{a}\frac{{\rm d}a}{Ha^{2}}=\int_{0}^{a}{\rm d}\ln a\left(\frac{1}{aH}\right)$ [src: Sect. 4.2, Eq. 36]; for a universe dominated by a fluid of equation of state w, $(aH)^{-1}=H_{0}^{-1}a^{\frac{1}{2}(1+3w)}\,.$ [src: Sect. 4.2, Eq. 37], so that the qualitative behaviour depends on the sign of $(1+3w)$ [src: Sect. 4.2, text after Eq. 37]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- In radiation and matter domination, $\tau=\int_{0}^{a}\frac{{\rm d}a}{Ha^{2}}\propto\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}a\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\,\rm{RD}\\ a^{1/2}\hskip 9.24994pt\rm{MD}\end{array}\right.\,.$ (arXiv:0907.5424) [src: Sect. 4.2, Eq. 39].
- Light-cone formalism at second order: Béchaz et al. 2025 (arXiv:2510.25690) build second-order perturbation theory on a background written in light-cone coordinates, identify the second-order gauge that corresponds to the non-linear geodesic light-cone gauge (the "Observational Synchronous Gauge"), and compute the luminosity distance–redshift relation to second order with anisotropic stress for a free-falling observer, showing "how divergences at the observer position can be eliminated in a completely model independent way" [src: abstract].

## Scalar–vector–tensor decomposition
<!-- key: scalar-vector-tensor-decomposition -->

Malik & Wands 2009 (arXiv:0809.4944), flat FRW background in conformal time η, all orders unless a subscript gives the order:

- Background: $ds^{2}=a^{2}\left[-d\eta^{2}+\delta_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}\right]\,,$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 2.2.2, Eq. 2.7].
- Metric perturbations: $\delta g_{00}=-2a^{2}{\phi}\,,$ $\delta g_{0i}=a^{2}B_{i}\,,$ $\delta g_{ij}=2a^{2}C_{ij}\,.$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 2.2.2, Eqs. 2.8–2.10].
- Split into types: $B_{i}=B_{,i}-S_{i}\,,$ $C_{ij}=-\psi\;\delta_{ij}+E_{,ij}+F_{(i,j)}+\frac{1}{2}h_{ij}\,.$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 2.2.2, Eqs. 2.11–2.12], with ϕ, B, ψ, E scalars, $S_i$ and $F_i$ divergence-free vectors and $h_{ij}$ transverse and trace-free [src: Sect. 2.2.2, text after Eq. 2.12].
- Counting: four scalar functions, two three-component vectors and a six-component symmetric tensor, minus four constraints on $h_{ij}$ and one each on $F_i$ and $S_i$, leave 10 degrees of freedom, "the same number as the independent components of the perturbed metric" (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 2.2.2, text after Eq. 2.12].
- The notation follows Mukhanov et al. "so that the metric perturbation, ψ, can be identified directly with the intrinsic scalar curvature of spatial hypersurfaces at first order"; the trace of the perturbed spatial metric is $C=C_{i}^{i}=-3\psi+\nabla^{2}E$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 2.2.2, Eq. 2.13 and text before].
- Decoupling: the three types decouple at linear order; at second order the second-order parts still decouple, but "their governing equations have source terms quadratic in the first order variables … mixing the different types", and at all higher orders n > 1 the types of order n "decouple, but are sourced by terms comprising perturbations of lower order" (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 2.2.2, text after Eq. 2.12; the authors cite their Ref. 127]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Divergence-free and curl-free are defined "with respect to the flat-space metric, rather than using covariant derivatives" (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 2.2.1, last paragraph]; the scalar and vector names go back to Bardeen, and the split of a vector field into curl-free and divergence-free parts "in Euclidean space" is Helmholtz's theorem [src: Sect. 2.2.1, text after Eq. 2.4].
- Second-order metric, with the factor convention of the expansion: $g_{00}=-a^{2}\left(1+2{\phi_{{1}}}+{\phi_{{2}}}\right)\,,$ $g_{0i}=a^{2}\left(B_{1i}+\frac{1}{2}B_{2i}\right)\,,$ $g_{ij}=a^{2}\left[\delta_{ij}+2C_{1ij}+C_{2ij}\right]\,,$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 2.2.2, Eq. 2.15].
- `[der]` The second-order parts carry a factor one half relative to the first-order ones; a second-order variable is quoted with this expansion convention.
- A non-local projection: Compère & Hoque 2026 (arXiv:2610.26623) build gauge-invariant multipole moments of the linearized field of localized sources on flat FLRW "directly in position space", which they describe as "an exterior description without the nonlocal spatial projection through the source interior required by the usual scalar-vector-tensor decomposition" [src: abstract].

## Gauge transformations and gauge-invariant variables
<!-- key: gauge-transformations-and-gauge-invariant -->

Malik & Wands 2009 (arXiv:0809.4944), first order, in the active approach, where the transformation "is evaluated at the same coordinate point" [src: Sect. 6.1]:

- Generator: $\xi_{1}^{\mu}=\left(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1,}^{~~i}+{\gamma_{{1}}^{~i}}\right)\,,$ with α the temporal scalar, β the spatial scalar and γ the spatial vector part (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 6.2.1, Eq. 6.17]; α and β represent the choices of time-slicing and threading [src: Sect. 7.1.1, text after Eq. 7.2].
- Scalars: $\widetilde{{\phi_{{1}}}}={\phi_{{1}}}+{\cal H}\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{1}^{\prime}\,,$ $\widetilde{\psi}_{1}=\psi_{1}-{\cal H}\alpha_{1}\,,$ $\widetilde{B}_{1}=B_{1}-\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}^{\prime}\,,$ $\widetilde{E}_{1}=E_{1}+\beta_{1}\,,$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 6.4.1, Eqs. 6.37–6.40].
- Vectors: $\widetilde{S_{1}^{~i}}=S_{1}^{~i}-{\gamma_{{1}}^{~i}}^{\prime}\,,$ $\widetilde{F_{1}^{~i}}=F_{1}^{~i}+{\gamma_{{1}}^{~i}}\,.$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 6.4.1, Eqs. 6.41–6.42]; the first-order tensor is gauge invariant, $\widetilde{h}_{1ij}=h_{1ij}\,.$ [src: Sect. 6.4.1, Eq. 6.43].
- Shear and momentum: with $\sigma_{1}=E_{1}^{\prime}-B_{1}$, $\widetilde{{\sigma}_{1}}={\sigma}_{1}+\alpha_{1}\,,$ $\widetilde{v_{1}}+\widetilde{B_{1}}=v_{1}+B_{1}-\alpha_{1}\,.$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 6.4.1, Eqs. 6.44–6.45 and text before].
- Bardeen's potentials, in Malik & Wands' notation: $\Phi\equiv{\phi_{{1}}}+{\cal H}(B_{1}-E_{1}^{\prime})+(B_{1}-E_{1}^{\prime})^{\prime}\,,$ $\Psi\equiv\psi_{1}-{\cal H}\left(B_{1}-E_{1}^{\prime}\right)\,.$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 7.1.1, Eqs. 7.1–7.2]; footnote 2 there gives Bardeen's own notation as $\Phi\equiv\Phi_{A}Q^{(0)}$ and $\Psi\equiv-\Phi_{H}Q^{(0)}$ [src: Sect. 7.1.1, footnote 2].
- `[der]` The minus sign relating Ψ to Bardeen's $\Phi_H$ is a convention trap when Bardeen-notation results are quoted.

Gauges, as Malik & Wands define them (arXiv:0809.4944):

| Gauge | Condition | Gauge-invariant variables it defines | Place read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Longitudinal (zero-shear, conformal Newtonian) | $\alpha_{1\ell}=-{\sigma}_{1}=B_{1}-E_{1}^{\prime}\,.$ and $\beta_{1\ell}=-E_{1}\,.$ | $\Phi$, $\Psi$; $\widetilde{\delta\rho_{1\ell}}=\delta\rho_{1}+\rho_{0}^{\prime}\left(B_{1}-E_{1}^{\prime}\right)\,,$ | [src: Sect. 7.1.1, Eqs. 7.3, 7.4, 7.7] |
| Poisson | longitudinal plus vector condition: $\gamma_{1\ell}^{i}=\int S_{1}^{i}d\eta+\hat{\cal{C}}_{1}^{i}(x^{j})\,,$ | remaining vector $\widetilde{F_{1\ell}}^{i}$ (Eq. 7.10) | [src: Sect. 7.1.1, Eq. 7.9] |
| Spatially flat (uniform curvature, off-diagonal) | $\alpha_{1{\rm{flat}}}=\frac{\psi_{1}}{{\cal H}}\,,\qquad\beta_{1{\rm{flat}}}=-E_{1}\,,\qquad\gamma_{1{\rm{flat}}}^{i}=-F_{1}^{i}\,.$ | Kodama–Sasaki $\cal A$ and $\cal B$ (Eqs. 7.16–7.17); Sasaki–Mukhanov variable $\widetilde{\delta{\varphi}_{1{\rm{flat}}}}\equiv\delta\varphi_{{1}}+\varphi_{0}^{\prime}\frac{\psi_{1}}{{\cal H}}\,,$ | [src: Sect. 7.2.1, Eqs. 7.15, 7.23] |

- Longitudinal gauge: Φ and Ψ "coincide with the scalar metric perturbations in a particular gauge", but "any unambiguous choice of time-slicing and threading can be used to define explicitly gauge-invariant perturbations" (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 7.1.1, text after Eq. 7.2]. The gauge "can be difficult to define … in the super-horizon limit, since in the super-horizon limit the shear vanishes and hence numerical instabilities can occur on large scales" [src: Sect. 7.1.1, text after Eq. 7.8; the authors cite their Refs. 101, 60, 82]. "In many cases of physical interest (in the absence of anisotropic stress) one finds Φ = Ψ" [src: Sect. 7.1.1, text after Eq. 7.8, the paragraph before]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Spatially flat gauge: its shear is related to the longitudinal-gauge curvature by $\widetilde{B_{1{\rm{flat}}}}=-\frac{\widetilde{\psi_{1\ell}}}{{\cal H}}=-\frac{\Psi}{{\cal H}}\,.$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 7.2.1, Eq. 7.21]; $\widetilde{B_{1{\rm{flat}}}}$ "grows on large scales in radiation or matter dominated eras, $\widetilde{B_{1{\rm{flat}}}}\propto{\cal H}^{-1}\propto\eta$, when the longitudinal gauge metric perturbation Ψ remains constant", while in a collapsing pre-Big-Bang era the flat-gauge variables "may remain small even when Φ and Ψ become large" [src: Sect. 7.2.1, text after Eq. 7.22].
- Synchronous gauge: "it is not possible to define gauge-invariant quantities in general using this gauge condition", citing their Ref. 114; the residual freedom can be fixed, following the authors' Ref. 28, by setting the initial cold-dark-matter velocity to zero, ${\cal{C}}_{1}(x)=a(v_{1{\rm cdm}}+B_{1})\,.$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 7.3, Eq. 7.43 and text before]. Malik & Wands call it "very popular for numerical studies, and used in many Boltzmann solvers such as CMBFAST" [src: Sect. 7.3, first paragraph]. In that paragraph they define the synchronous gauge by $\widetilde{{\phi}}=\widetilde{B}_{i}=0$, "so that the proper time for observers at fixed spatial coordinates coincides with cosmic time in the FRW background, i.e.," $d\tau=ad\eta$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 7.3, first paragraph, inline].
- `[der]` Here τ is cosmic time, whereas Laine & Procacci (arXiv:2607.06983, Eq. 1.3) and Ma & Bertschinger use τ for conformal time.
- Names: Laine & Procacci (arXiv:2607.06983) title their subsection "Newtonian (or zero-shear, or longitudinal, or Poisson) gauge" [src: Sect. 4.2, subsection title], whereas Malik & Wands (arXiv:0809.4944) reserve "Poisson gauge" for the extension of the longitudinal gauge to vector and tensor perturbations [src: Sect. 7.1.1, text before Eq. 7.9].

## First-order Einstein equations
<!-- key: first-order-einstein-equations -->

Malik & Wands 2009 (arXiv:0809.4944), conformal time, their potentials (ϕ in $g_{00}$, ψ in $C_{ij}$, shear σ, anisotropic stress Π):

- Energy and momentum constraints in an arbitrary gauge: $3{\cal H}\left(\psi^{\prime}+{\cal H}{\phi}\right)-\nabla^{2}\left[\psi+{\cal H}{\sigma}\right]=-4\pi Ga^{2}\delta\rho,$ $\psi^{\prime}+{\cal H}{\phi}=-4\pi Ga^{2}(\rho+P)V$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 8.2.1, Eqs. 8.16–8.17], with $V\equiv v+B\,,$ [src: Sect. 8.2.1, Eq. 8.18]; the authors credit these to their Refs. 66 and 123.
- Gauge-invariant Poisson equation: $\nabla^{2}\Psi=-3\left({\cal H}^{\prime}-{\cal H}^{2}\right)(\zeta+{{\cal{R}}})=4\pi Ga^{2}\delta\rho_{\rm com}\,,$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 8.2.1, Eq. 8.25]; the comoving density "is suppressed relative to the metric perturbation Ψ on large scales", and ζ and −ℛ "coincide in the large scale limit so long as Ψ is finite in this limit" [src: Sect. 8.2.1, text after Eq. 8.25].
- Footnote 4 there writes the ζ of Mukhanov, Feldman and Brandenberger as $\zeta_{\rm MFB}\equiv\Phi-\frac{{\cal H}(\Phi^{\prime}+{\cal H}\Phi)}{{\cal H}^{\prime}-{\cal H}^{2}}\,,$ which "coincides with the comoving curvature perturbation when Ψ = Φ" (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 8.2.1, footnote 4, Eq. 8.26].
- `[der]` The ζ of Mukhanov, Feldman and Brandenberger is thus a comoving-curvature variable when Ψ = Φ, not the uniform-density ζ of Malik & Wands' Eq. 7.61.
- Anisotropic stress and the two potentials: $\Psi-\Phi=8\pi Ga^{2}\Pi\,,$ "and hence we have Ψ = Φ in the absence of anisotropic stresses" (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 8.2.1, Eq. 8.29 and text after].
- Evolution of the longitudinal-gauge Ψ for adiabatic perturbations with $\delta P=c_{s}^{2}\delta\rho$, from their Eqs. 8.30 and 8.16: $\Psi^{\prime\prime}+3(1+c_{s}^{2}){\cal H}\Psi^{\prime}+[2{\cal H}^{\prime}+(1+3c_{s}^{2}){\cal H}^{2}-c_{s}^{2}\nabla^{2}]\Psi=0\,.$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 8.2.1, Eq. 8.31], credited to their Ref. 123.
- Vectors: ${\delta q}_{i}^{\prime}+4{\cal H}\delta q_{i}=-\nabla^{2}\Pi_{i}\,,$ and $\nabla^{2}\left({F}_{i}^{\prime}+S_{i}\right)=-16\pi Ga^{2}\delta q_{i}\,.$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 8.3, Eqs. 8.62–8.63]; the gauge-invariant vector perturbation vanishes "in the presence of only scalar fields", and vectors "are redshifted away by the Hubble expansion on large scales unless they are driven by an anisotropic stress" [src: Sect. 8.3, text after Eq. 8.63].
- Tensors, the free gravitational degrees of freedom with no constraint: ${h}_{ij}^{\prime\prime}+2{\cal H}{h}_{ij}^{\prime}-{\nabla^{2}}h_{ij}=8\pi Ga^{2}\Pi_{ij}\,,$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 8.4, Eq. 8.64], and for each polarisation amplitude without anisotropic stress ${h}^{\prime\prime}+2{\cal H}h^{\prime}+k^{2}h=0\,.$ [src: Sect. 8.4, Eq. 8.66]. As printed, the text between the two writes the Laplacian eigenvalue as $-(k^{2}/a^{2})$ for a comoving wavenumber k, whereas Eq. 8.66 carries $k^{2}$ [src: Sect. 8.4, text before Eq. 8.65].

## Curvature perturbations ζ and ℛ and their conservation
<!-- key: curvature-perturbations-and-r-and-their -->

The definitions of ζ and ℛ in Malik & Wands (Eqs. 7.46, 7.61, 7.63) and Baumann (Eqs. 136–141), the relation between them and the sign conventions are in the hub atlas, section "Curvature perturbation: ζ or ℛ, and its sign"; they are not repeated here. <!-- num: not a value -->

Linear evolution (Malik & Wands 2009, arXiv:0809.4944, conformal time, their sign of ζ):

- Evolution of ζ from energy conservation: $\zeta^{\prime}=-{\cal H}\frac{\delta P_{\rm nad}}{\rho+P}-{\Sigma_{V}}\,,$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 8.2.2, Eq. 8.35], with the scalar shear along comoving worldlines ${\Sigma_{V}}\equiv\frac{1}{3}\nabla^{2}\left(V+{\sigma}\right)=\frac{1}{3}\nabla^{2}\widetilde{v_{\ell}}\,.$ [src: Sect. 8.2.2, Eq. 8.36] and the non-adiabatic pressure $\delta P_{\rm nad}\equiv\delta P-\frac{P^{\prime}}{\rho^{\prime}}\delta\rho\,.$ [src: Sect. 9.1, Eq. 9.5].
- Hence "ζ is constant for adiabatic perturbations (δP_nad = 0) on super-Hubble scales (k/𝓗 ≪ 1), so long as Ψ remains finite"; the authors call this "an excellent approximation throughout reheating at the end of inflation and the subsequent radiation era on scales relevant for observations of degree-scale anisotropies in the cosmic microwave background and large-scale galaxy surveys", citing their Ref. 84 (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 8.2.2, text after Eq. 8.37]. The text after Eq. 8.36 states the weaker form: constant "for adiabatic perturbations on large scales when the shear of comoving worldlines becomes negligible" [src: Sect. 8.2.2, text after Eq. 8.36]. A non-adiabatic pressure perturbation changes ζ on super-Hubble scales, the mechanism "at the heart of the curvaton scenario" [src: Sect. 8.2.2, second paragraph after Eq. 8.37; the authors cite their Refs. 116, 50, 89 and 48, 95, 118].
- Wands, Malik, Lyth & Liddle 2000 (arXiv:astro-ph/0003278) show for linear perturbations about a Friedmann–Robertson–Walker background, "using only the local conservation of energy-momentum", that on sufficiently large scales the curvature perturbation on uniform-density hypersurfaces is conserved "when the non-adiabatic pressure perturbation is negligible", and state that this is "the first time that this result has been demonstrated independently of the gravitational field equations" [src: abstract].

Non-linear definitions:

- Lyth, Malik & Sasaki 2004 (arXiv:astro-ph/0411220) work in the ADM form $ds^{2}=-{\cal{N}}^{2}dt^{2}+\gamma_{ij}(dx^{i}+\beta^{i}dt)(dx^{j}+\beta^{j}dt)\,,$ [src: Sect. II.1, Eq. 1], with the local scale factor split as $e^{\alpha}=a(t)e^{\psi(t,x^{i})}\,.$ [src: Sect. II.1, Eq. 5], so that ψ enters the spatial metric through $e^{2\psi}$ (Eqs. 3 and 5), and denote ψ on uniform-density slices by −ζ [src: Sect. III.1].
- In a gradient expansion, to first order in ε with the anisotropic stress "negligible at that order" [src: Sect. III.1], and assuming that P is a unique function of ρ, the conserved quantity independent of the slicing is $-\zeta(x^{i})\equiv\psi(t,x^{i})+\frac{1}{3}\int_{\rho(t)}^{\rho(t,x^{i})}\frac{d\rho}{\rho+P}\,.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0411220) [src: Sect. IV, Eq. 37], which reduces in linear theory to $-\zeta(x^{i})={\cal R}_{c}(x^{i})=\psi(t,x^{i})+\frac{\delta\rho(t,x^{i})}{3(\rho+P)}\,.$ [src: Sect. IV, Eq. 38]. The abstract states the result as: "the curvature perturbation is conserved provided that the pressure is a unique function of the energy density" [src: abstract].
- Lyth, Malik & Sasaki's ψ enters the spatial metric through $e^{2\psi}$ (their Eqs. 3 and 5), Malik & Wands' ψ with a minus sign in $C_{ij}$ (arXiv:0809.4944, Eq. 2.12), and both call ψ on uniform-density slices −ζ. No paper read states the relation between the two ζ; check each paper's metric before comparing signs of ζ, ℛ or $f_{\rm NL}$ between them.
- Langlois & Vernizzi 2005 (arXiv:astro-ph/0503416) define "fully non-perturbative generalizations of the uniform density and comoving curvature perturbations", geometrically and "independently of any coordinate system", give their evolution "on all scales", and recover earlier first- and second-order results "on large scales" [src: abstract].
- Loop level: Inomata 2026 (arXiv:2606.28247) shows the conservation of superhorizon curvature perturbations at one loop in spatially flat gauge, including loop wavenumbers on all scales "in the decoupling limit, $ε(\equiv -\dot H/H^2) \to 0$", without assuming a hierarchy between the loop and external wavenumbers; the apparent time dependence cancels "once all contributions, including boundary terms, are combined consistently", taking into account the non-linear δN relation between the inflaton fluctuation and ζ [src: abstract, inline]. The loop debate itself belongs to `thcosmo-primordial-correlators`.

## Separate universe and δN
<!-- key: separate-universe-and-n -->

- Separate universe: on large scales "where anisotropy and spatial gradients can be neglected", the local density and expansion obey "the same evolution equations as in a homogeneous FRW universe", so that homogeneous solutions describe the local evolution (Malik & Wands, arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 11, text after Eq. 11.2; the authors cite their Refs. 144, 146, 147, 176, 96, 136, 98]. Wands et al. (arXiv:astro-ph/0003278) describe long-wavelength perturbations "as being composed of separate Robertson-Walker universes", which clarifies "the conditions under which super-horizon curvature perturbations may vary" [src: abstract].
- e-folds along comoving worldlines, a purely geometrical definition (Lyth, Malik & Sasaki, arXiv:astro-ph/0411220): $N(t_{2},t_{1};x^{i})\equiv\frac{1}{3}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\theta\,{\cal{N}}dt=-\frac{1}{3}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}dt\left.\frac{\dot{\rho}}{\rho+P}\right|_{x^{i}}\,,$ [src: Sect. IV, Eq. 32]; the change of ψ between two slices is $\psi(t_{2},x^{i})-\psi(t_{1},x^{i})=N(t_{2},t_{1};x^{i})-\ln\left[\frac{a(t_{2})}{a(t_{1})}\right]\,.$ [src: Sect. IV, Eq. 33]. From flat to uniform-density slicing, $\psi_{A}(t_{2},x^{i})=N_{A}(t_{2},t_{1};x^{i})-N_{0}(t_{2},t_{1})=\Delta N_{F}(t_{2},t_{1};x^{i})\,,$ [src: Sect. IV, Eq. 35], "a non-linear version of the ΔN formula that generalises the first-order result of Sasaki and Stewart" [src: Sect. IV, text after Eq. 35].
- Sasaki & Stewart 1995 (arXiv:astro-ph/9507001) derive "general analytic formulae for the spectrum and spectral index of the density perturbations produced during inflation", without assuming "only one real dynamical degree of freedom during inflation" [src: abstract].
- δN in Malik & Wands' sign of ζ (arXiv:0809.4944): $\zeta=\delta N\,,$ [src: Sect. 11, Eq. 11.1; the authors cite their Refs. 156, 157, 146, 98], to first order $\zeta=-H\frac{\delta\rho_{\rm{flat}}}{\dot{\rho}}\,,$ [src: Sect. 11, Eq. 11.2]; with initial field values on a flat slice, $\zeta=\sum_{I}N_{I}\delta\varphi_{I{\rm{flat}}}\,,$ [src: Sect. 11, Eq. 11.3] and ${\cal P}_{\zeta}=\sum_{I}N_{I}^{2}{\cal P}_{\delta\varphi_{I{\rm{flat}}}}\,.$ at leading order [src: Sect. 11, Eq. 11.4]. Eq. 11.3 rests on the integrated expansion being "some function of the local field values on the initial hypersurface", which the authors state "in the slow-roll approximation", adding: "More generally we expect this to hold whenever we can neglect the decaying mode for the field perturbations on super-Hubble scales" [src: Sect. 11, text before Eq. 11.3].
- Non-linear ζ in the radiation era: $\zeta=\frac{1}{4}\ln\left(\frac{\rho_{\rm{flat}}}{\rho_{0}}\right)\,,$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 11, Eq. 11.5], credited there to their Ref. 98.
- Taylor expansion: $\zeta\simeq\sum_{I}N_{I}\delta\varphi_{I{\rm{flat}}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{I,J}N_{IJ}\delta\varphi_{I{\rm{flat}}}\delta\varphi_{J{\rm{flat}}}+\frac{1}{6}\sum_{I,J,K}N_{IJK}\delta\varphi_{I{\rm{flat}}}\delta\varphi_{J{\rm{flat}}}\delta\varphi_{K{\rm{flat}}}+\ldots\,.$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 11, Eq. 11.6]; assuming field perturbations at Hubble exit close to Gaussian, which the authors "expect" for weakly coupled scalar fields [src: Sect. 11, text before Eq. 11.7], the δN non-linearity parameter is ${f_{NL}}=\frac{5}{6}\frac{N_{A}N_{B}N^{AB}}{\left(N_{C}N^{C}\right)^{2}}\,.$ [src: Sect. 11, Eq. 11.8], and when a single field direction perturbs the local expansion $\tau_{NL}=\frac{(N^{\prime\prime})^{2}}{(N^{\prime})^{4}}=\frac{36}{25}f_{NL}^{2}\,.$ [src: Sect. 11, Eq. 11.16], the proportionality being "first shown in [130] using the Bardeen potential, and in [94] using this notation" [src: Sect. 11, text after Eq. 11.16]. Footnote 5 there says the 5/6 is "a historical convention", from a definition through the Newtonian potential, "which on large scales in the matter era is given by $\Phi=-(3/5)\zeta$" [src: Sect. 11, footnote 5].
- `[der]` The atlas quotes Planck 2018 IX and Komatsu with Φ = +(3/5)ζ; the sign of Φ against ζ differs between these sources.
- Multi-field δN, stochastic δN and the tails: `thcosmo-inflation` (formalism and `deep/multi-field-perturbations.md`, `deep/stochastic-delta-n-tails.md`); intrinsic non-Gaussianity at horizon exit and the consistency relation: `thcosmo-primordial-correlators`.

Recent work on the validity of the separate universe:

- Holland & Grain 2026 (arXiv:2609.34788), multi-field non-linear sigma models non-minimally coupled to gravity: the separate-universe approach "adequately reproduces the dynamics of the large scale perturbations, provided the wavelength of the modes considered is greater than a list of lower bounds derived from the background evolution", compared between the Jordan and the "classically equivalent Einstein frame" description, for "a Higgs-like toy model of inflation in the large field limit" [src: abstract].
- Raveendran 2025 (arXiv:2506.23571), transient ultra-slow roll: the failure of the separate-universe approximation from spatial gradient terms, "particularly at the transition from slow-roll to ultra-slow-roll phase", is described as "well known"; Raveendran shows a similar breakdown at the transition back to slow roll for the conjugate momentum Π of ℛ; the homogeneous solution for Π is accurate across the first transition and that for ℛ across the second; the spatial curvature term of the extended δN formalism it cites as Artigas:2024ajh "can be interpreted as arising from the contribution of Π to the energy density perturbation", and "this modification of the local Hubble parameter is valid only when the first slow-roll parameter is both small and strictly constant" [src: abstract].
- Ahmadi & Ahmadi 2026 (arXiv:2602.00902) add an effective gradient source term to the background Klein–Gordon equation of δN, keeping the non-linear separate-universe dynamics at zeroth order; with it the equilateral $f_{\rm NL}^{\rm eq}$ "captures some essential physical features missed by the standard δN approach" [src: abstract].
- Saito & Naruko 2026 (arXiv:2607.11814) study linear superhorizon perturbations of a Bianchi I background with a scalar coupled to a U(1) gauge field, in the uniform-$\mathcal{N}$ gauge, where the conserved comoving curvature perturbation is the δN contribution plus an intrinsic-curvature contribution [src: abstract].

## Adiabatic and entropy perturbations
<!-- key: adiabatic-and-entropy-perturbations -->

Malik & Wands 2009 (arXiv:0809.4944), at first order, "primordial" meaning at the epoch of primordial nucleosynthesis [src: Sect. 9.1, first paragraph]:

- Baryon-entropy perturbation: $S_{B}\equiv\frac{\delta(n_{B}/n_{\gamma})}{n_{B}/n_{\gamma}}=\frac{\delta n_{B}}{n_{B}}-\frac{\delta n_{\gamma}}{n_{\gamma}}\,.$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 9.1, Eq. 9.1], assuming the effective number of light species $g_s$ constant and crediting their Ref. 116, which at nucleosynthesis becomes $S_{B}=\frac{\delta\rho_{B}}{\rho_{B}}-\frac{3}{4}\frac{\delta\rho_{\gamma}}{\rho_{\gamma}}\,.$ [src: Sect. 9.1, Eq. 9.2].
- Relative entropy perturbation between two fluids, gauge invariant: $S_{IJ}\equiv 3{\cal H}\left(\frac{\delta\rho_{J}}{\rho_{J}^{\prime}}-\frac{\delta\rho_{I}}{\rho_{I}^{\prime}}\right)\equiv 3\left(\zeta_{I}-\zeta_{J}\right)\,,$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 9.1, Eq. 9.4], which the authors call "the correct generalisation of the the entropy perturbation defined in Ref. [66] to the case of non-interacting fluids" (the doubled article as printed) [src: Sect. 9.1, text after Eq. 9.4]; they credit the definition to their Refs. 113 and 111.
- Split of the non-adiabatic pressure: $\delta P_{\rm nad}\equiv\delta P_{\rm intr}+\delta P_{\rm rel}\,.$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 9.1, Eq. 9.6], with the relative part $\delta P_{\rm rel}\equiv-\frac{1}{6{\cal H}\rho^{\prime}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\rho_{\alpha}^{\prime}\rho_{\beta}^{\prime}\left(c^{2}_{\alpha}-c^{2}_{\beta}\right){\cal{S}}_{\alpha\beta}\,.$ [src: Sect. 9.1, Eq. 9.8], following their Ref. 66 (Kodama & Sasaki) [src: Sect. 9.1, text before Eq. 9.6].
- The non-adiabatic pressure of a single field is proportional to the comoving density, $\delta P_{\rm nad}=-\frac{2a^{2}U_{,\varphi}}{3{\cal H}\varphi^{\prime}}\delta\rho_{\rm{com}}\,.$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 8.2.4, Eq. 8.56].
- In linearized perturbation theory, the general regular primordial perturbation in a universe of photons, baryons, neutrinos and a hypothetical CDM component has, besides the growing adiabatic mode, baryon and CDM isocurvature modes and two neutrino isocurvature modes, density and velocity; "all quadratic correlators of observables … are completely determined by a 5×5, real, symmetric matrix-valued function of co-moving wavenumber" (Bucher, Moodley & Turok 1999, arXiv:astro-ph/9904231) [src: abstract]. In the neutrino density mode "the ratio of neutrinos to photons varies spatially but the net density perturbation vanishes"; in the neutrino velocity mode the photon–baryon plasma and the neutrinos "have a spatially varying relative bulk velocity, balanced so that the net momentum density vanishes" [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Laine & Procacci 2026 (arXiv:2607.06983) give "a gauge-invariant demonstration for why the isocurvature perturbations vanish at the same time as $\mathcal{R}_{\varphi}$ freezes out" for an inflaton thermalizing with a plasma, under "the implicit assumption … that a non-vanishing background temperature exists"; searching for a stationary solution outside the horizon, the three equations for the two isocurvature variables are overconstrained, the coefficients being "in general, non-degenerate", so that "omitting the right-hand side" (the noise) the only solution is zero [src: Sect. 8.3, Eqs. 8.19–8.21 and text around]. The arguments of their Sects. 8.2 and 8.3 "are specific to single-field inflation" [src: Sect. 8.4, first sentence]. The authors say the usual justification in the literature is given "rather succinctly … from the solutions of gauge-fixed evolution equations" [src: Sect. 8.3, first paragraph], and demonstrate the result for two scalar fields without temperature in their Sect. 8.4 [src: same paragraph].
- Superhorizon isocurvature in a dark radiation sector: Gerlach, Ratzinger & Schwaller 2025 (arXiv:2510.21917) state that if a radiation-like dark sector "constitutes a significant fraction of the energy density, its isocurvature can imply isocurvature in the inflaton sector even without direct interactions between the sectors" [src: abstract].
- Initial conditions beyond general relativity: Pan et al. 2025 (arXiv:2506.17411) derive consistent initial conditions for Horndeski theory in the EFT approach, "assuming constant EFT functions at early times", implement them in EFTCAMB, and find "significant deviations" in CMB spectra between consistent modified-gravity initial conditions and "inconsistent GR ICs" [src: abstract].

## Einstein–Boltzmann hierarchy and line-of-sight integration
<!-- key: einstein-boltzmann-hierarchy-and-line-of-sight -->

Ma & Bertschinger 1995 (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072, v1), "only spatially flat (Ω=1) background spacetimes with isentropic scalar metric perturbations" [src: Sect. 2, first paragraph], conformal time τ with a dot for $\partial_\tau$, synchronous-gauge metric potentials h and η, conformal-Newtonian potentials ψ (in $g_{00}$) and φ (see the atlas for both line elements):

- Massless neutrinos, angular expansion after integrating out the momentum: $F_{\nu}(\vec{k},\hat{n},\tau)\equiv{\int q^{2}dq\,qf_{0}(q)\Psi\over\int q^{2}dq\,qf_{0}(q)}\equiv\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-i)^{l}(2l+1)F_{\nu\,l}(\vec{k},\tau)P_{l}(\hat{k}\cdot\hat{n})\,.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072) [src: Sect. 5.3, Eq. 46]; the factor $(-i)^{l}(2l+1)$ "is chosen to simplify the expansion of a plane wave" [src: Sect. 5.3, text after Eq. 46].
- Moments: $\delta_{\nu}={1\over 4\pi}\int d\Omega F_{\nu}(\vec{k},\hat{n},\tau)=F_{\nu\,0}\,,$ $\theta_{\nu}={3i\over 16\pi}\int d\Omega\,(\vec{k}\cdot\hat{n})F_{\nu}(\vec{k},\hat{n},\tau)={3\over 4}kF_{\nu\,1}\,,$ $\sigma_{\nu}=-{3\over 16\pi}\int d\Omega\left[(\hat{k}\cdot\hat{n})^{2}-{1\over 3}\right]F_{\nu}(\vec{k},\hat{n},\tau)={1\over 2}F_{\nu\,2}\,,$ (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072) [src: Sect. 5.3, Eq. 47].
- Boltzmann equation in the two gauges: ${\partial F_{\nu}\over\partial\tau}+ik\mu F_{\nu}=-\frac{2}{3}\dot{h}-\frac{4}{3}(\dot{h}+6\dot{\eta})P_{2}(\mu)\qquad\mbox{in synchronous gauge}\,,$ ${\partial F_{\nu}\over\partial\tau}+ik\mu F_{\nu}=4\,(\dot{\phi}-ik\mu\psi)\qquad\mbox{in conformal Newtonian gauge}\,,$ (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072) [src: Sect. 5.3, Eq. 48].
- Synchronous-gauge hierarchy: $\dot{\delta}_{\nu}=-{4\over 3}\theta_{\nu}-{2\over 3}\dot{h}\,,$ $\dot{\theta}_{\nu}=k^{2}\left(\frac{1}{4}\delta_{\nu}-\sigma_{\nu}\right)\,,$ $\dot{F}_{\nu\,2}=2\dot{\sigma}_{\nu}={8\over 15}\theta_{\nu}-{3\over 5}kF_{\nu\,3}+{4\over 15}\dot{h}+{8\over 5}\dot{\eta}\,,$ $\dot{F}_{\nu\,l}={k\over 2l+1}\left[lF_{\nu\,(l-1)}-(l+1)F_{\nu\,(l+1)}\right]\,,\quad l\geq 3\,.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072) [src: Sect. 5.3, Eq. 49]; "a given mode $F_{l}$ is coupled only to the (l−1) and (l+1) neighboring modes" [src: Sect. 5.3, text after Eq. 50]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Truncation: setting $F_{\nu\,l}=0$ beyond $l_{\rm max}$ is "simple but inaccurate", because errors propagate to l = 0 in a time $\tau\approx l_{\rm max}/k$ and reflect back; the improved scheme extrapolates with the spherical-Bessel recurrence, $F_{\nu\,(l_{\rm max}+1)}\approx{(2l_{\rm max}+1)\over k\tau}\,F_{\nu\,l_{\rm max}}-F_{\nu\,(l_{\rm max}-1)}\ .$ (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072) [src: Sect. 5.3, Eq. 51 and text before], motivated by $F_{\nu\,l}\propto j_{l}(k\tau)$ being the exact solution for l > 0 "if $\partial_{\tau}(\phi+\psi)=0$" in conformal Newtonian gauge, and assuming that this relation "holds approximately for time-varying potentials" [src: same paragraph]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- In their introduction the authors quote Hu et al. (1995): truncating the massless-neutrino moments beyond l ≥ 2 in the pure CDM model "leads to a 10% error in the anisotropy power spectrum" (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072) [src: Sect. 1].
- Adiabatic super-horizon initial conditions in synchronous gauge in the radiation era, the "leading-order behavior" in kτ with "only the fastest-growing physical mode" present and constant entropy per baryon, with C and $R_\nu$ as defined in their Sect. 7 [src: Sect. 7, text before Eq. 96]: $\delta_{\gamma}=-{2\over 3}C(k\tau)^{2}\,,\qquad\delta_{c}=\delta_{b}={3\over 4}\delta_{\nu}={3\over 4}\delta_{\gamma}\,,$ $\theta_{c}=0\,,\quad\theta_{\gamma}=\theta_{b}=-{1\over 18}C(k^{4}\tau^{3})\,,\quad\theta_{\nu}={23+4R_{\nu}\over 15+4R_{\nu}}\theta_{\gamma}\,,$ $\sigma_{\nu}={4C\over 3(15+4R_{\nu})}(k\tau)^{2}\,,$ (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072) [src: Sect. 7, Eq. 96, three rows of the display]. Outside the horizon "the behavior of δ … is strongly gauge-dependent": in synchronous gauge all δ grow as $a^2$ in radiation domination, in conformal Newtonian gauge they "remain constant outside the horizon" [src: Sect. 8, "Before horizon crossing", item 1]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- `[der]` The vanishing of $\theta_c$ in these initial conditions is of the same kind as the condition with which Malik & Wands fix the residual synchronous-gauge freedom, a vanishing initial CDM velocity (see the gauge table above).

Line-of-sight integration (Seljak & Zaldarriaga 1996, arXiv:astro-ph/9603033, v1), "for a spatially flat universe", to which they "restrict the analysis" [src: Sect. 2, first paragraph]: longitudinal gauge with the two scalar potentials φ and ψ, derivatives with respect to conformal time τ, κ the optical depth from τ to τ₀ and g the visibility function [src: Sect. 2.1]; their line element and Π were not read in this run:

- Formal integral of the photon Boltzmann equation along the past light cone, scalar modes: $\Delta_{T}^{(S)}=\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}}d\tau e^{ik\mu(\tau-\tau_{0})}e^{-\kappa}\{\dot{\kappa}e^{-\kappa}[\Delta_{T0}+i\mu v_{b}+{1\over 2}P_{2}(\mu)\Pi]+\dot{\phi}-ik\mu\psi\}$ $\Delta_{P}^{(S)}=-{1\over 2}\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}}d\tau e^{ik\mu(\tau-\tau_{0})}e^{-\kappa}\dot{\kappa}[1-P_{2}(\mu)]\Pi.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/9603033) [src: Sect. 2.2, Eq. 11], citing "e.g." Zaldarriaga & Harari (1995). As printed in the v1 rendering, the temperature line carries the factor $e^{-\kappa}$ twice, once outside and once inside the braces; no correction was found.
- After integration by parts (the boundary terms vanish as τ → 0 and affect only the unobservable monopole at $\tau_0$), the multipoles today are $\Delta^{(S)}_{(T,P)l}(k,\tau=\tau_{0})=\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}}S^{(S)}_{T,P}(k,\tau)j_{l}[k(\tau_{0}-\tau)]d\tau,$ (arXiv:astro-ph/9603033) [src: Sect. 2.2, Eq. 13 and text before Eq. 12], with the temperature source $S^{(S)}_{T}(k,\tau)=g\left(\Delta_{T0}+\psi-{\dot{v_{b}}\over k}-{\Pi\over 4}-{3\ddot{\Pi}\over 4k^{2}}\right)+e^{-\kappa}(\dot{\phi}+\dot{\psi})-\dot{g}\left({v_{b}\over k}+{3\dot{\Pi}\over 4k^{2}}\right)-{3\ddot{g}\Pi\over 4k^{2}}$ [src: Sect. 2.2, Eq. 12, second and third rows]. The term $e^{-\kappa}(\dot{\phi}+\dot{\psi})$ is the integrated Sachs–Wolfe term [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 12].
- The method separates a model-independent geometrical term $j_l$, which "can be precomputed in advance", from a source term that "is a slowly varying function of wavelength and needs to be evaluated only in a small number of points", and depends only on the potentials, the baryon velocity and photon moments up to l = 4; it gives "about two orders of magnitude reduction in CPU time when compared to standard methods" (arXiv:astro-ph/9603033) [src: abstract; Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 13]. The ensemble average assumes "that only the amplitude and not the phase of a given mode evolves in time", which is "valid in linear theory for most models of structure formation" but "may not be correct in some versions of models with topological defects" [src: Sect. 2.2, footnote 1]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Tensor modes use the eigenfunctions $\chi^{l}_{k}(\tau)=\sqrt{{(l+2)!\over 2(l-2)!}}{j_{l}(k\tau)\over(k\tau)^{2}}.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/9603033) [src: Sect. 2.2, Eq. 14].
- `thcosmo-cmb-physics` (not built) is the home of the physics of the CMB sources (recombination, Silk damping, polarisation); here only the algorithm.

Codes:

- CLASS (Lesgourgues 2011, arXiv:1104.2932) is presented as "a new accurate Boltzmann code", "very structured, easy to modify", offering "a rigorous way to control the accuracy of output quantities" and "incidentally a bit faster than other codes" [src: abstract].
- CLASS II (Blas, Lesgourgues & Tram 2011, arXiv:1104.2933) describes three approximations for basic ΛCDM models: a baryon–photon tight-coupling approximation "which can be set to first order, second order or to a compromise between the two", an ultra-relativistic fluid approximation, and a radiation streaming approximation "taking reionisation into account" [src: abstract]. ## Second-order perturbations and their gauge issues
<!-- key: second-order-perturbations-and-their-gauge -->

- Why gauge dependence arises: "splitting variables into a background part and a perturbation is not a covariant procedure, and therefore introduces a coordinate or gauge dependence"; Malik & Wands credit the systematic resolution to Bardeen (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 6, second paragraph]. They describe two approaches, the passive one, where the transformation "is taken at the same physical point", and the active one, where it "is evaluated at the same coordinate point", and use the active one [src: Sect. 6.1].
- Bruni, Matarrese, Mollerach & Sonego 1996 (arXiv:gr-qc/9609040) define gauge invariance to arbitrary order n, give "a generating formula for the gauge transformation to an arbitrary order and explicit rules to second and third order", and, for a flat Robertson–Walker background, explicit second-order transformations between the synchronous and the Poisson (generalized longitudinal) gauges [src: abstract].
- Second-order generator and the transformation of a four-scalar (Malik & Wands, arXiv:0809.4944): $\xi_{2}^{\mu}=\left(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2,}^{~~i}+{\gamma_{{2}}^{~i}}\right)\,,$ [src: Sect. 6.2.2, Eq. 6.19] and $\widetilde{\delta\rho_{2}}={\delta\rho_{2}}+\rho_{0}^{\prime}\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{1}\left(\rho_{0}^{\prime\prime}\alpha_{1}+\rho_{0}^{\prime}{\alpha_{1}}^{\prime}+2{\delta\rho_{1}}^{\prime}\right)+\left(2{\delta\rho_{1}}+\rho_{0}^{\prime}{\alpha_{1}}\right)_{,k}(\beta_{1,}^{~~k}+{\gamma_{{1}}^{~k}})\,.$ [src: Sect. 6.2.2, Eq. 6.20]. The last term already couples vector and scalar perturbations, and "the gauge is only specified once the scalar temporal gauge perturbations at first and second order, $\alpha_{1}$ and $\alpha_{2}$, and the first order spatial gauge perturbations, $\beta_{1}$ and ${\gamma_{{1}}^{~i}}$, are specified" [src: Sect. 6.2.2, text after Eq. 6.20].
- Mode mixing: in the Einstein–de Sitter universe, "primordial density perturbations act as a source for gravitational waves, while primordial gravitational waves give rise to second-order density fluctuations" (Matarrese, Mollerach & Bruni 1997, arXiv:astro-ph/9707278) [src: abstract]; the first-order types decouple but second-order equations carry quadratic sources "mixing the different types" (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 2.2.2].
- Second-order tensors: Xue & Cao 2025 (arXiv:2509.26159) introduce a transverse-traceless gauge on cosmological backgrounds and find that with the TT gauge at first order "the second order tensor mode would be gauge invariant"; the TT gauge exists under a vacuum condition, under which the Poisson, uniform-curvature, synchronous and total-matter gauges "are all equivalent to the TT gauge", and in the sub-horizon limit the Poisson, uniform-curvature and synchronous gauges are all approximately TT [src: abstract]. The gauge question of scalar-induced gravitational waves, its resolution in radiation domination and what remains open are in `thcosmo-cosmological-gravitational-waves` (open-questions, "The gauge question for scalar-induced waves"; `deep/scalar-induced-kernels.md`). <!-- num: not a value -->
- Laine & Procacci (arXiv:2607.06983) title the remarks (i)–(v) of their section on scalar-induced waves "Gauge dependence", "Index placement", "Simplifications if one enthalpy component dominates", "Remarks on the anisotropic stress" and "Example of how double counting can be avoided" [src: Sect. 10.4, item titles]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Second-order Einstein–Boltzmann: Pettinari et al. 2013 (arXiv:1302.0832) solve the second-order Einstein–Boltzmann equations to estimate the intrinsic CMB bispectrum and find, "when including all physical effects but lensing and time-delay", a bias of the local-type $f_{\rm nl}$ of $f_{\rm nl}\sim 0.5$, "below the expected sensitivity of the Planck satellite"; "the inclusion or omission of certain line of sight terms can make a large impact" [src: abstract, inline].
- Observables at second order: the light-cone approach of Béchaz et al. (arXiv:2510.25690), in "Distances and horizons" above.
- Large-scale white noise at second order: Ireland 2026 (arXiv:2608.09897) finds that the second-order comoving curvature perturbation obeys $\lim_{k \rightarrow 0} \left( k^2 R_2 \right) = 0$ from "a conservation law in the soft limit" [src: abstract, inline]; the debate is in `open-questions.md`.
