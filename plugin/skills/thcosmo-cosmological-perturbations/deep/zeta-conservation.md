# Conservation of ζ — thcosmo-cosmological-perturbations dossier

Scope: how the large-scale conservation of the uniform-density curvature perturbation ζ (or of ℛ) is derived, linearly from energy conservation, non-linearly in the gradient expansion and covariantly, and which conditions break it (non-adiabatic pressure, anisotropic stress and shear, gradients, non-attractor phases).
Snapshot: 2026-11-21.
From: Malik & Wands arXiv:0809.4944, Sects. 7.4, 7.6, 8.2.2; Laine & Procacci arXiv:2607.06983, Sect. 8.2 — `references/subtopics.tsv`, row `curvature-perturbations`.
Audit questions: (2) How is the conservation of ζ (or ℛ) on large scales derived, linearly from the energy conservation equation and non-linearly in the gradient expansion and δN, and exactly which conditions (non-adiabatic pressure, anisotropic stress, gradients, non-attractor phases) break it?

The results with their conditions are tabulated in `results.md#conservation-of-curvature-perturbations`; Malik & Wands' Eqs. 8.35–8.37 and Lyth, Malik & Sasaki's Eqs. 37–38 are in `formalism.md#curvature-perturbations-and-r-and-their`; the δN equations (Lyth, Malik & Sasaki Eqs. 32–35; Malik & Wands Sect. 11) in `formalism.md#separate-universe-and-n`; the recent separate-universe limits in `open-questions.md#where-the-separate-universe-stops`. They are not repeated here. ζ during inflation, multi-field δN and stochastic tails belong to `thcosmo-inflation` (`deep/multi-field-perturbations.md`, `deep/stochastic-delta-n-tails.md`); loop corrections to ζ to `thcosmo-primordial-correlators`.

## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

Each route below is given in its own conventions; the sign of ζ and the name of ψ differ (`approximations.md#convention-traps`).

- Wands, Malik, Lyth & Liddle (arXiv:astro-ph/0003278) use cosmic time t on a background of constant curvature κ, with $-(1+2A)dt^{2}+2a^{2}(t)\nabla_{i}B\,dx^{i}dt$ as the first row of their line element [src: Sect. II, Eq. 2], ψ entering the spatial part as $(1-2\psi)\gamma_{ij}$ [src: same display, second row], and fix the sign of ζ "to coincide with Refs. [5, 6]" [src: Sect. II, footnote 4].
- Lyth, Malik & Sasaki (arXiv:astro-ph/0411220) use the ADM metric with the local scale factor $a\,e^{\psi}$ and call ψ on uniform-density slices −ζ (`formalism.md#curvature-perturbations-and-r-and-their`); their small parameter is the gradient-expansion parameter $\epsilon\equiv k/aH\,,$ [src: Sect. II.2, Eq. 7], not a slow-roll parameter.
- Langlois & Vernizzi (arXiv:astro-ph/0503416) work covariantly with α, "the integrated expansion", which "represents the number of e-folds of the local expansion measured by an observer comoving with the fluid" [src: conclusion paragraph]; in a coordinate system they write $\alpha\simeq\ln a-\psi-\psi^{2}.$ [src: Eq. 11] and relate their second-order ζ to "the quantity defined by Malik and Wands in [6]" as $\zeta_{2}\simeq\zeta_{2\rm(MW)}-2\zeta^{2}_{1\rm(MW)}.$ [src: unnumbered display after Eq. 11], a relation between conventions that a paper states.
- Leach, Sasaki, Wands & Liddle work with the comoving curvature perturbation $\mathcal{R}_c$ of single-field inflation in conformal time, with $z=a\dot{\phi}/H$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0101406) [src: Sect. II, inline, text after Eq. 3]; Namjoo, Firouzjahi & Sasaki (arXiv:1210.3692) and Jackson et al. (arXiv:2311.03281) also use ℛ in conformal time.

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — the linear law from energy conservation alone
<!-- key: step-1-the-linear-law-from-energy-conservation -->

Wands et al. (arXiv:astro-ph/0003278) do not use field equations. The expansion of the constant-t hypersurfaces with respect to coordinate time is $\tilde{\theta}=(1+A)\theta=3H-3\dot{\psi}+\nabla^{2}\sigma\,,$ [src: Sect. III.1, Eq. 15], with shear $\sigma=\dot{E}-B\,,$ [src: Eq. 14], so that $\dot{\psi}=-{1\over 3}\delta\tilde{\theta}+{1\over 3}\nabla^{2}\sigma\,.$ [src: Eq. 16], which "is independent of the field equations and follows simply from the geometry" [src: Sect. III.1, text after Eq. 16]. The energy conservation equation $n^{\nu}T^{\mu}_{~\nu;\mu}=0$ at first order is $\dot{\delta\rho}=-3H(\delta\rho+\delta p)+(\rho+p)\left[3\dot{\psi}-\nabla^{2}\left(\sigma+v+B\right)\right]\,,$ [src: Eq. 17]; in the uniform-density gauge, where δρ = 0 and ψ = −ζ, it gives $\dot{\zeta}=-{H\over\rho+p}\delta p_{\rm nad}-{1\over 3}\nabla^{2}\left(\sigma+v+B\right)\,.$ [src: Eq. 18], with $\delta p_{\rm nad}\equiv\dot{p}\Gamma$ and $\Gamma\equiv{\delta p\over\dot{p}}-{\delta\rho\over\dot{\rho}}\,.$ [src: Sect. II, Eq. 11]. Hence "ζ is constant if (i) there is no non-adiabatic pressure perturbation, and (ii) the divergence of the 3-momentum on zero-shear hypersurfaces, $\nabla^{2}(v+B+\sigma)$, is negligible" [src: Sect. III.1, text after Eq. 18]. "On sufficiently large scales, gradient terms can be neglected" and $\dot{\zeta}=-{H\over\rho+p}\delta p_{\rm nad}\,,$ [src: Eq. 19], citing their Refs. 18 and 8; the authors add that "It has been argued [8] that the divergence is likely to be negligible on all super-horizon scales, and in the following we shall make that assumption" [src: Sect. III.1, text after Eq. 19]. The result "holds for linear perturbations about an FRW metric for any relativistic theory of gravity, as a consequence of local energy conservation" [src: Sect. III.1, last paragraph].

Malik & Wands (arXiv:0809.4944) write the same chain in conformal time, flat background, from the energy equation $\delta\rho^{\prime}+3{\cal H}\left(\delta\rho+\delta P\right)-3\left(\rho+P\right)\psi^{\prime}+(\rho+P)\nabla^{2}\left(V+{\sigma}\right)=0\,,$ [src: Sect. 8.2.2, Eq. 8.32] to their Eq. 8.35 (`formalism.md#curvature-perturbations-and-r-and-their`). The step that removes the shear term is not energy conservation: "Using the definition of ζ and Ψ in Eqs. (7.61) and (7.2) and the constraint equation for the comoving density perturbation (8.25)", they obtain $\frac{\Sigma_{V}}{{\cal H}}=\frac{\nabla^{2}}{3{\cal H}^{2}}\left(\zeta+\Psi\right)+\frac{2\rho}{3(\rho+P)}\left(\frac{\nabla^{2}}{{\cal H}^{2}}\right)^{2}\Psi\,.$ [src: Eq. 8.37], whence ζ is constant for $\delta P_{\rm nad}=0$ on super-Hubble scales "so long as Ψ remains finite" [src: Sect. 8.2.2, text after Eq. 8.37].

- `[der]` The two papers split the work the same way: energy conservation alone gives the evolution equation with a gradient term; showing that the gradient term is small on super-Hubble scales needs either an assumption (Wands et al.) or the Einstein constraint and a finite Ψ (Malik & Wands).

### Step 2 — non-linear, in the gradient expansion
<!-- key: step-2-non-linear-in-the-gradient-expansion -->

Lyth, Malik & Sasaki (arXiv:astro-ph/0411220) multiply each spatial gradient by ε, "expand the exact equations as a power series in ε", "keep only the zero- and first-order terms and finally set ε=1" [src: Sect. II.2, first paragraph]. Their "key physical assumption is that in the limit ε→0, corresponding to a sufficiently large smoothing scale, the universe becomes locally homogeneous and isotropic", and unless a scale of the stress–energy tensor is bigger than 1/k, "local homogeneity and isotropy will be a good approximation achieved throughout the entire super-horizon era k≪aH"; "one expects this 'separate universe' hypothesis" to be valid for cosmological scales [src: Sect. II.2, paragraph after Eq. 7]. The metric conditions are $\beta_{i}=O(\epsilon)\,,$ [src: Eq. 9] and $\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}_{ij}=O(\epsilon^{2})\,.$ [src: Eq. 10]; the second rests on ignoring a mode that decays "like $\tilde{a}^{-3}$ in Einstein gravity", and "in alternative theories of gravity, the assumption ... may not be as natural as in the Einstein case" [src: Sect. II.2, text before and after Eq. 10].

With the perfect-fluid form $T_{\mu\nu}\equiv\left(\rho+P\right)u_{\mu}u_{\nu}+g_{\mu\nu}P\,,$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0411220) [src: Sect. II.3, Eq. 12], which holds "By virtue of the separate universe assumption", the energy conservation equation in comoving threading reduces to $\sqrt{{\cal{N}}^{2}-\beta^{k}\beta_{k}}\left[\frac{d}{d\tau}\rho+\left(\rho+P\right)\theta\right]=\dot{\rho}+3\left(\rho+P\right)\dot{\alpha}+O(\epsilon^{2})=0\,,$ [src: Eq. 18], an argument that "uses only the energy conservation law" [src: Sect. II.3, text after Eq. 20]. The local Hubble rate is $\tilde{H}=\frac{1}{3}\theta_{n}=\frac{1}{{\cal{N}}}\left(\frac{\dot{a}}{a}+\dot{\psi}\right)+O(\epsilon^{2})\,.$ [src: Eq. 21]. Then, taking "the anisotropic stress of the fluid to be negligible at that order", that is "of second order in ε" [src: Sect. III.1, third paragraph], $\frac{d}{d\tau}\rho=-3\tilde{H}\left(\rho+P\right)+O(\epsilon^{2})\,.$ [src: Eq. 22] and $\frac{\dot{a}}{a}+\dot{\psi}=-\frac{1}{3}\frac{\dot{\rho}}{\rho+P}+O(\epsilon^{2})\,.$ [src: Eq. 23], "valid independently of the slicing". On uniform-density slices, if "to first order in ε, P is a unique function of ρ (the 'adiabatic pressure' condition)", $\dot\psi$ is spatially homogeneous, and since ψ can be set to vanish at one position, $-\dot{\psi}=\dot{\zeta}=O(\epsilon^{2})\,.$ [src: Eq. 24]; ζ "can refer to the total cosmic fluid, or to a single component which does not exchange energy with the remainder" [src: Sect. III.1, text after Eq. 24].

- `[der]` Their Sect. IV, not re-read here, builds the slicing-independent Eq. 37 and the ΔN formula, Eq. 35, on this result (`formalism.md#separate-universe-and-n`).

### Step 3 — non-linear and exact, covariantly
<!-- key: step-3-non-linear-and-exact-covariantly -->

Langlois & Vernizzi (arXiv:astro-ph/0503416) define the covector $\zeta_{a}\equiv W_{a}+{X_{a}\over{3(\rho+P)}}=D_{a}\alpha-\frac{\dot{\alpha}}{\dot{\rho}}D_{a}\rho,$ [src: Eq. 5], "our non-perturbative generalization of the curvature perturbation on uniform density hypersurfaces or, more exactly, of its 'spatial' gradient" [src: text after Eq. 5]. The spatial projection of energy conservation gives $e^{-\alpha}h_{a}^{\ b}{{\left(e^{\alpha}\zeta_{b}\right)}^{\hbox{.}}}=-{\Theta\over{3(\rho+P)}}\Gamma_{a}-\zeta^{b}\left(\sigma_{ba}+\omega_{ba}\right),$ [src: Eq. 6], with $\Gamma_{a}\equiv Y_{a}-c_{s}^{2}X_{a}\equiv D_{a}P-{\dot{P}\over\dot{\rho}}D_{a}\rho$ [src: Eq. 7], which "vanishes for purely adiabatic perturbations, i.e., when the pressure P is solely a function of the density ρ" [src: text after Eq. 7]. Eq. 6 is "fully non-linear" and "obtained directly from the local conservation of the energy-momentum tensor and independently of Einstein's equations", "valid in any metric theory of gravity" [src: text after Eq. 7; conclusion paragraph]. In coordinates, "If one ignores the second term on the right hand side of Eq. (6)", they find at first and second order $\zeta_{1}^{\prime}\simeq-\frac{{\cal H}}{\bar{\rho}+\bar{P}}\Gamma_{1},$ $\zeta_{2}^{\prime}\simeq-\frac{{\cal H}}{\bar{\rho}+\bar{P}}\Gamma_{2}-\frac{2}{\bar{\rho}+\bar{P}}\Gamma_{1}\zeta_{1}{}^{\prime},$ [src: second unnumbered display after Eq. 11], recovering the results of their Ref. 6 [src: text after that display]. For the comoving curvature, ${\cal R}_{a}\equiv-D_{a}\alpha=-W_{a}.$ [src: Eq. 12]; for a single scalar field, "if $\sigma_{ab}$ can be neglected", $\Gamma_a$ is negligible on large scales and ${\cal R}_a$ "is conserved, on super-Hubble scales, to all orders in perturbation theory", whereas with more fields $\Gamma_{a}\simeq-2D_{a}V\neq 0$ and ${\cal R}_a$ "can evolve on large scales during inflation" [src: paragraph beginning "For a scalar field"].

- `[der]` The shear and vorticity term of Eq. 6 is what the gradient expansion of Step 2 drops at its order: Langlois & Vernizzi keep it, so their equation states exactly which term must be negligible besides $\Gamma_a$.

### Step 4 — single field: attractor needed
<!-- key: step-4-single-field-attractor-needed -->

Naruko & Sasaki (arXiv:1101.3180) show "that the same conservation holds for a universe dominated by a single scalar field provided that the field is in an attractor regime, for a very general class of scalar field theories", and that "if the scalar field equation contains a second time derivative of the metric, as in the case of the Galileon theory, one has to invoke the gravitational field equations to show the conservation" [src: abstract].

Leach, Sasaki, Wands & Liddle (arXiv:astro-ph/0101406) make the mode content explicit. From ${\cal R}_{c}^{\prime\prime}+2{z^{\prime}\over z}{\cal R}_{c}^{\prime}+k^{2}{\cal R}_{c}=0,$ [src: Sect. II, Eq. 3], the comoving curvature perturbation is "known to stay constant in time on superhorizon scales in the absence of any entropy perturbation", and the constant solution "corresponds to the growing adiabatic mode", but "this does not necessarily mean that ${\cal R}_{c}$ must stay constant in time after its scale crosses the Hubble horizon": the decaying mode "does not necessarily start to decay right after horizon crossing" [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 3]. The Wronskian obeys $W^{\prime}=-2{z^{\prime}\over z}W\,,$ [src: Eq. 4], so the decaying mode is $v(\eta)=u(\eta){D(\eta)\over D(\eta_{k})}\,,$ [src: Eq. 6] with $D(\eta)=3{\cal H}_{k}\int_{\eta}^{\eta_{*}}d\eta^{\prime}{z^{2}(\eta_{k})u^{2}(\eta_{k})\over z^{2}(\eta^{\prime})u^{2}(\eta^{\prime})}\,,$ [src: Eq. 7], and, if the amplitude at horizon crossing is "determined by an initial vacuum state for quantum fluctuations", the mode coefficients can become large "if $D_{k}\gg 1$ or $(D_{k}/{\cal H}_{k})|u^{\prime}/u|\gg 1$" [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 10]. The abstract states the condition as "if the quantity $a\dot{\phi}/H$ becomes sufficiently small, as compared to its value at horizon crossing, for some time interval during inflation" [src: abstract].

Namjoo, Firouzjahi & Sasaki (arXiv:1210.3692) give the same structure for a non-attractor phase: from $\left(a^{2}\epsilon{{\cal R}}^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}+k^{2}a^{2}\epsilon{\cal R}=0\,.$ [src: Eq. 8], on super-horizon scales ${\cal R}=C_{1}+C_{2}\int\frac{d\tau}{a^{2}\epsilon}$ [src: Eq. 9]; "in our case in which the system is in the non-attractor phase, $\epsilon\propto a^{-6}$ so the would be decaying mode actually dominates over the constant mode" [src: text after Eq. 9], and "the curvature perturbation must be evaluated at (or after) the end of inflation rather than the time of horizon crossing" [src: paragraph beginning "where the subscript k denotes"]. The abstract calls this "a simple, toy model of single field inflation" [src: abstract].

### Step 5 — the separate universe and δN at a transition
<!-- key: step-5-the-separate-universe-and-deltan-at-a -->

Jackson et al. (arXiv:2311.03281) write the homogeneous (separate-universe) solution as $\mathcal{R}_{\textrm{h}}=C+D\int^{\eta_{1}}_{\eta}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\tilde{\eta}}{z^{2}(\tilde{\eta})}\,,$ [src: Sect. 2.3, Eq. 13], where D "can be identified with a non-adiabatic homogeneous perturbation since it controls $\dot{\mathcal{R}}_{\textrm{h}}$"; the decaying term "decreases in slow roll where $1/z^{2}\propto\eta^{2}$ and increases in ultra-slow roll where $1/z^{2}\propto\eta^{-4}$" [src: Sect. 2.3, text after Eq. 13]. Gradient corrections follow order by order in $k^2$ from $\mathcal{G}_{n}^{\prime\prime}+2\frac{z^{\prime}}{z}\mathcal{G}_{n}^{\prime}=-\mathcal{G}_{n-1}\,,$ [src: Sect. 2.3.1, Eq. 17], and "the $k^{2}$-correction to the growing mode, $\mathcal{G}_{1}(\eta)$, is only defined up to terms proportional to the leading-order decaying mode" [src: Sect. 2.3.1, last paragraph]. They find that the approach "breaks down on a finite range of super-Hubble scales at a sudden transition during inflation from slow roll to ultra-slow roll", that "The sudden transition leads to particle production in the inflaton field on sub-Hubble scales and non-adiabatic pressure perturbations on super-Hubble scales", and that it "works in a piece-wise fashion, before the transition and in the ultra-slow-roll phase after" [src: Sect. 6, second and third paragraphs]; with the corrected homogeneous solution used post-transition in the classical δN formalism, "including a non-adiabatic decaying mode", "the correct linear power spectrum can be recovered at the end of inflation", which "suggests that a modified separate-universe approach might still be used" [src: Sect. 6, sixth paragraph].

Artigas, Pi & Tanaka (arXiv:2408.09964) note that the δN formalism discards "all the gradient terms ... which are not negligibly small in models beyond slow-roll", and encode them "in a homogeneous-spatial-curvature contribution assigned to each FLRW patch"; applied to ultra-slow-roll inflation, it "can correctly describe the large-scale evolution of the comoving curvature perturbation from the horizon exit" [src: abstract]. Raveendran's reading of this curvature term and its limit are in `open-questions.md#where-the-separate-universe-stops`.

### Step 6 — anisotropic stress that is not negligible
<!-- key: step-6-anisotropic-stress-that-is-not-negligible -->

Shaw et al. (arXiv:0911.2714) give a worked case: a primordial magnetic field produced quickly at $\tau_B$ before neutrino decoupling at $\tau_\nu$, the decoupling assumed "effectively instantaneous"; in the radiation era, "The fluid is tightly bound to the trace amount of Baryons and cannot develop any anisotropic stress, and so the only anisotropic stress comes from the primordial magnetic source, the constant $\Pi_{B}$", which nothing compensates until neutrino decoupling [src: Sect. VII.1, paragraph after Eq. 81]. From the radiation-dominated Bardeen equation $3k^{2}\tau^{2}\left[\tau^{2}\ddot{\Phi}+4\tau\dot{\Phi}\right]+k^{4}\tau^{4}\Phi=-R_{\gamma}\Pi_{B}\left(6+k^{2}\tau^{2}\right)$ [src: Eq. 82], their curvature perturbation evolves on super-horizon scales as $\zeta(\tau)=\zeta(\tau_{B})-\frac{1}{3}R_{\gamma}\Pi_{B}\left[\log{(\tau/\tau_{B})}+\frac{\tau_{B}}{2\tau}-\frac{1}{2}\right]$ [src: Eq. 84], and once the neutrino anisotropic stress compensates the magnetic one, $\zeta\approx\zeta(\tau_{B})-\frac{1}{3}R_{\gamma}\Pi_{B}\left[\log{(\tau_{\nu}/\tau_{B})}+\left(\frac{5}{8R_{\nu}}-1\right)\right]\,,$ [src: Eq. 86], "where we have neglected terms in $\tau_{B}/\tau_{\nu}\ll 1$" [src: text after Eq. 86]. Their ζ, which they call "the co-moving curvature perturbation", is written in their own gauge-invariant notation, $\zeta=\Phi+2(\Psi+\dot{\Phi}/\mathcal{H})/3(1+w)$ [src: Sect. VII.1, inline, text after Eq. 83], whose potentials were not read.

- `[der]` This is the shear term of Step 1 at work: a super-Hubble anisotropic stress sources Ψ − Φ and the comoving shear, so ζ changes with δP_nad = 0.

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

| Route | Gravity used | Order | What it neglects | Paper and place |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Energy conservation on uniform-density slices | none | linear | the divergence of the 3-momentum on zero-shear slices, assumed negligible on super-horizon scales | Wands et al., arXiv:astro-ph/0003278 [src: Sect. III.1, text after Eqs. 18–19] |
| Same, with the Einstein constraint | Einstein, for the shear term | linear | requires Ψ finite | Malik & Wands, arXiv:0809.4944 [src: Sect. 8.2.2, text after Eq. 8.37] |
| Gradient expansion | none for Eq. 24; Einstein for the decay of $\dot{\tilde\gamma}$ | first order in ε | anisotropic stress, taken second order in ε | Lyth, Malik & Sasaki, arXiv:astro-ph/0411220 [src: Sect. II.2; Sect. III.1] |
| Covariant | none | exact, all scales | nothing; the shear–vorticity term is kept | Langlois & Vernizzi, arXiv:astro-ph/0503416 [src: Eq. 6 and text after Eq. 7] |
| Newtonian-gauge mode argument | Einstein | linear, limit q → 0 | assumes continuity in q of the field-equation coefficients | Weinberg, arXiv:astro-ph/0302326 [src: paragraph beginning "Our 'mild technical' assumption"] |

- **Weinberg's route.** Instead of a conservation law, Weinberg (arXiv:astro-ph/0302326) uses a "remnant gauge symmetry of the field equations in Newtonian gauge" at q = 0 to find exact solutions, keeps only those that are limits of q ≠ 0 solutions, and shows that two adiabatic modes always exist, one with ℛ ≠ 0 constant and one with ℛ = 0 [src: paragraph beginning "We consider a general cosmological model"]. Weinberg's assumption is that the coefficients of the field equations "are continuous functions of q in at least a neighborhood of q=0", which "we expect ... will always be satisfied, and in any case it is easy to check for any specific model" [src: paragraph beginning "Our 'mild technical' assumption"]. Weinberg's quantity X vanishes when the perturbed pressure is a function only of the perturbed density, "as is the case in a universe dominated either by pure radiation or by pure cold matter, but not when both radiation and cold matter are important, and also not during inflation or in the curvaton model"; it vanishes as q → 0 for inflation "with a single scalar field, but this is not true with several scalar fields" [src: introduction, paragraph beginning "Now, the quantity X"]. Weinberg also notes that the q = 0 analysis assumes all Newtonian-gauge fluctuations finite, an assumption "less innocent than it may seem" [src: paragraph beginning "We work in the limit q=0"].
- **Symmetry route at loop level.** Fang et al. (arXiv:2507.00077), for "strongly interacting inflationary models" and non-attractor inflation, state that super-horizon conservation "is essentially a direct consequence of the symmetry in perturbation theory" and that a Ward identity "imposes strict non-perturbative constraints on the infrared power spectrum" [src: abstract]; the loop debate is in `thcosmo-primordial-correlators`.

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Uniform-density slices that do not exist.** These hypersurfaces "become ill-defined if the density is not strictly decreasing, as can occur in a scalar field dominated universe when the kinetic energy of the scalar field vanishes"; one can then use the density perturbation on uniform-curvature slices, $\delta\rho_{\psi}=\dot{\rho}\,\xi\,,$ "which remains finite" (arXiv:astro-ph/0003278) [src: Sect. II, Eq. 9 and text after].
- **ℛ read at horizon crossing in a non-attractor phase.** The would-be decaying mode dominates; evaluate at or after the end of inflation (arXiv:1210.3692) [src: text after Eq. 9]. 
- `[der]` Check: whether z decreases after exit (Leach et al., Eq. 3) or whether the decaying term grows (Jackson et al., Sect. 2.3).
- **δN started with H/2π across a sudden transition.** See the Numbers table (arXiv:2311.03281) [src: Sect. 6]; check: match to the mode functions after exit with the $k^2$ corrections, as Jackson et al. do.
- **Anisotropic stress assumed away.** Lyth, Malik & Sasaki take it second order in ε, which "can be verified in specific cases, in particular if the fluid consists of a gas and/or scalar fields" (arXiv:astro-ph/0411220) [src: Sect. III.1, third paragraph]; Weinberg's universal large-wavelength formulas hold "In the absence of anisotropic inertia" (arXiv:astro-ph/0302326) [src: abstract]. In Malik & Wands' linear form it enters through the momentum equation $V^{\prime}+(1-3c^{2}_{\rm s}){\cal H}V+\phi+\frac{1}{\rho+P}\left(\delta P+\frac{2}{3}\nabla^{2}\Pi\right)=0\,,$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 8.2.2, Eq. 8.33].
- `[der]` Check that the shear term of Eq. 8.35 stays suppressed when Π is not negligible.
- **A modified-gravity claim of conservation.** Lyth, Malik & Sasaki's condition on $\dot{\tilde\gamma}$ uses Einstein gravity (Step 2); Naruko & Sasaki need the field equations for Galileon-type theories (arXiv:1101.3180) [src: abstract].
- **Two signs of ζ at second order.** Langlois & Vernizzi state the relation of their $\zeta_2$ to that of their Ref. 6 by Malik and Wands (Conventions); whether the 2008 review arXiv:0809.4944 uses the same $\zeta_2$ was not checked, so no relation is assumed.
- **Printed slips met in this reading.** Jackson et al. print the de Sitter limit as "$\mu^{2}=\to-2/\eta^{2}$" (arXiv:2311.03281) [src: Sect. 2.3.1, subsection "Solution for massless inflaton field in de Sitter", inline]; Namjoo et al.'s abstract reads "non-atractor phase" (arXiv:1210.3692) [src: abstract]; Malik & Wands introduce the shear term as Σ and define it as $\Sigma_V$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 8.2.2, text after Eq. 8.35]; Shaw et al. write "their are effectively two types of perturbation" (arXiv:0911.2714) [src: Sect. VII.1, paragraph beginning "At some later time"]. <!-- num: not a value -->
