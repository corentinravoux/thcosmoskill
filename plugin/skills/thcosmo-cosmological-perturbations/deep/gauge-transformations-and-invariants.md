## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

Four routes are followed below, each in its own conventions; no paper read writes the map between them, and none is written here.

- Malik & Wands: active approach, in which the transformation "is evaluated at the same coordinate point" (`formalism.md#second-order-perturbations-and-their-gauge`); flat background in conformal time η; ϕ in $g_{00}$, ψ entering $C_{ij}$ with a minus sign, scalar shifts B and E; generator split into α (slicing) and β, $\gamma^i$ (threading).
- Durrer (arXiv:astro-ph/0402129) works on a curved background, $g_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}=-a^{2}(\eta)d\eta^{2}+a^{2}(\eta)\gamma_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}.$ [src: Sect. 2, Eq. 6], with $\gamma_{ij}$ "the metric of a space with constant curvature κ" rescaled so that κ = ±1 or 0 [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 1]; perturbations are expanded in harmonics $Y^{(S)}$, $Y^{(V)}$, $Y^{(T)}$ with wavenumber k, A in $h_{00}$, and Durrer's Bardeen potential Ψ sits in $g_{00}$, Φ in the spatial part (Eq. 43 below), the reverse of Malik & Wands' letters.
- Laine & Procacci (arXiv:2607.06983) adopt the passive picture, "physics remains unchanged, but coordinates are transformed, leading to a relabelling", and note that "In the framework of cosmological perturbation theory, both points of view are valid", citing their Ref. 1 [src: Sect. 4.1, second paragraph]; their new coordinates are $\tilde{x}^{\alpha}(q)\;=\;x^{\alpha}(q)+\xi^{\alpha}(q)+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{2})\ .$ [src: Sect. 4.1, Eq. 4.1], with conformal time τ and Bardeen potentials φ (in $h_0$) and ψ (in $h_{\rm D}$).
- Ma & Bertschinger (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072) take $\hat{x}^{\mu}=x^{\mu}+d^{\mu}(x^{\nu})$ [src: Sect. 3, Eq. 8] on a flat background, with ψ in $g_{00}$ and φ in the trace of $g_{ij}$, conformal time τ.
- `[der]` The sign in front of the time shift differs between these routes (compare Laine & Procacci's Eq. 4.4 with the active rule of Malik & Wands quoted in `formalism.md`); an equation is combined across papers only after its generator is read in its own paper.

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — the transformation law of a tensor
<!-- key: step-1-the-transformation-law-of-a-tensor -->

Durrer fixes an "admissible" Friedmann background and allows only diffeomorphisms that leave it invariant, "i.e. which deviate only in first order form the identity" (as printed), generated by the flow of a vector field X (arXiv:astro-ph/0402129) [src: Sect. 3.1, third paragraph]. The pullback to first order is $\phi^{*}=id+\epsilon L_{X}$ [src: Sect. 3.1, unnumbered display before Eq. 17], so that the metric perturbation transforms as $h\rightarrow h+a^{-2}L_{X}\bar{g}~.$ [src: Eq. 17] and any tensor perturbation as $Q^{(1)}\rightarrow Q^{(1)}+L_{X}\bar{Q}~.$ [src: Eq. 18]. Hence only perturbations of tensors with a vanishing Lie derivative of the background along every X, "i.e., with vanishing (or constant) 'background contribution' are gauge invariant", a result "sometimes referred to as the 'Stewart-Walker Lemma'", citing Ref. 3 of the lectures [src: Sect. 3.1, text after Eq. 18].

Malik & Wands reach the same structure from the exponential map, $\widetilde{\bf T}=e^{\pounds_{\xi}}{\bf T}\,,$ [src: Eq. 6.1], which at first order gives $\epsilon\widetilde{\delta{\bf T}_{1}}=\epsilon{\delta{\bf T}_{1}}+\epsilon{\pounds}_{\xi_{1}}{\bf T}_{0}\,,$ (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 6.1.1, Eq. 6.5], crediting the expansion to their Refs. 121 and 26. In their passive approach, "all quantities ... are evaluated at the same physical point", the coordinates are related at first order by $\tilde{x}^{\mu}=x^{\mu}-\xi_{1}^{\mu}\,.$ [src: Sect. 6.1.2, Eq. 6.10], and the invariance of the density, $\tilde{\rho}(\tilde{x}^{\mu})=\rho(x^{\mu})\,.$ [src: Eq. 6.11], gives $\widetilde{\delta\rho_{1}}=\delta\rho_{1}+\rho_{0}^{\prime}\xi_{1}^{0}\,.$ [src: Eq. 6.14], while the active approach gives the rule with $\alpha_1$ in place of $\xi_1^0$ [src: Sect. 6.2.1, Eq. 6.18]. The authors state that the passive view "is very popular at first order", citing for example Bardeen and in the reviews of Kodama & Sasaki and of Mukhanov, Feldman & Brandenberger [src: Sect. 6.1.2, text after Eq. 6.9], and they do not follow the line-element route further, pointing to their Refs. 66 and 104 [src: Sect. 6.1.2, text after Eq. 6.15].

Laine & Procacci derive the rules in the passive picture by evaluating each quantity at the shifted point: for a scalar, $\tilde{A}\;=\;\bar{\tilde{A}}+\delta\tilde{A}\;\overset{{\mbox{\tiny\rm{\eqref{eq_coor_transf}}}}}{=}\;\bar{A}-(\partial_{\alpha}\bar{A})\xi^{\alpha}+\delta A+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{2})\hskip 10.00002pt\Rightarrow\hskip 10.00002pt\boxed{\hskip 10.00002pt\delta\tilde{A}\;\approx\;\delta A-\bar{A}^{\prime}\xi^{0}\;.\hskip 10.00002pt\vphantom{\Big|}}$ (arXiv:2607.06983) [src: Sect. 4.1, Eq. 4.4], and they stress, for scalars, that "the only quantities which do not transform at all, are those whose background value vanishes or is time-independent" [src: Sect. 4.1, text after Eq. 4.4]. With the spatial parameter split as $\xi^{i}\;=\;-\partial^{i}\xi+\xi^{i}_{\text{v}}\ ,\hskip 20.00003pt\partial_{i}\xi^{i}_{\text{v}}\;=\;0\ ,$ [src: Eq. 4.8], a tensor obeys $\boxed{\hskip 10.00002pt\delta\tilde{C}_{\mu\nu}\;\approx\;\delta C_{\mu\nu}-\xi^{\rho}_{,\mu}\bar{C}_{\rho\nu}-\xi^{\rho}_{,\nu}\bar{C}_{\mu\rho}-\bar{C}_{\mu\nu}^{\hskip 0.85358pt\prime}\,\xi^{0}\;.\hskip 10.00002pt\vphantom{\Big|}}$ [src: Eq. 4.13]. Counting: "In addition to two scalar gauge parameters, $\xi^{0}$ and ξ, there are two transverse vector gauge parameters", so "only two of the four scalar perturbations ... are physical, and likewise, only two of the four vector perturbations" [src: Sect. 4.1, last paragraph].

Ma & Bertschinger take the coordinate route instead: requiring $ds^2$ invariant gives $\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}(x)=g_{\mu\nu}(x)-g_{\mu\beta}(x)\partial_{\nu}d^{\beta}-g_{\alpha\nu}(x)\partial_{\mu}d^{\alpha}-d^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}g_{\mu\nu}(x)+O(d^{2})\,.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072) [src: Sect. 3, Eq. 10], where "both sides of this equation are evaluated at the same coordinate values x in the two gauges, which do not correspond to the same physical point in general" [src: Sect. 3, text after Eq. 10].

### Step 2 — the metric potentials
<!-- key: step-2-the-metric-potentials -->

Applying Eq. 17 with ${\bf X}=T\partial_{\eta}+L^{i}\partial_{i}.$ [src: Eq. 34] to the parameterisation $h_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}=-2Ad\eta^{2}-2B_{i}d\eta dx^{i}+2H_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j},$ [src: Eq. 33], Durrer obtains, per harmonic, $A\rightarrow A+\frac{\dot{a}}{a}T+\dot{T}$ [src: Eq. 36], $B\rightarrow B-\dot{L}-kT$ [src: Eq. 37], $H_{L}\rightarrow H_{L}+\frac{\dot{a}}{a}T+\frac{k}{3}L$ [src: Eq. 39], $H_{T}\rightarrow H_{T}-kL$ [src: Eq. 40] and $H^{(T)}\rightarrow H^{(T)}.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0402129) [src: Eq. 42]; "Two scalar and one vector variable can be set to zero by a cleverly chosen gauge transformations" (as printed) [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 42]. The same rules in the other routes: Malik & Wands' Eqs. 6.37–6.43 (`formalism.md#gauge-transformations-and-gauge-invariant`), Laine & Procacci's Eqs. 4.21–4.27, and Ma & Bertschinger's Eqs. 11a–11d, each in its own sign of the generator.

### Step 3 — Bardeen potentials
<!-- key: step-3-bardeen-potentials -->

Durrer notes that "One often chooses" $kL=H_{T}$ and $kT=B+\dot{L}$ [src: Sect. 3.3, inline, text after Eq. 42], so that $H_T$ and B vanish (the longitudinal gauge), and writes the scalar metric as $h_{\mu\nu}^{(S)}=-2\Psi d\eta^{2}-2\Phi\gamma_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}.$ [src: Eq. 43]; in a generic gauge, $\Psi=A-\frac{\dot{a}}{a}k^{-1}\sigma-k^{-1}\dot{\sigma}$ [src: Eq. 44] and $\Phi=-H_{L}-\frac{1}{3}H_{T}+\frac{\dot{a}}{a}k^{-1}\sigma$ [src: Eq. 45], with $\sigma=k^{-1}\dot{H}_{T}-B$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0402129) [src: Sect. 3.3, inline, text after Eq. 45], and "A short calculation using Eqs. (36) to (40) shows that Ψ and Φ are gauge invariant" [src: same place]. Durrer adds a geometric check: the Weyl tensor vanishes in a Friedmann universe, so its perturbation is gauge invariant, and for scalar perturbations $C^{0}_{~i0j}={1\over 2}\left[(\Psi+\Phi)_{|ij}-{1\over 3}\triangle(\Psi+\Phi)\gamma_{ij}\right]$ [src: Eq. 46].

Laine & Procacci perform the same construction explicitly: the transformation to the Newtonian gauge is $\xi\;\overset{{\mbox{\tiny\rm{(\ref{eq_hijs-gauge})}}}}{=}\;-\vartheta\ ,\hskip 20.00003pt\xi^{0}\;\overset{{\mbox{\tiny\rm{(\ref{eq_his-gauge})}}}}{=}\;\vartheta^{\prime}-h\ .$ [src: Sect. 4.2, Eq. 4.29], and the remaining potentials, evaluated after it, define $\hskip 10.00002pt\vphantom{\bigg|}\phi\;\equiv\;h_{0}^{\mbox{\tiny\rm{N}}}\;\underset{{\mbox{\tiny\rm{(\ref{eq_Ngauge})}}}}{\overset{{\mbox{\tiny\rm{(\ref{eq_h0-gauge})}}}\vphantom{|^{b}_{q}}}{=}}\;h_{0}+(\partial_{\tau}+\mathcal{H})(\,h-\vartheta^{\prime}\,)\ ,$ [src: Eq. 4.30] and $\psi\;\equiv\;h_{\mbox{\tiny\rm{D}}}^{\mbox{\tiny\rm{N}}}\;\underset{{\mbox{\tiny\rm{(\ref{eq_Ngauge})}}}}{\overset{{\mbox{\tiny\rm{(\ref{eq_hD-gauge})}}}\vphantom{|^{b}_{q}}}{=}}\;h_{\mbox{\tiny\rm{D}}}+\frac{1}{3}\nabla^{2}\vartheta+\mathcal{H}(\vartheta^{\prime}-h)\ ,$ (arXiv:2607.06983) [src: Eq. 4.31], "known as the Bardeen potentials", citing their Ref. 2, whose invariance they prove term by term from $\tilde{\vartheta}^{\prime}-\tilde{h}=\vartheta^{\prime}-h-\xi^{0}$ [src: Sect. 4.2, text after Eq. 4.31, and Eqs. 4.32–4.33]. Their vector invariant is $\tilde{h}^{\mbox{\scriptsize v}}_{i}-\tilde{\vartheta}^{{\mbox{\scriptsize v}}\hskip 0.85358pt\prime}_{i}=h^{\mbox{\scriptsize v}}_{i}-\vartheta^{{\mbox{\scriptsize v}}\hskip 0.85358pt\prime}_{i}$ [src: Sect. 4.2, inline, text after Eq. 4.27].

- `[der]` The general recipe all three papers follow: pick the generator that sets two scalar potentials to zero; the remaining potentials, written in the original gauge, are invariants. Malik & Wands state it as "any unambiguous choice of time-slicing and threading can be used to define explicitly gauge-invariant perturbations" (quoted in `formalism.md#gauge-transformations-and-gauge-invariant`).

### Step 4 — ℛ, ζ and the Sasaki–Mukhanov variable from three slicings
<!-- key: step-4-r-and-the-sasaki-mukhanov-variable-from -->

Malik & Wands (arXiv:0809.4944) build the three curvature-type variables by fixing α through a condition on the slice (a matter condition, or a flat spatial metric), then reading ψ or the field perturbation on that slice. Comoving orthogonal slicing, $\tilde{v}_i=0$ and $\tilde{v}_{i}+\tilde{B}_{i}=0$: $\alpha_{1{\rm{com}}}=v_{1}+B_{1}\,,$ $\beta_{1{\rm{com}}}=\int v_{1}d\eta+\hat{\cal{C}}(x^{i})\,,$ [src: Sect. 7.4, Eq. 7.44], where $\hat{\cal{C}}$ "represents a residual gauge freedom, corresponding to a constant shift of the spatial coordinates", on which "All the 3-scalars like curvature, expansion, acceleration and shear" do not depend [src: Sect. 7.4, text after Eq. 7.44]. Then ${{\cal{R}}}\equiv\widetilde{\psi_{1{\rm{com}}}}=\psi_{1}-{\cal H}\left(v_{1}+B_{1}\right)\,,$ [src: Eq. 7.46], used "with a constant pre-factor" by Lukash and denoted ℛ by Lyth, as the authors state with their Refs. 91 and 92 [src: Sect. 7.4, text after Eq. 7.47]. With the Einstein equations, $v_{1}+B_{1}=\frac{{\cal H}\phi_{1}+\psi_{1}^{\prime}}{{\cal H}^{\prime}-{\cal H}^{2}}\,.$ [src: Eq. 7.49], so that ${{\cal{R}}}=\Psi-\frac{{\cal H}({\cal H}\Phi+\Psi^{\prime})}{{\cal H}^{\prime}-{\cal H}^{2}}\,,$ [src: Eq. 7.50], which they say coincides with the ζ of Mukhanov, Feldman & Brandenberger [src: Sect. 7.4, text after Eq. 7.50]; for one scalar field, ${{\cal{R}}}=\psi_{1}+\frac{{\cal H}}{{\varphi}_{0}^{\prime}}\delta\varphi_{{1}}\,.$ [src: Eq. 7.51]. Uniform-density slicing: $\widetilde{\delta\rho_{1}}=0$ needs $\alpha_{1{\delta\rho}}=-\frac{\delta\rho_{1}}{\rho_{0}^{\prime}}\,.$ [src: Sect. 7.6.1, Eq. 7.60], and the curvature on these slices is $-\zeta_{1}\equiv\widetilde{\psi_{1{\delta\rho}}}=\psi_{1}+{\cal H}\frac{\delta\rho_{1}}{\rho_{0}^{\prime}}\,.$ [src: Eq. 7.61], credited to their Refs. 40 and 114, with footnote 3 relating it to the ζ of their Ref. 143 by $\zeta_{\rm{SBB}}\equiv 3\zeta_{1}$ [src: Sect. 7.6.1, footnote 3]. "There is still the freedom to choose the spatial gauge. In particular we can choose either $\tilde{B}$, $\tilde{E}$ or $\tilde{v}$ to be zero and thus fix β" [src: Sect. 7.6.1, text after Eq. 7.61]. The two curvatures differ by the comoving density, $\zeta_{1}=-{{\cal{R}}}_{1}-\frac{{\cal H}}{\rho_{0}^{\prime}}\delta\rho_{1{\rm{com}}}\,,$ [src: Eq. 7.63], which, by the Einstein equations of their Sect. 8, vanishes in the large-scale limit [src: Sect. 7.6.1, text after Eq. 7.63]. Flat slicing, with the time shift of `formalism.md#gauge-transformations-and-gauge-invariant`: the field perturbation on these slices, $\widetilde{\delta{\varphi}_{1{\rm{flat}}}}\equiv\delta\varphi_{{1}}+\varphi_{0}^{\prime}\frac{\psi_{1}}{{\cal H}}\,,$ [src: Sect. 7.2.1, Eq. 7.23], "is the gauge-invariant Sasaki-Mukhanov variable", citing their Refs. 145 and 120, "often denoted $\cal{Q}$" [src: Sect. 7.2.1, text after Eq. 7.23]; it is tied to ℛ by $\widetilde{{\delta\varphi}_{1{\rm{flat}}}}=\frac{\varphi_{0}^{\prime}}{{\cal H}}{{\cal{R}}}\,.$ [src: Sect. 7.4, Eq. 7.52]. On flat slices the density is $-\zeta_{1}=\frac{{\cal H}}{\rho_{0}^{\prime}}\delta\rho_{1{\rm{flat}}}\,.$ [src: Sect. 7.6.1, Eq. 7.62].

Laine & Procacci (arXiv:2607.06983) build the comoving slicing in the passive picture: the slicing is comoving if its normal is the fluid four-velocity, "A comoving slicing does not necessarily exist in general. However, for scalar perturbations ... it always exists", as $v=h$, reached by $\xi^{0}\;=\;v-h\ .$ [src: Sect. 4.2, Eqs. 4.40–4.41 and text before]; "This does not yet define a (scalar) gauge, as ξ is still arbitrary"; comoving threading, $v=0$, follows from $\xi^{\prime}\;=\;-v\;.$ [src: Eq. 4.44], and "A time-independent part of ξ remains unspecified by this condition" [src: Sect. 4.2, text after Eq. 4.44]. Both conditions together give their comoving gauge, in which the velocity and shift potentials both vanish [src: Eq. 4.45].

- `[der]` The leftover time-independent ξ of Laine & Procacci and the $\hat{\cal C}(x^i)$ of Malik & Wands' Eq. 7.44 play the same role: a relabelling of the spatial coordinates that leaves every 3-scalar unchanged.

Cho (arXiv:2608.13498), for the quadratic effective energy–momentum tensor of scalar perturbations "during slow-roll inflation", states the uniform-density gauge completely by imposing "the complete scalar gauge conditions δρ=0 and E=0", and finds that the uniform-density time shift differs from the comoving one by "an explicitly gradient-suppressed term", $Q_{\mathrm{UD}}-Q_{\mathrm{C}}=-ΔΨ/[3{\mathcal H}({\mathcal H}'-{\mathcal H}^2)]$ [src: abstract, inline], in Cho's conventions, which were not read beyond the abstract.

### Step 5 — what each gauge fixes
<!-- key: step-5-what-each-gauge-fixes -->

| Gauge | Slicing (time shift) | Threading (spatial shift) | Left free | Paper and place |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Longitudinal / Newtonian | fixed by zero shear | fixed by E = 0 | for Ω = 1 only "the addition of spatial constants to ψ and φ"; at q = 0 "a remnant gauge symmetry of the field equations" | Ma & Bertschinger, arXiv:astro-ph/9506072 [src: Sect. 2, paragraph beginning "No gauge modes are present"]; Weinberg, arXiv:astro-ph/0302326 [src: paragraph beginning "We consider a general cosmological model"] |
| Spatially flat | fixed by a vanishing curvature potential | fixed by vanishing scalar and vector shifts of the spatial metric | `[der]` no integration function appears in the generator | Malik & Wands, Sect. 7.2.1 (`formalism.md#gauge-transformations-and-gauge-invariant`) |
| Comoving orthogonal | by the fluid velocity plus shift | by the integrated velocity | a constant spatial shift | Malik & Wands, Step 4 above |
| Comoving (fluid) | by comoving slicing | by comoving threading | a time-independent part of ξ | Laine & Procacci, Step 4 above |
| Uniform density | by the density perturbation | by setting one of the shift, E or the velocity to zero | β until that choice is made | Malik & Wands and Cho, Step 4 above |
| Synchronous | by a vanishing lapse perturbation, up to one function of space | by a vanishing shift, up to functions of space | two scalar functions of space | Malik & Wands, Step 6 below |

### Step 6 — the residual mode of the synchronous gauge
<!-- key: step-6-the-residual-mode-of-the-synchronous -->

Malik & Wands (arXiv:0809.4944): the conditions $\widetilde{{\phi_{{1}}}}=\widetilde{B_{1}}_{i}=0$ give $\alpha_{1{\rm{syn}}}=-\frac{1}{a}\left(\int a\phi_{1}d\eta-{\cal{C}}_{1}(x^{i})\right)\,,$ [src: Sect. 7.3, Eq. 7.36], $\beta_{1{\rm{syn}}}=\int\left(\alpha_{1{\rm{syn}}}-B_{1}\right)d\eta+\hat{\cal{C}}_{1}(x^{i})\,,$ [src: Eq. 7.37] and $\gamma_{1{\rm{syn}}}^{i}=\int S_{1}^{i}d\eta+\hat{\cal{C}}_{1}^{i}(x^{i})\,.$ [src: Eq. 7.38]; "This does not determine the time-slicing unambiguously and we are left with two arbitrary scalar functions of the spatial coordinates", of which $\hat{\cal C}_1$ "affects only the labelling of the coordinates on the initial spatial hypersurface, but ${\cal C}_1$ affects scalar perturbations on spatial hypersurfaces" [src: Sect. 7.3, text after Eq. 7.38]. The mode enters the curvature as $\widetilde{\psi_{1{\rm{syn}}}}=\psi_{1}+\frac{{\cal H}}{a}\left(\int a\phi_{1}d\eta-{\cal{C}}(x^{i})\right)\,,$ [src: Eq. 7.39] and the density as $\widetilde{\delta\rho_{1{\rm{syn}}}}=\delta\rho_{1}-\frac{\rho_{0}^{\prime}}{a}\left(\int a\phi_{1}d\eta-{\cal{C}}(x^{i})\right)\,,$ [src: Eq. 7.41], so that "it is not possible to define gauge-invariant quantities in general using this gauge condition", citing their Ref. 114; Lifshitz, who first used the gauge, "dealt with the residual gauge freedom by eliminating the unphysical gauge modes through symmetry arguments" [src: Sect. 7.3, text after Eq. 7.42]. The authors note that "we can follow Ref. [28]" and set the initial CDM velocity to zero, which fixes ${\cal{C}}_{1}(x)=a(v_{1{\rm cdm}}+B_{1})\,.$ [src: Eq. 7.43], and "for pressureless matter, momentum conservation equation ensures that $a(v_{1{\rm cdm}}+B_{1})$ is a constant" [src: Sect. 7.3, text after Eq. 7.43].

Ma & Bertschinger (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072) show the same freedom in two places. In the transformation from conformal Newtonian to synchronous coordinates, $\alpha(\vec{x},\tau)=\dot{\beta}(\vec{x},\tau)+\xi(\tau)\,,$ [src: Sect. 3, Eq. 14a], "where ξ(τ) is an arbitrary function of time", corresponding to "a global redefinition of the units of time with no physical significance; therefore we shall set ξ=0 from now on" [src: Sect. 3, text after Eq. 14d]. The relation between the two gauges' potentials then follows in k-space as their Eq. 18 [src: Sect. 3, Eq. 18]. In the super-horizon solutions of the radiation era, with neutrinos and photons only and the $k^2$ terms dropped, the metric trace obeys $\tau{d^{4}h\over d\tau^{4}}+5{d^{3}h\over d\tau^{3}}=0\,,$ [src: Sect. 7, Eq. 93], whose solutions give $h=A+B(k\tau)^{-2}+C(k\tau)^{2}+D(k\tau)\,,$ $\delta\equiv(1-R_{\nu})\delta_{\gamma}+R_{\nu}\delta_{\nu}=-{2\over 3}B(k\tau)^{-2}-{2\over 3}C(k\tau)^{2}-{1\over 6}D(k\tau)\,,$ $\theta\equiv(1-R_{\nu})\theta_{\gamma}+R_{\nu}\theta_{\nu}=-{3\over 8}Dk\,,$ [src: Eq. 94] with $\eta=2C+{3\over 4}D(k\tau)^{-1}\,.$ [src: Eq. 95]. Quoting Press & Vishniac (1980), "the first two (proportional to A and B) are gauge modes that can be eliminated by a suitable coordinate transformation"; C and D are physical, and "The two gauge modes (A and B in eq. [94]) do not exist in the conformal Newtonian gauge" [src: Sect. 7, text after Eq. 95 and last paragraph]. They attribute the gauge modes to the arbitrary "choice of the initial hypersurface and its coordinate assignments" [src: Sect. 2, paragraph beginning "In spite of its wide-spread use"].
- `[der]` The B mode, with δ falling as an inverse square of kτ, is the time-slicing freedom ${\cal C}_1$ of Malik & Wands seen in a solution; Malik & Wands' fix (Eq. 7.43) and Ma & Bertschinger's vanishing initial CDM velocity divergence in their initial conditions remove it in the same way. Neither paper writes this identification.

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **Harmonic, curved-space route (Durrer).** Works for κ = 0, ±1 from the start and gives the matter invariants per harmonic: $V\equiv v-\frac{1}{k}\dot{H}_{T}=v^{(\mathrm{long})}$ [src: Eq. 59], $D_{s}\equiv\delta+3(1+w){\dot{a}\over a}(k^{-2}\dot{H}_{T}-k^{-1}B)\equiv\delta^{(\mathrm{long})}$ [src: Eq. 60], $D\equiv\delta^{(\mathrm{long})}+3(1+w)\left(\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\right)\frac{V}{k}$ [src: Eq. 61] and $D_{g}\equiv\delta+3(1+w)\left(H_{L}+\frac{1}{3}H_{T}\right)=\delta^{(\mathrm{long})}-3(1+w)\Phi$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0402129) [src: Eq. 62], with the entropy variable $\Gamma=\pi_{L}-\frac{c_{s}^{2}}{w}\delta,$ [src: Eq. 58], "Adiabatic perturbations are characterized by Γ=0" [src: Sect. 3.4, text after Eq. 58]. Durrer writes "We shall not derive the gauge transformation properties of these perturbation variables in detail, but just state some results which can be obtained as an exercise" [src: Sect. 3.4, text after Eq. 57]. Durrer's order-of-magnitude argument, Eqs. 66–67, shows that for kη ≫ 1 "the difference between δ, $\delta^{(\mathrm{long})}$, $D_g$ and D is negligible" [src: Sect. 3.4, last paragraph].
- **Gauge-ready, keep the gauge open (Laine & Procacci).** They "fix to none of these gauges, but rather keep $\xi^{0}$ and ξ general, verifying that the results do not depend on the gauge choice" (arXiv:2607.06983) [src: Sect. 4.2, Table 4.1 caption], the philosophy "we in general endorse, because it offers for strong crosschecks of the computations" (as printed) [src: Sect. 4.2, first paragraph]. The caption of their Table 4.1 adds the synchronous gauge, $\tilde{h}_{0}=\tilde{h}=0$, as "Yet another gauge discussed in the literature", citing Ma & Bertschinger [src: Table 4.1 caption, inline].
- **Coordinate route (Ma & Bertschinger).** `[der]` It gives the two-gauge dictionary used to run a synchronous-gauge Boltzmann code and quote Newtonian-gauge results (`deep/einstein-boltzmann-system.md`), with the residual ξ(τ) set to zero by the authors (Step 6).
- **Cosmic time (Baumann).** In cosmic time t, with $-(1+2\Phi){\rm d}t^{2}+2aB_{i}{\rm d}x^{i}{\rm d}t+a^{2}[(1-2\Psi)\delta_{ij}+E_{ij}]{\rm d}x^{i}{\rm d}x^{j}$ as the line element [src: Eq. 119, second row], Baumann writes the transformation as $t\to t+\alpha$ [src: Eq. 122] and the rules $\Phi\to\Phi-\dot{\alpha}$ [src: Eq. 124], $B\to B+a^{-1}\alpha-a\dot{\beta}$ [src: Eq. 125], $E\to E-\beta$ [src: Eq. 126] and $\Psi\to\Psi+H\alpha\,.$ (arXiv:0907.5424) [src: Sect. 9.2.1, Eq. 127], leaving their derivation as Exercise 4 from the invariance of the interval [src: Exercise 4, Eq. 128]; Baumann ignores vector perturbations "in these lectures" because they "aren't created by inflation (and in any case decay with the expansion of the universe)" [src: Sect. 9.2.1, text after Eq. 121].
- **Gauge-invariant analysis beyond general relativity.** Palomares, Zhang & Kim (arXiv:2604.22725) derive the scalar sector of quadratic gravity in the Einstein frame "in a gauge-independent manner" with three sets of gauge-invariant variables, which "allows us to distinguish genuine physical effects from gauge artefacts" [src: abstract].

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **A gauge mode read as a growing mode.** In synchronous gauge the A and B solutions of Eq. 94 are gauge modes (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072) [src: Sect. 7, text after Eq. 95]; check: transform to conformal Newtonian gauge (their Eq. 18), where they are absent [src: Sect. 7, last paragraph].
- **A gauge-dependent instability.** Palomares et al. find, for scalar perturbations of quadratic gravity in the Einstein frame and in the superhorizon limit, that "the Newtonian gauge exhibits an apparent instability, characterised by the exponential growth of the metric perturbations", but that "this growth is non-generic and gauge-dependent"; in the other gauges they analyse, the perturbations "remain well behaved within the perturbative regime" (arXiv:2604.22725) [src: abstract]. Cho likewise finds, during slow-roll inflation, a common 1/ε enhancement of the quadratic effective energy–momentum tensor in the matter-clock gauges and an additional one in the uniform-density gauge, and writes that "Neither enhancement is interpreted as a gauge-independent physical instability, and the gauge reconstruction must remain perturbatively small" (arXiv:2608.13498) [src: abstract]. Check: compute a gauge-invariant combination (Step 3) before calling growth physical.
- **An incomplete gauge.** A slicing condition alone (comoving or uniform density) leaves the threading free (Steps 4–5); Cho imposes E = 0 together with δρ = 0 and calls the pair "the complete scalar gauge conditions" (arXiv:2608.13498) [src: abstract].
- **The large-wavelength limit taken differently in two gauges.** Weinberg finds that, "when we take the limit of vanishing wave number in the field equations of synchronous gauge", a quantity is conserved "in all modes whatever the contents of the universe, provided only that none of the perturbations in synchronous gauge blow up in the limit q → 0", and resolves the apparent paradox with the Newtonian-gauge result by noting that "when we speak of the limit q=0 we mean different things in different gauges" (arXiv:astro-ph/0302326) [src: text before Sect. IV, paragraph beginning "Things appear very different in synchronous gauge"]; "although equivalent, synchronous and Newtonian gauges suggest inequivalent assumptions about the behavior of the perturbations for large wavelength" [src: abstract].
- **Letters swapped between papers.** Durrer's Ψ is in $g_{00}$ (Eq. 43), Malik & Wands' Ψ in the spatial part; Laine & Procacci's φ is in $h_0$. Check the line element of each paper before combining (`approximations.md#convention-traps`).
- **Printed slips met in this reading.** Malik & Wands write ${\cal C}(x^i)$ without the subscript 1 in Eqs. 7.39 and 7.41 after ${\cal C}_1(x^i)$ in Eq. 7.36, and the sentences after Eqs. 7.50 and 7.51 read "which coincides the quantity" and "From the definition above we immediately that" (arXiv:0809.4944) [src: Sect. 7.3; Sect. 7.4]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Durrer prints the background energy equation as $\dot{\rho}=-3\left(\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\right)(\rho+p)=-3(1+w)\mathcal{H},$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0402129) [src: Sect. 2, Eq. 9], the last member without a factor ρ, and calls w "the enthalpy" [src: Sect. 3.4, text after Eq. 58].
