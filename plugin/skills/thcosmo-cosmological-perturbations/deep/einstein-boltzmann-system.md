# Einstein–Boltzmann system — thcosmo-cosmological-perturbations dossier

## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- Ma & Bertschinger (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072) treat only flat backgrounds: "We consider only models with total Ω=1 in this paper, so we set κ=0" [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 20]. Time is conformal τ, a dot is $\partial_\tau$; the synchronous scalar potentials are defined by $h_{ij}(\vec{x},\tau)=\int d^{3}ke^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}\left\{\hat{k}_{i}\hat{k}_{j}h(\vec{k},\tau)+(\hat{k}_{i}\hat{k}_{j}-{1\over 3}\delta_{ij})\,6\eta(\vec{k},\tau)\right\}\,,\quad\vec{k}=k\hat{k}\,.$ [src: Sect. 2, Eq. 4], a Fourier convention with no factor of 2π; the conformal Newtonian potentials are ψ in $g_{00}$ and φ in $g_{ij}$ (`deep/gauge-transformations-and-invariants.md`, Conventions). Velocity divergence and shear are $(\bar{\rho}+\bar{P})\theta\equiv ik^{j}\delta T^{0}{}_{\!j}\,,\qquad(\bar{\rho}+\bar{P})\sigma\equiv-(\hat{k}_{i}\hat{k}_{j}-{1\over 3}\delta_{ij})\Sigma^{i}{}_{\!j}\,,$ [src: Sect. 4, Eq. 22], and the authors relate σ to the Kodama–Sasaki anisotropic stress by $\sigma=2\Pi\bar{P}/3(\bar{\rho}+\bar{P})$ [src: Sect. 4, inline, text after Eq. 22], a relation between conventions that the paper states.
- Blas et al. (arXiv:1104.2933) "adopt the notation of Ma & Bertschinger", "stick to the synchronous gauge in conformal time", write a prime for $\partial_\tau$, and define $\Theta_{\gamma b}\equiv\theta_{\gamma}-\theta_{b}$, $R\equiv\frac{4\bar{\rho}_{\gamma}}{3\bar{\rho}_{b}}$ and the Thomson scattering time $\tau_c$ [src: Sect. 2.1, footnote 4; inline, text after Eq. 1b].
- Hu et al. (arXiv:astro-ph/9702170) expand in total angular momentum, with moments $\Theta_\ell^{(m)}$, $E_\ell^{(m)}$, $B_\ell^{(m)}$ and a scattering term in $\dot\tau$ that "implies an exponential suppression of anisotropies with optical depth" [src: Sect. III.3.3, text after Eq. 68]; "The expressions above were all derived assuming a flat spatial geometry" [src: Sect. III.3.3, footnote 4].
- Seljak & Zaldarriaga (arXiv:astro-ph/9603033) work in longitudinal gauge, flat universe (`formalism.md#einstein-boltzmann-hierarchy-and-line-of-sight`).
- `[der]` The symbol τ is conformal time in Ma & Bertschinger and in Blas et al., while Hu et al.'s $\dot\tau$ is a scattering rate tied to optical depth; the CLASS trigger parameters use $\tau_H$ and $\tau_k$ for the Hubble and wavelength time scales.

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — Einstein equations in the two gauges
<!-- key: step-1-einstein-equations-in-the-two-gauges -->

Ma & Bertschinger (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072) close the system with four Einstein equations per gauge. Synchronous gauge: $k^{2}\eta-{1\over 2}{\dot{a}\over a}\dot{h}=4\pi Ga^{2}\delta T^{0}{}_{\!0}(\mbox{Syn})\,,$ [src: Sect. 4, Eq. 21a], $k^{2}\dot{\eta}=4\pi Ga^{2}(\bar{\rho}+\bar{P})\theta(\mbox{Syn})\,,$ [src: Eq. 21b], $\ddot{h}+2{\dot{a}\over a}\dot{h}-2k^{2}\eta=-8\pi Ga^{2}\delta T^{i}{}_{\!i}(\mbox{Syn})\,,$ [src: Eq. 21c] and $\ddot{h}+6\ddot{\eta}+2{\dot{a}\over a}\left(\dot{h}+6\dot{\eta}\right)-2k^{2}\eta=-24\pi Ga^{2}(\bar{\rho}+\bar{P})\sigma(\mbox{Syn})\,.$ [src: Eq. 21d]. Conformal Newtonian gauge: $k^{2}\phi+3{\dot{a}\over a}\left(\dot{\phi}+{\dot{a}\over a}\psi\right)=4\pi Ga^{2}\delta T^{0}{}_{\!0}(\mbox{Con})\,,$ [src: Eq. 23a], $k^{2}\left(\dot{\phi}+{\dot{a}\over a}\psi\right)=4\pi Ga^{2}(\bar{\rho}+\bar{P})\theta(\mbox{Con})\,,$ [src: Eq. 23b] and $k^{2}(\phi-\psi)=12\pi Ga^{2}(\bar{\rho}+\bar{P})\sigma(\mbox{Con})\,,$ [src: Eq. 23d], plus the trace equation 23c. Matter variables pass between the gauges by $\delta(\mbox{Syn})=\delta(\mbox{Con})-\alpha{\dot{\bar{\rho}}\over{\bar{\rho}}}\,,$ [src: Eq. 27a] and $\theta(\mbox{Syn})=\theta(\mbox{Con})-\alpha k^{2}\,,$ [src: Eq. 27b], with σ unchanged [src: Eq. 27d] and $\alpha=\hat{x}^{0}-x^{0}=(\dot{h}+6\dot{\eta})/2k^{2}$ [src: Sect. 4, inline, text after Eq. 26]; the transformation "also applies to individual species" with each species' $\bar\rho$ and $\bar P$ [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 27d].

### Step 2 — CDM and baryons
<!-- key: step-2-cdm-and-baryons -->

The CDM "can be used to define the synchronous coordinates and therefore have zero peculiar velocities in this gauge", leaving $\dot{\delta_{c}}=-\frac{1}{2}\,\dot{h}\,.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072) [src: Sect. 5.2, Eq. 42]; in conformal Newtonian gauge, $\dot{\delta_{c}}=-\theta_{c}+3\dot{\phi}\,,\quad\dot{\theta}_{c}=-{\dot{a}\over a}\,\theta_{c}+k^{2}\psi\,.$ [src: Eq. 43]. The baryons obey the fluid equations with the Thomson drag added by momentum conservation: in synchronous gauge $\dot{\delta}_{b}=-\theta_{b}-{1\over 2}\dot{h}\,,$ $\dot{\theta}_{b}=-{\dot{a}\over a}\theta_{b}+c_{s}^{2}k^{2}\delta_{b}+{4\bar{\rho}_{\gamma}\over 3\bar{\rho}_{b}}an_{e}\sigma_{T}(\theta_{\gamma}-\theta_{b})\,,$ [src: Sect. 5.6, Eq. 66], and in conformal Newtonian gauge the same with $3\dot\phi$ added to $\dot\delta_b$ and $k^2\psi$ to $\dot\theta_b$ [src: Eq. 67]. The shear term is neglected and w and δP/δρ are kept only in the acoustic term, because the baryons "are very nonrelativistic after neutrino decoupling" [src: Sect. 5.6, first paragraph]. The sound speed is $c_{s}^{2}={\dot{P}_{b}\over\dot{\rho}_{b}}={k_{\rm B}T_{b}\over\mu}\left(1-{1\over 3}{d\ln T_{b}\over d\ln a}\right)\,,$ [src: Eq. 68], neglecting "the slow time variation of μ", and, assuming "kinetic equilibrium ... with a common temperature $T_{b}$ for electrons and all baryon species" [src: Sect. 5.6, text after Eq. 69], the baryon temperature $\dot{T}_{b}=-2{\dot{a}\over a}T_{b}+{8\over 3}{\mu\over m_{e}}{\bar{\rho}_{\gamma}\over\bar{\rho}_{b}}an_{e}\sigma_{T}\left(T_{\gamma}-T_{b}\right)\,.$ [src: Eq. 69].

Blas et al. (arXiv:1104.2933) note that identifying the baryon sound speed with this adiabatic one "has been proved to be inaccurate in refs. [15, 16], but the difference is only important for computing the matter power spectrum for k≫10 h Mpc-1", and that CLASS v1.1 and CAMB of January 2011 "still relies on" it [src: Sect. 2.1, text after Eq. 3].

### Step 3 — massless neutrinos
<!-- key: step-3-massless-neutrinos -->

Ma & Bertschinger's Boltzmann equation for the momentum-integrated $F_\nu$, its moments and the synchronous hierarchy are in `formalism.md#einstein-boltzmann-hierarchy-and-line-of-sight`; in conformal Newtonian gauge the multipoles from l = 2 on obey $\dot{F}_{\nu\,l}={k\over 2l+1}\left[lF_{\nu\,(l-1)}-(l+1)F_{\nu\,(l+1)}\right]\,,\quad l\geq 2\,.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072) [src: Sect. 5.3, Eq. 50]. Blas et al. copy the synchronous system, "Eq. (49) in [10]", for their ultra-relativistic species, e.g. $2\sigma_{\tt ur}{}^{\prime}=\frac{8}{15}\theta_{\tt ur}-\frac{3}{5}kF_{{\tt ur}3}+\frac{4}{15}(h^{\prime}+6\eta^{\prime}),$ (arXiv:1104.2933) [src: Sect. 3.3, Eq. 30c].

### Step 4 — photons: Thomson collision terms and the polarisation hierarchy
<!-- key: step-4-photons-thomson-collision-terms-and-the -->

Ma & Bertschinger (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072) track the total intensity $F_\gamma$ and the polarisation difference $G_\gamma$ (Stokes Q), with the linearised Thomson collision operators, credited to Bond & Efstathiou (1984, 1987) and Kosowsky (1995), $\left({\partial F_{\gamma}\over\partial\tau}\right)_{C}=an_{e}\sigma_{T}\left[-F_{\gamma}+F_{\gamma\,0}+4\hat{n}\cdot\vec{v}_{e}-{1\over 2}\left(F_{\gamma\,2}+G_{\gamma\,0}+G_{\gamma\,2}\right)P_{2}\right]\,,$ [src: Sect. 5.5, Eq. 59] and $\left({\partial G_{\gamma}\over\partial\tau}\right)_{C}=an_{e}\sigma_{T}\left[-G_{\gamma}+{1\over 2}\left(F_{\gamma\,2}+G_{\gamma\,0}+G_{\gamma\,2}\right)\left(1-P_{2}\right)\right]\,,$ [src: Eq. 60]; "The terms proportional to $P_{2}$ come from the polarization-dependence of the Thomson cross section", and the anisotropic scattering and the net polarisation "were both neglected by Peebles & Yu (1970); the former (but not the latter) were included by Wilson & Silk (1980, 1981) and most later workers" [src: Sect. 5.5, text after Eq. 60]. With the Legendre expansion of Eq. 46 the left-hand side is that of the neutrinos, and in synchronous gauge the hierarchy is $\dot{\delta}_{\gamma}=-{4\over 3}\theta_{\gamma}-{2\over 3}\dot{h}\,,$ $\dot{\theta}_{\gamma}=k^{2}\left(\frac{1}{4}\delta_{\gamma}-\sigma_{\gamma}\right)+an_{e}\sigma_{T}(\theta_{b}-\theta_{\gamma})\,,$ $\dot{F}_{\gamma\,2}=2\dot{\sigma}_{\gamma}={8\over 15}\theta_{\gamma}-{3\over 5}kF_{\gamma\,3}+{4\over 15}\dot{h}+{8\over 5}\dot{\eta}-{9\over 5}an_{e}\sigma_{T}\sigma_{\gamma}+{1\over 10}an_{e}\sigma_{T}\left(G_{\gamma\,0}+G_{\gamma\,2}\right)\,,$ $\dot{F}_{\gamma\,l}={k\over 2l+1}\left[lF_{\gamma\,(l-1)}-(l+1)F_{\gamma\,(l+1)}\right]-an_{e}\sigma_{T}F_{\gamma\,l}\,,\quad l\geq 3\,,$ $\dot{G}_{\gamma\,l}={k\over 2l+1}\left[lG_{\gamma\,(l-1)}-(l+1)G_{\gamma\,(l+1)}\right]+an_{e}\sigma_{T}\left[-G_{\gamma\,l}+{1\over 2}\left(F_{\gamma\,2}+G_{\gamma\,0}+G_{\gamma\,2}\right)\left(\delta_{l0}+{\delta_{l2}\over 5}\right)\right]\,,$ [src: Sect. 5.5, unnumbered display after Eq. 62, "Synchronous gauge" rows]. In conformal Newtonian gauge the same display carries $4\dot\phi$ instead of $-\frac{2}{3}\dot h$ in $\dot\delta_\gamma$, an added $k^2\psi$ in $\dot\theta_\gamma$, and no metric term in $\dot F_{\gamma 2}$ [src: Sect. 5.5, same display, "Conformal Newtonian gauge" rows]. The rendering prints no number for these two displays (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072, v1) [src: Sect. 5.5].

Blas et al. quote the synchronous display as "Eqs. (63) in [10]" (arXiv:1104.2933) [src: Sect. 2.1, text before Eq. 4a]. <!-- num: not a value -->

Blas et al. (arXiv:1104.2933) write the same synchronous system with the Thomson time, e.g. $\theta_{\gamma}^{\prime}=k^{2}\left(\frac{1}{4}\delta_{\gamma}-\sigma_{\gamma}\right)-\frac{\Theta_{\gamma b}}{\tau_{c}},$ [src: Sect. 2.1, Eq. 4b] and $2\sigma_{\gamma}^{\prime}=\frac{8}{15}\theta_{\gamma}-\frac{3}{5}kF_{\gamma 3}+\frac{4}{15}(h^{\prime}+6\eta^{\prime})-\frac{9}{5\tau_{c}}\sigma_{\gamma}+\frac{1}{10\tau_{c}}\left(G_{\gamma 0}+G_{\gamma 2}\right),$ [src: Eq. 4c].

### Step 5 — tight coupling and its breakdown
<!-- key: step-5-tight-coupling-and-its-breakdown -->

Ma & Bertschinger (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072): before recombination $an_{e}\sigma_{T}\equiv\tau_{c}^{-1}\gg\dot{a}/a\sim\tau^{-1}$, and "The large values of the Thomson drag terms ... make them numerically difficult to solve"; following Peebles & Yu (1970) they derive equations "valid for $\tau_{c}/\tau\ll 1$ and $k\tau_{c}\ll 1$" [src: Sect. 5.7, first paragraph]. Combining the photon and baryon momentum equations exactly, $(1+R)\dot{\theta}_{b}+{\dot{a}\over a}\theta_{b}-c_{s}^{2}k^{2}\delta_{b}-k^{2}R\left(\frac{1}{4}\delta_{\gamma}-\sigma_{\gamma}\right)+R(\dot{\theta}_{\gamma}-\dot{\theta}_{b})=(1+R)k^{2}\psi\ ,$ [src: Eq. 70], the right side kept only in conformal Newtonian gauge; the slip is $\theta_{b}-\theta_{\gamma}={\tau_{c}\over 1+R}\left[-{\dot{a}\over a}\theta_{b}+k^{2}\left(c_{s}^{2}\delta_{b}-\frac{1}{4}\delta_{\gamma}+\sigma_{\gamma}\right)+\dot{\theta}_{\gamma}-\dot{\theta}_{b}\right]\ ,$ [src: Eq. 72], "valid in both gauges", and the photon shear in synchronous gauge is $\sigma_{\gamma}=\frac{\tau_{c}}{9}\left(\frac{8}{3}\theta_{\gamma}+\frac{4}{3}\dot{h}+8\dot{\eta}-10\dot{\sigma}_{\gamma}-3kF_{\gamma\,3}\right)$ [src: Eq. 73], so that $\sigma_\gamma$ is of order $\delta_\gamma$ times max[kτ_c, τ_c/τ] [src: Sect. 5.7, text after Eq. 73]. Assuming that "the gas is nearly fully ionized", with n_e ∝ a⁻³ and c_s² ∝ a⁻¹, the slip derivative, with an $O(\tau_c^2)$ remainder, is their Eq. 74, substituted into Eq. 70, the goal being equations "accurate to second order in $\tau_{c}$"; "If this condition is violated, then one should use the explicit form of equations (66) and (67)" [src: Sect. 5.7, text before and after Eq. 74]. The photon velocity follows from $\dot{\theta}_{\gamma}=-R^{-1}\left(\dot{\theta}_{b}+{\dot{a}\over a}\theta_{b}-c_{s}^{2}k^{2}\delta_{b}\right)+k^{2}\left(\frac{1}{4}\delta_{\gamma}-\sigma_{\gamma}\right)+{(1+R)\over R}k^{2}\psi$ [src: Eq. 75]. They switch to the explicit baryon equation at $T_b = 2\times10^4$ K and to the explicit photon moments l > 1 at $T_b = 2\times10^5$ K, earlier setting those moments to zero, and "have verified that these switches occur early enough to preserve good accuracy" [src: Sect. 5.7, last paragraph].

- `[der]` As printed, Ma & Bertschinger's Eq. 73 carries no polarisation term, whereas Blas et al.'s exact shear relation (Eq. 10, below) adds a term in $G_{\gamma0}+G_{\gamma2}$; compare both before copying a tight-coupling shear.

Blas et al. (arXiv:1104.2933) make the expansion systematic. Multiplying the momentum equations by $\tau_c$ gives an exact equation for the slip, $\Theta_{\gamma b}$ [src: Sect. 2.3, Eq. 9], and an exact shear, $\sigma_{\gamma}=\frac{\tau_{c}}{9}\left[\frac{8}{3}\theta_{\gamma}+\frac{4}{3}h^{\prime}+{8}\eta^{\prime}-10{\sigma_{\gamma}}^{\prime}-3kF_{\gamma 3}\right]+\frac{1}{18}\left(G_{\gamma 0}+G_{\gamma 2}\right).$ [src: Eq. 10]; "In the limit of interest, both of them can be schematically written as" $\epsilon y(t)^{\prime}+y(t)/f(t)+\epsilon g(t)=0,$ [src: Eq. 11], with the recursive solution $y(t)=\sum_{n=1}\epsilon^{n}y_{n}(t),\quad y_{1}=-fg,\quad y_{n+1}=-fy_{n}^{\prime}~.$ [src: Eq. 12], the homogeneous part being "suppressed very fast" because f is positive [src: Sect. 2.3, text after Eq. 13]. The closed tight-coupling system is their Eqs. 1a, 4a and 7a–7b [src: Sect. 2.2]. CLASS offers first-order schemes (first_order_MB, first_order_CAMB, first_order_CLASS), the second-order expressions of their Ref. 12 (second_order_CRS), its own second order (second_order_CLASS), and the default compromise_CLASS, second order for the shear and "a reduced expression for the slip, involving only the leading order-two terms" (their Eq. 29), which "implements the leading second order correction for the modes with a big comoving momentum. It is precisely for these modes that the first order approximation fails first" [src: Sect. 2.5]. The approximation is turned off for each wavenumber when $\tau_c/\tau_H$ or $\tau_c/\tau_k$ exceeds a trigger value [src: Sect. 2.7, second item].

Cyr-Racine et al. (arXiv:1012.0569) solve "the exact perturbations equations with a stiff solver starting deep in the radiation-dominated epoch", build a second-order approximation "in the inverse Thomson opacity expansion", and find the parameter bias from the first-order approximation "for most parameters, negligible" [src: abstract]; they conclude that this bias "is insignificant for today's cosmological experiments, unless CAMB's default accuracy is used" [src: Sect. VI, second paragraph].

### Step 6 — closing the hierarchies
<!-- key: step-6-closing-the-hierarchies -->

- **Truncation with free-streaming closure.** For photons, Ma & Bertschinger replace the last equations at $l=l_{\rm max}$ by $\dot{F}_{\gamma\,l}=kF_{\gamma\,(l-1)}-{l+1\over\tau}F_{\gamma\,l}-an_{e}\sigma_{T}F_{\gamma\,l}\,,$ $\dot{G}_{\gamma\,l}=kG_{\gamma\,(l-1)}-{l+1\over\tau}G_{\gamma\,l}-an_{e}\sigma_{T}G_{\gamma\,l}\,.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072) [src: Sect. 5.5, Eq. 65], the photon analogue of their neutrino Eq. 51 "except that Thomson opacity terms must be added" [src: Sect. 5.5, text before Eq. 65]. Seljak & Zaldarriaga (arXiv:astro-ph/9603033) explain why: "One has to be careful however to avoid unwanted reflections of the power being transferred from low l to high l, which occur for example if a simple cut-off in the hierarchy is imposed"; the Bessel-recurrence closure "works extremely well and only a few multipoles beyond l=4 are needed for an accurate calculation of the source term" [src: Sect. 3.1].
- **Ultra-relativistic fluid approximation.** Inside the Hubble radius Blas et al. close the massless-neutrino system with ${\sigma_{\tt ur}{}}^{\prime}=-\frac{3}{\tau}\sigma_{\tt ur}{}+\frac{2}{3}\theta_{\tt ur}{}+\frac{1}{3}h^{\prime}~.$ (arXiv:1104.2933) [src: Sect. 3.3, Eq. 31], valid "at leading order in an expansion in metric perturbation derivatives ... and in powers of $(k\tau)^{-1}$" [src: Sect. 3.3, text before Eq. 31].
- **Radiation streaming approximation.** In this approximation CLASS neglects $\delta_\gamma$ and $\delta_{\tt ur}$ in the first Einstein equation and imposes, for example, $\delta_{{\tt ur}{}}=\frac{4}{k^{2}}\left(\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}h^{\prime}-k^{2}\eta\right)~,$ (arXiv:1104.2933) [src: Sect. 4.3, Eq. 45d], $\theta_{{\tt ur}{}}=-\frac{1}{2}h^{\prime}~,$ [src: Eq. 45e] and vanishing shears, with $\tau_c$ terms in the photon expressions (Eqs. 45a–45b) for the default rsa_MD_with_reio [src: Sect. 4.3, text after Eq. 45f].
- **No hierarchy at all.** CLASSIER "replaces the traditional truncated Boltzmann hierarchy of non-cold relic multipoles with a small set of integral equations solved iteratively" (Lee et al. 2025, arXiv:2510.20821) [src: abstract]; its decaying-dark-matter extension requires "neither a Boltzmann hierarchy nor any fluid approximation" (Lee et al. 2026, arXiv:2607.24910) [src: abstract]. For massive neutrinos see `thcosmo-neutrinos-light-relics`.

### Step 7 — the line-of-sight integral as an algorithm
<!-- key: step-7-the-line-of-sight-integral-as-an -->

Seljak & Zaldarriaga (arXiv:astro-ph/9603033) integrate the photon equation along the past light cone (`formalism.md#einstein-boltzmann-hierarchy-and-line-of-sight`), with the polarisation source $S^{(S)}_{P}(k,\tau)=-{3\over 4k^{2}}\left(g\{k^{2}\Pi+\ddot{\Pi}\}+2\dot{g}\dot{\Pi}+\ddot{g}\Pi\right).$ [src: Sect. 2.2, Eq. 12, last row]. The algorithm: evolve the hierarchy only to low l, because "Equation (13) is formally an integral system of equations, because l<4 moments appear on both sides of equations. To solve for these moments it is best to use the equations in their differential form (3)" [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 13]; sample the source in time, finely across recombination because the terms in Eq. 12 "depend on the derivatives of the visibility function" [src: Sect. 3.4]; then project with precomputed $j_l$. In the free-streaming regime the method needs no separate algorithm: "one can stop the integration at the time $\tau_{\rm fs}$ beyond which the sources are not important" [src: Sect. 3.3]. The Π terms, from polarisation and anisotropic Thomson scattering, "affect the anisotropy spectra at the 10% level", and the tight-coupling, instantaneous-recombination limit is recovered "in the limit where the visibility function is a delta-function and Π can be neglected" [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 12].

### Step 8 — initial conditions of the regular modes
<!-- key: step-8-initial-conditions-of-the-regular-modes -->

Ma & Bertschinger (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072) start each k in the radiation era with $k\tau\ll 1$, where "The large Thomson damping terms ... drive the l≥2 moments of the photon distribution function $F_{\gamma\,l}$ and the polarization function $G_{\gamma\,l}$ to zero" and $F_{\nu l}$ for l ≥ 3 "can be ignored because they are smaller than $F_{\nu\,2}$ by successive powers of kτ" [src: Sect. 7, second paragraph]; the four power-law solutions, two of them gauge modes, are in `deep/gauge-transformations-and-invariants.md` (Step 6), and only "the fastest-growing physical mode" is kept, with constant entropy per baryon [src: Sect. 7, text after Eq. 95]. The synchronous result is their Eq. 96 (`formalism.md`); in conformal Newtonian gauge the growing mode is $\delta_{\gamma}=-{40C\over 15+4R_{\nu}}=-2\psi\,,\qquad\delta_{c}=\delta_{b}={3\over 4}\delta_{\nu}={3\over 4}\delta_{\gamma}\,,$ $\theta_{\gamma}=\theta_{\nu}=\theta_{c}=\theta_{b}={10C\over 15+4R_{\nu}}(k^{2}\tau)=\frac{1}{2}(k^{2}\tau)\psi\,,$ $\sigma_{\nu}={4C\over 3(15+4R_{\nu})}(k\tau)^{2}=\frac{1}{15}(k\tau)^{2}\psi\,,$ $\psi={20C\over 15+4R_{\nu}}\,,\qquad\phi=\left(1+{2\over 5}R_{\nu}\right)\psi\,.$ [src: Sect. 7, Eq. 98], where "The neutrino energy fraction $R_{\nu}$ enters only in the second potential φ as a result of the shear stress produced by the free-streaming neutrinos" [src: Sect. 7, text after Eq. 98].

Shaw et al. (arXiv:0911.2714) give series solutions for "the complete set of all known regular modes for the standard matter species (dark matter, baryons, photons and neutrinos) for the scalar, vector and tensor type perturbations", "after neutrino decoupling but well before recombination", assuming tight coupling with kτ_c ≪ 1 and τ_c/τ ≪ 1, a negligible photon anisotropic stress, pressureless baryons, constant ionisation and degrees of freedom, and "as before assume a flat universe" [src: Sect. VI.1]. "There are six regular scalar modes, one adiabatic, four isocurvature, and one magnetic", given in synchronous gauge, which "has the further advantage that the neutrino velocity isocurvature mode is completely regular as τ→0" [src: App. B.1, first paragraph]. For example, the CDM isocurvature mode starts as $h(\tau)=R_{c}\omega\tau-\frac{3}{8}\left(R_{c}\omega^{2}\right)\tau^{2}+O(\tau^{3})$, $\eta(\tau)=-\frac{1}{6}(R_{c}\omega)\tau+\frac{1}{16}R_{c}\omega^{2}\tau^{2}+O(\tau^{3})$, $\delta_{c}(\tau)=1-\frac{1}{2}(R_{c}\omega)\tau+\frac{3}{16}R_{c}\omega^{2}\tau^{2}+O(\tau^{3})$, $v_{c}(\tau)=0$ [src: App. B.1, unnumbered display under "CDM Isocurvature Mode", first four rows]; the definitions of ω and R_c were not read.

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **Total angular momentum (Hu et al.).** The temperature hierarchy reads $\dot{\Theta}_{\ell}^{(m)}=k\Bigg[{\,{}_{0}^{\vphantom{m}}\kappa_{\ell}^{m}\over(2\ell-1)}\Theta_{\ell-1}^{(m)}-{\,{}_{0}^{\vphantom{m}}\kappa_{\ell+1}^{m}\over(2\ell+3)}\Theta_{\ell+1}^{(m)}\Bigg]-\dot{\tau}\Theta_{\ell}^{(m)}+S_{\ell}^{(m)},\qquad(\ell\geq m).$ (arXiv:astro-ph/9702170) [src: Sect. III.3.3, Eq. 68], with the anisotropic Compton source $P^{(m)}={1\over 10}\left[\Theta_{2}^{(m)}-\sqrt{6}E_{2}^{(m)}\right],$ [src: Eq. 70], which "involves the quadrupole moments of the temperature and E-polarization distribution only" and enters only the E quadrupole [src: Sect. III.3.3, text after Eqs. 70 and 72]. Scalar, vector and tensor sources (m = 0, 1, 2) enter the same hierarchy [src: Eq. 69], and "each moment corresponds directly to the observable angular pattern on the sky" [src: abstract]. Curvature enters by multiplying the ℓ ± 1 terms by $[1-(\ell^{2}-m-1)K/k^{2}]^{1/2}$ [src: Sect. III.3.3, footnote 4, inline].
- **Closed models.** Lewis et al. implement the line-of-sight method "for scalar and tensor modes in almost-Friedmann-Robertson-Walker models with positive spatial curvature" (arXiv:astro-ph/9911177) [src: abstract]; the derivation was not read.
- **Gauge.** Synchronous gauge is what Blas et al. use throughout (arXiv:1104.2933) [src: Sect. 2.1, footnote 4] and what Shaw et al. use for initial conditions (arXiv:0911.2714) [src: App. B.1]; conformal Newtonian gauge has no gauge modes for Ω = 1, and ψ enters its Euler equations (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072) [src: Sect. 2; Eqs. 43, 67].

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Stiffness.** Explicit Thomson terms with an explicit integrator: in CLASS "The ndf15 integrator is always better, by a huge amount in the no-tca case" (arXiv:1104.2933) [src: Sect. 2.7, paragraph beginning "In Table 2"; Table 2]; the check is to use a stiff integrator or a tight-coupling scheme.
- **Tight coupling kept too long.** The first-order approximation fails first for large comoving momenta (arXiv:1104.2933) [src: Sect. 2.5]; Ma & Bertschinger's scheme needs max[kτ_c, τ_c/τ] ≪ 1 (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072) [src: Sect. 5.7]. Check: vary the trigger parameters as in Table 1 of Blas et al. and compare with no-tca.
- **Hierarchy reflection.** A plain cut-off reflects power back to low l (arXiv:astro-ph/9603033) [src: Sect. 3.1]; check: free-streaming closure and convergence in $l_{\rm max}$ (`approximations.md#truncation-of-the-boltzmann-hierarchy`).
- **Polarisation dropped.** The Π terms change the spectra "at the 10% level" (arXiv:astro-ph/9603033) [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 12]; Peebles & Yu neglected both anisotropic scattering and polarisation, as Ma & Bertschinger note (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072) [src: Sect. 5.5, text after Eq. 60].
- **Flat-only equations used with curvature.** Ma & Bertschinger, Seljak & Zaldarriaga, Hu et al.'s main text and Shaw et al. all assume a flat background (Conventions; arXiv:0911.2714, Sect. VI.1); the curvature factor of Hu et al. (footnote 4) or the closed-model treatment of Lewis et al. is needed otherwise.
- **Synchronous-gauge initial conditions with a gauge mode.** The CDM defines the synchronous coordinates, so its velocity is set to zero (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072) [src: Sect. 5.2].
- `[der]` A nonzero initial CDM velocity divergence in synchronous gauge reintroduces the residual mode (`deep/gauge-transformations-and-invariants.md`, Step 6).
- **Printed slips met in this reading.** The v1 rendering of Ma & Bertschinger prints the numbers of the photon-hierarchy displays as "(5.5)", for example "we replace equations (5.5) and (5.5) by" (arXiv:astro-ph/9506072) [src: Sect. 5.5, text before Eq. 65]; Blas et al. write "indistighuishable" and "over-seeds" (arXiv:1104.2933) [src: Sect. 2.7]; Cyr-Racine et al. write "better tracts the evolution" and "deviates much less from the exact solution then the usual first-order scheme" (arXiv:1012.0569) [src: Sect. VI, first paragraph]; Shaw et al. write "two parameters which much be small" (arXiv:0911.2714) [src: Sect. VI.1, second paragraph]. <!-- num: not a value -->
