# Formalism of large-scale-structure perturbation theory

Scope: definitions, governing equations and conventions of standard, Lagrangian and effective perturbation theory for the large-scale structure, with bias, redshift space and the related statistics.

Snapshot: 2026-10-31

## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

The two main sources of this skill use different Fourier conventions, and a coefficient read in one cannot be moved to the other without its convention. The hub's atlas (`theoretical-cosmology/references/conventions.md`, sections "Fourier convention and power-spectrum normalisation" and "Perturbation-theory kernels") collects the choices of other works.

- Bernardeau et al. (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) write the inverse transform as $\delta({\bf x})=\int{{\rm d}^{3}{\bf k}}\,\delta({\bf k})\,\exp({\rm i}{\bf k}\cdot{\bf x})$ [src: Eq. 116]; there is no $(2\pi)^{3}$ in the inverse because it sits in the forward transform.
- Ivanov (arXiv:2212.08488) defines the bispectrum with the $(2\pi)^{3}$ factor: $\langle\delta({\bf k}_{1})\delta({\bf k}_{2})\delta({\bf k}_{3})\rangle\equiv(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{D}^{(3)}({\bf k}_{123})B({\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2},{\bf k}_{3})$ [src: Eq. 33].
- Ivanov (arXiv:2212.08488) normalises the velocity divergence by the growth rate, $\Theta=\frac{\theta}{f}=-\frac{\partial_{i}v^{i}}{f\mathcal{H}}\,,\quad\text{where}\quad f\equiv\frac{d\ln D_{+}}{d\ln a}$ [src: Eq. 23]: the sign of Θ is opposite to the sign of the divergence of the peculiar velocity, which is the convention that makes $G_{1}=1$.
- Before transferring a loop coefficient between the two papers, compare their forms of the one-loop integrals (Eqs. 165–166 of arXiv:astro-ph/0112551 against Eq. 32 of Ivanov's review): the measures differ by the Fourier convention. <!-- num: not a value -->

## Standard perturbation theory: equations and kernels
<!-- key: standard-perturbation-theory-equations-and -->

- Bernardeau et al. (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) write the continuity equation in Fourier space for a pressureless fluid as ${\partial\tilde{\delta}({\bf k},\tau)\over{\partial\tau}}+\tilde{\theta}({\bf k},\tau)=-\int{\rm d}^{3}{\bf k}_{1}{\rm d}^{3}{\bf k}_{2}\delta_{D}({\bf k}-{\bf k}_{12})\alpha({\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2})\tilde{\theta}({\bf k}_{1},\tau)\tilde{\delta}({\bf k}_{2},\tau),$ [src: Eq. 37]; the Euler equation (Eq. 38) has the same structure with the coupling β acting on two velocity divergences.
- The mode-coupling functions are $\alpha({\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2})\equiv{{\bf k}_{12}\cdot{\bf k}_{1}\over{k_{1}^{2}}},\ \ \ \ \ \beta({\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2})\equiv{k_{12}^{2}({\bf k}_{1}\cdot{\bf k}_{2})\over{2k_{1}^{2}k_{2}^{2}}}$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Eq. 39]; α is not symmetric in its arguments, β is.
- The second-order density kernel in an Einstein–de Sitter universe is $F_{2}({\bf q}_{1},{\bf q}_{2})=\frac{5}{7}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\bf q}_{1}\cdot{\bf q}_{2}}{q_{1}q_{2}}(\frac{q_{1}}{q_{2}}+\frac{q_{2}}{q_{1}})+\frac{2}{7}\frac{({\bf q}_{1}\cdot{\bf q}_{2})^{2}}{q_{1}^{2}q_{2}^{2}}$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Eq. 45].
- The second-order velocity-divergence kernel is $G_{2}({\bf q}_{1},{\bf q}_{2})=\frac{3}{7}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\bf q}_{1}\cdot{\bf q}_{2}}{q_{1}q_{2}}(\frac{q_{1}}{q_{2}}+\frac{q_{2}}{q_{1}})+\frac{4}{7}\frac{({\bf q}_{1}\cdot{\bf q}_{2})^{2}}{q_{1}^{2}q_{2}^{2}}.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Eq. 46].
- The kernels at all orders follow from the recursion relations of Bernardeau et al. (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551, Eqs. 43–44), reproduced in Eq. 27 of Ivanov's review. Ivanov's Eq. 28 prints the dipole term of $F_{2}$ and $G_{2}$ without the factor one half of Eqs. 45–46 above; this slip is recorded in the hub's `stale-claims.md` (no correction published as of the snapshot). Quote the kernels from Bernardeau et al.
- Mass and momentum conservation make the symmetrised kernels vanish when two arguments cancel: $F_{n}^{(s)}({\bf q}_{1},\dots,{\bf q}_{n-2},{\bf p},-{\bf p})\propto k^{2}/p^{2},$ for p ≫ k (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Eq. 47].
- Ivanov states the infrared counterpart, the scaling of a kernel when $n-l$ of its momenta go soft: $\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{n}({\bf k}_{1},...,{\bf k}_{l},\varepsilon{\bf q}^{\prime}_{l+1},...,\varepsilon{\bf q}^{\prime}_{n})=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{l-n})\,,$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 29]; these IR enhancements cancel in equal-time correlators of the density (Sect. 3.3).
- The vertex coefficients of the spherically averaged kernels are $\nu_{1}=\mu_{1}=1~;~\nu_{2}={34\over 21}~;~\nu_{3}={682\over 189}~;~\mu_{2}=-{26\over 21}~;~\mu_{3}={142\over 63}$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Eq. 52]; they control the tree-level moments of the one-point distribution (section "Large deviations and one-point statistics").
- The one-loop power spectrum is $P^{(1)}(k,\tau)=P_{22}(k,\tau)+P_{13}(k,\tau),$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Eq. 164], with $P_{22}(k,\tau)\equiv 2\int[F_{2}^{(s)}({\bf k}-{\bf q},{\bf q})]^{2}P_{L}(|{\bf k}-{\bf q}|,\tau)P_{L}(q,\tau){\rm d}^{3}{\bf q},$ [src: Eq. 165] and $P_{13}(k,\tau)\equiv 6\int F_{3}^{(s)}({\bf k},{\bf q},-{\bf q})P_{L}(k,\tau)P_{L}(q,\tau){\rm d}^{3}{\bf q}.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Eq. 166]. Bernardeau et al. note that $P_{22}$ is positive definite and $P_{13}$ in general negative (Sect. 4.2.2).
- The tree-level bispectrum is $B({\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2},{\bf k}_{3})=\langle\delta^{(2)}_{{\bf k}_{1}}\delta^{(1)}_{{\bf k}_{2}}\delta^{(1)}_{{\bf k}_{3}}\rangle^{\prime}+\text{2 cyc.}=2F_{2}({\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2})P_{11}(k_{1})P_{11}(k_{2})+\text{2 cyc.}$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 34].
- In the infrared limit the one-loop spectrum does not vanish but leaves a term set by the derivatives of the linear spectrum, $P_{{\rm 1-loop}}\Big|_{q\ll k}=\left(\frac{569}{735}-\frac{47}{105}kP^{\prime}_{11}(k)+\frac{1}{10}k^{2}P^{\prime\prime}_{11}(k)\right)\sigma^{2}_{l}(k)\,,$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 36]: for a smooth spectrum it is small, for the BAO wiggles it is not, which is the origin of IR resummation.

## Effective field theory: stress tensor, counterterms and power counting
<!-- key: effective-field-theory-stress-tensor -->

- The EFTofLSS adds to the Euler equation the divergence of an effective stress tensor of the long-wavelength fluid: $\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\delta_{\ell}+\partial_{i}[(1+\delta_{\ell})v^{i}_{\ell}]=0\,,\\ \frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}v^{i}_{\ell}+\mathcal{H}v^{i}_{\ell}+v^{j}_{\ell}\partial_{j}v^{i}_{\ell}+\partial_{i}\Phi_{\ell}=-\frac{1}{\rho_{\ell}}[\partial_{j}\sigma^{ij}]_{\Lambda}\,,$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 53].
- At leading order in derivatives and fields the stress term is a sound speed, $\frac{1}{\rho_{\ell}}[\partial_{j}\sigma^{ij}]_{\Lambda}=c_{s}^{2}(\tau)\partial^{i}\delta({\bf x},\tau)\,,$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 56]; because the short modes evolve on a Hubble time, the response is non-local in time, $\frac{1}{\rho_{\ell}}[\partial_{j}\sigma^{ij}]_{\Lambda}=\int_{\tau_{\rm ini}}^{\tau}d\tau^{\prime}~K(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\partial^{i}\delta({\bf x}_{fl}[{\bf x},\tau;\tau^{\prime}],\tau^{\prime})\,,$ [src: Eq. 57], which at linear order gives back the local sound-speed form, while at higher orders convective derivatives induce operators non-local in space (Sect. 4.1, text after Eq. 61).
- The stochastic part of the stress tensor is uncorrelated with the long modes, $\frac{1}{\rho_{\ell}}[\partial_{j}\sigma^{ij}]_{\Lambda}\supset J^{i}\,,\quad\text{where}\quad\langle J^{i}\delta_{\ell}\rangle=0\,.$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 62], and mass and momentum conservation make its power spectrum start at $k^{4}$: $\langle\delta^{\rm stoch.}({\bf k}^{\prime})\delta^{\rm stoch.}({\bf k})\rangle^{\prime}=P_{J}(k)=R_{\rm stoch.}^{7}k^{4}+...\,,$ [src: Eq. 64].
- The counterterm solution is $\delta^{\rm stress.}_{\bf k}=-\gamma_{\Lambda}k^{2}\delta^{(1)}_{\bf k}\equiv-\int da^{\prime}G_{\delta}(a,a^{\prime})k^{2}c_{s}^{2}(a^{\prime})\delta^{(1)}_{\bf k}(a^{\prime})\,.$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 69], so the renormalised one-loop matter spectrum is $P_{\text{non-linear}}(k)=P_{11}(k,\eta)+P_{22}^{\Lambda}(k,\eta)+P_{13}^{\Lambda}(k,\eta)-2\gamma_{\Lambda}k^{2}P_{11}(k,\eta)+P_{J}(k)\,.$ [src: Eq. 70].
- The UV part of $P_{13}$ has the shape of the counterterm: $6P_{11}(k)\int_{{\bf q},~q_{\rm trust}\leq q\leq\Lambda}~F_{3}({\bf k},{\bf q},-{\bf q})P_{11}(q)=-\frac{61}{630\pi^{2}}k^{2}P_{11}(k)\int^{\Lambda}_{q_{\rm trust}}dq~P_{11}(q)\,.$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 72], and it is absorbed by the running $\gamma_{\Lambda}=\gamma_{\rm finite}-\frac{61}{1260\pi^{2}}\int^{\Lambda}_{q_{\rm trust}}dq~P_{11}(q)\,.$ [src: Eq. 73]; the UV part of $P_{22}$ is absorbed in the same way by the stochastic term, $R^{7}_{\rm stoch}=(R^{\rm finite}_{\rm stoch})^{7}-\frac{9}{196\pi^{2}}\int_{q_{\rm trust}}^{\Lambda}~\frac{dq}{q^{2}}P^{2}_{11}(q)\,.$ [src: Eq. 76].
- Power counting for a scaling universe with $\Delta(k)=\frac{k^{3}}{2\pi^{2}}P_{\rm lin}(k)=\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{n+3}\,.$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 78] gives an L-loop contribution $\Delta^{2}_{\rm L-loop}(k)=\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{(L+1)(n+3)}\,.$ [src: Eq. 80] and counterterm and stochastic contributions $\Delta^{2}_{c_{s}^{2}}(k)=\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{n+5}\,,\quad\Delta^{2}_{J}(k)=\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{7}\,.$ [src: Eq. 81].
- Ivanov approximates the quasi-linear ΛCDM spectrum by a power law with n = −1.5 and k_NL ~ 0.3 h/Mpc at z = 0, and for k ~ 0.1 writes $\Delta^{2}_{\rm linear}<\Delta^{2}_{\rm one-loop}<\Delta^{2}_{\rm counterterm}\ll\Delta^{2}_{\rm two-loop}<\Delta^{2}_{\rm stochastic}\,.$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Sect. 4.4, Eq. 83], as printed: the text that follows concludes that the stochastic term can be neglected at one loop, so the display is meant as an ordering by decreasing size, with the inequality signs printed the other way. He adds that the scaling-universe estimates are quite accurate for ΛCDM (Sect. 4.4).

- Extensions listed by the Snowmass white paper: non-Gaussian initial conditions, IR resummation of primordial oscillating features, massive neutrinos (split into "fast" and "slow" neutrinos, which gives a small parameter for wavenumbers above the inverse free-streaming length, even though the free-streaming length exceeds the non-linear scale), selection effects, CMB and galaxy lensing, intrinsic alignments, and dark-energy degrees of freedom (Cabass et al. 2022, arXiv:2203.08232) [src: Sect. 3.10]. The massive-neutrino one-loop power spectrum and tree-level matter bispectrum had been computed consistently by 2022 [src: Sect. 3.11]. Linear neutrino perturbations belong to `thcosmo-neutrinos-light-relics`.

- Scale-dependent growth: an Einstein–Boltzmann code can find the gauge transformation (a "Newtonian motion gauge") in which the linear equations of the clustering matter take the Newtonian form with scale-independent growth; the non-linear calculation is done there with EdS kernels and transformed back, which adds linear relativistic and scale-dependent-growth effects at no extra cost (Fidler et al. 2026, arXiv:2605.05114) [src: abstract]. Mixed dark matter (a fraction of ultra-light axions or light thermal relics) breaks the scale-free solutions used for cold dark matter and massive neutrinos; Verdiani et al. extend the EFTofLSS to the coupled cold and non-cold components, the latter as a perfect fluid with finite sound speed at linear level (arXiv:2507.08792) [src: abstract].

## Lagrangian perturbation theory and the Zel'dovich approximation
<!-- key: lagrangian-perturbation-theory-and-the-zel -->

- The Eulerian position of a fluid element is its Lagrangian position plus a displacement, ${\bf x}(\tau)={\bf q}+{\bf\Psi}({\bf q},\tau).$ (Bernardeau et al. 2002, arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Eq. 88], and mass conservation gives the density as the inverse Jacobian, $1+\delta({\bf x})=\frac{1}{{\rm Det}\Big(\delta_{ij}+\Psi_{i,j}\Big)}\equiv\frac{1}{J({\bf q},\tau)},$ [src: Eq. 91]; at shell crossing the Jacobian vanishes and the mapping description no longer holds [src: Sect. 2.5].
- The equation of motion for the displacement is $J({\bf q},\tau)\ \nabla\cdot\Big[\frac{{\rm d}^{2}{\bf\Psi}}{{\rm d}\tau^{2}}+{\cal H}(\tau)\ \frac{{\rm d}{\bf\Psi}}{{\rm d}\tau}\Big]=\frac{3}{2}\Omega_{m}{\cal H}^{2}(J-1),$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Eq. 90].
- The Zel'dovich approximation is the linear solution, $\nabla_{q}\cdot{\bf\Psi}^{(1)}=-D_{1}(\tau)\ \delta({\bf q}),$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Eq. 92]; in terms of the eigenvalues $\lambda_{i}$ of the deformation tensor it gives $1+\delta({\bf x},\tau)=\frac{1}{[1-\lambda_{1}D_{1}(\tau)][1-\lambda_{2}D_{1}(\tau)][1-\lambda_{3}D_{1}(\tau)]},$ [src: Eq. 93].
- Second-order LPT: $\nabla_{q}\cdot{\bf\Psi}^{(2)}=\frac{1}{2}D_{2}(\tau)\sum_{i\neq j}(\Psi_{i,i}^{(1)}\Psi_{j,j}^{(1)}-\Psi_{i,j}^{(1)}\Psi_{j,i}^{(1)}),$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Eq. 94], with $D_{2}(\tau)\approx-\frac{3}{7}D_{1}^{2}(\tau),$ [src: Eq. 95]. For flat models with a cosmological constant and $0.01\leq\Omega_{m}\leq 1$ the paper gives $D_{2}(\tau)\approx-\frac{3}{7}D_{1}^{2}(\tau)\ \Omega_{m}^{-1/143},$ [src: Sect. 2.7, Eq. 97]; Eq. 96 is the open-model counterpart.
- The 2LPT positions are ${\bf x}({\bf q})={\bf q}-D_{1}\ \nabla_{q}\phi^{(1)}+D_{2}\ \nabla_{q}\phi^{(2)},$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Eq. 98], with $\nabla_{q}^{2}\phi^{(2)}({\bf q})=\sum_{i>j}[\phi_{,ii}^{(1)}({\bf q})\ \phi_{,jj}^{(1)}({\bf q})-(\phi_{,ij}^{(1)}({\bf q}))^{2}].$ [src: Eq. 103].
- In Fourier space the matter field is an average over displacements, $1+\delta_{m}(\textbf{x})=\int d^{3}\textbf{q}\ \delta_{D}(\textbf{x}-\textbf{q}-\boldsymbol{\Psi}(\textbf{q}))\quad,\quad(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{D}(\textbf{k})+\tilde{\delta}_{m}(\textbf{k})=\int d^{3}\textbf{q}\ e^{i\textbf{k}\cdot(\textbf{q}+\boldsymbol{\Psi})}$ (Chen, Vlah, Castorina & White 2020, arXiv:2012.04636) [src: Eq. 1].
- For Gaussian linear displacements the generating function is exactly $\left\langle e^{i\textbf{k}\cdot\Delta^{(1)}}\right\rangle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}k_{i}k_{j}A^{(11)}_{ij}}$ (arXiv:2012.04636) [src: Eq. 6], where $\Delta$ is the pairwise displacement and $A_{ij}$ its second cumulant; Baldauf et al. write the same second cumulant as $A^{ij}({\bm{z}})=\left\langle\Delta s^{i}({\bm{z}})\Delta s^{j}({\bm{z}})\right\rangle\\[8.0pt] =\int\!\!{\rm d}^{3}{\bm{q}}\>\frac{q^{i}q^{j}}{q^{4}}P_{\rm lin}(q)\sin^{2}\left(\frac{{\bm{q}}\cdot{\bm{z}}}{2}\right).$ (arXiv:1504.04366) [src: Eq. 24].
- The Zel'dovich power spectrum, after the angular integral, is $P_{\rm Zel}(k)=4\pi\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int dq\,q^{2}\ e^{-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}(X+Y)}\Big(\frac{kY}{q}\Big)^{n}j_{n}(kq)$ (arXiv:2012.04636) [src: Eq. 8], with X and Y the two independent components of $A^{\rm lin}_{ij}$.
- Baldauf et al. write the Zel'dovich power spectrum as $P_{z}({\bm{k}})=\int{\rm d}^{3}{\bm{z}}e^{-i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{z}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}A^{ij}({\bm{z}})k^{i}k^{j}},$ (arXiv:1504.04366) [src: Eq. 25]; splitting $A^{ij}$ into short and long parts, $A^{ij}({\bm{z}})=A^{ij}_{S}({\bm{z}},\Lambda)+A^{ij}_{L}({\bm{z}},\Lambda).$ [src: Eq. 26], the wiggly part is damped by the long displacements evaluated at the BAO scale, $P_{z}^{w}({\bm{k}})\approx e^{-\frac{1}{2}A^{ij}_{L}(\ell_{\rm BAO}\hat{\bm{k}},\Lambda)k^{i}k^{j}}P_{z,S}^{w}({\bm{k}},\Lambda)\\[8.0pt] \approx e^{-\Sigma^{2}_{\Lambda}k^{2}}P_{z,S}^{w}({\bm{k}},\Lambda).$ [src: Eq. 27], which is the Lagrangian origin of the IR damping of the next section.
- Convolution LPT keeps exponentiated all terms that tend to a constant at large separation, a partial resummation of Matsubara's formalism, and recovers the Zel'dovich approximation at lowest order (Carlson, Reid & White 2013, arXiv:1209.0780) [src: abstract]. The one-loop redshift-space LPT of Chen et al. resums both the long-wavelength displacements and the associated velocities, and adds counterterms $\alpha_{0},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{4},\alpha_{6}$ multiplying $k^{2}\mu^{2n}P_{s,\rm Zel}$ and stochastic terms in $R_{h}^{3}$, $\sigma_{2}$, $\sigma_{4}$ (arXiv:2012.04636) [src: Sect. 3, text after Eq. 4] <!-- num: not a value -->.
- Unified Lagrangian perturbation theory decomposes the galaxy density into a Jacobian deviation and a displacement-mapping effect, which gives an IR-safe resummation with exact IR cancellation in real space, redshift space and after reconstruction, and generates Galileon-type bias operators (Sugiyama 2025, arXiv:2508.17331) [src: abstract].

## Infrared resummation
<!-- key: infrared-resummation -->

- Large bulk flows displace the BAO feature; by the equivalence principle a uniform displacement drops out of equal-time correlators, but not across a feature of width smaller than the displacement (Baldauf et al. 2015, arXiv:1504.04366) [src: abstract]. The rms displacement is about $6\,h^{-1}$Mpc, $\langle s_{i}(0)s^{i}(0)\rangle\approx[6~h^{-1}\text{Mpc}]^{2}\,.$ (Ivanov 2022, arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 18].
- The leading effect is a Gaussian damping of the wiggly part of the spectrum. Ivanov, following time-sliced perturbation theory, writes $P_{w}^{\text{IR res, LO}}=e^{-\Sigma^{2}k^{2}}P_{w}(\eta;k)\quad\text{where}\\ \Sigma^{2}=\frac{4\pi}{3}\int_{0}^{k_{S}}dqP_{\rm smooth}(q)\left[1-j_{0}\left(qr_{\rm BAO}\right)+2j_{2}\left(qr_{\rm BAO}\right)\right]\,.$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 109], with the linear spectrum split into smooth and wiggly parts, $P_{11}(k)=P_{smooth}(k)+P_{w}(k)\,,$ [src: Eq. 37], and $k_{S}$ the separation scale between soft and hard modes.
- At next-to-leading order (one hard loop), Baldauf et al. write the IR-resummed spectrum as $\tilde{P}(k)=P_{\rm lin}^{nw}(k)+P_{\rm 1-loop}^{nw}(k)\\[8.0pt] +e^{-\Sigma^{2}_{\epsilon k}k^{2}}(1+\Sigma^{2}_{\epsilon k}k^{2})P^{w}_{\rm lin}(k)+e^{-\Sigma^{2}_{\epsilon k}k^{2}}P^{w}_{\rm 1-loop}(k),$ (arXiv:1504.04366) [src: Eq. 21], with the counterterm included in $P_{\rm 1-loop}(k)=P_{13}(k)+P_{22}(k)-2R^{2}k^{2}P_{\rm lin}(k),$ [src: Eq. 22].
- In time-sliced perturbation theory the resummation is systematic, has a diagrammatic representation, and gives the resummed power spectrum to next-to-leading order and the bispectrum at leading order (Blas et al. 2016, arXiv:1605.02149) [src: abstract]. The IR resummation is formally available for an arbitrary n-point function, any number of hard loops, real and redshift space and any biased tracer (Cabass et al., arXiv:2203.08232) [src: Sect. 3.11].
- Senatore and Zaldarriaga (arXiv:1404.5954) resum the large-scale flows in the Lagrangian picture and give a way to upgrade Eulerian calculations; they show that the 2% BAO-related residual of earlier Eulerian EFT calculations is removed, and that their two-loop result agrees with simulations to within 1% up to k ~ 0.6 h/Mpc [src: abstract].

## Bias expansion
<!-- key: bias-expansion -->

- The galaxy density is a past-light-cone integral over the local gravitational observables along the fluid trajectory, with time-dependent kernels of characteristic time $\mathcal{H}^{-1}$, a stochastic field ε uncorrelated with δ, and a new UV scale $R_{*}$, the typical size of the object (Cabass et al. 2022, arXiv:2203.08232) [src: Sect. 3.6, Eq. 3.23]. Ivanov writes the same time integral with kernels $K_{\delta}$, $K_{\delta^{2}}$, $K_{t^{2}}$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Sect. 7, Eqs. 122–123].
- Taylor-expanding around the fluid trajectory gives the local-in-time basis used for the one-loop power spectrum, $\delta_{g}=b_{1}\delta+\frac{b_{2}}{2}\delta^{2}+b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\mathcal{G}_{2}+b_{\nabla^{2}\delta}\nabla^{2}\delta+b_{\Gamma_{3}}\Gamma_{3}+\varepsilon+...\,,$ (arXiv:2203.08232) [src: Eq. 3.24], with the Galileon operators $\mathcal{G}_{2}(\Phi)\equiv(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi)^{2}-(\Delta\Phi)^{2}\,,\quad\Gamma_{3}\equiv\mathcal{G}_{2}(\Phi)-\mathcal{G}_{2}(\Phi_{v})\,,$ [src: Eq. 3.25], where $\Phi_{v}$ is the velocity potential. The local-in-time rewriting is exact only in the Einstein–de Sitter approximation for the time evolution, which the white paper calls accurate to $\mathcal{O}(0.1\div 1)\%$ for the redshifts of current and future surveys [src: Sect. 3.6, footnote 5].
- Unlike matter, the stochastic term of a biased tracer has a power spectrum that tends to a constant as k goes to zero, because mass and momentum are not conserved for tracers counted with equal weight; this is the Poisson-like shot noise (arXiv:2203.08232) [src: Sect. 3.6].
- Completeness: Eulerian bias is consistent with renormalisation only if non-local (tidal) terms and higher-derivative terms are included; at quadratic order the tidal tensor enters, at cubic order the velocity potential appears as an independent degree of freedom, and Galileon operators are not renormalised at leading order in derivatives (Assassi et al. 2014, arXiv:1402.5916) [src: abstract]. McDonald and Roy had shown that only two new parameters beyond the local-in-density series are needed for the power spectrum and bispectrum to fourth order in the initial perturbations (arXiv:0902.0991) [src: abstract].
- Time evolution: at any order the galaxy density can be written with a finite set of operators built from spatial and temporal derivatives of the Newtonian potential; this basis is larger than the expansion in gravitational and velocity potentials only from fourth order on, and is closed under renormalisation (Mirbabayi, Schmidt & Zaldarriaga 2014, arXiv:1412.5169) [src: abstract].
- The review of Desjacques, Jeong and Schmidt (arXiv:1611.09787) derives the general expansion in all leading local gravitational observables (density, tidal field and their time derivatives) under general relativity and Gaussian adiabatic initial conditions, and reviews the peak-background split, excursion sets and peaks [src: abstract]; its bases and conversion formulae were not read (no HTML rendering).
- Basis conversions between codes (velocileptors, PyBird, FOLPS, CLASS-PT) and the co-evolution relations are collected, with their sources, in the hub's atlas (`theoretical-cosmology/references/conventions.md#bias-basis-and-the-normalisation-of-each`); a tidal bias is quoted with its operator, because its normalisation differs between codes.

## Redshift-space distortions
<!-- key: redshift-space-distortions -->

- The redshift-space position adds the line-of-sight velocity, $x^{i}_{\rm RS}=x^{i}+\frac{v^{j}\hat{z}_{j}}{\mathcal{H}}\hat{z}_{i}\,,$ (Ivanov 2022, arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 124], and number conservation, $(1+\delta^{\text{(RS)}}_{g}({\bf x}_{\rm RS}))d^{3}x_{\rm RS}=(1+\delta_{g}({\bf x}))d^{3}x\,.$ [src: Eq. 125], gives the exact mapping $\delta^{\text{(RS)}}_{g}({\bf k})=\delta_{g}({\bf k})+\int d^{3}x~e^{i{\bf k}{\bf x}}\left[\exp\{ik^{i}\hat{z}_{i}(v^{j}\hat{z}_{j})/\mathcal{H}\}-1\right][1+\delta_{g}({\bf x})]\,.$ [src: Eq. 126]. Ivanov writes them with a single fixed line-of-sight direction ẑ; wide-angle and relativistic corrections belong to `thcosmo-relativistic-cosmology`.
- At linear order the mapping gives Kaiser's result, $\delta^{\text{(RS)}}_{g}({\bf k})=\left(b_{1}+f\frac{k_{z}^{2}}{k^{2}}\right)\delta({\bf k})\,.$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 127]; Kaiser's 1987 paper predates arXiv and was not read.
- The mapping multiplies density and velocity fields at the same point, so these products are sensitive to short-distance physics and need their own counterterms: the one-loop matter power spectrum in redshift space has two new counterterms relative to real space, and the IR resummation generalises with reduced symmetry (Senatore & Zaldarriaga 2014, arXiv:1409.1225) [src: abstract].
- The TNS model writes the redshift-space matter spectrum as $P^{\rm(S)}(k,\mu)=D_{\rm FoG}[k\mu\,f\,\sigma_{\rm v}]\,\Bigl\{P_{\delta\delta}(k)+2\,f\,\mu^{2}\,P_{\delta\theta}(k)\qquad+f^{2}\,\mu^{4}\,P_{\theta\theta}(k)+A(k,\mu)+B(k,\mu)\Bigr\}.$ (Taruya, Nishimichi & Saito 2010, arXiv:1006.0699) [src: Sect. IV.1, Eq. 24], where A and B are the corrections from the non-linear coupling of density and velocity and $D_{\rm FoG}$ replaces an exponential prefactor by a phenomenological damping function [src: Sect. IV.1, text after Eq. 24].
- In the one-loop LPT of Chen et al. the fingers of god and redshift errors are absorbed by counterterms $\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{6}$ and stochastic terms, rather than by a damping function (arXiv:2012.04636) [src: Sect. 3, text after Eq. 4] <!-- num: not a value -->.

## Consistency relations
<!-- key: consistency-relations -->

- The consistency relations of LSS relate an (n+1)-point function with one soft momentum to an n-point function; they follow from the symmetries of the Newtonian fluid coupled to gravity (Kehagias & Riotto 2013, arXiv:1302.0130) [src: abstract] or, equivalently, from Ward identities of Galilean invariance, valid up to $\mathcal{O}(f_{NL}^{2})$ corrections (Peloso & Pietroni 2013, arXiv:1302.0223) [src: abstract].
- In single-field inflation a long mode is a diffeomorphism from inflation to the late universe, which gives relations non-perturbative in the short-scale δ at first and second order in the soft momentum q, of the form $\langle\Phi_{\vec{q}}(\eta)\delta_{\vec{k}_{1}}(\eta_{1})\cdots\delta_{\vec{k}_{n}}(\eta_{n})\rangle^{\prime}_{q\to 0}=P_{\Phi}(q)\sum_{a}\mathcal{O}_{a}\langle\delta_{\vec{k}_{1}}(\eta_{1})\cdots\delta_{\vec{k}_{n}}(\eta_{n})\rangle^{\prime}\;,$ (Creminelli et al. 2013, arXiv:1309.3557) [src: Eq. 44], with operators $\mathcal{O}_{a}$ acting on the short modes; in the non-relativistic limit they match Kehagias–Riotto and Peloso–Pietroni [src: abstract].
- Among the terms of the single-field relation, the one at zeroth order in q is $\langle\Phi_{\vec{q}}\,\delta_{\vec{k}_{1}}(\eta_{1})\cdots\delta_{\vec{k}_{n}}(\eta_{n})\rangle^{\prime}_{q\to 0}\supset\frac{5}{3}P_{\Phi}(q)\left(3(n-1)+\sum_{a}\vec{k}_{a}\cdot\vec{\partial}_{k_{a}}+\frac{1}{2}q^{i}D_{i}\right)\langle\delta_{\vec{k}_{1}}(\eta_{1})\cdots\delta_{\vec{k}_{n}}(\eta_{n})\rangle^{\prime}\;,$ (Creminelli et al. 2013, arXiv:1309.3557) [src: Eq. 49], a dilation acting on the short modes; terms in $\vec{q}\cdot\vec{k}_{a}$ follow in Eqs. 53 and 57.
- In the DHOST example of the Snowmass white paper the tree-level bispectrum keeps the standard form $B_{t}({\bf q},{\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2})=2F_{2}({\bf q},{\bf k}_{1})P_{11}(q)P_{11}(k_{1})+\text{2 perms.}\ ,$ (arXiv:2203.08232) [src: Eq. 4.38], with deviations from ΛCDM parameterised as $A_{\alpha}=1+\Delta A_{\alpha}\ ,\quad\text{and}\quad A_{\gamma}=A_{\gamma}^{\Lambda\text{CDM}}+\Delta A_{\gamma}\ .$ [src: Eq. 4.40].
- The relations fail if the equivalence principle is violated (two fluids, one with a fifth force); Galileon models are not expected to violate them (Creminelli et al. 2013, arXiv:1312.6074) [src: abstract]. A violation appears as a dipole with a $1/K$ scale dependence in the squeezed bispectrum of two distinct tracers (Dam & Darwish 2025, arXiv:2510.13803) [src: abstract].
- In ΛCDM the consistency relations fix the coefficient of the shift term of $F_{2}$. In DHOST and GLPV dark-energy theories they are violated because the scalar introduces a large-scale relative velocity that no coordinate change removes; writing $F_{2}({\bf q},{\bf k})=A_{\alpha}\alpha_{s}({\bf q},{\bf k})+A_{\gamma}\gamma({\bf q},{\bf k})\ ,$ (Cabass et al. 2022, arXiv:2203.08232) [src: Sect. 4.5, Eq. 4.39], $A_{\alpha}=1$ in ΛCDM and the squeezed oscillatory bispectrum is $\lim_{q\rightarrow 0}\frac{B_{t}^{\rm osc}({\bf q},{\bf k}-{\bf q}/2,-{\bf k}-{\bf q}/2)}{P_{11}^{\rm s}(q)}\approx-A_{\alpha}\frac{{\bf q}\cdot{\bf k}}{q^{2}}\frac{{\bf q}\cdot{\bf k}}{k}\frac{\partial P_{11}^{\rm osc}(k)}{\partial k}+\mathcal{O}\left(P^{\rm osc}_{11}(k)\right)\ ,$ [src: Eq. 4.42], proportional to $A_{\alpha}$ only. The IR-dominant one-loop term $P_{1\text{-loop}}(k)\Big|_{\rm IR}\sim\lambda_{D}\lambda_{\Delta v}^{2}P_{11}(k)\int_{q\lesssim k}\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left(\frac{{\bf q}\cdot{\bf k}}{q^{2}}\right)^{2}P_{11}(q)\ ,$ [src: Eq. 4.37] is absent in ΛCDM and can appear in DHOST theories. The theories themselves belong to `thcosmo-modified-gravity`.

## Primordial non-Gaussianity and scale-dependent bias
<!-- key: primordial-non-gaussianity-and-scale-dependent -->

- Local primordial non-Gaussianity adds to the Lagrangian bias of halos a term that grows as $k^{-2}$, $\Delta b(k)=2b_{L}f_{\rm NL}\delta_{c}\frac{3\Omega_{m}}{2ag(a)\,r_{H}^{2}k^{2}}\;,$ (Dalal et al. 2008, arXiv:0710.4560) [src: Eq. 8], where $b_{L}$ is the Gaussian Lagrangian bias; in Eulerian bias it reads $\Delta b(k)=2(b-1)f_{\rm NL}\delta_{c}\frac{3\Omega_{m}}{2a\,g(a)r_{H}^{2}k^{2}}$ [src: Eq. 9]. Dalal et al. confirmed the effect with N-body simulations [src: abstract]. Here g(a) and r_H follow the paper's notation.
- Writing the coefficient as a bias parameter $b_{\phi}$ multiplying $f_{\rm NL}$, the "universality relation" fixes $b_{\phi}=2\delta_{c}(b_{1}-p)$ with p = 1; Barreira parameterises departures by a free p (arXiv:2009.06622) [src: abstract, inline]. $b_{\phi}$ is completely degenerate with $f_{\rm NL}$ in the galaxy power spectrum, and the universality relation breaks down for realistic galaxy populations because of PNG assembly bias (de S. Nascimento & Dalal 2026, arXiv:2607.18229) [src: abstract].
- Chudaykin, Ivanov and Philcox constrain local, equilateral and orthogonal PNG from DESI DR1 power spectra and bispectra with a one-loop EFT model for both statistics; they find $f^{\rm loc}_{\rm NL}=-0.1\pm 7.4$ at 68% CL, and $f^{\rm loc}_{\rm NL}=-0.0\pm 4.1$ in combination with the CMB (arXiv:2512.04266) [src: abstract]. Measured $f_{\rm NL}$ values belong to cosmoskill `cosmo-inflation-primordial`; the shapes and templates to `thcosmo-primordial-correlators`.

## Velocity statistics and the momentum field
<!-- key: velocity-statistics-and-the-momentum-field -->

- Power spectra involving the momentum are significantly more affected by large-scale flows than the density spectrum; keeping track of these velocities in the IR resummation significantly enhances the UV reach of the momentum power spectrum and removes its BAO residuals (Senatore & Zaldarriaga 2014, arXiv:1404.5954) [src: abstract].
- Peculiar-velocity statistics depend on long-wavelength bulk flows in a way that needs a variant of the usual IR resummation, implemented recursively in powers of the velocity in both Lagrangian and Eulerian frameworks; one-loop pairwise velocity statistics up to the second moment can be modelled with the same EFT coefficients as the redshift-space galaxy density, and recover the growth rate to better than percent precision against N-body simulations (Chen, Howlett, Lai & Qin 2025, arXiv:2508.00066) [src: abstract].
- The one-loop matter–momentum bispectrum, the dark-matter version of the galaxy–galaxy–electron-momentum bispectrum measured by kSZ, needs counterterms of which two acquire a transverse direction, one generated entirely by vorticity; its dipole agrees with simulations to 5% up to k_max = 0.23 h/Mpc, against k_max = 0.07 h/Mpc for tree level, at $z=0.5$ (Raikman, Sullivan & Ivanov 2026, arXiv:2609.15750) [src: abstract].
- The counterterms of the renormalised two-loop matter power spectrum include the effect of generated vorticity (Anastasiou et al. 2025, arXiv:2509.05187) [src: abstract].
- Fits of velocity data (flip-type field-level likelihoods, momentum power spectra, $f\sigma_8$ from peculiar velocities) belong to cosmoskill `cosmo-peculiar-velocities`.

## Intrinsic alignments and shape bias
<!-- key: intrinsic-alignments-and-shape-bias -->

- Galaxy and halo shapes are a symmetric tensor field $S_{ij}$; their power spectrum is defined by $(2\pi)^{3}P_{ijkl}(\mathbf{k})\delta^{D}(\mathbf{k+k^{\prime}}):=\langle S_{ij}(\mathbf{k})S_{kl}(\mathbf{k^{\prime}})\rangle$ (Bakx et al. 2023, arXiv:2303.15565) [src: Eq. 1].
- The bias operators are built from $\Pi^{[1]}_{ij}(\mathbf{x},\eta):=\frac{\partial_{i}\partial_{j}}{\nabla^{2}}\delta(\mathbf{x},\eta)=K_{ij}(\mathbf{x},\eta)+\frac{1}{3}\delta_{ij}\delta(\mathbf{x},\eta);$ (arXiv:2303.15565) [src: Eq. 12], with $K_{ij}$ the tidal field, and recursively with convective time derivatives (Eq. 13). The scalar tracer takes the traces, $\delta_{s}(\mathbf{x},\eta)=\sum_{\mathcal{O}}b_{\mathcal{O}}(\eta)\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{x},\eta).$ [src: Eq. 11]; the shape field takes the trace-free parts, $g_{ij}(\mathbf{x},\eta)=\text{TF}(S)_{ij}(\mathbf{x},\eta)=\sum_{\mathcal{O^{\prime}}}b_{\mathcal{O^{\prime}}}(\eta)\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{ij}(\mathbf{x},\eta).$ [src: Eq. 16].
- Higher-order operators follow from the recursion $\Pi^{[n]}_{ij}(\mathbf{x},\eta):=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\bigg((\mathcal{H}f)^{-1}\frac{D}{D\eta}\Pi^{[n-1]}_{ij}(\mathbf{x},\eta)-(n-1)\Pi^{[n-1]}_{ij}(\mathbf{x},\eta)\bigg).$ (Bakx et al. 2023, arXiv:2303.15565) [src: Eq. 13], with the convective derivative along the fluid velocity; the three second-order trace-free shape operators are $\bigg(\text{TF}(\Pi^{[2]})\bigg)^{(2)},\quad\bigg(\text{TF}((\Pi^{[1]})^{2})\bigg)^{(2)},\quad\bigg(\text{Tr}(\Pi^{[1]})\text{TF}(\Pi^{[1]})\bigg)^{(2)}.$ [src: Eq. 17]. The higher-derivative counterterm enters the matter field as $\delta(\mathbf{k},\eta)=\delta^{(1)}(\mathbf{k},\eta)+\delta^{(2)}(\mathbf{k},\eta)+\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{k},\eta)+b_{R}^{\prime}(\eta)R^{2}k^{2}\delta^{(1)}(\mathbf{k},\eta);$ [src: Eq. 18].
- At second order the three scalar operators are not independent: $7\bigg(\text{Tr}(\Pi^{[2]})\bigg)^{(2)}-5\bigg(\text{Tr}((\Pi^{[1]})^{2})\bigg)^{(2)}-2\bigg((\text{Tr}(\Pi^{[1]}))^{2}\bigg)^{(2)}=0.$ (arXiv:2303.15565) [src: Eq. 15]; the three trace-free second-order shape operators of Eq. 17 are all independent.
- The one-loop EFT of IA contains 8 free bias parameters and is presented as a theoretically motivated extension of the non-linear alignment (NLA) and tidal-alignment–tidal-torquing (TATT) models (arXiv:2303.15565) [src: abstract]. The tidal-alignment idea goes back to Catelan, Kamionkowski and Blandford, who argued that if halo shapes or angular momenta set the galaxy image orientation, images align with the component of the tidal field perpendicular to the line of sight and mimic the lensing shear correlation (arXiv:astro-ph/0005470) [src: abstract].
- The IA bispectrum: tree-level perturbation theory describes the multipoles of halo IA bispectra for $k\lesssim 0.11\,h$/Mpc at $z=0$; higher multipoles of $B_{\delta\delta E}$ reduce the errors on second-order shape biases by factors of 5 or more; parity-odd bispectra are detected at about $10\sigma$ or more for $k<0.15\,h$/Mpc in a $1\,({\rm Gpc}/h)^{3}$ simulation (Bakx et al. 2025, arXiv:2507.06818) [src: abstract].
- A field-level EFT of IA for spin-2 fields, with the Zel'dovich displacements treated non-linearly, contains all shape operators through third order and describes the helicity fields of halo shapes in Quijote better than NLA and TATT, as judged by correlation with the initial conditions (Kokron, Ivanov & Sullivan 2026, arXiv:2610.36027) [src: abstract].

## Large deviations and one-point statistics
<!-- key: large-deviations-and-one-point-statistics -->

- Weakly non-linear cumulants: the tree-level skewness is set by the second-order kernel (results.md, "Computed coefficients and theorems"), and the vertex coefficients $\nu_{n}$ of the spherically averaged kernels fix the tree-level cumulants at all orders (Bernardeau et al. 2002, arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Eq. 52, Sect. 2.4.2].
- Large-deviation principle: for Gaussian initial overdensities on concentric spherical shells, the densities on the corresponding shells after gravitational evolution obey a large-deviation principle as long as no shell crossing happens; this gives the cumulant generating function of the non-linear densities (Bernardeau 2015, arXiv:1511.08641) [src: abstract]. The principle is stated as $\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\epsilon\,\log\left[P_{\epsilon}(\{\alpha_{i}\}\in A)\right]=-\inf_{\underline{\alpha}\in A}I(\underline{\alpha})$ (arXiv:1511.08641) [src: Eq. 4], with the initial rate function $I({\underline{\tau}})=\frac{\sigma^{2}(r_{N})}{2}\langle{\underline{\tau}},\Xi{\underline{\tau}}\rangle\,.$ [src: Eq. 7], and the final rate function obtained by the contraction principle, $\Psi({\underline{\rho}})=\inf_{{\underline{\rho}}=\zeta({\underline{\tau}})}I({\underline{\tau}})\,,$ [src: Eq. 9], with ζ the spherical-collapse mapping of Eq. 6.
- The cell densities are the linear density contrast averaged in spheres, $\tau_{i}:=\frac{1}{\frac{4}{3}\pi r_{i}^{3}}\int_{|{\bf x}|<r_{i}}\tau({\bf x})\,{\rm d}^{3}{\bf x}\,,$ (arXiv:1511.08641) [src: Eq. 5], mapped to the final densities and radii by spherical collapse, $\rho_{i}=\zeta(\eta,\tau_{i})\qquad R_{i}=r_{i}/\rho_{i}^{1/3}\,.$ [src: Eq. 6]; the covariance of the initial cell densities is $\Sigma_{ij}=\int\frac{{\rm d}{\bf k}^{3}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,P(k)\,w_{T}(kr_{i})\,w_{T}(kr_{j})$ [src: Eq. 8], with $w_{T}$ the top-hat window, and the final rate function is $\Psi({\underline{\rho}})=\frac{\sigma^{2}(R_{N})}{2}\langle{\underline{\tau}}({\underline{\rho}}),\Xi\,{\underline{\tau}}({\underline{\rho}})\rangle\,$ [src: Eq. 10].
- With a logarithmic density transformation, the spherical-collapse cumulant generating function is free of critical points, and a saddle-point approximation gives one- and two-cell PDFs accurate to a few percent against the numerical integration and in excellent agreement with N-body simulations over a wide range of densities (Uhlemann et al. 2016, arXiv:1512.05793) [src: abstract].
- Recent extensions apply the same machinery to tracers with non-linear bias and stochasticity, for example the HI intensity-mapping PDF with foreground removal and beam effects, which breaks the degeneracy between linear bias and clustering amplitude (Vos-Gines & Uhlemann 2026, arXiv:2607.06526) [src: abstract]. The excursion-set side (halo and void abundances) belongs to `thcosmo-nonlinear-structure`.

## Perturbative forward models at the field level
<!-- key: perturbative-forward-models-at-the-field-level -->

- The EFTofLSS predicts the full non-linear field for a given realisation of the initial conditions. This allows comparisons with simulations without cosmic variance, which gave the first reliable measurements of EFT parameters and of the non-linear scale, and a field-level EFT likelihood for inference without summary statistics (Cabass et al. 2022, arXiv:2203.08232) [src: Sect. 3.9]. The same section describes the "LSS bootstrap", which fixes the form of galaxy correlators from symmetries and the equivalence principle at the field level [src: Sect. 3.9].
- The LSS bootstrap has since been fitted to data: an MCMC analysis of joint power spectrum and bispectrum constrains departures from ΛCDM in the linear growth rate and the quadratic kernels using symmetry arguments alone (Biselli et al. 2026, arXiv:2605.13298) [src: abstract].
- A field-level perturbative forward model of the Lyα forest matches simulated 3D and 1D power spectra at the 1% (5%) level up to k ≤ 0.3 (1.0) h/Mpc for $z=2.0$–3.2, detects the full set of quadratic line-of-sight bias operators, and finds that phenomenological flux-power models based on modulations of the linear spectrum fail at the field level even on quasi-linear scales (de Belsunce et al. 2026, arXiv:2606.26234) [src: abstract].
- For local PNG, the field-level Cramér–Rao bound shows that multi-tracer scale-dependent bias can exceed conservative higher-point analyses but falls short of the optimum when all modes down to halo scales are used (Chen, Green & Lee 2026, arXiv:2603.22415) [src: abstract] (forecast).
- Field-level velocity likelihoods and their data use belong to cosmoskill `cosmo-peculiar-velocities`; the inference machinery (HMC, simulation-based inference) to cosmoskill `cosmo-statistics-inference`.
