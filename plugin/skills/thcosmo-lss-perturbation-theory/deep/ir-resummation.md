## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- The leading-order damping and the one-loop resummed formula of Baldauf et al. are in `formalism.md#infrared-resummation`; the Zel'dovich origin of the damping is in `formalism.md#lagrangian-perturbation-theory-and-the-zel`; validity and the wiggle/no-wiggle assumption in `approximations.md#infrared-resummation-and-the-wiggle-no-wiggle`. They are not repeated here.
- Normalisation of the damping integral: Baldauf et al. write $\Sigma_{\Lambda}^{2}$ with a prefactor $\frac{1}{6\pi^{2}}$ (arXiv:1504.04366) [src: Eq. 15], while Blas et al. and Ivanov and Sibiryakov write $\Sigma^{2}$ with $\frac{4\pi}{3}$ (arXiv:1605.02149, Eq. 7.2; arXiv:1804.05080, Eq. 7.5a). CLASS-PT writes Σ² with $\frac{1}{6\pi^{2}}$ and states that the extra factors of 2π relative to the TSPT papers come from its different Fourier convention (Chudaykin et al. 2020, arXiv:2004.10607) [src: Eq. 2.25; Sect. 2.5, footnote 9]; a value of Σ is therefore quoted with its paper and its Fourier convention.
- BAO scale: Blas et al. and Ivanov and Sibiryakov use k_osc = h/(110 Mpc) for the period of the oscillations (arXiv:1605.02149) [src: Sect. 7.1], (arXiv:1804.05080) [src: Sect. 7.1]; Baldauf et al. write $\ell_{\rm BAO}$ and Ivanov's review $r_{\rm BAO}$. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Normalisation of $P_{13}$: Blas et al. write $P^{1-loop}=P_{22}+2P_{13}$ (arXiv:1605.02149) [src: Sect. 7.1, inline, text after Eq. 7.4], half of the $P_{13}$ of Ivanov's review (see `deep/one-loop-eft-power-spectrum.md`, "Conventions"). <!-- num: not a value -->
- Time: Blas et al. carry the growth factor explicitly, $D(z)^{2}\Sigma^{2}$ with Σ evaluated at z = 0 (arXiv:1605.02149) [src: Sect. 7.1, footnote 8]; Ivanov and Sibiryakov absorb it, $P(k)\equiv D^{2}(z)\bar{P}(k)\,.$ (arXiv:1804.05080) [src: Eq. 7.1].

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1: why a resummation is needed
<!-- key: step-1-why-a-resummation-is-needed -->

The individual one-loop terms are IR enhanced and cancel (`deep/one-loop-eft-power-spectrum.md`, Step 4); the remainder, Eq. 36 of Ivanov's review, assumes that $P_{11}(\vert{\bf k}-{\bf q}\vert)$ can be Taylor expanded, which fails for $q\sim k_{BAO}\ll k$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 36]. With $P_{w}(k)=f_{s}(k)\sin(k/k_{\rm BAO})$ [src: Sect. 3.3, inline, text after Eq. 37] the one-loop wiggly spectrum shows a smoothing and a phase shift with effective coupling $(k/k_{\rm BAO})^{2}\sigma_{l}^{2}$, which can be of order one, so that the two-loop term is not suppressed [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 38]. <!-- num: not a value --> A uniform displacement drops out of equal-time correlators by the equivalence principle, but not across a feature narrower than the displacement (`formalism.md#infrared-resummation`).

### Step 2: Lagrangian resummation (Senatore and Zaldarriaga 2014)
<!-- key: step-2-lagrangian-resummation-senatore-and -->

1. In the Lagrangian expression of the power spectrum the displacement enters through the cumulant exponential $K(\vec{k},\vec{q};t_{1},t_{2})=\exp\left[\sum_{N=1}^{\infty}{i^{N}\over N!}\langle X(\vec{k}_{1},\vec{q};t_{1},t_{2})^{N}\rangle_{c}\right]\ .$ (arXiv:1404.5954) [src: Eq. 32]. Three small parameters are distinguished: $\epsilon_{s<}$ for the long displacements, which "in our Universe controls the largest non-linearity in the range of scales of interest for the BAO" (arXiv:1404.5954) [src: Sect. 1], and $\epsilon_{\delta<}$ and $\epsilon_{s>}$ for the long density and the short displacements; Eulerian perturbation theory expands in all three on the same footing [src: Sect. 2.1, first paragraph].
2. The part to be resummed is the Gaussian linear displacement, $K_{0}(\vec{k},\vec{q};t_{1},t_{2})=\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\langle X_{0}(\vec{k},\vec{q};t_{1},t_{2})^{2}\rangle\right]$ (arXiv:1404.5954) [src: Eq. 35], with $X_{0}$ built from the linear solutions.
3. Multiplying and dividing by $K_{0}$ and expanding $K/K_{0}$ to order N in all parameters gives $\left.K(\vec{k},\vec{q};t_{1},t_{2})\right|_{N}\simeq K_{0}(\vec{k},\vec{q};t_{1},t_{2})\cdot\left.\left.{K(\vec{k},\vec{q};t_{1},t_{2})\over K_{0}(\vec{k},\vec{q};t_{1},t_{2})}\right|\right|_{N}=K_{0}(\vec{k},\vec{q};t_{1},t_{2})\sum_{j=0}^{N}\left.\left.K_{0}^{-1}(\vec{k},\vec{q};t_{1},t_{2})\right|\right|_{N-j}K(\vec{k},\vec{q};t_{1},t_{2})_{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\left(K_{0}(\vec{k},\vec{q};t_{1},t_{2})\cdot\left.\left.K_{0}^{-1}(\vec{k},\vec{q};t_{1},t_{2})\right|\right|_{N-j}\right)\cdot K(\vec{k},\vec{q};t_{1},t_{2})_{j}\ .$ (arXiv:1404.5954) [src: Eq. 36], where a single bar with subscript N means order N in the long density and short displacements and all orders in the long displacements, and a double bar order N in all of them [src: Sect. 2.1, text after Eq. 32]. By construction the result agrees with the Eulerian one to order N, and it is exact to all orders in $\epsilon_{s}$ when the long displacements are free [src: Sect. 2.1, text after Eq. 36].
4. With $F_{||_{N-j}}(\vec{k},\vec{q};t_{1},t_{2})=K_{0}(\vec{k},\vec{q};t_{1},t_{2})\cdot\left.\left.K_{0}^{-1}(\vec{k},\vec{q};t_{1},t_{2})\right|\right|_{N-j}\ ,$ (arXiv:1404.5954) [src: Eq. 43], the resummed spectrum is $\left.\langle\delta(\vec{k}_{1},t_{1})\delta(\vec{k}_{2},t_{2})\rangle\right|_{N}=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\vec{k}_{1}+\vec{k}_{2})\int d^{3}q\ e^{-i\vec{k}_{1}\cdot\vec{q}}\;\sum_{j=0}^{N}\left[F_{||_{N-j}}(\vec{k}_{1},\vec{q},t_{1},t_{2})\cdot K(\vec{k}_{1},\vec{q};t_{1},t_{2})_{j}\right]\ .$ (arXiv:1404.5954) [src: Eq. 44]. The Eulerian terms $K_{j}$ are thus convolved with a resummation kernel built from the linear displacements; the paper then moves to real space (Eqs. 45–47), where the kernel acts on the Eulerian correlation function [src: Sect. 2.1, Eqs. 45–47].
5. Residual: the non-linear evolution of the displacements is not resummed; the leading neglected terms scale as $\epsilon_{\delta<}^{\rm high}(\epsilon_{\delta<}^{\rm low})^{n-1}\epsilon_{s<}^{n}$ with $n\geq 2$ at two loops and $n\geq 1$ at one loop, roughly two-loop terms in the Lagrangian counting; the short-mode displacements are not resummed either (arXiv:1404.5954) [src: Sect. 2.1, text after Eq. 37]. <!-- num: not a value --> Higher cumulants can be included by the substitution of Eq. 37, with the Lagrangian EFT counterterms [src: Eq. 37].

Lewandowski and Senatore give an analytic implementation of this resummation for the one-loop correlation function, accurate to better than 0.2% against the numerical integration, and an approximate scheme with the linear displacements of a fixed fiducial cosmology that is about six times faster (arXiv:1810.11855) [src: abstract].

### Step 3: the equivalence-principle formula (Baldauf et al. 2015)
<!-- key: step-3-the-equivalence-principle-formula -->

1. Averaging the exponential of the long-mode displacement over Gaussian long modes gives the dressed correlation function near the BAO scale, $\tilde{\xi}_{g}({\bm{x}})\simeq\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}{\bm{k}}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\>e^{i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{x}}}e^{-\Sigma_{\epsilon k}^{2}k^{2}}\left\langle\delta_{g}({\bm{k}},t)\delta_{g}(-{\bm{k}},t)\right\rangle_{\epsilon}.$ (arXiv:1504.04366) [src: Eq. 20], where the subscript ε means that the expectation value is computed without modes $q<\epsilon k$ but with all short-scale non-linearities, and $\nabla^{2}\approx\partial_{r}^{2}$ is used up to corrections of order σ/ℓ_BAO [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 20]. <!-- num: not a value -->
2. The damping is the long-mode relative displacement across the BAO scale, $\Sigma_{\Lambda}^{2}\approx\frac{1}{6\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\!\!\!{\rm d}q\>P_{\rm lin}(q)[1-j_{0}(q\ell_{\rm BAO})+2j_{2}(q\ell_{\rm BAO})],$ (arXiv:1504.04366) [src: Eq. 15]; the exponential should multiply only the wiggly power.
3. Inserting the one-loop spectrum for the exclusive average would double count the long modes, already present at one loop as $\Sigma^{2}_{\epsilon k}k^{2}P^{w}_{\rm lin}$; subtracting it gives the one-loop formula Eq. 21 of `formalism.md#infrared-resummation`, with the EFT counterterm in Eq. 22 [src: Sect. III, text before Eq. 21].
4. Without resummation the one-loop correlation function shows a spurious double peak at the BAO scale, because only the $\Sigma^{2}\xi''$ term is kept and not the higher derivatives that partly cancel it; the failure of SPT for the correlation function is therefore not a UV problem [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 22]. The peak shift is of order $\Sigma^{2}\xi_{g}^{\prime}/\ell_{\rm BAO}$, smaller than the broadening by σ/ℓ_BAO, not fixed by symmetry, and included at leading order in the one-loop term [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 22]. <!-- num: not a value -->

### Step 4: TSPT (Blas et al. 2016; Ivanov's review, Sect. 5.3)
<!-- key: step-4-tspt-blas-et-al-2016-ivanov-s-review -->

1. The split $P_{11}=P_{\rm smooth}+P_{w}$ induces a split of the TSPT vertices, $\bar{\Gamma}_{n}=\bar{\Gamma}_{n}^{s}+\bar{\Gamma}_{n}^{w}\,.$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 104]; the wiggly vertices are enhanced in the soft limit, $\bar{\Gamma}_{3}\to\frac{{\bf k}\cdot{\bf q}}{q^{2}}\left(\frac{P_{w}(|{\bf k}+{\bf q}|)-P_{w}(q)}{P_{\rm smooth}^{2}(k)}\right)\,.$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 105], with the 1/q cancelled only for $q\to 0$ and not for $k_{\rm BAO}\lesssim q\ll k$ [src: Sect. 5.3, text after Eq. 105].
2. For any number of soft legs the wiggly vertex factorises, $\bar{\Gamma}^{\prime w}_{n}\left({\bf k}_{1},...,{\bf k}_{m}-\sum_{j=1}^{n-m}{\bf q}_{j},{\bf q}_{1},...,{\bf q}_{n-m}\right)=(-1)^{n-m}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-m}D_{{\bf q}_{j}}\right)\Gamma^{\prime w}_{m}\left({\bf k}_{1},...,{\bf k}_{m}\right)\,,$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 106], with the finite-difference operator $D_{\bf q}P_{w}(k)=\frac{{\bf k}\cdot{\bf q}}{q^{2}}\left(e^{{\bf q}\cdot\nabla_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}}-1\right)P_{w}(k^{\prime})\Big|_{k^{\prime}=k}\,.$ [src: Eq. 107], each finite-difference operator scaling as the inverse of ε = q/k (Eq. 108). Splitting loop momenta at $k_{S}$ and counting soft legs on wiggly vertices gives power-counting rules; at leading enhancement and zero hard loops the daisy diagrams dominate and exponentiate into the damping of Eq. 109 (`formalism.md#infrared-resummation`) (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Sect. 5.3, text after Eq. 108].
3. The operator acting on the wiggles reduces at leading order in ε to a multiplicative factor, $\mathcal{S}P_{w}(k)=k^{2}\Sigma^{2}P_{w}(k)\times\big(1+O(\varepsilon)\big)\,,$ (arXiv:1605.02149) [src: Eq. 7.1], with Σ² of Eq. 7.2, the same integral as Ivanov's Eq. 109. The exact result does not depend on $k_{S}$, so the residual dependence estimates the perturbative error [src: Sect. 3.2].
4. At next-to-leading order (one hard loop and the first subleading soft correction) $\begin{split}P^{IR\;res,LO+NLO}&(z,k)=D(z)^{2}\left(P_{s}(k)+\big(1+k^{2}D(z)^{2}\Sigma^{2}\big)e^{-k^{2}D(z)^{2}\Sigma^{2}}P_{w}(k)\right)\\ &+\!D(z)^{4}P^{1-loop}[P_{s}+e^{-k^{2}D(z)^{2}\Sigma^{2}}P_{w}]+D(z)^{6}e^{-k^{2}D(z)^{2}\Sigma^{2}}\!(\mathcal{S}^{a}\!+\!\varkappa\mathcal{S}^{b})P_{w}(k)\,,\end{split}$ (arXiv:1605.02149) [src: Eq. 7.4], with $\varkappa=1$ for the exact dynamics and 0 in the Zel'dovich approximation [src: Sect. 7.1, text after Eq. 7.4]. In practice the one-loop integrals are evaluated with the LO resummed spectrum as input; the last term, with the operators of Eqs. 7.5a–7.5b, carries the subleading soft corrections that shift the BAO phase [src: Sect. 7.1, text after Eq. 7.4; Sect. 7.4].
5. The wiggle/no-wiggle split is bookkeeping: smooth contributions are never IR enhanced, so the factorisation can be promoted to the full vertex after resummation, and the ambiguity of the split appears only at higher orders (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Sect. 5.3, text after Eq. 109].

### Step 5: Lagrangian resummation below $k_{\rm IR}$ (Chen et al. 2020)
<!-- key: step-5-lagrangian-resummation-below-k-rm-ir -->

In one-loop LPT the linear displacements can be kept exponentiated; Chen, Vlah, Castorina and White resum them only up to a scale $k_{\rm IR}$, splitting the linear cumulant with an exponential cutoff $\exp[-\frac{1}{2}(k/k_{\rm IR})^{2}]$ into a resummed "less than" part and a "greater than" part expanded to second order (arXiv:2012.04636) [src: Sect. 3, text after Eq. 6]. The corresponding displacement variances are $\Sigma^{2}_{<}=\frac{2}{3}\int\frac{dk}{2\pi^{2}}P_{\rm lin}(k)\ e^{-(k/k_{\rm IR})^{2}},\quad\Sigma^{2}_{>}=\frac{2}{3}\int\frac{dk}{2\pi^{2}}P_{\rm lin}(k)\ (1-e^{-(k/k_{\rm IR})^{2}}),$ (arXiv:2012.04636) [src: Eq. 15], whose sum is the mean square pairwise Zel'dovich displacement of two distant points [src: Sect. 5.1, footnote 4]. No wiggle/no-wiggle split is needed, and unequal-time correlators come out directly [src: Sect. 3, text after Eq. 6]. The limit of vanishing $k_{\rm IR}$ is unresummed Eulerian theory [src: Appendix A, Fig. 9 caption].

### Step 6: redshift space (Ivanov and Sibiryakov 2018)
<!-- key: step-6-redshift-space-ivanov-and-sibiryakov-2018 -->

Mapping TSPT to redshift space, the operator acting on the wiggles at leading order in ε is $g^{2}\mathcal{S}^{(s)}[P_{w}(k)]=k^{2}\big[\big(1+f\mu^{2}(2+f)\big)\Sigma^{2}+f^{2}\mu^{2}(\mu^{2}-1)\delta\Sigma^{2}\big]P_{w}(k)\times\big(1+O(\varepsilon)\big)\,,$ (arXiv:1804.05080) [src: Eq. 7.4], with the new integral $\delta\Sigma^{2}\equiv 4\pi\int_{0}^{k_{S}}dq\,P_{nw}(q)\,j_{2}\left(\frac{q}{k_{osc}}\right)\,.$ [src: Eq. 7.5b]. The leading-order resummed spectrum of biased tracers is $\begin{split}P_{hh}^{(s)\,\text{IR res,LO}}(k,\mu)=&(b_{1}+f\mu^{2})^{2}\Big(P_{nw}(k)+e^{-k^{2}\Sigma^{2}_{\text{tot}}(\mu;k_{S})}P_{w}(k)\Big)\,,\end{split}$ (arXiv:1804.05080) [src: Eq. 7.6], with $\begin{split}\Sigma^{2}_{\text{tot}}(\mu;k_{S})~\equiv~&\big(1+f\mu^{2}(2+f)\big)\Sigma^{2}+f^{2}\mu^{2}(\mu^{2}-1)\delta\Sigma^{2}\,.\end{split}$ (arXiv:1804.05080) [src: Eq. 7.7]. The first term multiplies the line-of-sight displacement by (1 + f) as in the Kaiser formula, $k^{2}_{\parallel}(1+f)^{2}\Sigma^{2}+k^{2}_{\perp}\Sigma^{2}=\Sigma^{2}(1+f\mu^{2}(2+f))\,,$ (arXiv:1804.05080) [src: Eq. 7.8]; the δΣ² term is negative and reduces the damping slightly; the damping does not depend on the bias parameters, as the equivalence principle requires [src: Sect. 7.1, text after Eq. 7.8]. The redshift-space EFT built on this damping is in `deep/redshift-space-eft.md`.

- **TSPT against the equivalence-principle formula.** Blas et al. state that their LO result coincides with that of their ref. [8], a consistency-relation approach, when k_S = k/2, and that the agreement extends to the hard-loop corrections; subleading soft corrections were not included there (arXiv:1605.02149) [src: Sect. 7.2]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **TSPT against RPT.** For the real universe the two damping factors happen to be numerically close, but they would differ for a universe with more power at q ≪ k_osc; at NLO TSPT is no longer a simple exponential damping (arXiv:1605.02149) [src: Sect. 7.2].
- **TSPT against the Zel'dovich approximation.** The NLO correlation function differs from the Zel'dovich one by around 5% in the BAO range, and agrees with N-body data somewhat better than the Zel'dovich approximation in the peak region, within the uncertainty of the simulation (arXiv:1605.02149) [src: Sect. 7.3]. Blas et al. also check their result against exact Zel'dovich expressions and rederive it within SPT (Appendices F–G) [src: Sect. 7.2, last paragraph].
- **Choice of $k_{S}$.** The integrand of Σ² peaks at $q\sim 0.03h/$ Mpc but contributes up to $q\sim 0.2h/$ Mpc; small $k_{S}$ gives $\Sigma^{2}\to 2\pi/5\int_{0}^{k_{S}}dq\,q^{2}P_{s}(q)/k_{osc}^{2}$ and large $k_{S}$ the velocity dispersion; Blas et al. use values around $k_{BAO}\sim 0.1h/$ Mpc and find a very mild dependence (arXiv:1605.02149) [src: Sect. 7.1, inline, text after Eq. 7.3]. Ivanov and Sibiryakov vary $k_{S}$ in the range 0.05 to 0.2 h/Mpc and take the spread as a theoretical uncertainty (arXiv:1804.05080) [src: Sect. 7.1, text after Eq. 7.10].
- **Choice of $k_{\rm IR}$.** Chen et al. use k_IR = 0.2 h/Mpc as fiducial, which reproduces the hexadecapole better than an infinite or a vanishing cutoff; the monopole and quadrupole of the second moment of the pairwise velocity prefer large and small $k_{\rm IR}$ respectively, and the configuration-space BAO is robust to the choice (arXiv:2012.04636) [src: Sect. 5.1; Sect. 6]. Even k_IR = 0.05 h/Mpc removes most of the oscillation excess of unresummed theory [src: Appendix A, Fig. 9 caption].
- **What the DESI codes do.** velocileptors-LPT and PyBird exponentiate the long-wavelength displacements below $k_{\rm IR}$, while the EPT variant of velocileptors and Folps$\nu$ damp only the wiggly part of a wiggle/no-wiggle split; for realistic volumes the choice of $k_{\rm IR}$ has little effect and the final sum is quite insensitive to the split method, while for an unrealisable volume two-loop effects make the schemes differ (Maus et al. 2024, arXiv:2404.07272) [src: Sect. 3.6].
- **Velocity statistics** need a modified resummation, recursive in powers of the velocity (Chen et al. 2025, arXiv:2508.00066; `approximations.md#infrared-resummation-and-the-wiggle-no-wiggle`).
- **Bispectrum.** At leading order the TSPT bispectrum damps each wiggly linear spectrum in the tree-level formula (arXiv:1605.02149) [src: Eq. 7.7]; in unified LPT the wiggle–wiggle part of the IR-resummed bispectrum, and the cross bispectra before and after reconstruction, are treated analytically, with exact IR cancellation (Sugiyama 2026, arXiv:2602.20431) [src: abstract].
- **Configuration-space view.** The two-point function can be written with a transition probability of Lagrangian trajectories that broadens diffusively, more for particles separated by the BAO scale, which is the effective suppression usually called IR resummation (Dam 2026, arXiv:2609.30251) [src: abstract].

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Double counting.** Multiplying a full one-loop wiggly spectrum by the damping counts the long modes twice; the $(1+\Sigma^{2}k^{2})$ factor of Baldauf et al.'s Eq. 21 and of Blas et al.'s Eq. 7.4 removes the one-loop part of the exponential (arXiv:1504.04366) [src: Sect. III, text before Eq. 21]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Damping the smooth part.** RPT-type damping of the whole spectrum removes IR terms that the equivalence principle cancels for a smooth spectrum (arXiv:1605.02149) [src: Sect. 7.2]; resummation schemes that break Galilean invariance give non-physical IR effects (Peloso & Pietroni, `approximations.md#infrared-resummation-and-the-wiggle-no-wiggle`).
- **Unresummed correlation function.** One-loop SPT or EFT without resummation gives a spurious double-peaked BAO feature (arXiv:1504.04366) [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 22].
- **Split dependence.** Two split algorithms are in use, a fitted Eisenstein–Hu-type smooth spectrum and a spline through about 20 points on each side of the BAO with a pivot at $k=0.03\,h/$ Mpc (arXiv:1605.02149) [src: Appendix B]; the split ambiguity enters only at higher order in TSPT (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Sect. 5.3, text after Eq. 109], and the LPT scheme needs no split (arXiv:2012.04636) [src: Sect. 3, text after Eq. 6]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Printing details.** Ivanov's Eq. 110 prints the NLO formula with no plus sign between $(1+\Sigma^{2}k^{2})$ and the one-loop functional at the line break (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 110]; Blas et al.'s Eq. 7.3 prints the damped wiggly spectrum as $P_{w}(z)$ where Eq. 7.4 has $P_{w}(k)$ (arXiv:1605.02149) [src: Eqs. 7.3–7.4]. Quote Eq. 7.4 of arXiv:1605.02149 for the NLO formula. Neither is in the hub's `stale-claims.md` yet. <!-- eq: notation -->
- **A small $k_{S}$ or a large one.** Σ² grows with $k_{S}$ up to about 0.2 h/Mpc, but $k_{S}$ must stay below the k of interest for the IR expansion to hold (arXiv:1605.02149) [src: Sect. 7.1, text after Eq. 7.3]; the LO correlation function depends visibly on $k_{S}$, the NLO one hardly (Sect. 7.3, Figs. 6–7).
- **Velocity statistics with the density scheme.** The broadband of higher velocity moments is sensitive to the IR cutoff (arXiv:2012.04636) [src: Sect. 3, text after Eq. 6]; use the velocity-adapted resummation of arXiv:2508.00066.
