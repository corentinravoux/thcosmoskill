## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- Local PNG: Slosar et al. write $\Phi=\phi+f_{\rm NL}\phi^{2},$ (arXiv:0805.3580) [src: Eq. 1], without subtracting the mean; Barreira et al. subtract it, $\phi(\bm{x})=\phi_{\rm G}(\bm{x})+f_{\textnormal{{nl}}}\left[\phi_{\rm G}(\bm{x})^{2}-\left<\phi_{\rm G}(\bm{x})^{2}\right>\right],$ with φ the primordial Bardeen potential during matter domination (arXiv:2006.09368) [src: Eq. 2]. The constant affects only the mean density, not the scale-dependent term (arXiv:0805.3580) [src: Sect. II.1, text after Eq. 13]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Growth normalisation: Dalal et al. use g(a) ∝ D(a)/a for the decay of the potential, so the peak shift involves the primordial potential $\phi_{p}=\phi/g(a)$ (arXiv:0710.4560) [src: Sect. II, inline, text after Eq. 4]. Slosar et al. normalise D(z) to $(1+z)^{-1}$ in matter domination (arXiv:0805.3580) [src: Sect. II.1, text after Eq. 7], as do de S. Nascimento and Dalal (arXiv:2607.18229) [src: Sect. II, footnote 6]; Barreira et al. put the growth in the transfer function $T_{m}(k,z)$ of their Eq. 5 (arXiv:2006.09368). <!-- num: not a value --> The three forms of $\Delta b(k)$ below are therefore written with different growth factors and are not compared term by term here.
- Collapse threshold: δ_c = 1.686 in Slosar et al. (arXiv:0805.3580) [src: Sect. II.2, text after Eq. 15] and Barreira et al. (arXiv:2006.09368) [src: Sect. 1, text after Eq. 6]; $\delta_{\textrm{crit}}\approx 1.686$ in de S. Nascimento and Dalal (arXiv:2607.18229) [src: Sect. II, inline, text after Eq. 3].
- Bias parameter: $b_{\phi}$ multiplies $f_{\rm NL}\phi$ in the bias expansion, $\delta_{g}(\bm{x},z)=b_{1}(z)\delta_{m}(\bm{x},z)+b_{\phi}(z)f_{\textnormal{{nl}}}\phi(\bm{x})+\epsilon(\bm{x}),$ (arXiv:2006.09368) [src: Eq. 3]. Assassi, Baumann and Schmidt (arXiv:1510.03723) use instead a field ψ with coefficient $c_{\psi}$ and a reference scale μ (Eqs. 2.17–2.20 below); they reduce to $b_\phi$ for local PNG, but that identification is not printed in the sections read. <!-- num: not a value -->

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Route 1 — peak shift by the long-wavelength potential (Dalal et al. 2008)
<!-- key: route-1-peak-shift-by-the-long-wavelength -->

Dalal, Doré, Huterer and Shirokov (arXiv:0710.4560) work near high peaks, where the term $\phi\nabla^{2}\phi$ dominates over $|\nabla\phi|^{2}$; in terms of late-time fields the non-Gaussian density is $\delta_{\rm NG}\approx\delta[1+2f_{\rm NL}\phi/g(a)].$ [src: Eq. 4]. Because δ and φ are correlated, rare peaks are systematically raised or lowered [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 5]. A long mode raises sub-threshold peaks as in the Gaussian case and also raises the peak height by $2f_{\rm NL}\phi_{p}\delta_{c}$ for peaks near threshold, so that $\delta_{h}=b_{L}(\delta+2f_{\rm NL}\phi_{p}\delta_{c}).$ (arXiv:0710.4560) [src: Eq. 7]. Poisson's equation then gives the Lagrangian correction $\Delta b(k)=2b_{L}f_{\rm NL}\delta_{c}\frac{3\Omega_{m}}{2ag(a)\,r_{H}^{2}k^{2}}\;,$ [src: Eq. 8], and the Eulerian bias $b=1+b_L$ [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 8] turns it into Eq. 9 (quoted in `formalism.md`). The derivation assumes peaks near threshold and neglects $|\nabla\phi|^{2}$; the authors confirm the result with N-body simulations [src: Sect. II, last sentence of the section].

### Route 2 — peak–background split with a modulated σ8 (Slosar et al. 2008)
<!-- key: route-2-peak-background-split-with-a-modulated -->

Slosar, Hirata, Seljak, Ho and Padmanabhan (arXiv:0805.3580) split the Gaussian potential into independent long and short parts, $\phi=\phi_{l}+\phi_{s},$ [src: Eq. 5], so that the non-Gaussian potential becomes $\Phi=\phi_{l}+f_{\rm NL}\phi_{l}^{2}+(1+2f_{\rm NL}\phi_{l})\phi_{s}+f_{\rm NL}\phi_{s}^{2}+{\rm const}.$ (arXiv:0805.3580) [src: Eq. 6]. With $\delta_{l}(k)=\alpha(k)\phi_{l}(k)$ and $\alpha(k)=\frac{2c^{2}k^{2}T(k)D(z)}{3\Omega_{m}H_{0}^{2}}.$ [src: Eqs. 7–8], the short modes inside a long-wavelength region are $\delta_{s}=\alpha\left[(1+2f_{\rm NL}\phi_{l})\phi_{s}+f_{\rm NL}\phi_{s}^{2}\right].$ [src: Eq. 9]: the long potential rescales the local amplitude of small-scale fluctuations by $X_{1}=1+2f_{\rm NL}\phi_{l}$ (arXiv:0805.3580) [src: Sect. II.1, inline, text after Eq. 10]. The halo number density then depends on $\delta_l$ and $X_1$, and the bias is $b_{L}(M,k)=\bar{n}^{-1}\left[\frac{\partial n}{\partial\delta_{l}({\bf x})}+2f_{\rm NL}\frac{{\rm d}\phi_{l}(k)}{{\rm d}\delta_{l}(k)}\frac{\partial n}{\partial X_{1}}\right],$ (arXiv:0805.3580) [src: Eq. 11]. Writing the rescaling as a local σ8, $\sigma_{8}^{\rm local}({\bf x})=\sigma_{8}X_{1}({\bf x}),$ [src: Eq. 12], this becomes $b_{L}(M,k)=b_{L}^{\rm Gaussian}(M)+2f_{\rm NL}\frac{{\rm d}\phi_{l}(k)}{{\rm d}\delta_{l}(k)}\frac{\partial\ln n}{\partial\ln\sigma_{8}^{\rm local}}.$ [src: Eq. 13], and, with the derivative of $\phi_l$ with respect to $\delta_l$ equal to the inverse of α (text after Eq. 13), $\Delta b(M,k)=\frac{3\Omega_{m}H_{0}^{2}}{c^{2}k^{2}T(k)D(z)}f_{\rm NL}\frac{\partial\ln n}{\partial\ln\sigma_{8}}.$ (arXiv:0805.3580) [src: Eq. 14].

Eq. 14 is the general result: it holds for any tracer and needs only the response of the tracer abundance to σ8 in Gaussian cosmologies [src: Sect. II.1, last paragraph]. The universality relation is a further step. For a universal mass function, $n(M)=n(M,\nu)=M^{-2}\nu f(\nu)\frac{{\rm d}\ln\nu}{{\rm d}\ln M},$ (arXiv:0805.3580) [src: Eq. 15], with $\nu=\delta_{c}^{2}/\sigma^{2}(M)$ (arXiv:0805.3580) [src: Sect. II.2, inline, text after Eq. 15], the Gaussian bias is $b=1-\frac{2}{\delta_{c}}\nu\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\nu}\ln[\nu f(\nu)].$ [src: Eq. 16] and the σ8 response is $\frac{\partial\ln n}{\partial\ln\sigma_{8}}=\frac{\partial\ln\nu}{\partial\ln\sigma_{8}}\frac{\partial\ln[\nu f(\nu)]}{\partial\ln\nu}=-2\nu\frac{d}{d\nu}\ln[\nu f(\nu)].$ [src: Eq. 17]; the same derivative of $\ln[\nu f(\nu)]$ appears in both, which gives $\Delta b(M,k)=3f_{\rm NL}(b-1)\delta_{c}\frac{\Omega_{m}}{k^{2}T(k)D(z)}\left(\frac{H_{0}}{c}\right)^{2}$ (arXiv:0805.3580) [src: Eq. 18], independent of the form of $f(\nu)$ and valid for any halo occupation that depends only on mass [src: Sect. II.2, text after Eq. 18]. For objects populating only recently merged halos the extended Press–Schechter calculation gives $\Delta b(M,k)=3f_{\rm NL}(b-p)\delta_{c}\frac{\Omega_{m}}{k^{2}T(k)D(z)}\left(\frac{H_{0}}{c}\right)^{2},$ (arXiv:0805.3580) [src: Sect. II.3.3, Eq. 31], with p = 1 for a fair sample of halos and $p=1+\delta_{c}^{-1}\sim 1.6$ for recent mergers [src: Sect. II.3.3, text after Eq. 31]. This is the origin of the parameter p used today.

The same two-line argument in the present notation (de S. Nascimento & Dalal 2026, arXiv:2607.18229): the separate-universe definition $b_{\phi}=2\frac{\partial\log n_{\textrm{g}}}{\partial\log\sigma_{8}}\,,$ [src: Eq. 1]; if the abundance depends on peak height alone, $b_{\phi}=-2\frac{d\log n_{\rm g}}{d\log\nu}\,.$ [src: Eq. 4] with $\nu=\delta_{\textrm{crit}}/\sigma_{R}$ in this paper (arXiv:2607.18229) [src: Sect. II, inline, text after Eq. 3]; a long mode lowers the threshold, giving $b_{1}^{L}\equiv\frac{\partial\log n_{\rm g}}{\partial\delta_{L}}=-\frac{1}{\delta_{\rm crit}}\frac{d\log n_{\rm g}}{d\log\nu}\,.$ (arXiv:2607.18229) [src: Eq. 5]; the ratio removes the unknown derivative and yields $b_{\phi}=2\delta_{\rm crit}(b_{1}-1)\,,$ (arXiv:2607.18229) [src: Eq. 6]. The peak height ν is defined as the square of the threshold over σ by Slosar et al. (arXiv:0805.3580) [src: Sect. II.2, text after Eq. 15] and as the threshold over $\sigma_R$ by de S. Nascimento and Dalal (arXiv:2607.18229) [src: Sect. II, text before Eq. 4]; derivatives with respect to ν are not interchangeable between the two papers. <!-- num: not a value -->

### Route 3 — separate universe with a modified primordial amplitude
<!-- key: route-3-separate-universe-with-a-modified -->

Local PNG modulates the small-scale primordial power by the long potential, $P_{\phi\phi}(k_{\tiny\rm short},z|\bm{x})=P_{\phi\phi}(k_{\tiny\rm short},z)\big[1+4f_{\textnormal{{nl}}}\phi(\bm{x})\big],$ (Barreira et al. 2020, arXiv:2006.09368) [src: Sect. 2.3, Eq. 13], citing Sect. 7.1.2 of the Desjacques et al. review (arXiv:1611.09787) [sec: arXiv:2006.09368, Sect. 2.3, text before Eq. 13]. Galaxies forming inside a long mode see a separate cosmology with $\tilde{{\cal A}}_{s}=\mathcal{A}_{s}\left[1+\delta\mathcal{A}_{s}\right],\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ {\rm with}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \delta\mathcal{A}_{s}=4f_{\textnormal{{nl}}}\phi_{L}$ (arXiv:2006.09368) [src: Eq. 14], so that $b_{\phi}(z,M)=4\frac{{\rm d}\ln n_{g}(z)}{{\rm d}\delta\mathcal{A}_{s}}\bigg|_{\delta\mathcal{A}_{s}=0};$ [src: Eq. 15], the factor 4 coming from the ratio between $\delta\mathcal{A}_s$ and $f_{\rm NL}\phi_L$ [src: Sect. 2.3, text after Eq. 15]. The derivative is a finite difference between simulations with Gaussian initial conditions and amplitude steps of +0.05 and −0.05 in the relative amplitude (arXiv:2006.09368) [src: Sect. 2.3, text before Eq. 15], combined as in its Eqs. 16–18; the authors judge the neglect of non-Gaussian initial conditions in $n_g$ unimportant given $f_{\textnormal{{nl}}}\lesssim 10$ [src: Sect. 2.3, last sentence]. Route 3 is Eq. 1 of arXiv:2607.18229 evaluated without any assumption on how $n_g$ depends on ν. `[der]`

Biagetti et al. verified this "consistency relation" for halos: the large-scale bias measured in simulations with local non-Gaussian initial conditions agrees with the response of the mass function to the primordial amplitude, for three halo finders (arXiv:1611.04901) [src: abstract].

### The power spectrum
<!-- key: the-power-spectrum -->

The scale-dependent term enters the galaxy power spectrum as $P_{gg}(k,z)=b_{1}^{2}P_{mm}(k,z)+2b_{1}b_{\phi}f_{\textnormal{{nl}}}P_{m\phi}(k,z)+b_{\phi}^{2}f_{\textnormal{{nl}}}^{2}P_{\phi\phi}(k)+P_{\epsilon\epsilon}(k)=\left[b_{1}^{2}+\frac{2b_{1}b_{\phi}f_{\textnormal{{nl}}}}{\mathcal{M}(k,z)}+\frac{b_{\phi}^{2}f_{\textnormal{{nl}}}^{2}}{\mathcal{M}(k,z)^{2}}\right]P_{mm}(k,z)+P_{\epsilon\epsilon},$ (arXiv:2006.09368) [src: Eq. 4], with $\mathcal{M}(k,z)=\frac{2}{3}\frac{k^{2}T_{m}(k,z)}{\Omega_{m0}H_{0}^{2}},$ [src: Eq. 5]. On scales where the transfer function is constant, $f_{\rm NL}$ produces corrections in $b_1b_\phi f_{\rm NL}/k^2$ and $b_\phi^2 f_{\rm NL}^2/k^4$ whose amplitude is completely degenerate with $b_\phi$ [src: Sect. 1, text after Eq. 5].

### The same term in the renormalised bias expansion
<!-- key: the-same-term-in-the-renormalised-bias-expansion -->

Assassi, Baumann and Schmidt (arXiv:1510.03723) treat general squeezed limits. They write the primordial potential as $\varphi({\boldsymbol{k}})=\varphi_{\rm G}({\boldsymbol{k}})+f_{{\rm NL}}\int_{{\boldsymbol{p}}}K_{{\rm NL}}({\boldsymbol{p}},{\boldsymbol{k}}-{\boldsymbol{p}})\big[\varphi_{\rm G}({\boldsymbol{p}})\varphi_{\rm G}({\boldsymbol{k}}-{\boldsymbol{p}})-P_{\rm G}(p)\,\hat{\delta}_{D}({\boldsymbol{k}})\big]+\cdots\,,$ [src: Eq. 2.11], expand the squeezed limit of the kernel in Legendre polynomials and powers $(k_\ell/k_s)^{\Delta}$ [src: Eq. 2.14], and compute the short-scale power in a long-mode background [src: Eq. 2.15]. For the isotropic (spin-0) part, $P_{\varphi}({\boldsymbol{k}}_{s}|{\boldsymbol{q}})=\Big[1+4\hskip 1.0pta_{0}f_{{\rm NL}}\hskip 1.0pt(\mu/k_{s})^{\Delta}\psi({\boldsymbol{q}})\Big]P_{\varphi}(k_{s})\,,$ with $\psi({\boldsymbol{k}})\equiv\left(\frac{k}{\mu}\right)^{\Delta}\varphi_{\rm G}^{\ell}({\boldsymbol{k}})\,.$ (arXiv:1510.03723) [src: Eqs. 2.16–2.17]; Δ = 0 is local PNG [src: Sect. 2.2, text before Eq. 2.16]. The bias coefficients become functions of ψ evaluated in Lagrangian space, and the expansion gains the terms $\delta_{g,\ell}=c_{0}+c_{\psi}\hskip 1.0pt\Psi+c_{\delta}\hskip 1.0pt\delta_{\ell}+c_{\psi\delta}\hskip 1.0pt\Psi\delta_{\ell}+c_{\delta^{2}}\hskip 1.0pt\delta^{2}_{\ell}+{c}_{\Pi^{2}}\hskip 1.0pt(\Pi_{ij}^{\ell})^{2}+\cdots\,,$ (arXiv:1510.03723) [src: Eq. 2.20], where the displacement term of Ψ (ψ evaluated at the Lagrangian position of the fluid element) can only appear together with ψ by the equivalence principle [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 2.21]. A spin-2 squeezed limit adds $\Psi^{ij}\Pi^{\ell}_{ij}$, visible in the bispectrum [src: Eq. 2.24].

The renormalisation step explains why $b_\phi$ is a free coefficient. With PNG the one-loop correlator of $\delta^{2}$ with one linear leg acquires the divergence $a_{0}f_{{\rm NL}}\sigma^{2}_{\Delta}(\Lambda)\,P_{1\psi}(k)$ as k goes to zero (arXiv:1510.03723) [src: Eq. 2.51], removed by $[\delta^{2}]^{\rm NG}=\delta^{2}-a_{0}f_{{\rm NL}}\sigma^{2}_{\Delta}(\Lambda)\Psi\,,$ (arXiv:1510.03723) [src: Eq. 2.53]. `[der]` Every composite operator therefore feeds the coefficient of Ψ, so in the EFT the coefficient of the PNG term is an independent bias parameter, and a relation such as universality is an input from a model of tracer formation, not a symmetry consequence. The bias-renormalisation machinery itself is in `deep/bias-renormalisation-and-bases.md`.

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **Peak shift (Route 1) against PBS (Route 2).** Route 1 assumes high peaks near threshold; Route 2 gives the general response formula (Eq. 14 of arXiv:0805.3580) and needs mass-function universality only for the final step (Eq. 18). Slosar et al. state that Eq. 18 is equivalent to previously derived expressions but more general, because it does not depend on the form of $f(\nu)$ (arXiv:0805.3580) [src: Sect. II.2, text after Eq. 18].
- **Separate universe (Route 3).** Measures $b_\phi$ for any selection, including hydrodynamical galaxies, at the cost of one pair of simulations per amplitude step (arXiv:2006.09368) [src: Sect. 2.3]. With IllustrisTNG it gives $2\delta_{c}(b_{1}-p)$ with $p\in[0.4,0.7]$ for stellar-mass-selected galaxies, traced to different responses of the stellar-to-halo-mass relation to δ and to $f_{\rm NL}\phi$ [src: Sect. 1, second-to-last paragraph].
- **Time-evolution estimator.** If the abundance depends on time only through the local amplitude $\sigma_R$, which grows as the linear growth factor D, then $2\frac{d\log n_{\rm g}}{d\log D}=2\frac{\partial\log n_{\textrm{g}}}{\partial\log\sigma_{R}}=b_{\phi}\,.$ (de S. Nascimento & Dalal 2026, arXiv:2607.18229) [src: Eq. 8]; in general $\frac{d\log n_{\rm g}}{d\log D}=\frac{b_{\phi}}{2}+\left.\frac{\partial\log n_{\rm g}}{\partial\log D}\right|_{\sigma_{R}}\,.$ [src: Eq. 9], whose left side is the evolution bias of relativistic number counts [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 9]. The second term must be corrected; for observer-frame photometric selections this needs correction factors from galaxy SEDs and, for magnitude cuts, passive fading, after which the estimator agrees with the separate-universe one in CAMELS [src: abstract].
- **Free p with a simulation prior.** For DESI-like quasars in IllustrisTNG the distribution of p has mean $\bar{p}\simeq1.4$ with weak redshift dependence, robust in CAMELS, giving $f_{\rm NL}=-3.3\pm9.2$ with DESI DR1 (Fondi et al. 2026, arXiv:2602.12357) [src: abstract, inline].

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Universality assumed for non-mass selections.** The relation holds only if the abundance is a function of ν alone; secondary halo properties and the response of the occupation itself at fixed halo mass break it (PNG assembly bias, "HOD response") (arXiv:2607.18229) [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 6, footnote 7]. Halo concentration moves $b_1$ and $b_\phi$ in opposite directions and can change the sign of $b_\phi$ (arXiv:2209.07251) [src: abstract]. Check: the $b_\phi$ prior is calibrated on simulations with the sample's selection, or $f_{\rm NL}b_\phi$ is quoted instead of $f_{\rm NL}$. `[der]`
- **The universal mass function is only approximately universal.** Even for halos, the prediction proportional to the Gaussian Lagrangian bias does not agree well with simulations, and the sign of the deviation depends on mass and halo finder (arXiv:1611.04901) [src: abstract].
- **Lagrangian against Eulerian bias.** The factor is $b-1$ (or $b-p$), not b (arXiv:0710.4560, Eq. 9; arXiv:0805.3580, Eq. 18); see `approximations.md#convention-traps`. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Growth-factor and transfer-function conventions.** Dalal et al. write the result with $g(a)$ and $r_H$, Slosar et al. with $D(z)$ normalised to $(1+z)^{-1}$ in matter domination and $T(k)$, Barreira et al. with $T_m(k,z)$ (arXiv:0710.4560, Eq. 9; arXiv:0805.3580, Eq. 18; arXiv:2006.09368, Eq. 5). <!-- num: not a value --> An earlier version of Dalal et al. omitted the factor g(a) by not distinguishing primordial from late-time potential (arXiv:0710.4560) [src: Sect. II, footnote 1]: check which normalisation of D enters before comparing amplitudes.
- **Relativistic projection effects.** They are degenerate with the scale-dependent bias; ignoring integrated and local relativistic terms and wide-separation corrections biases $f_{\rm NL}$ by around $ 3\,σ$ for a Euclid-like survey and $ 20 \, σ$ for a MegaMapper-like survey in a **forecast** (Addis et al. 2025, arXiv:2511.09466) [src: abstract]. The relativistic terms belong to `thcosmo-relativistic-cosmology`.
- **Equilateral and other shapes.** For Δ = 2 the field Ψ scales like δ on large scales and the two are indistinguishable there, differing only through the transfer function on intermediate scales, where higher-derivative Gaussian operators have similar scale dependence (arXiv:1510.03723) [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 2.21]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Mean-density shift.** Terms of order $f_{\rm NL}$ in the mean abundance give a scale-independent correction absorbed by the Gaussian bias (arXiv:0805.3580) [src: Sect. II.1, text after Eq. 13]; they do not affect the $k^{-2}$ term.
