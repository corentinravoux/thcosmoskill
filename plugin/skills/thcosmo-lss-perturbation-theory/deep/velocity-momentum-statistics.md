## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- **Density-weighted velocity moments.** Seljak and McDonald write the redshift-space density as a sum over velocity moments of the phase-space distribution function, density weighted, whose lowest orders are density, momentum density and stress-energy density (arXiv:1109.1888) [src: abstract]. Vlah et al. define the moments along a unit vector **h** (Eq. 6) and show that, when velocity dispersion and anisotropic stress are neglected, they reduce to $T^{L}_{\textbf{h}}(\textbf{x})=\left(1+\delta(\textbf{x})\right)\left(\textbf{h}\cdot\textbf{v}(\textbf{x})\right)^{L},$ (arXiv:1207.0839) [src: Eq. 7]. The momentum field is the first moment. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Fourier sign.** Vlah et al. put $\exp(+i\textbf{k}\cdot\textbf{x})$ in the forward transform and $(2\pi)^{-3}$ with $\exp(-i\textbf{k}\cdot\textbf{x})$ in the inverse (arXiv:1207.0839) [src: Eq. 8], the opposite sign of the exponent to Ivanov's review (`formalism.md#conventions`). <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Velocity divergence.** In Vlah et al. θ is dimensional, the linear spectrum being $(f\mathcal{H})^{2}P_{L}(k)$ (arXiv:1207.0839) [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 48]; Ivanov's Θ divides by $f\mathcal{H}$ (`formalism.md#conventions`). <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Mass against volume weighting.** In the EFT the momentum is defined by the linear continuity equation, $\partial_{\tau}\delta+\mu = 0\,,$ with μ the divergence of the momentum over the mean density (Mercolli & Pajer 2014, arXiv:1307.3220) [src: Sect. 3.1, Eq. 39]; the velocity is then the mass-weighted velocity, momentum over density plus a prescription for this composite operator, as opposed to the volume-weighted velocity; only the mass-weighted velocity is used in the EFTofLSS literature [src: Sect. 3.1, text after Eq. 40].
- **Velocity statistics of tracers.** Chen, Howlett, Lai and Qin define the spectra of powers of the line-of-sight galaxy velocity, density weighted, as $P^{s}_{LL^{\prime}}(\textbf{k})=\int d^{3}\textbf{s}\ e^{i\textbf{k}\cdot\textbf{s}}\left\langle(1+\delta_{g,s}(\textbf{s}_{1}))(1+\delta_{g,s}(\textbf{s}_{2}))\ \boldsymbol{u}_{s,\hat{n}}^{L}(\textbf{s}_{1})\boldsymbol{u}_{s,\hat{n}}^{L^{\prime}}(\textbf{s}_{2})\right\rangle_{\textbf{s}=\textbf{s}_{1}-\textbf{s}_{2}}$ (arXiv:2508.00066) [src: Eq. 4], following Gorski et al. (1989), Groth et al. (1989) and Park (2000) [src: Sect. 2.2, text before Eq. 4]. The density–momentum cross spectrum has L and L′ equal to 1 and 0, and the momentum autospectrum both equal to 1 [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 5]. <!-- num: not a value -->

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — momentum and pairwise velocities as moments of the redshift-space spectrum
<!-- key: step-1-momentum-and-pairwise-velocities-as -->

Scoccimarro (arXiv:astro-ph/0407214) derived the exact relation, including all non-linearities, between real- and redshift-space two-point statistics through the pairwise velocity distribution function [src: abstract]. Seljak and McDonald expanded the density-weighted velocity moments into helicity modes and showed that only equal-helicity moments correlate; on large scales the dominant $\mu^{2}$ term is the density–scalar-momentum cross-correlation (arXiv:1109.1888) [src: abstract].

In the notation of Chen et al. (arXiv:2508.00066), the density-weighted pairwise velocity spectra $\tilde{\Xi}^{(n)}_s$ (Eq. 6) are linear combinations of the $P_{LL'}$ whose indices sum to n [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 6]; the first two are $v_{s}(\textbf{k})=\tilde{\Xi}^{(1)}_{s}=2P^{s}_{01},\quad\sigma^{2}_{s}(\textbf{k})=\tilde{\Xi}^{(2)}_{s}=2P^{s}_{02}-2P_{11},$ (arXiv:2508.00066) [src: Eq. 7]. The redshift-space power spectrum with the velocity scaled by λ is their generating function, $\tilde{\Xi}_{s}^{(n)}(\textbf{k})=\frac{1}{(ik\mu)^{n}}\left(\frac{d^{n}P_{s}(\textbf{k};\lambda)}{d\lambda^{n}}\right)_{\lambda=1}.$ (arXiv:2508.00066) [src: Eq. 8], following Sugiyama et al. (2016); the real-space spectra follow at vanishing λ (Scoccimarro 2004), and conversely $P_{s}(\textbf{k};\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(ik\mu)^{n}\lambda^{n}}{n!}\tilde{\Xi}^{(n)}(\textbf{k}).$ (arXiv:2508.00066) [src: Eq. 9], the moment expansion of Seljak and McDonald [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 9]. Because these spectra depend only on pairwise velocities, they are Galilean invariant in the flat-sky limit [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 7]. `[der]` A model of the redshift-space power spectrum with the λ dependence kept therefore fixes every velocity statistic at the same order.

### Step 2 — one-loop SPT for the momentum autospectrum
<!-- key: step-2-one-loop-spt-for-the-momentum -->

Vlah, Seljak, McDonald, Okumura and Baldauf (arXiv:1207.0839) compute each moment correlator in SPT. The momentum autospectrum has a scalar (helicity 0) and a vector (helicity ±1) part, and its contribution to the redshift-space spectrum is $P^{ss}_{11}(k)=\mathcal{H}^{-2}k^{2}\mu^{2}\left[P^{1,1,0}_{1,1}(k)\mu^{2}+P^{1,1,1}_{1,1}(k)(1-\mu^{2})\right].$ (arXiv:1207.0839) [src: Eq. 45]. In linear theory only the scalar part survives and equals the last term of the Kaiser formula; the vector part enters at second order, computable at one loop, and is called the vorticity part of the momentum because the vorticity of the momentum does not vanish even when that of the velocity does for a single-stream fluid [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 45]. It always adds power to the $\mu^{2}$ term and subtracts it from the $\mu^{4}$ term [src: same place].

Expanding the momentum in density and velocity divergence gives $P_{11}(\textbf{k})=\frac{\mu^{2}}{k^{2}}P_{\theta\theta}(\textbf{k})+2\frac{\mu}{k}B_{11}(\textbf{k})+C_{11}(\textbf{k}),$ (arXiv:1207.0839) [src: Eq. 46], with $B_{11}$ and $C_{11}$ the correlators of θδ with θ and with itself [src: Eq. 47]. The first term is the one-loop velocity-divergence spectrum, built from $G_2$ and $G_3$ [src: Eqs. 48–49]; the others bring terms in $\sigma_v^2$ and in the integrals $I_{nm}$, $J_{nm}$ of the paper's Appendix D [src: Eqs. 50–52], collected in Eq. 53. Which of these terms SPT reproduces and which need small-scale velocity-dispersion terms beyond PT is the subject of the paper's Sect. 3 (see "What goes wrong").

### Step 3 — what mass and momentum conservation impose (EFT)
<!-- key: step-3-what-mass-and-momentum-conservation -->

Mercolli and Pajer (arXiv:1307.3220) derive the low-k behaviour of short-scale contributions. The momentum carries one power of k as k goes to zero, so the short-scale parts of its divergence μ and curl ν satisfy $\langle\mu_{s}\mu_{s}\rangle\sim\langle{\bm{\nu}}_{s}\cdot{\bm{\nu}}_{s}\rangle\sim k^{4}\,,$ (arXiv:1307.3220) [src: Eq. 42]. The derivatives of the momentum are not Galilean invariant and carry uncancelled IR divergences, so the authors move to the mass-weighted velocity and show, for equal-mass particles and with the cutoff removed, that $\langle\theta_{s}\theta_{s}\rangle\sim\langle\bm{\omega}_{s}\cdot\bm{\omega}_{s}\rangle\sim k^{4}\,,$ (arXiv:1307.3220) [src: Sect. 3.2, Eq. 43]. The relation between momentum and velocity is corrected, once short modes are integrated out, by an infinite series of operators in powers of perturbations and derivatives, because $\delta\,\mathbf{v}$ is a composite operator [src: Eq. 40 and text after it]; cancelling all divergences of velocity correlators requires new counterterms, which fix the definition of the velocity rather than adding physics [src: abstract].

**Vorticity.** Primordial vorticity decays, and in SPT the one-loop correction is at most constant in time; in the EFT the noise term of the stress tensor sources vorticity at leading order [src: Sect. 4.4, first two paragraphs]. With a second contribution from stress-tensor terms quadratic in δ, the dimensionless vorticity correlator takes the form $\mathcal{I}_{\omega\omega}\;=\;\gamma_{\omega\omega}\left(\frac{k}{k_{\mathrm{NL}}}\right)^{7}+\beta_{\omega\omega}\left(\frac{k}{k_{\mathrm{NL}}}\right)^{10+2n}+...\;,$ (arXiv:1307.3220) [src: Sect. 4.4, Eq. 86], for an EdS scaling universe, n being the power-law index of the paper's scaling spectrum; the leading vorticity spectrum is therefore entirely induced by the effective stress tensor, and in ΛCDM the wavenumber dependences $P_{\omega\omega}\sim k^{4}$ and $P_{\omega\omega}\sim k^{7+2n}$ are expected to be the same, with small changes in time dependence for $z\gtrsim 1$ [src: Sect. 4.4, text after Eq. 86].

Pueblas and Scoccimarro (arXiv:0809.4606) measured the vorticity spectrum with Delaunay tessellations and fitted its large-scale growth as $P_{w}(k,z)\propto[D_{+}(z)]^{n_{w}}.$ [src: Sect. II.3, Eq. 3], with $n_{w}=7\pm 0.3$ [src: Sect. II.3, text after Eq. 3]. They argue qualitatively that orbit mixing produces a vorticity whose low-k spectrum scales as $k^{2}$ times the square of the orbit-crossing volume fraction times $D_+^6$ [src: Sect. II.3, Eq. 4 and the unnumbered display after it], and that vector modes have very little effect on the density spectrum at k~0.1 h/Mpc while velocity dispersion can induce percent-level corrections at z=0, particularly in the velocity-divergence spectrum [src: abstract]. The equations of motion with vorticity as a forcing term are their Eqs. 52–53 (arXiv:0809.4606) [src: Sect. IV.3]. The vorticity in the two-loop matter counterterms and in the matter–momentum bispectrum is summarised in `formalism.md#velocity-statistics-and-the-momentum-field`.

### Step 4 — infrared resummation adapted to velocities
<!-- key: step-4-infrared-resummation-adapted-to -->

`formalism.md#velocity-statistics-and-the-momentum-field` records that momentum spectra are more sensitive to bulk flows than the density spectrum (Senatore & Zaldarriaga 2014, arXiv:1404.5954). Chen et al. (arXiv:2508.00066) derive the resummation for pairwise velocities. For the first moment, Wick contractions of linear displacements are of two kinds: those that factor out of the density product and give the usual Gaussian damping, and those in which the correlated velocity interacts with the displacements [src: Sect. 4.2.2, Eq. 37 and text after it]. Summing the second kind gives a term proportional to $ifk\mu(1+f)\Sigma_{d}^{2}$ times the damped wiggle part of the power spectrum [src: Eq. 38], and the full wiggle part of the mean pairwise velocity is $v_{s,w}(\textbf{k})=e^{-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}\Sigma^{2}_{d}(\mu)}\llbracket v_{s}(\textbf{k})\rrbracket_{w}+ifk\mu(1+f)\Sigma_{d}^{2}\ e^{-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}\Sigma^{2}_{d}(\mu)}\llbracket P_{s}(\textbf{k})\rrbracket_{w}.$ (arXiv:2508.00066) [src: Eq. 39]. Earlier models, labelled C21 in the paper, resummed only the exponentially damped diagrams, $v_{s,w}^{\rm C21}(\textbf{k})=e^{-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}\Sigma^{2}_{d}(\mu)}\{v_{s}(\textbf{k})\}_{w},\quad\{v_{s}(\textbf{k})\}_{w}=v^{w}_{s,\rm lin}(\textbf{k})+v^{w}_{s,\rm loop}(\textbf{k})+\frac{1}{2}k^{2}\Sigma^{2}(\mu)v^{w}_{s,\rm lin}(\textbf{k})$ (arXiv:2508.00066) [src: Eq. 41], neglecting the enhanced diagrams multiplying the wiggle power spectrum [src: text after Eq. 41]. The scheme follows from differentiating the resummed power spectrum with respect to λ, and the second moment is built the same way, recursively [src: Sect. 4.2.2, text after Eqs. 40 and 42]. In the Zel'dovich approximation the new scheme agrees with the exact prediction where linear theory and the C21 scheme deviate at fairly large scales [src: Fig. 1 caption].

### Step 5 — counterterms and stochastic terms of the velocity statistics
<!-- key: step-5-counterterms-and-stochastic-terms-of-the -->

At one loop the counterterms of the redshift-space power spectrum, with the λ dependence kept, are $P_{s}(\textbf{k};\lambda)\supset 2\ \big(1+b_{\delta}+\lambda f\mu^{2}\big)\Big(\beta_{0}^{0}+(\lambda f\beta_{2}^{1}+\lambda^{2}f^{2}\beta_{2}^{2})\mu^{2}+(\lambda^{2}f^{2}\beta_{4}^{2}+\lambda^{3}f^{3}\beta_{4}^{3})\mu^{4}\Big)\ k^{2}P_{\rm L}(k),$ (arXiv:2508.00066) [src: Sect. 3.3, Eq. 18]. There are 5 independent counterterms in the generating function and only 3 in the power spectrum itself, so all one-loop pairwise velocity spectra, in real and redshift space, share the same 5 [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 18]. The stochastic terms are $P_{s}(\textbf{k};\lambda)\supset s_{0}^{0}+s_{0}^{2}k^{2}+\big(\lambda fs_{2}^{1}+\lambda^{2}f^{2}s_{2}^{2}\big)(k\mu)^{2}.$ (arXiv:2508.00066) [src: Eq. 19], the anisotropic ones describing stochasticity from small-scale velocities [src: text after Eq. 19]. Fingers of god enter beyond one loop through the terms of Eq. 20, because the velocity dispersion converted to a distance can exceed the inverse non-linear scale [src: Sect. 3.3, text before Eq. 20]. Counterterms degenerate in the power spectrum have distinct λ dependence and become separate parameters for the velocity statistics [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 8].

### Step 6 — velocity bias
<!-- key: step-6-velocity-bias -->

In the peak model the large-scale limit of the peak correlation and pairwise velocity corresponds to the mean-field relation for the velocity, giving $\displaystyle\bm{v}_{\text{h}}(\bm{k})/W_{R}(k)=b_{v}(k)\bm{v}_{\text{m}}(\bm{k})=\left(1-R_{v}^{2}k^{2}\right)\bm{v}_{\text{m}}(\bm{k})\,,$ (Baldauf, Desjacques & Seljak 2015, arXiv:1405.5885) [src: Eq. 2], with $R_{v}^{2}=\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}.$ [src: Appendix A, Eq. 17], independent of the collapse threshold [src: text after Eq. 17]. The velocity bias is unity on large scales, as mass and momentum conservation require [src: Sect. IV]. The standard co-evolution formalism predicts that an initial velocity bias decays rapidly; modifying the coupling in the momentum-conservation equation of tracers instead keeps the statistical velocity bias constant in time, in agreement with peak theory and with the evolution of a conserved halo population in N-body simulations (arXiv:1405.5885) [src: abstract]. In the bias expansion, velocity bias brings new contributions, a dipole field being its signature at second order (Chan, Scoccimarro & Sheth 2012, arXiv:1201.3614) [src: abstract].

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **SPT moment by moment (distribution-function approach).** Systematic in the moments and valid in multi-stream situations, with an expansion convergent on large scales (arXiv:1109.1888) [src: abstract]; several terms need small-scale dispersion terms modelled with the halo model (arXiv:1207.0839) [src: abstract].
- **Lagrangian and Eulerian EFT of pairwise velocities.** Chen et al. compute the statistics up to the second moment at one loop in both frameworks, with the same counterterms as the redshift-space density (Step 5) and the recursive IR resummation (Step 4) (arXiv:2508.00066) [src: Sects. 3–4]; against N-body simulations they recover the growth rate to better than percent precision (`results.md#validity-ranges-against-simulations`).
- **Momentum power spectrum from peculiar-velocity surveys.** Howlett derived an estimator of the redshift-space momentum power spectrum with shot noise, survey geometry and optimal weights, and theoretical descriptions of the non-linear density and momentum multipoles (arXiv:1906.02875) [src: abstract]; the model details were not read here. Applications belong to cosmoskill `cosmo-peculiar-velocities`.
- **Velocity field from simulations.** Delaunay tessellation gives unbiased divergence and vorticity spectra with better noise than interpolation of mass-weighted velocities (arXiv:0809.4606) [src: abstract]; the EFT works with the mass-weighted velocity and discusses the volume-weighted one only in its Appendix C.2 (arXiv:1307.3220) [src: Sect. 3.1, last sentence]. A comparison of a perturbative velocity spectrum with a simulation must use the same weighting. `[der]`

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Linear theory for velocities on large scales.** The large-scale evolution of velocity fields is not well described by linear theory (arXiv:astro-ph/0407214) [src: abstract]; the velocity power spectrum stays below the linear prediction until well into the non-linear regime, with little contribution from virial velocities (arXiv:0809.4606) [src: abstract]. Check: a velocity or momentum model goes beyond linear theory at the scales fitted.
- **Single-stream PT and vorticity.** Vorticity and velocity dispersion are generated by orbit crossing, which the pressureless perfect fluid excludes (arXiv:0809.4606) [src: abstract]; in the EFT the leading vorticity spectrum comes entirely from the effective stress tensor (arXiv:1307.3220) [src: Sect. 4.4, text after Eq. 86], so no SPT calculation predicts its amplitude. See `approximations.md#pressureless-perfect-fluid-single-stream`.
- **Spurious vorticity in simulations.** Poor resolution can amplify the large-scale vorticity spectrum by more than an order of magnitude (arXiv:0809.4606) [src: abstract]. `[der]` A vorticity measurement used to validate PT therefore needs a resolution test.
- **Fingers of god in the higher moments.** Most of the terms of the moment expansion that SPT misses are tied to small-scale velocity dispersion, which affects some but not all terms; several dispersions with very different amplitudes enter (arXiv:1207.0839) [src: abstract]. In the EFT these are the λ-dependent counterterms and the beyond-one-loop terms of Eq. 20 of arXiv:2508.00066 [src: Sect. 3.3].
- **IR sensitivity of the momentum.** Momentum derivatives are not Galilean invariant and have uncancelled IR divergences (arXiv:1307.3220) [src: Sect. 3.2]; resumming only the exponentially damped diagrams, as in earlier velocity models, leaves the BAO in pairwise velocities mis-modelled (arXiv:2508.00066) [src: Sect. 4.2.2, text after Eq. 41; Fig. 1].
- **Non-Gaussian pairwise velocities.** The pairwise velocity PDF is strongly non-Gaussian at all scales, a commonly used ansatz for the redshift-space spectrum implies an unphysical pairwise-velocity distribution, and the distribution cannot in general be recovered from redshift-space clustering (arXiv:astro-ph/0407214) [src: abstract].
- **Decaying against constant velocity bias.** Co-evolution with the standard momentum equation makes velocity bias decay, which the halo simulations of Baldauf et al. contradict (arXiv:1405.5885) [src: abstract; Sect. IV].
- **Unequal particle masses.** The $k^{4}$ argument for the velocity uses equal particle masses; unequal masses "require further consideration" (arXiv:1307.3220) [src: Sect. 3.2, text before Eq. 43].
