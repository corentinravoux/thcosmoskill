## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- The exact mapping in Ivanov's convention (Eqs. 124–127) and the TNS model are in `formalism.md#redshift-space-distortions` and `models.md#tns-model`; the phenomenological fingers of god in `approximations.md#phenomenological-fingers-of-god`. Not repeated here.
- Velocity variable: Bernardeau et al. write the mapping with ${\hbox{\BF v}}({\bf x})\equiv-{\bf u}({\bf x})/({\cal H}f)$, minus the peculiar velocity in units of $\mathcal{H}f$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Sect. 7.4.1, inline, text after Eq. 613]; Senatore and Zaldarriaga use the peculiar velocity itself divided by aH (arXiv:1409.1225) [src: Eq. 2]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- Fourier sign: Senatore and Zaldarriaga use $e^{-i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}$ in the forward transform with $\exp[-ik_{z}v_{z}/(aH)]$ in the mapping (arXiv:1409.1225) [src: Eq. 5]; Ivanov uses $e^{i{\bf k}{\bf x}}$ with $\exp\{ik^{i}\hat{z}_{i}(v^{j}\hat{z}_{j})/\mathcal{H}\}$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 126]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- All papers below use the plane-parallel (distant-observer) approximation with a fixed line of sight ẑ and μ the cosine between k and ẑ; wide-angle and relativistic terms belong to `thcosmo-relativistic-cosmology`.
- Normalisation of the counterterms differs between papers and codes: Senatore and Zaldarriaga write them in units of $(k/k_{\rm NL})^{2}$ with order-one coefficients after extracting a factor $(2\pi)D^{2}$ (arXiv:1409.1225) [src: Sect. 2.1, text after Eq. 21]; Chudaykin et al. (CLASS-PT) quote $\tilde{c}_{0},\tilde{c}_{2},\tilde{c}_{4}$ in units of $[\text{Mpc}/h]^{2}$ (arXiv:2004.10607) [src: Sect. 2.4, text after Eq. 2.15]; the LPT model of Chen et al. multiplies its counterterms by the Zel'dovich spectrum, the other DESI codes by the linear one, which differ at high k by formally two-loop terms (Maus et al., arXiv:2404.07272) [src: Sect. 3.6]. A counterterm value is therefore quoted with its code. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Bias basis: the kernels of Bernardeau et al. use local bias $b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}$; CLASS-PT uses the Galileon basis of `formalism.md#bias-expansion` ($b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}$, $b_{\Gamma_{3}}$). Conversions are in the hub atlas.

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1: the mapping and its expansion
<!-- key: step-1-the-mapping-and-its-expansion -->

Mass conservation, $(1+\delta_{s}){\rm d}^{3}{\bf s}=(1+\delta){\rm d}^{3}{\bf x}~.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Eq. 614], gives the fully non-linear plane-parallel expression $\delta_{s}({\bf k})=\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}{\bf x}}{(2\pi)^{3}}{\rm e}^{-i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf x}}{\rm e}^{ifk_{z}{\hbox{$v_{z}$}}({\bf x})}\Big[\delta({\bf x})+f\nabla_{z}{\hbox{$v_{z}$}}({\bf x})\Big]~,$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Eq. 616], valid where $f\nabla_{z}v_{z}<1$, that is away from caustics [src: Sect. 7.4.1, text after Eq. 616]. <!-- eq: notation --> Expanding the second exponential in a power series gives $\delta_{s}({\bf k})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int{\rm d}^{3}{\bf k}_{1}\ldots{\rm d}^{3}{\bf k}_{n}[\delta_{\rm D}]_{n}\Big[\delta({\bf k}_{1})+f\mu_{1}^{2}\theta({\bf k}_{1})\Big]\frac{(f\mu k)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\times\frac{\mu_{2}}{k_{2}}\theta({\bf k}_{2})\ldots\frac{\mu_{n}}{k_{n}}\theta({\bf k}_{n}),$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Eq. 617], whose n = 1 term is Kaiser's $\delta_{s}({\bf k})=\delta({\bf k})(1+f\mu^{2}).$ [src: Eq. 618]. Senatore and Zaldarriaga make the same Taylor expansion, keeping terms to cubic order for a one-loop calculation (arXiv:1409.1225) [src: Sect. 2.1, Eq. 6].

### Step 2: the redshift-space kernels
<!-- key: step-2-the-redshift-space-kernels -->

Writing $\delta_{s}$ as a sum of convolutions of linear fields with kernels $Z_{n}$, and assuming $D_{n}\propto D_{1}^{n}$, gives for local bias $Z_{1}({\bf k})=(b_{1}+f\mu^{2}),$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Eq. 620], $Z_{2}({\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2})=b_{1}F_{2}({\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2})+f\mu^{2}G_{2}({\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2})+\frac{f\mu k}{2}\Big[\frac{\mu_{1}}{k_{1}}(b_{1}+f\mu_{2}^{2})+\frac{\mu_{2}}{k_{2}}(b_{1}+f\mu_{1}^{2})\Big]+\frac{b_{2}}{2},$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Eq. 621] and $Z_{3}({\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2},{\bf k}_{3})=b_{1}F_{3}^{(s)}({\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2},{\bf k}_{3})+f\mu^{2}G_{3}^{(s)}({\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2},{\bf k}_{3})$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Eq. 622, numbered row], continued in the same display by $+f\mu k\Big[b_{1}F_{2}^{(s)}({\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2})+f\mu_{12}^{2}G_{2}^{(s)}({\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2})\Big]\frac{\mu_{3}}{k_{3}}+f\mu k(b_{1}+f\mu_{1}^{2})\frac{\mu_{23}}{k_{23}}G_{2}^{(s)}({\bf k}_{2},{\bf k}_{3})+\frac{(f\mu k)^{2}}{2}(b_{1}+f\mu_{1}^{2})\frac{\mu_{2}}{k_{2}}\frac{\mu_{3}}{k_{3}}+3b_{2}F_{2}^{(s)}({\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2})+\frac{b_{3}}{6},$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Sect. 7.4.1, unnumbered continuation rows of the same display], where μ refers to the total wave vector, $\mu_{i}$ to each argument, and $Z_{3}$ must still be symmetrised [src: Sect. 7.4.1, text after Eq. 622].

In the Galileon basis, CLASS-PT uses $Z_{1}({\bf k})=b_{1}+f\mu^{2}\,,$ (arXiv:2004.10607) [src: Eq. 2.14a] and $Z_{2}({\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2})=\frac{b_{2}}{2}+b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\left(\frac{({\bf k}_{1}\cdot{\bf k}_{2})^{2}}{k_{1}^{2}k_{2}^{2}}-1\right)+b_{1}F_{2}({\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2})+f\mu^{2}G_{2}({\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2})\penalty\ \penalty\ +\frac{f\mu k}{2}\left(\frac{\mu_{1}}{k_{1}}(b_{1}+f\mu_{2}^{2})+\frac{\mu_{2}}{k_{2}}(b_{1}+f\mu_{1}^{2})\right)\,,$ (arXiv:2004.10607) [src: Eq. 2.14b]; its $Z_{3}$ (Eq. 2.14c) keeps only the bias operators that contribute to the one-loop power spectrum, $b_{\Gamma_{3}}$ included, and must be symmetrised [src: Sect. 2.4, text after Eq. 2.14c]. Perko et al. give the same structure for halos, with separate kernels for the halo density and the halo velocity divergence (arXiv:1610.09321) [src: Sect. 3.3, Eq. 3.23].

With these kernels the deterministic one-loop spectrum is $\begin{split}P_{\rm gg,RSD}(z,k,\mu)=&Z^{2}_{1}({\bf k})P_{\text{lin}}(z,k)+2\int_{{\bf q}}Z^{2}_{2}({\bf q},{\bf k}-{\bf q})P_{\text{lin}}(z,|{\bf k}-{\bf q}|)P_{\text{lin}}(z,q)\\ &+6Z_{1}({\bf k})P_{\text{lin}}(z,k)\int_{{\bf q}}Z_{3}({\bf q},-{\bf q},{\bf k})P_{\text{lin}}(z,q)\\ &+P_{\text{ctr,RSD}}(z,k,\mu)+P_{\epsilon\epsilon,\text{RSD}}(z,k,\mu)\,,\end{split}$ (arXiv:2004.10607) [src: Eq. 2.13], where the last two terms are fixed in Steps 3–5.

Bernardeau et al. warn that the one-loop spectrum computed from Eqs. 620–622 alone "does not give satisfactory results", because the power-series expansion of the exponential is a poor approximation and breaks down at larger scales than the dynamics (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Sect. 7.4.1, text after Eq. 622]. The EFT answer is the next step: the expansion is kept, and its UV sensitivity is absorbed by counterterms.

### Step 3: counterterms of the contact operators (matter)
<!-- key: step-3-counterterms-of-the-contact-operators -->

1. The expanded mapping contains products of fields at the same point, $[v_{z}^{2}]$, $[v_{z}^{3}]$, $[v_{z}^{2}\delta]$, and the velocity is the ratio of momentum and density, $v^{i}(\vec{x},t)=\frac{\pi^{i}(\vec{x},t)}{\rho(\vec{x},t)}\ .$ (arXiv:1409.1225) [src: Eq. 7], a ratio not controlled below the non-linear scale [src: Sect. 2.1, text after Eq. 7]. At one loop the renormalised velocity is $v_{R}^{i}(\vec{x},t)=v^{i}(\vec{x},t)-c_{v,\delta}(t)\,\frac{H}{{k_{\rm NL}}}\frac{{\partial}^{i}}{{k_{\rm NL}}}\delta(\vec{x},t)+\ldots\ .$ (arXiv:1409.1225) [src: Eq. 10], the time non-locality being degenerate with a local term at this order.
2. Rewriting the linear term through the momentum, which is renormalised by the same counterterms as δ, and splitting the momentum into scalar and vector parts, the vector part starts at cubic order and does not correlate with a linear field by parity, so it needs no counterterm at one loop [src: Sect. 2.1, Eqs. 11–13].
3. The equivalence principle allows counterterms built from $\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\phi$ and $\partial_{i}v_{j}$ only; at linear level the first contact operator is renormalised as $[v_{z}^{2}]_{R,{\vec{k}}}=\hat{z}^{i}\hat{z}^{j}\left\{[v_{i}v_{j}]_{{\vec{k}}}+\left(\frac{aH}{{k_{\rm NL}}}\right)^{2}\left[c_{1}\delta^{ij}+\left(c_{2}\delta^{ij}+c_{3}\frac{k^{i}k^{j}}{k^{2}}\right)\delta({\vec{k}})\right]+\ldots\right\}=[v_{z}^{2}]_{\vec{k}}+\left(\frac{aH}{{k_{\rm NL}}}\right)^{2}\left[c_{1}+c_{2}\,\delta({\vec{k}})\right]+\left(\frac{aH}{{k_{\rm NL}}}\right)^{2}c_{3}\,\frac{k_{z}^{2}}{k^{2}}\,\delta({\vec{k}})+\ldots\ ,$ (arXiv:1409.1225) [src: Eq. 15], and $[v_{z}^{3}]$ and $[v_{z}^{2}\delta]$ need only their vacuum expectation values, both with the same $c_{1}$ [src: Eq. 15]. Stochastic counterterms of these operators contribute as $(k/k_{\rm NL})^{4}$ and are negligible at one loop [src: Sect. 2.1, footnote 1].
4. The renormalised density in redshift space is then $\delta_{r}(\vec{k})=\delta(\vec{k})+\mu^{2}\,\frac{\dot{\delta}(\vec{k})}{H}-\epsilon^{zij}\frac{\mu\,k_{i}}{k}\frac{\pi_{V,j}}{H}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{k\,\mu}{aH}\right)^{2}\left([v_{z}^{2}]_{\vec{k}}+[v_{z}^{2}\delta]_{\vec{k}}\right)+\frac{i}{6}\left(\frac{k\,\mu}{aH}\right)^{3}[v_{z}^{3}]_{\vec{k}}+\frac{c_{1}}{2}\mu^{4}\left(\frac{k}{{k_{\rm NL}}}\right)^{2}\frac{\theta({\vec{k}})}{aH}-\frac{(c_{1}+c_{2})}{2}\mu^{2}\left(\frac{k}{{k_{\rm NL}}}\right)^{2}\delta({\vec{k}})-\frac{c_{3}}{2}\mu^{4}\left(\frac{k}{{k_{\rm NL}}}\right)^{2}\delta({\vec{k}})\ ,$ (arXiv:1409.1225) [src: Eq. 19]: the first line holds the SPT-like terms, evaluated with the EFT equations of motion and their own counterterms, and the second line the counterterms that affect only the mapping [src: Sect. 2.1, text after Eq. 19].
5. The one-loop spectrum, written with the EFT real-space spectra and the tree-level contact correlators, is $P_{r,\delta,\delta,\,||_{\text{1-loop}}}(k,\mu,t)=P_{\delta,\delta,||_{\text{1-loop}}}(k,t)+2\mu^{2}P_{\delta,\frac{\dot{\delta}}{H},||_{\text{1-loop}}}(k,t)+\mu^{4}P_{\frac{\dot{\delta}}{H},\frac{\dot{\delta}}{H},||_{\text{1-loop}}}(k,t)-\left(\frac{k\,\mu}{aH}\right)^{2}P_{\delta,[v_{z}^{2}],\text{tree}}(k,t)-\mu^{2}\left(\frac{k\,\mu}{aH}\right)^{2}P_{\frac{\dot{\delta}}{H},[v_{z}^{2}],\text{tree}}(k,t)+\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{k\,\mu}{aH}\right)^{4}P_{[v_{z}^{2}],[v_{z}^{2}],\text{tree}}(k,t)+\left(1+f\mu^{2}\right)\left(\frac{k\,\mu}{aH}\right)^{2}P_{\delta,[\delta\,v_{z}^{2}],\text{tree}}(k,t)+\frac{i}{3}\left(1+f\mu^{2}\right)\left(\frac{k\,\mu}{aH}\right)^{2}P_{\delta,[v_{z}^{3}],\text{tree}}(k,t)$ (arXiv:1409.1225) [src: Eq. 20], to which the counterterm line is added (the rendering read shows the display up to this row).
6. Of the three new coefficients one combination is degenerate at one loop, so two new counterterms are needed, scaling as $(1+f\mu^{2})\mu^{2}k^{2}P_{\delta,\delta,11}$ and $(1+f\mu^{2})\mu^{4}k^{2}P_{\delta,\delta,11}$, and there is no independent counterterm for each μ dependence of the SPT terms (arXiv:1409.1225) [src: Sect. 2.1, text after Eq. 20]. In terms of SPT pieces, $P_{r,\delta,\delta,||_{\text{1-loop}}}(k,\mu,a)=D^{2}\,P_{r,\delta,\delta,11}(k,\mu)+D^{4}\,P_{r,\delta,\delta,\text{1-loop,\,SPT}}(k,\mu)-(2\pi)D^{4}\left[2c_{s}^{2}+\mu^{2}\left(8\,c_{s}^{2}\,f+2\frac{d\,c_{s}^{2}}{d\log a}\,\right)+2\,\mu^{4}\left(3c_{s}^{2}\,f^{2}+\frac{d\,c_{s}^{2}}{d\log a}\,f\right)\right.\left.-\left(1+f\mu^{2}\right)\left(\left(c_{1}+c_{2}\right)\mu^{2}+\left(c_{1}+c_{3}\right)\mu^{4}\right)\right]\left(\frac{k}{{k_{\rm NL}}}\right)^{2}P_{\delta,\delta,11}(k)\ ,$ (arXiv:1409.1225) [src: Eq. 21]: the sound speed of real space enters through δ and its time derivative, the redshift-space counterterms through $c_{1}+c_{2}$ and $c_{1}+c_{3}$. The redshift-space matter spectrum is IR safe for the same reason as in real space [src: Sect. 2.1, text after Eq. 21].

### Step 4: biased tracers — counterterms and stochastic terms
<!-- key: step-4-biased-tracers-counterterms-and -->

1. Perko et al. extend the construction to halos. The halo velocity inherits the dark-matter velocity in each realisation, so its stochastic terms are derivative suppressed and the stochastic part of the halo velocity divergence starts at $k^{2}$: $\theta_{h}^{(\epsilon)}=\bar{c}^{2}_{1,ren}\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm M}}\right)^{2}\epsilon+\ \ldots\ .$ (arXiv:1610.09321) [src: Eq. 3.12].
2. The stochastic terms of the contact operators correlate as $\mu^{2}k^{2}\langle\epsilon^{2}\rangle$, degenerate with those of the halo density and velocity (Eqs. 3.18–3.19), so to order $k^{2}$ three parameters suffice: $\langle\delta_{h,r}\delta_{h,r}\rangle_{\epsilon}=\frac{1}{\bar{n}_{W}}\left(c_{\epsilon,1}+c_{\epsilon,2}\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm M}}\right)^{2}+c_{\epsilon,3}f\mu^{2}\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm M}}\right)^{2}\right)\ ,$ (arXiv:1610.09321) [src: Eq. 3.20]; all three were needed in the fits and the $k^{4}$ terms were negligible [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 3.20].
3. The deterministic counterterms are $\langle\delta_{h,r}(\vec{k})\delta_{h,r}(\vec{k})\rangle_{\rm ct}=2\langle\delta_{h,r}^{(1)}(\vec{k}){\delta_{h,r}^{(3,{\rm ct})}}(\vec{k})\rangle=2P_{11}(k)\left(K_{\delta_{h}}^{(1)}+f\mu^{2}K_{\theta_{h}}^{(1)}\right)\left(\mu^{2}\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm M}}\right)^{2}\tilde{c}_{r,1}+\mu^{4}\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm M}}\right)^{2}\tilde{c}_{r,2}+c_{\rm ct}^{(\delta_{h})}\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{2}\right)=2P_{11}(k)(b_{1}+f\mu^{2})\left(\mu^{2}\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm M}}\right)^{2}\tilde{c}_{r,1}+\mu^{4}\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm M}}\right)^{2}\tilde{c}_{r,2}+c_{\rm ct}^{(\delta_{h})}\left(\frac{k}{k_{\rm NL}}\right)^{2}\right)\ ,$ (arXiv:1610.09321) [src: Eq. 3.25], three coefficients with the matter structure of Step 3.

### Step 5: the analysis model and the fingers-of-god term (CLASS-PT)
<!-- key: step-5-the-analysis-model-and-the-fingers-of -->

1. Leading counterterms, a generalisation of the sound speed: $\begin{split}P_{{\rm ctr,RSD,}\nabla^{2}\delta}(z,k,\mu)=&-2\tilde{c}_{0}(z)k^{2}P_{\text{lin}}(z,k)\\ &-2\tilde{c}_{2}(z)f(z)\mu^{2}k^{2}P_{\text{lin}}(z,k)-2\tilde{c}_{4}(z)f^{2}(z)\mu^{4}k^{2}P_{\text{lin}}(z,k)\,,\end{split}$ (arXiv:2004.10607) [src: Eq. 2.15]; fingers of god can make them larger than naively expected [src: Sect. 2.4, text after Eq. 2.15].
2. Fingers-of-god proxy: since the fingers of god come from higher-derivative terms of the non-linear mapping, a $k^{4}\mu^{4}$ term is added, $P_{{\rm ctr,RSD},\,\nabla_{\bf z}^{4}\delta}(z,k,\mu)=-\tilde{c}(z)f^{4}(z)\mu^{4}k^{4}(b_{1}(z)+f(z)\mu^{2})^{2}P_{\rm lin}(z,k)\,,$ (arXiv:2004.10607) [src: Eq. 2.16], with the Kaiser factor inserted "for convenience"; the systematic derivation of all terms of this order is left for future work [src: Sect. 2.4, text after Eq. 2.16].
3. Stochastic terms at next-to-leading order in derivatives: $P_{\epsilon\epsilon,{\rm RSD}}(z,k,\mu)=P_{\rm shot}(z)+a_{0}(z)k^{2}+a_{2}(z)\mu^{2}k^{2}\,,$ (arXiv:2004.10607) [src: Eq. 2.18], the same structure as Perko et al.'s Eq. 3.20. The residual constant $P_{\rm shot}$ is kept even when $1/\bar{n}$ is subtracted [src: Sect. 2.4, text after Eq. 2.18].
4. Degeneracies: the $\tilde{c}$ term is very degenerate with $a_{2}\mu^{2}k^{2}$; BOSS reanalyses kept only the former, another analysis only the latter, without impact on cosmological parameters; $a_{0}$ can be neglected for $k\lesssim 0.3$ h/Mpc according to their ref. [67]; CLASS-PT drops $a_{0}$ and $a_{2}$ [src: Sect. 2.4, text after Eq. 2.18]. <!-- num: not a value -->
5. Multipoles: $P_{\rm gg,RSD}(z,k,\mu)=\sum_{\ell\;{\rm even}}{\cal L}_{\ell}(\mu)P_{\ell}(z,k)\,,$ (arXiv:2004.10607) [src: Eq. 2.19], $P_{\ell}(z,k)\equiv\frac{2\ell+1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}d\mu\,{\cal L}_{\ell}(\mu)P_{\rm gg,RSD}(z,k,\mu)\,.$ [src: Eq. 2.20]. Monopole, quadrupole and hexadecapole are kept, with all one-loop terms up to $\mathcal{O}(\mu^{8})$ [src: Sect. 2.4, text after Eq. 2.20]. <!-- num: not a value --> The counterterms are rebased to one coefficient per multipole, $\begin{split}P_{\ell,\nabla^{2}\delta}(z,k)\equiv\frac{2\ell+1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}d\mu\,\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(\mu)\mu^{\ell}f^{\ell/2}k^{2}P_{\rm lin}(k)\,.\end{split}$ (arXiv:2004.10607) [src: Eq. 2.22], with $\begin{split}c_{0}&\equiv\tilde{c}_{0}+\frac{f}{3}\tilde{c}_{2}+\frac{f^{2}}{5}\tilde{c}_{4}\,,\quad c_{2}\equiv\tilde{c}_{2}+\frac{6f}{7}\tilde{c}_{4}\,,\quad c_{4}\equiv\tilde{c}_{4}\,.\end{split}$ [src: Eq. 2.23]; the mapping is not exact with IR resummation and the Alcock–Paczynski effect, but the difference is below the 0.1% accuracy of the code [src: Sect. 2.4, footnote 8].

### Step 6: IR resummation in redshift space
<!-- key: step-6-ir-resummation-in-redshift-space -->

The anisotropic damping $\Sigma^{2}_{\rm tot}(\mu)$ of Ivanov and Sibiryakov (`deep/ir-resummation.md`, Step 6) enters the one-loop spectrum through a factorisation: the one-loop integrals are computed with the smooth spectrum, and with one insertion of the undamped wiggly spectrum to which the damping is applied, $\begin{split}&P_{\rm gg}(z,k,\mu)=(b_{1}(z)+f(z)\mu^{2})^{2}\left(P_{\text{nw}}(z,k)+{\rm e}^{-k^{2}\Sigma^{2}_{\text{tot}}(z,\mu)}P_{\text{w}}(z,k)(1+k^{2}\Sigma^{2}_{\rm tot}(z,\mu))\right)\\ &+P_{\text{gg, nw, RSD, 1-loop}}(z,k,\mu)+{\rm e}^{-k^{2}\Sigma^{2}_{\text{tot}}(z,\mu)}P_{\text{gg, w, RSD, 1-loop}}(z,k,\mu)\,.\end{split}$ (arXiv:2004.10607) [src: Eq. 2.32], with $\begin{split}&P_{\text{gg, nw, RSD},\,\text{1-loop}}(z,k,\mu)\equiv P_{\text{gg, RSD, 1-loop}}[P_{\text{nw}}]\,,\\ &P_{\text{gg, w, RSD},\,\text{1-loop}}(z,k,\mu)\equiv P_{\text{gg, RSD, 1-loop}}[P_{\text{nw}}+P_{\text{w}}]-P_{\text{gg, RSD, 1-loop}}[P_{\text{nw}}]\,.\end{split}$ (arXiv:2004.10607) [src: Eq. 2.33]. Terms quadratic in $P_{\rm w}$ are neglected; the four error sources (split, $k_{S}$, factorisation, $P_{\rm w}^{2}$ terms) are stated to be smaller than the two-loop contribution [src: Sect. 2.5, text after Eq. 2.33]. The multipoles of Step 5 are taken after the resummation [src: Sect. 2.5, text after Eq. 2.33].

### Step 7: the Lagrangian bookkeeping
<!-- key: step-7-the-lagrangian-bookkeeping -->

In the one-loop LPT model of Chen et al. the counterterms and stochastic terms enter as $+k^{2}(\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{2}\mu^{2}+\alpha_{4}\mu^{4}+\alpha_{6}\mu^{6})P_{s,\rm Zel}(\textbf{k})+R_{h}^{3}(1+\sigma_{2}k^{2}\mu^{2}+\sigma_{4}k^{4}\mu^{4}).$ (arXiv:2012.04636) [src: Eq. 4, last row], including what are traditionally called the shot-noise and fingers-of-god terms [src: Sect. 3, text after Eq. 4]. Only the long-wavelength part of the linear displacement cumulant is kept exponentiated (`deep/ir-resummation.md`, Step 5).

- **Consistency of the codes.** The models arise from the same systematic procedure, so once conventions are accounted for any difference should be of two-loop order or due to added higher-order counterterms; the IR resummation differs (exponentiated displacements below $k_{\rm IR}$ in velocileptors-LPT and PyBird, wiggle-only damping in the EPT variant and Folps$\nu$), with little effect for realistic volumes and the result insensitive to the split method (Maus et al., arXiv:2404.07272) [src: Sect. 3.6]. The resulting fits agree for the DESI Y1 volume (`results.md#validity-ranges-against-simulations`).
- **Exact time dependence** instead of EdS kernels changes the one-loop redshift-space monopole by a factor about 1.003 and the quadrupole by about 1.007 at z = 0.57 (Donath & Senatore 2020, arXiv:2005.04805) [src: abstract]; see `results.md#validity-ranges-against-simulations`.
- **Two loops.** At two loops the low-redshift LRG sample is harder to model than the ELG one because of stronger fingers of god; the quadrupole receives large, partly cancelling one- and two-loop contributions, "indicative of fingers-of-God effects", which the two-loop model is flexible enough to capture (Philcox 2026, arXiv:2610.13867) [src: Sect. IV.4.2]. Similar σ8 constraints come from one loop at $k_{\rm max}\approx 0.2h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$ and two loops at $k_{\rm max}\approx 0.3$–$0.5h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$ [src: Sect. V]. <!-- eq: notation -->

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Kernels without counterterms.** One-loop redshift-space SPT from the $Z_{n}$ alone fails at larger scales than real-space SPT, because of the expansion of the mapping (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Sect. 7.4.1, text after Eq. 622]; the counterterms of Step 3 are not optional.
- **One counterterm per μ power.** The SPT terms carry many μ dependences, but at one loop matter needs only two new redshift-space counterterms (arXiv:1409.1225) [src: Sect. 2.1, text after Eq. 20]; adding free coefficients for each μ power of the SPT terms is not what the EFT prescribes. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **The fingers-of-god term is a proxy.** The $k^{4}\mu^{4}$ term of CLASS-PT is not derived systematically and is degenerate with the $a_{2}\mu^{2}k^{2}$ stochastic term (arXiv:2004.10607) [src: Sect. 2.4, text after Eqs. 2.16 and 2.18]; reporting both as measured parameters double counts one degree of freedom. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Counterterm values between codes.** The basis ($\tilde{c}_{n}$ or $c_{\ell}$, Eq. 2.23 of arXiv:2004.10607), the spectrum they multiply (linear or Zel'dovich, arXiv:2404.07272, Sect. 3.6) and the normalisation scale ($k_{\rm NL}$, $k_{\rm M}$, or none) differ; a prior or a posterior on a counterterm does not transfer between codes without these. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Sign of the velocity.** Bernardeau et al.'s v is minus the peculiar velocity over $\mathcal{H}f$, so their mapping carries −f v_z while Senatore and Zaldarriaga's carries $+\hat{z}\cdot\vec{v}/(aH)$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551, Eq. 613; arXiv:1409.1225, Eq. 2). <!-- eq: notation -->
- **The separation scale in CLASS-PT.** The text uses $k_{S}$ = 0.2 h/Mpc while calling (0.05–0.1) h/Mpc the physically relevant range, a range that excludes the value used (arXiv:2004.10607) [src: Sect. 2.5, text after Eq. 2.25]; Ivanov and Sibiryakov vary $k_{S}$ from 0.05 to 0.2 h/Mpc (arXiv:1804.05080) [src: Sect. 7.1, text after Eq. 7.10]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Normalisation of δΣ².** CLASS-PT writes the redshift-space damping integral with $1/(2\pi^{2})$ (arXiv:2004.10607) [src: Eq. 2.31], Ivanov and Sibiryakov with 4π (arXiv:1804.05080) [src: Eq. 7.5b]; CLASS-PT attributes the extra factors of 2π to its Fourier convention (arXiv:2004.10607) [src: Sect. 2.5, footnote 9]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- **Quadratic wiggle terms and factorisation.** The IR-resummed one-loop redshift-space spectrum neglects $\mathcal{O}(P_{\rm w}^{2})$ terms and uses a factorisation; these are claimed smaller than two-loop terms (arXiv:2004.10607) [src: Sect. 2.5]. <!-- num: not a value -->
