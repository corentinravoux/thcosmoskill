## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- Assassi et al. (arXiv:1402.5916) regulate loops with a sharp momentum cutoff Λ and write the variance as $\langle(\delta^{2})_{\boldsymbol{q}}\rangle^{\prime}=\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\frac{{\rm d}p}{2\pi^{2}}\,p^{2}P(p)\equiv\sigma^{2}(\Lambda)\ ,$ [src: Eq. 2.37]. They use a rescaled gravitational potential, $\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\tilde{\Phi}_{g}\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}\tilde{\Phi}_{g}\ ,\quad{\rm with}\quad\tilde{\Phi}_{g}\equiv\nabla^{-2}\delta=\frac{2}{3{\cal H}^{2}\Omega_{m}}\Phi_{g}\ ,$ (arXiv:1402.5916) [src: Eq. 2.43], and drop the tilde afterwards [src: Sect. 2.2.1, text after Eq. 2.43].
- Ivanov's GGI lectures (arXiv:2607.28314) write the same variance with the measure $d^{3}q$ and no $(2\pi)^{3}$ or $2\pi^{2}$ factor (Eq. 197), and recommend dimensional regularisation, in which convergent integrals are evaluated at $\Lambda\to\infty$ and divergent ones are set to zero, so that $\sigma^{2}_{\Lambda}$ vanishes [src: Sect. 5.3, text after Eq. 200]. <!-- num: not a value --> A coefficient multiplying $\sigma^{2}$ is therefore scheme-dependent and is quoted with its paper.
- The cubic Galileon differs in normalisation between sources: Assassi et al. define it with an overall factor $-1/2$ (arXiv:1402.5916, Eq. 3.9), while Eggemeier et al. (arXiv:1812.03208, Eq. 31) and Ivanov (arXiv:2607.28314, Eq. 193) define it without that factor. <!-- num: not a value --> A coefficient $b_{\mathcal{G}_{3}}$ is therefore quoted with its definition.
- Eggemeier et al. call the coefficient of $\mathcal{G}_{2}$ "$\gamma_{2}$" and mark Lagrangian parameters with a subscript $\mathcal{L}$ (arXiv:1812.03208, Eq. 78); Ivanov's lectures mark them with a superscript L (arXiv:2607.28314, Eq. 307); the reference files use $b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}$ (Eq. 3.24 of arXiv:2203.08232). <!-- num: not a value -->
- Velocity potential: Eggemeier et al. define $\nabla^{2}\,\Phi_{v}(\bm{x},\tau)\equiv\theta(\bm{x},\tau)\,,$ (arXiv:1812.03208) [src: Eq. 28]; Ivanov's lectures normalise by the growth rate, $\Delta\hat{\Phi}_{v}=\Theta$ (arXiv:2607.28314) [src: Sect. 5.2, inline, text after Eq. 190], the convention of `formalism.md#conventions`.

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — the idea: redefine parameters to absorb cutoff dependence (McDonald 2006)
<!-- key: step-1-the-idea-redefine-parameters-to-absorb -->

McDonald (arXiv:astro-ph/0609413) starts from the local Taylor series of the galaxy density in the matter density plus a noise ε [src: Eq. 1]. Its mean contains $\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}^{\prime\prime}\sigma^{2}$, cutoff-sensitive, which he absorbs by defining the observed mean density [src: Eqs. 2–3]; the fluctuation then reads $\delta_{g}\left(\delta\right)=\frac{\rho_{g}\left(\delta\right)-\bar{\rho}_{g}}{\bar{\rho}_{g}}=c_{1}\delta+\frac{1}{2}c_{2}\left(\delta^{2}-\sigma^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{6}c_{3}\delta^{3}+\epsilon+\mathcal{O}\left(\delta_{1}^{4}\right)$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0609413) [src: Eq. 4]. In the power spectrum the terms $c_{1}c_{3}\sigma^{2}$ and $\frac{68}{21}c_{1}c_{2}\sigma^{2}$ multiply the linear spectrum like a correction to $c_{1}^{2}$ [src: Eq. 10], and are absorbed into a renormalised linear bias [src: Sect. II]. <!-- num: not a value --> Eq. 10 is a multi-row display of which only the first row was read; its other rows are not reproduced here.

### Step 2 — renormalising the composite operator δ² (Assassi et al. 2014)
<!-- key: step-2-renormalising-the-composite-operator -->

Assassi, Baumann, Green and Zaldarriaga (arXiv:1402.5916) make the procedure systematic. A composite operator is made finite by adding counterterms, $[\delta^{2}]({\boldsymbol{x}},\tau)=\delta^{2}({\boldsymbol{x}},\tau)+\sum_{\widetilde{\cal O}}Z^{(\delta^{2})}_{\widetilde{\cal O}}\,{\widetilde{\cal O}}({\boldsymbol{x}},\tau)\ ,$ [src: Eq. 2.33]. External legs are replaced by linear fields, so that only divergences internal to the operator are treated (those of the external legs belong to the matter EFT), and the counterterms are fixed by requiring tree-level correlators at zero external momenta, $\langle[\delta^{2}]_{\boldsymbol{q}}\,\delta_{{\boldsymbol{q}}_{1}}^{(1)}\cdots\,\delta_{{\boldsymbol{q}}_{m}}^{(1)}\rangle=\langle(\delta^{2})_{\boldsymbol{q}}\,\delta_{{\boldsymbol{q}}_{1}}^{(1)}\cdots\,\delta_{{\boldsymbol{q}}_{m}}^{(1)}\rangle_{\rm tree}\qquad{\rm at}\quad q_{i}=0\ ,\,\forall\,i\ ,$ (arXiv:1402.5916) [src: Eq. 2.34].

The calculation proceeds in the number m of external linear legs (arXiv:1402.5916, Sect. 2.2.1):

- m = 0: the tadpole is removed by a constant, $[\delta^{2}]=\delta^{2}-\sigma^{2}(\Lambda)\ .$ (arXiv:1402.5916) [src: Eq. 2.38], which keeps the mean halo density unperturbed at loop level.
- m = 1: the one-loop correlator with one linear leg equals $\frac{68}{21}\sigma^{2}(\Lambda)P(q)$ (arXiv:1402.5916) [src: Eq. 2.39], removed by a counterterm proportional to δ [src: Eq. 2.40]. <!-- num: not a value --> The same 68/21 is McDonald's coefficient of Step 1.
- m = 2: the loop gives $\sigma^{2}(\Lambda)\left[\frac{5248}{735}+\frac{508}{2205}\frac{({\boldsymbol{q}}_{1}\cdot{\boldsymbol{q}}_{2})^{2}}{q_{1}^{2}q_{2}^{2}}+{\cal O}\Big(\frac{q_{i}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\Big)\right]\,P(q_{1})P(q_{2})\ .$ (arXiv:1402.5916) [src: Eq. 2.42]. The angular term cannot be absorbed by a function of δ; it needs the square of the tidal tensor [src: Sect. 2.2.1, text after Eq. 2.42].

Collecting the counterterms gives $[\delta^{2}]=\delta^{2}-\sigma^{2}(\Lambda)\left[1+\frac{68}{21}\delta+\frac{2624}{735}\delta^{2}+\frac{254}{2205}(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\Phi_{g})^{2}\right]+\cdots\ ,$ (arXiv:1402.5916) [src: Eq. 2.44], rewritten in the Galileon basis as $[\delta^{2}]=\delta^{2}-\sigma^{2}(\Lambda)\left[1+\frac{68}{21}\hskip 1.0pt\delta+\frac{8126}{2205}\hskip 1.0pt\delta^{2}+\frac{254}{2205}\hskip 1.0pt{\cal G}_{2}(\Phi_{g})\right]+\cdots\ ,$ [src: Eq. 2.45], with ${\cal G}_{2}(\Phi_{g})\equiv(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\Phi_{g})^{2}-(\nabla^{2}\Phi_{g})^{2}\ .$ [src: Eq. 2.46]. The conclusion of the paper: renormalising the simplest operator of local Eulerian bias already requires an operator non-local in δ [src: Sect. 2.2.1, last sentence]. Each of the three diagrams separately has divergences in $q_i^{-2}$ that only the non-scalar operators $\Phi_g\delta$ and $\nabla_i\Phi_g\nabla^i\delta$ could absorb; they cancel in the sum (arXiv:1402.5916) [src: Sect. 2.2.2, fourth item]. Keeping subleading terms, higher-derivative operators such as $(\nabla\delta)^{2}/\Lambda^{2}$ are also generated [src: Eqs. 2.47–2.48].

### Step 3 — which operators can appear: symmetry and equations of motion
<!-- key: step-3-which-operators-can-appear-symmetry-and -->

Renormalised operators are defined as $[{\cal O}]=\sum_{\widetilde{\cal O}}{\cal Z}_{{\cal O},\widetilde{\cal O}}\,\widetilde{\cal O}\ ,$ (arXiv:1402.5916) [src: Eq. 3.1], and the counterterms contain every term allowed by symmetry. Under homogeneous boosts the gradients of the potentials shift, so the building block is the second derivative, $\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\Phi\ ,\quad{\rm where}\quad\Phi=\Phi_{g}\ {\rm or}\ \Phi_{v}\ .$ [src: Eq. 3.2]. The equations of motion then remove redundant operators order by order (arXiv:1402.5916, Sect. 3.1.1):

- first order: the two potentials coincide, $\Phi_{g}^{(1)}=\Phi_{v}^{(1)}\ .$ (arXiv:1402.5916) [src: Eq. 3.5];
- second order: of eight operators allowed by symmetry, three are independent, because $\nabla^{2}\Phi_{g}^{(2)}-\nabla^{2}\Phi_{v}^{(2)}=-\frac{2}{7}\hskip 1.0pt{\cal G}_{2}(\Phi^{(1)}_{g})\ .$ (arXiv:1402.5916) [src: Eq. 3.6]; the chosen set is $\delta\ ,\ \delta^{2}\ ,\ {\cal G}_{2}(\Phi_{g})\ .$ (arXiv:1402.5916) [src: Eq. 3.7];
- third order: the velocity potential becomes independent, and a basis is $\delta\ ,\ \delta^{2}\ ,\ \delta^{3}\ ,\ {\cal G}_{2}(\Phi_{g})\ ,\ {\cal G}_{2}(\Phi_{g})\delta\ ,\ {\cal G}_{3}(\Phi_{g})\ ,\ \Gamma_{3}(\Phi_{g},\Phi_{v})\equiv{\cal G}_{2}(\Phi_{g})-{\cal G}_{2}(\Phi_{v})\ ,$ (arXiv:1402.5916) [src: Eq. 3.8], taken from Chan, Scoccimarro and Sheth (arXiv:1201.3614) [src: arXiv:1402.5916, Sect. 3.1.1, reference 16].

The renormalisation conditions are imposed on (partially) one-particle-irreducible diagrams at vanishing external momenta, $\langle[{\cal O}]_{\boldsymbol{q}}\,\delta_{{\boldsymbol{q}}_{1}}^{(1)}\cdots\delta_{{\boldsymbol{q}}_{m}}^{(1)}\rangle_{\rm{\it(p)}1PI}=0\qquad{\rm at}\quad q_{i}=0\ ,\,\forall\,i\ ,$ (arXiv:1402.5916) [src: Eq. 3.10]. Imposed at a finite scale μ instead, the basis that is orthogonal at μ mixes at another scale and the renormalised bias parameters run with μ [src: Sect. 3.1.2, text after Eq. 3.10; Appendix A].

**Non-renormalisation of Galileons.** Every Galileon is a double divergence, ${\cal G}_{n}(\Phi)\,=\,\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}\,\big(\Phi\,T_{ij}^{(n-1)}\big)\ ,$ (arXiv:1402.5916) [src: Eq. 3.11], so its correlators with linear fields scale as $q^{2}$, provided no pole appears as external momenta vanish, which symmetry guarantees (Appendix C); hence $[{\cal G}_{n}(\Phi)]\,=\,{\cal G}_{n}(\Phi)\,+\,{\cal O}\Big(\frac{\nabla^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\Big)\ .$ (arXiv:1402.5916) [src: Eq. 3.12]: to all loop orders and at leading order in derivatives, Galileon operators are not renormalised [src: Sect. 3.1.3, last sentence]. Eggemeier et al. list the properties that make Galileons the convenient basis: their kernels vanish as $k^{2}$ when the total momentum goes to zero, so their expectation values vanish, and $\mathcal{G}_{3}$ cannot contribute to the one-loop propagator (arXiv:1812.03208) [src: Sect. III.1, text after Eq. 35].

### Step 4 — renormalised bias parameters
<!-- key: step-4-renormalised-bias-parameters -->

In the bare basis $\delta_{h}=\sum_{\cal O}b_{\cal O}^{(0)}{\cal O}\ .$ and in the renormalised one $\delta_{h}=\sum_{\cal O}b_{\cal O}^{(R)}[{\cal O}]\ ,$ (arXiv:1402.5916) [src: Eqs. 3.13–3.14], the two sets of coefficients are related by the inverse mixing matrix, $b_{\cal O}^{(R)}\equiv\sum_{\widetilde{\cal O}}{\cal Z}_{\widetilde{\cal O},{\cal O}}^{-1}\,b_{\widetilde{\cal O}}^{(0)}\ ,$ [src: Eq. 3.15]. The rule stated by the paper: a renormalised coefficient of an operator $\mathcal{O}_I$ is shifted by the bare coefficients of every operator that needs $\mathcal{O}_I$ as a counterterm; $b_1^{(0)}$ enters no other renormalised coefficient because δ is already finite in the matter EFT, and the Galileon coefficients enter none at leading order in derivatives [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 3.14]. To order $\sigma^{2}$ the paper finds

- $b_{1}^{(R)}\,=\,b_{1}^{(0)}\,+\,\sigma^{2}(\Lambda)\left[\frac{34}{21}b_{2}^{(0)}+\frac{1}{2}b_{3}^{(0)}-\frac{4}{3}b_{{\cal G}_{2}\delta}^{(0)}\right]\ .$ (arXiv:1402.5916) [src: Eq. 3.16], from the cubic operators (Appendix B);
- $b_{2}^{(R)}=b_{2}^{(0)}+\sigma^{2}(\Lambda)\,\bigg[\,\frac{8126}{2205}b_{2}^{(0)}+\frac{68}{21}b_{3}^{(0)}-\frac{752}{105}b_{{\cal G}_{2}\delta}^{(0)}+\frac{1}{2}b_{4}^{(0)}-\frac{16}{3}b^{(0)}_{{\cal G}_{2}\delta^{2}}-\frac{128}{105}b_{\Gamma_{3}\delta}^{(0)}+\frac{64}{15}b_{{({\cal G}_{2})^{2}}}^{(0)}\bigg]\ ,$ (arXiv:1402.5916) [src: Eq. 3.17], which needs the quartic operators;
- $b_{{\cal G}_{2}}^{(R)}=b_{{\cal G}_{2}}^{(0)}+\sigma^{2}(\Lambda)\,\left[\,\boxed{\frac{127}{2205}b_{2}^{(0)}}\,+\frac{116}{105}b_{{\cal G}_{2}\delta}^{(0)}+\frac{1}{2}b_{{\cal G}_{3}\delta}^{(0)}+b^{(0)}_{{{\cal G}_{2}\delta^{2}}}+\frac{8}{35}b_{{\Gamma_{3}\delta}}^{(0)}+\frac{8}{15}b_{{({\cal G}_{2})^{2}}}^{(0)}\right]\ .$ (arXiv:1402.5916) [src: Eq. 3.18].

The boxed term is the argument against local Eulerian bias: $b_{2}^{(0)}$ feeds the running of $b_{\mathcal{G}_2}$, so a tidal bias set to zero at one cutoff is non-zero at another, and the local Eulerian model is inconsistent beyond tree level (arXiv:1402.5916) [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 3.18]. The paper contrasts this with the co-evolution result of Chan et al., where the Eulerian $b_{\mathcal{G}_2}$ is tied to the Lagrangian linear bias at an earlier time: the two statements answer different questions (regulator independence against assumed time evolution) [src: Sect. 2.2.2, first item; Sect. 3.2, last sentence].

The GGI lectures redo the linear-bias step in a few lines and reach the same coefficients: the $P_{13}$-type term of $\delta^3$ has the shape of the linear term and combines with it as $b_{1}^{2}P_{11}+b_{1}b_{3}\;\sigma_{\Lambda}^{2}\;P_{11}(k)=\left(b_{1}+\frac{b_{3}}{2}\sigma^{2}_{\Lambda}\right)^{2}P_{11}(k)+O(\sigma^{4}_{\Lambda}P_{11}(k))\,,$ (Ivanov 2026, arXiv:2607.28314) [src: Eq. 198], and with $\mathcal{G}_2\delta$ and the evolved $\delta^2$, $b_{1}^{\mathrm{ren}}=b_{1}+\left(\frac{b_{3}}{2}+\frac{34b_{2}}{21}-\frac{4b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}\delta}}{3}\right)\sigma^{2}_{\Lambda}\,.$ [src: Eq. 200].

### Step 5 — which parameters survive at one loop
<!-- key: step-5-which-parameters-survive-at-one-loop -->

Because $\delta^{3}$, $\mathcal{G}_{3}$ and $\mathcal{G}_{2}\delta$ only renormalise $b_1$ in the one-loop power spectrum (the contribution of $\mathcal{G}_3$ vanishes identically), they are redundant at that order, and the independent deterministic parameters are $b_{1}\,,\quad b_{2}\,,\quad b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\,,\quad b_{\Gamma_{3}}\,,\quad b_{\nabla^{2}\delta}\,,$ (arXiv:2607.28314) [src: Sect. 5.3, Eq. 201]. The leading higher-derivative coefficient scales as the square of the formation scale, $b_{\nabla^{2}\delta}\sim R_{*}^{2}$ (arXiv:2607.28314) [src: Sect. 5.2, inline, text after Eq. 193]. Assassi et al. show that in a scaling universe the loop functions of $b_2$, $b_{\mathcal{G}_2}$ and $b_{\Gamma_3}$ are identical powers of q, so the halo–matter spectrum constrains only two combinations, $b_{\cal F}\equiv b_{{\cal G}_{2}}^{(R)}+\frac{2}{5}b_{\Gamma_{3}}^{(R)}\qquad{\rm and}\qquad b_{\cal I}\equiv b_{\delta^{2}}^{(R)}-\frac{4}{5}\thinspace b_{{\cal G}_{2}}^{(R)}\ .$ (arXiv:1402.5916) [src: Sect. 4.3, Eq. 4.34]; the halo–halo spectrum breaks the degeneracy [src: Sect. 4.3, Eq. 4.35].

At two loops the operators run through fifth order; after removing redundant operators the two-loop galaxy power spectrum needs 21 additional bias, higher-derivative and stochastic parameters per sample relative to one loop (Ivanov 2026, arXiv:2606.30713) [src: abstract]. The body of that paper, with its two-loop renormalisation conditions, was not read (see "Not verified").

### Step 6 — time non-locality and closure of the basis
<!-- key: step-6-time-non-locality-and-closure-of-the -->

Galaxy formation takes a Hubble time, so the bias relation is a functional along the past fluid trajectory, $\delta_{g}({\bm{x}},\tau)=\int^{\tau}d\tau^{\prime}\;F\!\left[\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Phi({\bm{x}}_{\mathrm{fl}},\tau^{\prime}),\;\partial_{i}v^{j}({\bm{x}}_{\mathrm{fl}},\tau^{\prime}),\;\varepsilon({\bm{x}}_{\mathrm{fl}},\tau^{\prime});\,\tau,\tau^{\prime}\right]\,.$ (arXiv:2607.28314) [src: Eq. 190]; because each perturbative order carries a fixed power of the growth factor, the memory integrals reduce to constants and the expansion is effectively local in time at one loop [src: Sect. 5.2, text after Eq. 190]. Senatore first showed that the Eulerian bias theory is local in space but non-local in time, argued why it can be treated as quasi time-local to some accuracy, and described the renormalisation of the bias coefficients that makes the series an expansion in $k/k_{\rm NL}$ and $k/k_{\rm M}$ (arXiv:1406.7843) [src: abstract].

Mirbabayi, Schmidt and Zaldarriaga (arXiv:1412.5169) build the basis from the gradient of the Lagrangian displacement, order by order: the scalars are traces of products of its perturbative orders, and the trace of a single order is never needed because it is a function of lower orders (arXiv:1412.5169) [src: Sect. 3.2, first paragraph]. Their list has one operator at first order, two at second, four at third and six at fourth (arXiv:1412.5169) [src: Eq. 57]. <!-- num: not a value --> They then argue that every counterterm needed to renormalise a local operator is a local function of the deformation tensor and of its spatial and convective time derivatives, so this basis is closed under renormalisation (arXiv:1412.5169) [src: Sect. 4.1, last paragraph]. Desjacques, Jeong and Schmidt extend this basis in their review (arXiv:1611.09787) [sec: arXiv:1812.03208, App. A.2, first sentence].

### Step 7 — converting between bases
<!-- key: step-7-converting-between-bases -->

**Galileon and convective-trace bases.** Eggemeier, Scoccimarro and Smith (arXiv:1812.03208) express the operators $\mathrm{Tr}[\Pi^{[n]}\cdots]$ of the Desjacques et al. review in their Galileon basis. At linear order the trace of the first-order tensor is δ, and at second order the only non-trivial operator is ${\rm Tr}[(\Pi^{[1]})^{2}]=\delta^{2}+{\cal G}_{2}$ (arXiv:1812.03208) [src: App. A.2, inline, text after Eq. 153]. At cubic order, ${\rm Tr}\Big[(\Pi^{[1]})^{3}\Big]=\delta^{3}+\frac{3}{2}\,{\cal G}_{2}\,\delta+\frac{1}{2}\,{\cal G}_{3},\ \ \ \ \ \mathrm{Tr}\left[\Pi^{[1]}\Pi^{[2]}\right]=\delta^{3}+\frac{11}{14}\,{\cal G}_{2}\,\delta+\frac{1}{2}\,{\cal G}_{3}-\frac{5}{7}\,{\cal G}_{2}(\varphi_{2},\varphi_{1})$ (arXiv:1812.03208) [src: App. A.2, Eq. 154]. The quartic relations are Eqs. 155–158 of the same appendix. Eggemeier et al. note that the convective-trace basis mixes operators that are local and non-local in second derivatives of the linear potential [src: App. A.2, text after Eq. 158]. <!-- num: not a value -->

**McDonald–Roy basis.** The cubic operators of McDonald and Roy (arXiv:0902.0991), also used by Senatore and collaborators, are, in the Galileon basis, $s^{3}={1\over 2}{\cal G}_{3}+{1\over 2}\delta\,{\cal G}_{2}+{2\over 9}\delta^{3},\ \ \ \ \ \delta s^{2}=\delta\,{\cal G}_{2}+{2\over 3}\delta^{3},\ \ \ \ \ st={2\over 7}\,{\cal G}_{2}(\varphi_{2},\varphi_{1})+{4\over 21}\delta\,{\cal G}_{2},\ \ \ \ \ \psi={5\over 7}{\cal G}_{3}+\delta\,{\cal G}_{2}-{30\over 49}\,{\cal G}_{2}(\varphi_{2},\varphi_{1})$ (arXiv:1812.03208) [src: App. A.2, Eq. 159]; only four of the five cubic operators $\delta^3, s^3, \delta s^2, st, \psi$ are independent, as Chan et al. pointed out, and one can be eliminated, for example $\psi=-{15\over 7}st+{10\over 7}s^{3}+{34\over 49}\delta s^{2}-{344\over 441}\delta^{3}.$ (arXiv:1812.03208) [src: App. A.2, Eq. 160].

**Lagrangian and Eulerian bases.** In the Lagrangian picture tracers are painted on the initial field and advected with the matter, $1+\delta_{g}({\bm{x}},\tau)=\int d^{3}q\;F\big[\delta_{1}({\bm{q}}),\ldots\big]\;\delta_{D}^{(3)}\big({\bm{x}}-{\bm{q}}-{\bm{\Psi}}({\bm{q}},\tau)\big)\,,$ (arXiv:2607.28314) [src: Eq. 306], with $F=1+b_{1}^{L}\,\delta_{1}({\bm{q}})+\frac{b_{2}^{L}}{2}\left(\delta_{1}^{2}({\bm{q}})-\langle\delta_{1}^{2}\rangle\right)+b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}^{L}\mathcal{G}_{2}({\bm{q}})+b_{\nabla^{2}}^{L}\,\Delta\delta_{1}({\bm{q}})+\varepsilon({\bm{q}})+\cdots,$ [src: Eq. 307]. Number conservation at linear order gives $\boxed{\;b_{1}=1+b_{1}^{L}\;}$ [src: Eq. 309]; the advected operators generate the full Eulerian basis, so the two expansions are equivalent at one loop [src: Sect. 7.8, text after Eq. 309].

The higher-order map is derived by Eggemeier et al. from the integrated continuity equation for conserved tracers without velocity bias, $1+\delta_{g}(\bm{x})=\frac{1+\delta_{g}^{\mathrm{IC}}(\bm{q})}{1+\delta^{\mathrm{IC}}(\bm{q})}\,\Big[1+\delta(\bm{x})\Big]\,,$ (arXiv:1812.03208) [src: Sect. IV.3.1, Eq. 77], taken from Eq. 48 of Chan et al. [src: Sect. IV.3.1, text before Eq. 77]. Neglecting decaying modes, the initial relation is written in the Galileon basis with Lagrangian coefficients [src: Eq. 78], and Galilean invariance lets one keep only the Galilean-invariant part of the non-linear density instead of expanding the Lagrangian position around the Eulerian one: $\delta_{g}(\bm{x})=\delta_{g}^{\text{IC}}(\bm{x})+\delta^{\text{GI}}(\bm{x})+\delta_{g}^{\text{IC}}(\bm{x})\,\delta^{\text{GI}}(\bm{x})$ [src: Eq. 79]. Matching order by order gives $b_{1}=1+b_{1,{\cal L}}\,,$ [src: Eq. 80], then $\ b_{2}=b_{2,{\cal L}},\ \ \ \ \ \gamma_{2}=-{2\over 7}\,b_{1,{\cal L}}+\gamma_{2,{\cal L}}\,,$ [src: Eq. 81], and at cubic order $b_{3}=b_{3,{\cal L}}-3b_{2},\ \ \ \ \ \gamma_{3}=-{1\over 9}b_{1,{\cal L}}-\gamma_{2}+\gamma_{3,{\cal L}},\ \ \ \ \ \gamma_{2}^{\times}=-{2\over 7}b_{2}+\gamma_{2,{\cal L}}^{\times},\ \ \ \ \ \gamma_{21}={2\over 21}b_{1,{\cal L}}+{6\over 7}\gamma_{2}+\gamma_{21,{\cal L}}$ (arXiv:1812.03208) [src: Eq. 82]; the quartic relations are Eqs. 83–85. These relations are derived for bare parameters but hold for renormalised ones too (Appendix D) [src: Sect. IV.3.1, last paragraph]. Setting the Lagrangian tidal coefficient to zero in Eq. 81 is the "co-evolution" or local-Lagrangian assumption whose code-specific forms are in `approximations.md#convention-traps`.

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **Cutoff against dimensional regularisation.** Assassi et al. keep σ²(Λ) explicit (arXiv:1402.5916, Eq. 2.37); Ivanov's lectures set divergent integrals to zero so that the linear bias of a linear fit equals that of a one-loop fit (arXiv:2607.28314) [src: Sect. 5.3, text after Eq. 200]. Physical predictions are the same at the order computed; the values of the bare parameters are not. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Eulerian against Lagrangian bias.** Equivalent at one loop; at two loops the Lagrangian expansion generates more terms than the naive Eulerian one through the curl part of the displacement, which a more systematic Eulerian expansion accounts for (arXiv:2607.28314) [src: Sect. 7.8, last paragraph].
- **Multipoint propagators.** Eggemeier et al. advocate evolving the initial multipoint propagators of the bias expansion rather than the bias parameters themselves (arXiv:1812.03208) [src: Sect. IV.3.1, last sentence].
- **Renormalisation-free Galileon bias in ULPT.** Sugiyama builds the biased field only from Galileon-type operators of the non-linear growth, which have vanishing ensemble and volume averages, so no renormalisation is needed; three bias parameters suffice for correlation functions and four for power spectra, the model reproduces halo auto- and cross-spectra within ∼1% up to $k \simeq 0.3\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$ for $b_1 \sim 0.8$-2, and predicts $b_{K^2}^{\mathrm{E}} = -\tfrac{3}{4} b_2^{\mathrm{E}}$ (arXiv:2509.04142) [src: abstract, inline]. The predicted relation between the tidal and quadratic biases is a model assumption, not a consequence of the EFT symmetries. `[der]`
- **Exact time dependence.** Beyond the EdS approximation the irreducible one-loop basis keeps its dimension but changes slightly (Donath & Senatore 2020, arXiv:2005.04805), as summarised in `models.md#eulerian-bias-expansion-in-the-galileon-basis`.

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Local Eulerian bias at one loop.** A model without the tidal operator is not stable under a change of cutoff, because the quadratic bias feeds the running of the tidal bias (arXiv:1402.5916) [src: Eq. 3.18 and text after it]. Check: the one-loop model contains the operators $\mathcal{G}_2$ and $\Gamma_3$.
- **Bare parameters reported as physical.** At one loop $b_1$ and $b_3\sigma^2_\Lambda$ enter only in combination (arXiv:2607.28314) [src: Eqs. 198–199]; a quoted $b_3$, $b_{\mathcal{G}_3}$ or $b_{\mathcal{G}_2\delta}$ from a one-loop power-spectrum fit has no meaning, since these operators are redundant there [src: Sect. 5.3, text after Eq. 200]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Degenerate quadratic and cubic biases.** From the halo–matter spectrum alone only $b_{\cal F}$ and $b_{\cal I}$ are measured (arXiv:1402.5916) [src: Eq. 4.34, Fig. 5]; individual values of $b_2$, $b_{\mathcal{G}_2}$, $b_{\Gamma_3}$ from a power-spectrum-only fit are prior-driven unless the auto-spectrum or the bispectrum is added. `[der]`
- **Over-complete cubic basis.** The five cubic operators of McDonald and Roy are not independent; only four are (arXiv:1812.03208) [src: App. A.2, text before Eq. 159], so a fit with all five has an exact degeneracy. `[der]`
- **Sign slip in the co-evolution literature.** Eggemeier et al. report a typo in Eq. 116 of Chan, Scoccimarro and Sheth (arXiv:1201.3614): its second term should carry a minus sign (arXiv:1812.03208) [src: Sect. IV.3.1, text after Eq. 82]. Use Eq. 82 of arXiv:1812.03208 for the cubic co-evolution relations.
- **Cubic Galileon normalisation.** Assassi et al.'s $\mathcal{G}_3$ carries a factor $-1/2$ relative to Eggemeier et al.'s and Ivanov's (Eq. 3.9 of arXiv:1402.5916 against Eq. 31 of arXiv:1812.03208 and Eq. 193 of arXiv:2607.28314). <!-- num: not a value --> Check the definition before moving $b_{\mathcal{G}_3}$ or a coefficient of $\mathcal{G}_3$ between papers.
- **Scale-dependent renormalisation conditions.** With conditions imposed at a finite scale μ the bias parameters run and an orthogonal basis stops being orthogonal (arXiv:1402.5916) [src: Sect. 3.1.2; Appendix A]; comparing parameters from two papers requires the same conditions.
- **Velocity bias.** All conversions above assume tracers comoving with matter. With velocity bias a dipole field appears at second order (Chan et al. 2012, arXiv:1201.3614) [src: abstract], and the Galilean-invariance shortcut of Eq. 79 of arXiv:1812.03208 no longer applies [src: Sect. IV.3.1, first sentence]. <!-- num: not a value -->

## Reading list

- Foundation: McDonald 2006, arXiv:astro-ph/0609413, Sect. II — the first renormalisation of bias parameters, mean density and linear bias.
- Foundation: Chan, Scoccimarro & Sheth 2012, arXiv:1201.3614 — non-local bias generated by gravity; Galileon invariants; co-evolution (with the sign typo of its Eq. 116).

- Foundation: Senatore 2015, arXiv:1406.7843 — time non-locality of the Eulerian bias expansion.
- Extension: Mirbabayi, Schmidt & Zaldarriaga 2014, arXiv:1412.5169, Sects. 3.2, 4.1 — basis of traces of the deformation tensor, closure under renormalisation.
- Review: Desjacques, Jeong & Schmidt 2016, arXiv:1611.09787 — the general expansion and bases (no HTML rendering; read through arXiv:1812.03208).
- Derivation and conversions: Eggemeier, Scoccimarro & Smith 2019, arXiv:1812.03208, Sects. III, IV.3.1, App. A.2 — Galileon basis to fourth order, Lagrangian-to-Eulerian evolution, conversion to other bases.
