# One-loop EFT power spectrum — thcosmo-lss-perturbation-theory dossier

Scope: how the one-loop matter power spectrum is built from the kernel recursion, why $P_{13}$ and $P_{22}$ are sensitive to the ultraviolet, and how the EFT counterterm and the stochastic term remove the cutoff dependence, step by step and with the alternatives (dimensional regularisation, FFTLog, shell ansatz).
Snapshot: 2026-11-02.
From: subtopics `spt-kernels-loops` (Ivanov review arXiv:2212.08488, Sect. 3.2; Bernardeau et al. arXiv:astro-ph/0112551, Sects. 2.4, 4.2) and `eftoflss-counterterms` (Ivanov arXiv:2212.08488, Sect. 4) of `references/subtopics.tsv`.
Audit questions: (1) How is the one-loop matter spectrum derived from the $F_n$ recursion, how do $P_{13}$ and $P_{22}$ behave in the UV, and how does the EFT counterterm absorb the cutoff dependence?

## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- The Fourier conventions of Bernardeau et al. (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) and Ivanov (arXiv:2212.08488) differ; they are tabulated, with places, in `formalism.md#conventions` and not repeated here. <!-- num: not a value --> The chain below follows Ivanov's convention (measure $\int_{\bf q}$ with the $(2\pi)^{3}$ in the inverse transform) except for the recursion, quoted from Bernardeau et al.
- Ivanov uses the time variable $\eta\equiv\ln D_{+}(\tau)$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Sect. 3.2, inline, text after Eq. 22]. He uses the rescaled velocity divergence Θ = θ/f of Eq. 23 (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Sect. 3.2]. Bernardeau et al. use conformal time and θ (`formalism.md#conventions`).
- Normalisation of $P_{13}$: Ivanov's $P_{13}$ contains the factor 6 and both orderings of the correlator (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Sect. 4.3, Eq. 71], as do Eqs. 165–166 of Bernardeau et al. Baldauf, Mercolli and Zaldarriaga write the one-loop term as $P_{\text{1loop}}^{\text{sh}}=2P_{13}^{\text{sh}}+P_{22}^{\text{sh}}$ (arXiv:1507.02256) [src: Sect. II.2, inline], so their $P_{13}$ is half of Ivanov's. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Units of the counterterm: Baldauf et al. quote $c_{s}^{2}$ as a length squared, of order $1\;h^{-2}\text{Mpc}^{2}$ (arXiv:1507.02256) [src: Sect. II.2, text after Eq. 12]; Carrasco, Hertzberg and Senatore quote $c^{2}_{\rm\,comb}(a_{0},\Lambda=\infty)\simeq 6.2\times 10^{-7}$ without a length unit (arXiv:1206.2926) [src: Sect. 3.2.3, text after Eq. 62]. The two numbers are not comparable without a relation that neither paper states in the sections read.
- Scale-free tables: Pajer and Zaldarriaga state that their one-loop coefficients are a factor of 4, written $(2\pi)^{3}/(2\pi^{2}\pi)$, lower than those of Table 5 of Bernardeau et al., because of the Fourier convention, the normalisation to $k_{NL}$ and the normalisation $P/(\pi A^{2}a^{4})$ of that table (arXiv:1301.7182) [src: Sect. 3.1, footnote 4].

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1: equations of motion in the EdS approximation
<!-- key: step-1-equations-of-motion-in-the-eds -->

Ivanov rewrites the continuity and Euler equations of `formalism.md#standard-perturbation-theory-equations-and` with $\eta=\ln D_{+}$ and Θ, as $\begin{split}&\partial_{\eta}\delta_{\bf k}-\Theta_{\bf k}=\int_{{\bf q}_{1}{\bf q}_{2}}\delta^{(3)}_{D}({\bf k}-{\bf q}_{12})\alpha({\bf q}_{1},{\bf q}_{2})\Theta_{{\bf q}_{1}}\delta_{{\bf q}_{2}}\,,\\ &\partial_{\eta}\Theta_{\bf k}-\frac{3\Omega_{m}}{2f^{2}}\delta_{\bf k}+\left(\frac{3\Omega_{m}}{2f^{2}}-1\right)\Theta_{\bf k}=\int_{{\bf q}_{1}{\bf q}_{2}}\delta^{(3)}_{D}({\bf k}-{\bf q}_{12})\beta({\bf q}_{1},{\bf q}_{2})\Theta_{{\bf q}_{1}}\Theta_{{\bf q}_{2}}\,.\end{split}$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 24]. In ΛCDM $\Omega_{m}\approx f^{2}$ to good accuracy at all times, and the perturbative scheme sets $\Omega_{m}/f^{2}=1$ at zeroth order, the "Einstein–de Sitter" approximation, which makes the coefficients time independent (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 24]. <!-- num: not a value --> Bernardeau et al. show that with $f=\Omega_{m}^{1/2}$ the system becomes separable with $D_{n}=E_{n}=(D_{1})^{n}$ and the recursion reduces to the EdS one (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Sect. 2.4.4, text after Eq. 66]. <!-- num: not a value --> The size of the error is discussed in `approximations.md#einstein-de-sitter-approximation-for-the-time`.

### Step 2: the kernel recursion
<!-- key: step-2-the-kernel-recursion -->

The n-th order fields are convolutions of n linear fields, $\delta_{n}({\bf k})=\int{\rm d}^{3}{\bf q}_{1}\ldots\int{\rm d}^{3}{\bf q}_{n}\,\delta_{D}({\bf k}-{\bf q}_{1\ldots n})F_{n}({\bf q}_{1},\ldots,{\bf q}_{n})\delta_{1}({\bf q}_{1})\ldots\delta_{1}({\bf q}_{n}),$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Eq. 41], and the same with $G_{n}$ for $\theta_{n}$ (Eq. 42), where $F_{n}$ and $G_{n}$ are homogeneous functions of degree zero in the wave vectors [src: Sect. 2.4.2, text after Eq. 42].

Inserting the ansatz into the equations of motion gives, for $n\geq 2$, the recursion $F_{n}({\bf q}_{1},\ldots,{\bf q}_{n})=\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}{G_{m}({\bf q}_{1},\ldots,{\bf q}_{m})\over{(2n+3)(n-1)}}\Bigl[(2n+1)\alpha({\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2})F_{n-m}({\bf q}_{m+1},\ldots,{\bf q}_{n})+2\beta({\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2})G_{n-m}({\bf q}_{m+1},\ldots,{\bf q}_{n})\Bigr],$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Eq. 43] and $G_{n}({\bf q}_{1},\ldots,{\bf q}_{n})=\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}{G_{m}({\bf q}_{1},\ldots,{\bf q}_{m})\over{(2n+3)(n-1)}}\Bigl[3\alpha({\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2})F_{n-m}({\bf q}_{m+1},\ldots,{\bf q}_{n})+2n\beta({\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2})G_{n-m}({\bf q}_{m+1},\ldots,{\bf q}_{n})\Bigr],$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Eq. 44]. Bernardeau et al. refer the derivation to earlier papers and do not give it in the review [src: Sect. 2.4.2, text before Eq. 43]; the step from Eq. 24 to the recursion is therefore skipped in both reviews read. Ivanov's version, with $\alpha({\bf q}_{1m},{\bf q}_{(m+1)...n})$ and $\beta({\bf q}_{1m},{\bf q}_{(m+1)n})$ in place of $\alpha({\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2})$, shows that the two arguments are the sums of the first m and of the last n − m momenta (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 27]. <!-- num: not a value -->

The recursion starts from $F_{1}=G_{1}=1$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Sect. 3.2, inline, text after Eq. 27] and gives the second-order kernels of `formalism.md#standard-perturbation-theory-equations-and` (Bernardeau et al., Eqs. 45–46; not Ivanov's Eq. 28, see "What goes wrong"). The kernels that enter the loop integrals are the symmetrised ones, $F_{n}^{(s)}$, obtained by summing $F_{n}$ over all permutations of its arguments (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Sect. 2.4.2, text after Eq. 46]; explicit $F_{3}$ and $F_{4}$ are not printed in the review, which refers to its ref. [270] [src: Sect. 2.4.2, text after Eq. 46].

### Step 3: the one-loop power spectrum
<!-- key: step-3-the-one-loop-power-spectrum -->

At order $[\delta_{0}]^{4}$ the power spectrum is $\begin{split}&\langle\delta_{\bf k}\delta_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}\rangle=\langle(\delta^{(1)}_{\bf k}+\delta^{(2)}_{\bf k}+\delta^{(3)}_{\bf k})(\delta^{(1)}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}+\delta^{(2)}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}+\delta^{(3)}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}})\rangle\\ &=\langle\delta^{(1)}_{\bf k}\delta^{(1)}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}\rangle+\langle\delta^{(2)}_{\bf k}\delta^{(2)}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}\rangle+2\langle\delta^{(3)}_{\bf k}\delta^{(1)}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}\rangle\\ &=(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{D}^{(3)}({\bf k}+{\bf k}^{\prime})(P_{11}+P_{13}+P_{22})\,,\end{split}$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 30]: the cross terms with an odd number of initial fields vanish for Gaussian initial conditions, and the factor 2 uses the exchange symmetry of the $P_{13}$ term [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 31]. <!-- num: not a value --> The Gaussian averages give the convolution integrals of Bernardeau et al., Eqs. 164–166, quoted in `formalism.md#standard-perturbation-theory-equations-and`. Ivanov's Eq. 32 writes the same integrals but labels $P_{13}$ as a correlator of $\delta^{(1)}$ with $\delta^{(2)}$ (hub `stale-claims.md`). In $P_{22}$ two short modes q and k − q produce a long mode, while $P_{13}$ is the modulation of the existing mode k by a mode q (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 32].

### Step 4: infrared limits cancel
<!-- key: step-4-infrared-limits-cancel -->

For soft loop momenta the two terms are separately enhanced by the displacement variance and cancel: $\begin{split}&P_{22}(k)\Big|_{q\ll k,~|{\bf k}-{\bf q}|\ll k}=\frac{k^{2}}{3}P_{11}(k)\int_{q\ll k}~\frac{d^{3}q}{q^{2}}P_{11}(q)\\ &P_{13}(k)\Big|_{q\ll k}=-\frac{k^{2}}{3}P_{11}(k)\int_{q\ll k}~\frac{d^{3}q}{q^{2}}P_{11}(q)\,.\end{split}$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 35]. The sum left after the cancellation is five times smaller than each term [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 35]; <!-- num: not a value --> the remainder, Eq. 36, and its failure for the BAO wiggles lead to IR resummation (`deep/ir-resummation.md`). The cancellation follows from the equivalence principle, which the expansion of the final field in the initial one violates term by term [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 35]; Bernardeau et al. attribute it to Galilean invariance and note that it holds for the leading IR divergences to any number of loops (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Sect. 4.2.2, text before Eq. 167].

### Step 5: ultraviolet limits
<!-- key: step-5-ultraviolet-limits -->

- For $q\gg k$ Ivanov gives the UV limits with coefficients $\frac{9}{196\pi^{2}}$ for $P_{22}$, proportional to $k^{4}$ and independent of $P_{11}(k)$, and $-\frac{61}{630\pi^{2}}$ for $P_{13}$, proportional to $k^{2}P_{11}(k)$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 39]; the display carries the label of the IR limit, a slip recorded in the hub's `stale-claims.md`, so the same coefficients are quoted from Eqs. 72 and 76 (`formalism.md#effective-field-theory-stress-tensor`). <!-- num: not a value --> <!-- eq: notation -->
- Baldauf et al. give the next term of the $P_{13}$ expansion as well, $P_{13}^{q_{1}\to\infty}=-k^{2}P\underbrace{\frac{61}{630}\int_{\bm{q}}\frac{P(q)}{q^{2}}}_{l^{2}\equiv\frac{61}{210}\sigma^{2}_{d}}+k^{4}P\frac{2}{105}\int_{\bm{q}}\frac{P(q)}{q^{4}}+\ldots$ (arXiv:1507.02256) [src: Eq. 10], with $\sigma_{d}^{2}=1/6\pi^{2}\int\text{d}q\;P(q)$ the one-dimensional displacement dispersion [src: Sect. II.2, inline, text after Eq. 10]; in their normalisation $P_{13}$ is half of Ivanov's (section "Conventions"). <!-- num: not a value -->
- Physical reading: the UV part of $P_{22}$ is two short modes generating power on large scales, as in Peebles' $k^{4}$ argument; the UV part of $P_{13}$ is the modulation of $P_{11}(k)$ by short-scale displacements (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 39].
- Divergence for power-law spectra $P_{11}\propto q^{\nu}$: Ivanov states that $P_{22}$ diverges for $\nu\geq 1/2$ and $P_{13}$ for $\nu\geq-1$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 39]; Simonović et al. that $P_{22}$ converges for $-1<\nu<1/2$ and that $P_{13}$ diverges in the UV for $\nu>-1$ and in the IR for $\nu<-1$, so is never finite in a power-law cosmology (arXiv:1708.08130) [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eqs. 2.20 and 2.27]. Bernardeau et al. state the $P_{22}$ threshold as $n\geq 1$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Sect. 4.2.2, text before Eq. 167]; the two later papers print 1/2. <!-- num: not a value --> <!-- eq: notation -->
- For the ΛCDM spectrum both integrals converge, but over modes q ≫ k that no perfect fluid describes (halo virialisation), so convergence is not to the right answer; the error is small at one loop and large at two and three loops, where the leading UV terms are negative and proportional to $k^{2}P_{11}$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 39]. A cutoff $\Lambda\sim O(R^{-1}_{\rm vir})$ makes the result cutoff dependent, and the pressureless fluid has no means to remove that dependence [src: Sect. 3.3, last paragraph]. Bernardeau et al. had called one-loop corrections "meaningless at face value" for scale-free spectra with $n\geq-1$, and the problem "rather academic" for CDM spectra (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Sect. 4.2.2, text before Eq. 167]. <!-- num: not a value -->

### Step 6: the EFT equations with a cutoff
<!-- key: step-6-the-eft-equations-with-a-cutoff -->

The smoothed equations acquire the stress tensor of `formalism.md#effective-field-theory-stress-tensor`; in Ivanov's variables, $\begin{split}&\dot{\delta}_{\bf k}-\Theta_{\bf k}=\int_{{\bf q}_{1}{\bf q}_{2}}^{\Lambda}\delta^{(3)}_{D}({\bf k}-{\bf q}_{12})\frac{{\bf q}_{12}\cdot{\bf q}_{1}}{q_{1}^{2}}\Theta_{{\bf q}_{1}}\delta_{{\bf q}_{2}}\,,\\ &\dot{\Theta}_{\bf k}-\frac{3}{2}\delta_{\bf k}+\frac{1}{2}\Theta_{\bf k}=\int^{\Lambda}_{{\bf q}_{1}{\bf q}_{2}}\delta^{(3)}_{D}({\bf k}-{\bf q}_{12})\frac{{\bf q}^{2}_{12}({\bf q}_{1}\cdot{\bf q}_{2})}{2q_{1}^{2}q_{2}^{2}}\Theta_{{\bf q}_{1}}\Theta_{{\bf q}_{2}}-\frac{k^{2}c_{s}^{2}(\tau)}{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\delta_{\bf k}+\frac{\partial_{i}J^{i}}{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\,,\end{split}$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 65], where the low-pass filter is assumed equivalent to cutting the integrals at Λ [src: Sect. 4.3, text after Eq. 65]. Equivalently the source of the θ equation is shifted, $\mathcal{H}^{2}\mathcal{S}_{\beta}\to\mathcal{H}^{2}\mathcal{S}_{\beta}+\tau_{\theta}\,,\quad\tau_{\theta}\equiv\partial^{i}\left[\frac{1}{\rho_{\ell}}\partial^{j}\sigma_{ij}\right]=\left(c_{s}^{2}\partial^{2}\delta+\partial^{i}J_{i}\right)\,.$ [src: Eq. 66], and the stress correction to the density is a Green's-function integral, $\delta_{\bf k}^{(\sigma)}=\int da^{\prime}G_{\delta}(a,a^{\prime})\tau_{\theta}(a^{\prime})\,,$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 67].

The density is split as $\delta^{\rm NL}_{\bf k}=\delta^{(1)}_{\bf k}+\delta^{(2)}_{\bf k}+\delta^{(3)}_{\bf k}+\delta^{\rm stress.}_{\bf k}+\delta^{\rm stoch.}_{\bf k}\,,$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 68], where the $\delta^{(n)}$ are the fluid solutions of Steps 2–3 restricted to $k\leq\Lambda$, the stochastic piece correlates only with itself, and the stress piece, with $\delta^{(1)}$ inserted in the pressure term, is Eq. 69 of `formalism.md#effective-field-theory-stress-tensor` [src: Sect. 4.3, text after Eq. 68]. The total is Eq. 70 of the same section, in which $\gamma_{\Lambda}$ and $R_{\rm stoch}$ are bare parameters containing Λ-dependent and finite parts [src: Sect. 4.3, text after Eq. 70].

### Step 7: renormalisation
<!-- key: step-7-renormalisation -->

1. Split the $P_{13}$ integral at a scale $q_{\rm trust}$ below which the EFT is trusted: $\begin{split}P_{13}^{\Lambda}=&6P_{11}(k)\int_{{\bf q},~q\leq q_{\rm trust}}~F_{3}({\bf k},{\bf q},-{\bf q})P_{11}(q)\\ &+6P_{11}(k)\int_{{\bf q},~q_{\rm trust}\leq q\leq\Lambda}~F_{3}({\bf k},{\bf q},-{\bf q})P_{11}(q)\,.\end{split}$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 71].
2. For $\Lambda>q_{\rm trust}\gg k$ the second term takes the UV form of Eq. 72 (`formalism.md#effective-field-theory-stress-tensor`), proportional to $k^{2}P_{11}(k)$, which diverges as Λ → ∞ and is cancelled by choosing $\gamma_{\Lambda}$ as in Eq. 73 (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Sect. 4.3, Eqs. 72–73]. <!-- num: not a value --> <!-- eq: notation -->
3. The physical $P_{13}$-like term is then cutoff independent: $P_{13}^{\Lambda}-2\gamma_{\Lambda}k^{2}P_{11}=-2\gamma_{\rm finite}k^{2}P_{11}+6P_{11}(k)\int_{{\bf q},~q\leq q_{\rm trust}}~F_{3}({\bf k},{\bf q},-{\bf q})P_{11}(q)\,,$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 74]; any Λ can be used, even Λ = ∞, and $\gamma_{\rm finite}$ is not fixed by the theory but fitted to data [src: Sect. 4.3, text after Eq. 74].
4. The UV part of $P_{22}$ is absorbed by the noise term in the same way, $\begin{split}&P_{22}^{\Lambda}+P_{J}=2\int_{{\bf q},~q\leq q_{\rm trust}}[F_{2}({\bf k}-{\bf q},{\bf q})]^{2}P_{11}(q)P_{11}({\bf k}-{\bf q})\\ &+2\int_{{\bf q},~q_{\rm trust}<q\leq\Lambda}[F_{2}({\bf k}-{\bf q},{\bf q})]^{2}P_{11}(q)P_{11}({\bf k}-{\bf q})+P_{J}\\ &=2\int_{{\bf q},~q\leq q_{\rm trust}}[F_{2}({\bf k}-{\bf q},{\bf q})]^{2}P_{11}(q)P_{11}({\bf k}-{\bf q})+k^{4}\left[\frac{9}{196\pi^{2}}\int_{q_{\rm trust}}^{\Lambda}~\frac{dq}{q^{2}}P^{2}_{11}(q)+R^{7}_{\rm stoch}\right]\\ &=2\int_{{\bf q},~q\leq q_{\rm trust}}[F_{2}({\bf k}-{\bf q},{\bf q})]^{2}P_{11}(q)P_{11}({\bf k}-{\bf q})+(R^{\rm finite}_{\rm stoch})^{7}k^{4}\,,\end{split}$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 75], with the running of $R_{\rm stoch}$ of Eq. 76 (`formalism.md#effective-field-theory-stress-tensor`).
5. The renormalised one-loop spectrum is $\begin{split}&P_{\text{non-linear}}(k)=P_{11}(k,\eta)+2\int_{{\bf q},~q\leq q_{\rm trust}}[F_{2}({\bf k}-{\bf q},{\bf q})]^{2}P_{11}(q)P_{11}({\bf k}-{\bf q})\\ &+6P_{11}(k)\int_{{\bf q},~q\leq q_{\rm trust}}~F_{3}({\bf k},{\bf q},-{\bf q})P_{11}(q)-2\gamma_{\rm finite}k^{2}P_{11}(k,\eta)+(R^{\rm finite}_{\rm stoch})^{7}k^{4}\,,\end{split}$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eq. 77], with two Wilson coefficients. The physical $q_{\rm trust}$ is about $0.3~h\text{Mpc}^{-1}$ at $z\sim 0.5$, but the ΛCDM one-loop integrals saturate around $k_{\rm eq}$, so the result hardly depends on $q_{\rm trust}>k_{\rm eq}$ and $q_{\rm trust}=\infty$ may be used [src: Sect. 4.3, text after Eq. 77]. The $k^{4}$ noise term is negligible at one loop by power counting (`formalism.md#effective-field-theory-stress-tensor`, Eq. 83 and its caveat).

### Step 8: the same chain in the original EFT paper
<!-- key: step-8-the-same-chain-in-the-original-eft-paper -->

Carrasco, Hertzberg and Senatore (arXiv:1206.2926) compute the counterterm diagram $P_{13,\;c^{2}_{\rm\,comb}}(k,a_{0})=-2\frac{k^{2}}{D(a_{0})}\int^{a_{0}}_{0}d\tilde{a}\;G(a_{0},\tilde{a})\,c_{\rm\,comb}^{2}(\tilde{a})\,D(\tilde{a})\,P_{11,l}(k,\Lambda)\ .$ [src: Eq. 57], with the exact Green's function in place of the EdS one, and split the coefficient as $c_{\rm\,comb}^{2}(a,\Lambda)=c_{\rm\,comb;ren.}^{2}(a,k_{\rm ren.})+c_{\rm\,comb;ctr.}^{2}(a,\Lambda)\ .$ (arXiv:1206.2926) [src: Eq. 58], the renormalised part being matched to simulations or data at $k=k_{\rm ren.}$ [src: Sect. 3.2.3, inline, text after Eq. 58]. They take the time dependence of the renormalised part equal to that of the counterterm, which follows from the k → 0 limit of $P_{13}$ (Eqs. 59–61), and flag this as an approximation to be checked [src: Sect. 3.2.3, text after Eq. 58]. The coefficient is fitted at Λ = ∞ and run down to finite Λ with Eq. 62; $c^{2}_{\rm\,comb}$ grows as Λ decreases, and the run matches direct N-body measurements at $\Lambda=1/3\,h$ Mpc-1 and $\Lambda=1/6\,h$ Mpc-1 [src: Sect. 3.2.3, text after Eq. 62]. That $c^{2}_{\rm\,comb}\neq 0$ at Λ = ∞ is read as the signature that the underlying theory is free-streaming particles, not a pressureless fluid [src: Sect. 3.2.3, Fig. 3 caption].

### Step 9: the scaling universe
<!-- key: step-9-the-scaling-universe -->

Pajer and Zaldarriaga (arXiv:1301.7182) show the same cancellation for $P_{\rm in}=Ak^{n}$ in EdS. Up to numerical coefficients the UV parts are $P_{13}\simeq a^{4}k^{2}P_{11}(k)\int^{\Lambda}dq\,P_{11}(q)\,,$ [src: Eq. 41] and $P_{22}\simeq a^{4}k^{4}\int^{\Lambda}\frac{dq}{q^{2}}P_{11}(q)^{2}\,,$ (arXiv:1301.7182) [src: Eq. 42], the k dependence of the sound-speed and noise terms. A cutoff breaks self-similarity, so the counterterms do not have the self-similar time dependence, but the renormalised result does [src: Sect. 3.1]. Logarithmic divergences appear at one loop for $n=-1+2m$ from $P_{13}$ and $n=1/2+m$ from $P_{22}$, m a positive integer; the $\ln\Lambda$ is cancelled by a counterterm and a $\ln k$ remains [src: Sect. 3.1, text after Table 2]. <!-- num: not a value --> The renormalised one-loop result is $\Delta^{2}_{1-loop}=\left(\frac{k}{k_{NL}}\right)^{3+n}+\left(\frac{k}{k_{NL}}\right)^{2(3+n)}\left[\alpha(n)+\tilde{\alpha}(n)\ln\left(\frac{k}{k_{NL}}\right)\right]\,.$ (arXiv:1301.7182) [src: Eq. 43], valid for any n > −3 once the counterterms of Eq. 44 are added [src: Sect. 3.1, text after Eq. 44]. <!-- num: not a value --> For n = −1.5 the pressure and dissipative corrections are larger than the two-loop ones (arXiv:1301.7182) [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value -->

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **Dimensional regularisation.** It preserves self-similarity at every step, while the cutoff breaks it at intermediate steps (Pajer & Zaldarriaga, arXiv:1301.7182) [src: Sect. 3.1]. Divergent power-law integrals are set to zero, so the finite coefficients α(n) of Table 2 come out directly; the log-divergent cases must be read with care, and n = −1 also has IR logarithms [src: Sect. 3.1, footnote 4].
- **FFTLog.** Simonović et al. decompose $P_{\rm lin}$ into complex power laws, which turns the loops into massless-QFT integrals with analytic solutions; the cosmology-independent part is computed once and a loop becomes a matrix multiplication (arXiv:1708.08130) [src: abstract]. The $P_{13}$ vector is $M_{13}(\nu_{1})=\frac{1+9\nu_{1}}{4}\frac{\tan(\nu_{1}\pi)}{28\pi(\nu_{1}+1)\nu_{1}(\nu_{1}-1)(\nu_{1}-2)(\nu_{1}-3)}\;.$ (arXiv:1708.08130) [src: Eq. 2.32]. Like dimensional regularisation, the decomposition sets the power-law divergences to zero, so the UV part of $P_{13}$ (for bias ν > −1) or its IR part (ν < −1) must be added by hand; the recommended bias is $-0.5<\nu<0$, and with ν = −0.3 the sum of the two terms differs from direct integration by less than 0.1% at all scales [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 2.32 and Fig. 2 caption]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **FFTLog at two loops.** The two-loop matter spectrum reduces to cosmology-independent kite integrals, evaluated with a Mellin–Barnes representation; it agrees with direct integration at the $10^{-4}$ level relative to the spectrum up to $k=0.2$ $h$Mpc$^{-1}$ after the counterterm and decomposition errors are accounted for (Cappellazzi, Henn & Simonović 2026, arXiv:2610.41914) [src: abstract].
- **Fixing the counterterm.** Three routes: fit at a renormalisation scale with Λ → ∞; direct measurement of the stress correlators in N-body simulations at finite Λ; or a finite-Λ fit including higher-derivative terms in k/Λ, all giving the same $c^{2}_{\rm\,comb}(a_{0},\Lambda)$ (Carrasco et al., arXiv:1206.2926) [src: Sect. 3.2.3, text after Fig. 4 caption].
- **Shell ansatz.** Baldauf et al. fix the ratio of the one- and two-loop counterterms from the response of SPT to a shell of high-k power, leaving one free amplitude (arXiv:1507.02256) [src: Sect. II.2]. The coefficient of the $k^{2}P_{11}$ term at low k is $\kappa_{2}=-\frac{61}{105}\sigma_{d}^{2}-2c_{s}^{2}.$ (arXiv:1507.02256) [src: Eq. 12], so $c_{s}^{2}$ contains all higher-loop contributions to $\kappa_{2}$ and those must not be computed again [src: Sect. II.2, text after Eq. 12]. The one-parameter model describes the density spectrum to $k\approx$ 0.3 h/Mpc at 1% at $z=0$ [src: abstract].
- **Two loops.** Three counterterms ($\delta$, $\delta^{2}$, $\partial^{2}\delta$) are needed; the two-loop EFT then matches simulations up to k ≃ 0.34 h/Mpc at z = 0, 0.55 h/Mpc at z = 1 and 1.1 h/Mpc at z = 2 (Foreman, Perrier & Senatore 2015, arXiv:1507.05326) [src: abstract]; later two-loop work (eight counterterms with vorticity) is in `results.md#computed-coefficients-and-theorems`.
- **Physical origin of the counterterms.** Relating the stress tensor to halos in N-body simulations, halos with virial mass above $10^{12}\,M_\odot/h$ account for ∼95%, ∼85% and ∼70% of the background pressure, effective sound speed and shear viscosity, depending on the cutoff, with accretion feeding the trace and anisotropic accretion the traceless part (Hebbar, de S. Nascimento & Banerjee 2026, arXiv:2610.19001) [src: abstract].
- **Exact time dependence and scale-dependent growth** instead of EdS kernels: `approximations.md#einstein-de-sitter-approximation-for-the-time` and `formalism.md#effective-field-theory-stress-tensor` (Newtonian motion gauge).

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Printing slips in Ivanov's review (v1).** Eq. 28 (dipole of $F_{2}$, $G_{2}$ without the factor one half), Eq. 32 ($P_{13}$ labelled with $\delta^{(2)}$) and Eq. 39 (UV limit labelled $q\ll k$) are recorded in the hub's `stale-claims.md`; quote Bernardeau et al. Eqs. 45–46 and 165–166 and Ivanov's Eqs. 72 and 76 instead (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eqs. 28, 32, 39]. <!-- num: not a value --> Two further details of the same pages, not yet in `stale-claims.md`: in Eq. 25 the factor $D^{n}_{+}(\eta)$ is printed outside the sum over n, and in Eq. 27 the lower summation limit is printed "m−1" (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eqs. 25, 27]; Bernardeau et al. print $\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0112551) [src: Eqs. 43–44]. In Eq. 32 the argument of the first kernel of $P_{22}$ is printed ${\bf q}-{\bf k}$, while Eq. 75 of the same review writes ${\bf k}-{\bf q}$ (arXiv:2212.08488) [src: Eqs. 32, 75]. <!-- num: not a value --> <!-- eq: notation -->
- **Apparent slips in the FFTLog paper (v1).** Eq. 2.26 prints the UV part of $P_{22}$ with $P_{\rm lin}(q)/q^{2}$ under the integral, where Ivanov's Eq. 76 and Pajer and Zaldarriaga's Eq. 42 have the square of the spectrum; Eq. 2.28 prints $P_{\rm lin}(q)$ outside the integral where the text speaks of fixed k (arXiv:1708.08130) [src: Eqs. 2.26, 2.28]. `[der]` On dimensional grounds the square is needed in Eq. 2.26, and the spectrum in Eq. 2.28 is evaluated at the external k. No correction published was found.
- **A factor 2 in $P_{13}$.** Papers that write $2P_{13}+P_{22}$ (Baldauf et al., arXiv:1507.02256, Sect. II.2) normalise $P_{13}$ as half of Ivanov's and Bernardeau's; a UV coefficient moved between them changes by 2. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **FFTLog bias out of range.** With a bias outside the convergent range the separate $P_{13}$ and $P_{22}$ are very different from their true values while their sum is right for $-3<\nu<-1$; a code that outputs $P_{13}$ alone, or forgets to add $P_{13}^{\rm UV}$ for ν > −1, is wrong (arXiv:1708.08130) [src: Sect. 2.2, Fig. 3 caption and last paragraph]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Counterterm read as a pure UV term.** After matching, $c_{s}^{2}$ also absorbs higher-loop contributions to the $k^{2}P_{11}$ term, so it acquires an apparent scale dependence from k = 0.1 h/Mpc at z = 0 once two-loop terms matter (Baldauf et al., arXiv:1507.02256) [src: abstract]; a value fitted at one loop is not the two-loop value.
- **Time dependence of the counterterm.** Carrasco et al. set the time dependence of the renormalised coefficient equal to that of the divergent part as an approximation (arXiv:1206.2926) [src: Sect. 3.2.3, text after Eq. 58]; Foreman et al. parametrise the time dependence of the two-loop coefficients separately (arXiv:1507.05326) [src: abstract].
- **An earlier dimensional-regularisation formula.** Pajer and Zaldarriaga report a sign typo in the fourth term of $P_{22}$ in the dimensional-regularisation formulae of their ref. [6], which should read −11/4, and re-derive it in their Appendix A.1 (arXiv:1301.7182) [src: Sect. 3.1, footnote 3]. <!-- num: not a value -->
