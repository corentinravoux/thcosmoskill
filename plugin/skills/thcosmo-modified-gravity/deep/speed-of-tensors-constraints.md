## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- Creminelli & Vernizzi (arXiv:1710.05877, Creminelli & Vernizzi 2017) define $X\equiv g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi$, without the factor −1/2 of Kobayashi's X, write the cubic term as $+G_3\Box\phi$ and the quartic term with $-2G_{4,X}$, and use the EFT of dark energy in unitary gauge on $ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+a^{2}(t)d\vec{x}^{2}$ (arXiv:1710.05877) [src: text before Eq. 1; Eq. 8 and text after]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- Creminelli, Lewandowski, Tambalo & Vernizzi (arXiv:1809.03484, Creminelli et al. 2018) keep $X\equiv g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi$ and name the beyond-Horndeski coefficient $\alpha_{\rm H}\equiv 2\tilde m_4^2/M_{\rm Pl}^2$ (arXiv:1809.03484) [src: Sect. 5, inline, text after Eq. 70; Eq. 47]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- Ezquiaga & Zumalacárregui (arXiv:1710.05901, Ezquiaga & Zumalacárregui 2017) define $X\equiv-\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi/2$, write $\mathcal{L}_4=G_4R+G_{4,X}[(\Box\phi)^2-\phi_{;\mu\nu}\phi^{;\mu\nu}]$ with a plus sign, use cosmic time in their tensor equation, and set c = 1 after their Eq. 1 (arXiv:1710.05901) [src: Eqs. 2, 5, text after Eq. 7]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- Baker et al. (arXiv:1710.06394, Baker et al. 2017) use Kobayashi's normalisation $X\equiv-\nabla^{\nu}\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi/2$ and Kobayashi's sign $\mathcal{L}_3=-G_3\Box\phi$; for generalised Proca, $X=-\frac{1}{2}A_{\mu}A^{\mu}$ (arXiv:1710.06394) [src: text after Eq. 3; text after Eq. 7]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- De Rham & Melville (arXiv:1806.09417, de Rham & Melville 2018) call $c_T$ "the GW phase velocity" and give scales as frequencies in Hz [src: text after Eq. 1; Eq. 5]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- The four normalisations of X and the two signs of the cubic term are not converted into each other here; the skill's conventions block is `formalism.md#conventions`.

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — the bound and what it measures
<!-- key: step-1-the-bound-and-what-it-measures -->

The LVK–Fermi–INTEGRAL paper uses the observed delay "(+1.74 ± 0.05) s" between GRB 170817A and GW170817 to "constrain the difference between the speed of gravity and the speed of light to be between −3 × 10⁻¹⁵ and +7 × 10⁻¹⁶ times the speed of light" (arXiv:1710.05834, LIGO Scientific Collaboration et al. 2017) [src: abstract]. Ezquiaga & Zumalacárregui state the assumptions behind it: "Taking the lowest limit $d_{L}=26$ Mpc and a conservative 10 s delay between the GW and sGRB", $-3\cdot 10^{-15}\leq c_{g}/c-1\leq 7\cdot 10^{-16}\,.$ (arXiv:1710.05901) [src: Eq. 1]; the symmetric $|c_{g}/c-1|\leq 5\cdot 10^{-16}$ that they use afterwards is chosen "For simplicity" (arXiv:1710.05901) [src: text after Eq. 1]. <!-- eq: notation -->

Baker et al. write the tensor action in the medium as $S_{h}=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{3}xdt\,M_{*}^{2}\left[{\dot{h}}_{A}^{2}-c^{2}_{T}(\nabla h_{A})^{2}\right]\,.$ [src: Eq. 1], with $c^{2}_{T}=1+\alpha_{T}\,.$ [src: Eq. 2], and, in the geometric-optics limit and from $\Delta t\simeq1.7$ s and $d_s\simeq40$ Mpc, obtain $\left|\alpha_{T}\right|\lesssim 1\times 10^{-15}.$ (arXiv:1710.06394) [src: Eq. 3]; an emission delay between the two signals "would only serve to tighten the bound" [src: text before Eq. 3]. <!-- eq: notation --> Ezquiaga & Zumalacárregui write the propagation as $\ddot{h}_{ij}+(3+\alpha_{{}_{M}})H\dot{h}_{ij}+(1+\alpha_{{}_{T}})k^{2}h_{ij}=0\,,$ (arXiv:1710.05901) [src: Eq. 2] and rescale the bound as $|\alpha_{{}_{T}}|<9\cdot 10^{-16}\left(\frac{40\text{Mpc}}{d}\right)\left(\frac{\Delta t}{1.7\text{s}}\right)\,,$ (arXiv:1710.05901) [src: Eq. 10].

Earlier bounds: gravitational Cherenkov radiation from cosmic rays constrains negative values, $\alpha_{T}>-10^{-15}$, and the travel time between the two LIGO detectors gave $\alpha_{T}<0.42$ (arXiv:1710.06394) [src: inline, text after Eq. 2]; the GW170817 bound "applies to $c_{g}>c$ unlike bounds from absence of gravitational Cherenkov radiation" (arXiv:1710.05901) [src: text after Eq. 1]. <!-- eq: notation --> Creminelli & Vernizzi add that the Cherenkov limits "are only applicable to high energy GWs, well outside the regime of validity of the EFTs describing Dark Energy and Modified Gravity", and, as printed, that "these bounds only apply to GWs travelling faster, and not slower, than light" (arXiv:1710.05877) [src: text before Eq. 1]: the direction printed there is the opposite of the one printed by Baker et al. and by Ezquiaga & Zumalacárregui at the places just cited.

What the bound probes, in the authors' words: "a low energy measurement, at a scale as low as 10 000 km", where "the cutoff may be of the same order as the measured GW momentum", although "one does not expect that high-energy corrections conspire to completely cancel the modification of the GW speed" (arXiv:1710.05877) [src: text before Eq. 1]; Baker et al. speak of "the GW with the wavelength of 3000 km (to which aLIGO/VIRGO are sensitive)" [src: Caveats paragraph, text after Eq. 8]; de Rham & Melville place it "at a frequency of 10-100 Hz", and the GW170817 signal "from 24 Hz to a few hundred Hz" (arXiv:1806.09417) [src: text after Eq. 1; text after Eq. 4]. <!-- num: not a value -->

### Step 2 — the speed of tensors in the EFT of dark energy
<!-- key: step-2-the-speed-of-tensors-in-the-eft-of-dark -->

Creminelli & Vernizzi start from the unitary-gauge action, in three rows, $\begin{split}S=&\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\bigg[\frac{M_{*}^{2}}{2}f\,{}^{(4)}\!R-\Lambda-cg^{00}+\frac{m_{2}^{4}}{2}(\delta g^{00})^{2}\\ &-\frac{m_{3}^{3}}{2}\,\delta K\delta g^{00}-m_{4}^{2}\delta{\cal K}_{2}+\frac{\tilde{m}_{4}^{2}}{2}\,\delta g^{00}R-\frac{m_{5}^{2}}{2}\delta g^{00}\delta{\cal K}_{2}\\ &-\frac{m_{6}}{3}\delta{\cal K}_{3}-\tilde{m}_{6}\delta g^{00}\delta{\cal G}_{2}-\frac{m_{7}}{3}\delta g^{00}\delta{\cal K}_{3}\bigg]\;.\end{split}$ [src: Eq. 1], with $\begin{split}\delta{\cal K}_{2}&\equiv\delta K^{2}-\delta K_{\mu}^{\nu}\delta K^{\mu}_{\nu}\;,\qquad\delta{\cal G}_{2}\equiv\delta K_{\mu}^{\nu}R^{\mu}_{\nu}-\delta KR/2\;,\\ \delta{\cal K}_{3}&\equiv\delta K^{3}-3\delta K\delta K_{\mu}^{\nu}\delta K^{\mu}_{\nu}+2\delta K_{\mu}^{\nu}\delta K^{\mu}_{\rho}\delta K_{\nu}^{\rho}\;.\end{split}$ (arXiv:1710.05877) [src: Eq. 2]. It describes Horndeski "for $\tilde{m}_{4}^{2}=m_{4}^{2}$ and $\tilde{m}_{6}=m_{6}$" and beyond Horndeski otherwise, and keeps at higher order only the operators "that contribute to the leading number of spatial derivatives" [src: text after Eq. 2]. <!-- eq: notation -->

Only $m_4^2\delta{\cal K}_2$ changes the graviton kinetic term at quadratic order, through the effective Planck mass $M^{2}\equiv M_{*}^{2}f+2m_{4}^{2}$ (arXiv:1710.05877) [src: inline, text after Eq. 2], so that $c_{T}^{2}-1=-{2m_{4}^{2}}/{M^{2}}\;.$ (arXiv:1710.05877) [src: Eq. 3], and GW170817 sets $m_{4}^{2}=0\;.$ [src: Eq. 4]. In the covariant theory with the beyond-Horndeski functions $F_4$, $F_5$ (their Eq. 8), the coefficients are, in seven rows, $\begin{split}M^{2}&=2G_{4}-4XG_{4,X}-X\big(G_{5,\phi}+2H\dot{\phi}G_{5,X}\big)\\ &+2X^{2}F_{4}-6H\dot{\phi}X^{2}F_{5}\;,\\ m_{4}^{2}&=\tilde{m}_{4}^{2}+X^{2}F_{4}-3H\dot{\phi}X^{2}F_{5}\;,\\ \tilde{m}_{4}^{2}&=-\big[2XG_{4,X}+XG_{5,\phi}+\big(H\dot{\phi}-\ddot{\phi}\big)XG_{5,X}\big]\;,\\ m_{5}^{2}&=X\big[2G_{4,X}+4XG_{4,XX}+H\dot{\phi}(3G_{5,X}+2XG_{5,XX})+G_{5,\phi}\\ &+XG_{5,X\phi}-4XF_{4}-2X^{2}F_{4,X}+H\dot{\phi}X\big(15F_{5}+6XF_{5,X}\big)\big]\;,\\ m_{6}&=\tilde{m}_{6}-3\dot{\phi}X^{2}F_{5}\;,\qquad\tilde{m}_{6}=-\dot{\phi}XG_{5,X}\;,\\ m_{7}&=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}X\big(3G_{5,X}+2XG_{5,XX}+15XF_{5}+6X^{2}F_{5,X}\big)\;.\end{split}$ (arXiv:1710.05877) [src: Eq. 10]. When both quartic and quintic beyond-Horndeski terms are present, degeneracy requires $XG_{5,X}F_{4}=3F_{5}\left[G_{4}-2XG_{4,X}-(X/2)G_{5,\phi}\right]\;,$ (arXiv:1710.05877) [src: Eq. 9].

The same quantity in Baker et al.'s convention, for Horndeski: $M^{2}_{*}\alpha_{T}\equiv 2X\left[2G_{4,X}-2G_{5,\phi}-\left(\ddot{\phi}-\dot{\phi}H\right)G_{5,X}\right]$ [src: Eq. 5] with $M^{2}_{*}\equiv 2\left(G_{4}-2XG_{4,X}+XG_{5,\phi}-{\dot{\phi}}HXG_{5,X}\right)$ (arXiv:1710.06394) [src: inline, text after Eq. 5]; and with the beyond-Horndeski functions $\tilde G_4$, $\tilde G_5$, in two rows, $\alpha_{\textrm{T}}M_{*}^{2}=4X\left(G_{4,X}-\tilde{G}_{4,X}-G_{5,\phi}\right)-2\ddot{\phi}XG_{5,X}$ $+2\dot{\phi}HX\left(G_{5,X}-\tilde{G}_{5,X}\right)\,,$ (arXiv:1710.06394) [src: Eq. 6]. <!-- eq: notation -->

### Step 3 — the tuning argument: luminal on nearby backgrounds too
<!-- key: step-3-the-tuning-argument-luminal-on-nearby -->

The step that turns one measurement into conditions on functions: "the value of this parameter depends on the particular background", and "To robustly set to zero $m_{4}^{2}$ we should set to zero also all those operators that can generate it by a small change of the background solution", for example "a small variation of the dark matter abundance" (arXiv:1710.05877) [src: text after Eq. 4; text before Eq. 1]. <!-- eq: notation -->

- $m_5^2$ shifts $m_4^2$ by $\delta m_{4}^{2}=m_{5}^{2}\delta g^{00}_{\rm bkgd}/2$, which can be compensated by $\tilde{m}_{4}^{2}=m_{5}^{2}\qquad(=0\;{\rm in\;Horndeski)}\;,$ (arXiv:1710.05877) [src: Eq. 5, inline, text after Eq. 4]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- $m_6$, $\tilde m_6$ and $m_7$ shift $m_4^2$ through $\delta H_{\rm bkgd}$ or through derivatives of $\delta g^{00}_{\rm bkgd}$, "Since the background enters differently in all these operators, they must be precisely set to zero", $m_{6}=\tilde{m}_{6}=m_{7}=0\;.$ (arXiv:1710.05877) [src: Eq. 6]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- Covariantly, $c_T=1$ "must hold for any value of $\ddot{\phi}$, H and $\dot{\phi}$ (or X)", which gives $G_{5,X}=0\;,\qquad F_{5}=0\;,\qquad 2G_{4,X}-XF_{4}+G_{5,\phi}=0\;,$ (arXiv:1710.05877) [src: Eq. 11]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- The exception, stated by the authors: if dark energy "has a fixed $\dot{\phi}$ independently of H", i.e. c = 0 in Eq. 1, a change of history does not change $\dot\phi$, and one only needs $G_{5,X}+3XF_{5}=0$ on the attractor; with the degeneracy condition (Eq. 9) and $m_4^2=0$ this implies "the pathological value M=0, unless $G_{5,X}$ and $F_{5}$ separately vanish" (arXiv:1710.05877) [src: inline, text after Eq. 13]. <!-- eq: notation -->

Ezquiaga & Zumalacárregui give the same argument through the effective metric of tensor modes, $\mathcal{G}^{\mu\nu}=Cg^{\mu\nu}+D\phi^{,\mu}\phi^{,\nu}+E\phi^{;\mu\nu}\,,$ [src: Eq. 14]: quartic theories produce D terms and quintic theories also E terms, and a cancellation "is robust against perturbations only if the different terms involved have the same tensor structure"; a D–E cancellation on the cosmological solution is broken by a perturbation φ, giving the direction-dependent $c_{g}^{2}=\frac{\omega^{2}}{k^{2}}=1+2\frac{E}{C}\hat{k}\cdot\vec{\partial}\dot{\varphi}+\cdots\,,$ (arXiv:1710.05901) [src: Eq. 15], hence "tuning the cosmological evolution is not a viable solution" [src: text after Eq. 15]. Baker et al.: a cancellation between $G_{4,X}$, $G_{5,\phi}$ and $G_{5,X}$ "would not only have to be time-dependent, but also sensitive to the matter content of the universe due to the dependence on H and $\ddot{\phi}$", and "would be accidental, with no symmetry to protect it" (arXiv:1710.06394) [src: text after Eq. 5]. <!-- eq: notation -->

The worked example is the covariant Galileon on its tracker, $\alpha_{{}_{T}}=\frac{1}{M_{*}^{2}E^{4}}\left[2c_{4}\xi^{4}+c_{5}\xi^{5}\left(1+\frac{\dot{H}}{H^{2}}\right)\right]\,,$ (arXiv:1710.05901) [src: Eq. 8]. "Assuming the non-fine tuned case with no cancellations", $|c_{4}|<\frac{\alpha_{{}_{T}}}{2\xi^{4}}\approx 2.8\cdot 10^{-17}\left(\frac{2}{\xi}\right)^{4}\,,$ [src: Eq. 11] and $|c_{5}|<\frac{\alpha_{{}_{T}}}{0.75\xi^{5}}\approx 3.8\cdot 10^{-17}\left(\frac{2}{\xi}\right)^{5}\,$ [src: Eq. 12], hence $|M_{*}^{2}-1|<1.9\cdot 10^{-15}\,,\qquad|\alpha_{M}|<1.9\cdot 10^{-15}\,.$ (arXiv:1710.05901) [src: Eq. 13], bounds "specific to Galileon gravity" [src: text after Eq. 13]. Quintic models compatible with GW170817 exist only where $\Delta t\approx\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{E}}^{t_{O}}\alpha_{T}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\lesssim 1.7$ s, a "very narrow and fine-tuned region" that "will not be robust to deviations from the cosmological solution" (arXiv:1710.05901) [src: inline, text after Eq. 13]. <!-- eq: notation -->

### Step 4 — radiative stability of the surviving relations
<!-- key: step-4-radiative-stability-of-the-surviving -->

With $G_{4}(X)=\frac{\Lambda_{2}^{8}}{\Lambda_{3}^{6}}\hat{G}_{4}\left(\frac{X}{\Lambda_{2}^{4}}\right)\;,\qquad G_{5}(X)=\frac{\Lambda_{2}^{8}}{\Lambda_{3}^{9}}\hat{G}_{5}\left(\frac{X}{\Lambda_{2}^{4}}\right)\;.$ (arXiv:1710.05877) [src: Eq. 14], shift symmetry assumed, $\Lambda_{2}\sim(M_{\rm Pl}H_{0})^{1/2}$ and $\Lambda_{3}\sim(M_{\rm Pl}H_{0}^{2})^{1/3}$, and polynomial $\hat G$ "with order one coefficients", the coefficients are corrected by $\delta c_{n}\sim(\Lambda_{3}/\Lambda_{2})^{4}\sim 10^{-40}$, "completely negligible unless one goes to extraordinary large n"; the relations are "technically natural in the sense that once imposed at tree level they are stable under quantum corrections" (arXiv:1710.05877) [src: inline, text after Eq. 14]. Baker et al. quote "A typical value is $\Lambda_{3}\sim 10^{-13}$ eV, leading to $\Lambda_{3}/\Lambda_{2}\sim 10^{-10}$", with the same $10^{-40}$ obtained "With relatively mild assumptions on the $G_{i}$ functions", and add that "perturbative control of quantum corrections in the fast-moving models depends on shift symmetry, which would disallow any dependence on φ in the action, specifically the conformal coupling f(φ)" (arXiv:1710.06394) [src: text after Eq. 5]. Creminelli et al. state the same suppression and that it implies "the stability of the condition that GWs travel luminally, at the 2-derivative level" (arXiv:1809.03484) [src: Sect. 4.2]. <!-- eq: notation -->

### Step 5 — the luminal theories
<!-- key: step-5-the-luminal-theories -->

From Eq. 11, after integrations by parts, $\begin{split}&L_{c_{T}=1}=G_{2}(\phi,X)+G_{3}(\phi,X)\Box\phi+B_{4}(\phi,X)\,{}^{(4)}\!R\\ &-\frac{4}{X}B_{4,X}(\phi,X)(\phi^{\mu}\phi^{\nu}\phi_{\mu\nu}\Box\phi-\phi^{\mu}\phi_{\mu\nu}\phi_{\lambda}\phi^{\lambda\nu})\;,\end{split}$ with $B_{4}\equiv G_{4}+XG_{5,\phi}/2$ (arXiv:1710.05877) [src: Eq. 12, inline, text after Eq. 12]; by Gauss–Codazzi, $L_{c_{T}=1}=G_{2}+G_{3}\Box\phi+B_{4}(R+K_{\mu}^{\nu}K^{\mu}_{\nu}-K^{2})\;,$ (arXiv:1710.05877) [src: Eq. 13], and without beyond-Horndeski operator $B_4$ is a function of φ only, "a standard conformal coupling to the 4d Ricci scalar" [src: text after Eq. 13]. Under a conformal transformation $g_{\mu\nu}\to C(\phi,X)g_{\mu\nu}$, C not linear in X, the most general degenerate theory obtained from Horndeski "compatible with $c_{T}^{2}=1$", a type Ia DHOST theory, is, in three rows, $\begin{split}&L_{c_{T}=1}=\tilde{G}_{2}+\tilde{G}_{3}\Box\phi+CB_{4}\,{}^{(4)}\!R-\frac{4CB_{4,X}}{X}\phi^{\mu}\phi^{\nu}\phi_{\mu\nu}\Box\phi\\ &+\bigg(\frac{4CB_{4,X}}{X}+\frac{6B_{4}C_{,X}{}^{2}}{C}+8C_{,X}B_{4,X}\bigg)\phi^{\mu}\phi_{\mu\nu}\phi_{\lambda}\phi^{\lambda\nu}\\ &-\frac{8C_{,X}B_{4,X}}{X}(\phi_{\mu}\phi^{\mu\nu}\phi_{\nu})^{2}\;.\end{split}$ (arXiv:1710.05877) [src: Eq. 16]. The DHOST quadratic operators $\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\frac{M^{2}}{2}\bigg(-\frac{2}{3}\alpha_{L}\delta K^{2}+4\beta_{1}\delta KV+\beta_{2}V^{2}+{\beta_{3}}a_{i}a^{i}\bigg)\;,$ (arXiv:1710.05877) [src: Eq. 15] "do not affect the speed of GWs", on the given background and on nearby ones [src: text after Eq. 15]. The acknowledgements thank colleagues for "pointing out sign typos respectively in eq. (16) and (8)"; the equations above are those of the v3 rendering [src: Acknowledgements]. <!-- eq: notation -->

Ezquiaga & Zumalacárregui reach the same set by two constructions. Within Horndeski, theories survive only if $G_{4,X}\approx 0\,,\quad G_{5}\approx\text{constant}\,,$ (arXiv:1710.05901) [src: Eq. 16], "satisfied only by the simple models contained in $G_{2}(X,\phi),G_{3}(X,\phi),G_{4}(\phi)$" [src: text after Eq. 16]. Beyond Horndeski, a transformation $\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}=\Omega^{2}(\phi,X)g_{\mu\nu}+\mathcal{D}(\phi,X)\phi_{,\mu}\phi_{,\nu}\,,$ [src: Eq. 17] of the gravity sector alone changes the speed to $\tilde{c}_{g}^{2}=\frac{c_{g}^{2}(\tilde{X})}{1+2\tilde{X}\mathcal{D}}\,,$ (arXiv:1710.05901) [src: Eq. 18]; one either starts from a luminal Horndeski theory and applies a conformal transformation, $\mathcal{L}_{C}=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\left(\Omega^{2}R+6\Omega_{,\alpha}\Omega^{,\alpha}\right)+\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{2}+\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{3}\,,$ [src: Eq. 19], or compensates the quartic anomaly with the GLPV term $\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{4}^{bH}=F_{4}(\phi,X)\big(\phi_{,\mu}\phi^{;\mu\nu}&\phi_{;\nu\rho}\phi^{\rho}-\phi_{,\mu}\phi^{\mu\nu}\phi_{,\nu}\Box\phi\\ &-X(\left(\Box\phi\right)^{2}-\phi_{;\mu\nu}\phi^{;\mu\nu})\big)\,.\end{split}$ [src: Eq. 20], so that $c_{g}^{2}=\frac{G_{4}}{G_{4}-2X(G_{4,X}-XF_{4})}=1\Leftrightarrow F_{4}=G_{4,X}/X\,.$ (arXiv:1710.05901) [src: Eq. 21], a cancellation that "holds over general backgrounds, as it involves D-terms" [src: text after Eq. 21]. Cubic DHOST terms "cannot help in erasing the anomalous GW speed" [src: text after Eq. 21]. <!-- eq: notation -->

Baker et al. phrase the beyond-Horndeski survivor as the choice $G_{5,X}=\tilde G_{5,X}=0$, $\tilde G_{4,X}=G_{4,X}-G_{5,\phi}$, "the only algebraic choice for the $G_{i}$ functions that ensures $\alpha_{T}=0$ regardless of the underlying cosmology", which "allows for $M_{*}\neq M_{P}$ and $\alpha_{H}\neq 0$"; for DHOST, the class that is not unstable on cosmological backgrounds "can be transformed to beyond Horndeski with a conformal transformation", so the same requirements apply to its beyond-Horndeski counterpart (arXiv:1710.06394) [src: text after Eq. 6]. <!-- eq: notation -->

`[der]` The three papers state the Horndeski survivor in different forms: Creminelli & Vernizzi let $G_5$ depend on φ only and absorb it into $B_4$ (their Eqs. 11–12), Ezquiaga & Zumalacárregui ask for a constant $G_5$ (their Eq. 16), and Baker et al. set each term of their Eq. 5 to zero, so that the quartic Lagrangian is a conformal coupling to R (text after their Eq. 5); Kobayashi's Eq. 124 is in `results.md`. Read the paper's own statement before quoting one. <!-- eq: notation -->

### Step 6 — graviton decay into dark-energy fluctuations
<!-- key: step-6-graviton-decay-into-dark-energy -->

Creminelli et al. restrict to luminal theories, $L_{c_{T}=1}=P(\phi,X)+Q(\phi,X)\Box\phi+f(\phi,X)\,{}^{(4)}\!R-\frac{4}{X}f_{,X}(\phi,X)(\phi^{;\mu}\phi^{;\nu}\phi_{;\mu\nu}\Box\phi-\phi^{;\mu}\phi_{;\mu\nu}\phi_{;\lambda}\phi^{;\lambda\nu})\;,$ (arXiv:1809.03484) [src: Eq. 70], for which $\alpha_{\rm H}=-{2Xf_{,X}}/{f}$ [src: Sect. 5, inline, text after Eq. 70]. The cutoff $\Lambda_3=(M_{\rm Pl}H_0^2)^{1/3}$ "corresponds to roughly 1000 km and therefore is within the LIGO/Virgo frequency band"; the calculation assumes "the validity of the EFT description" (arXiv:1809.03484) [src: Sect. 2.1]. <!-- eq: notation -->

1. The vertex $\gamma\pi\pi$ from $\tilde m_4^2\delta g^{00}R$, with on-shell scalars of speed $c_s$ and $0<c_s<1$, gives the rate $\Gamma_{\gamma\rightarrow\pi\pi}=\frac{p^{7}(1-c_{s}^{2})^{2}}{480\pi c_{s}^{7}\Lambda_{*}^{6}}\;.$ (arXiv:1809.03484) [src: Eq. 45]. <!-- eq: notation -->
2. For LIGO/Virgo $p\sim\Lambda_3$; stability over $H_0^{-1}$ requires $\frac{\Lambda_{3}}{H_{0}}\left(\frac{\Lambda_{3}}{\Lambda_{*}}\right)^{6}\frac{(1-c_{s}^{2})^{2}}{480\pi c_{s}^{7}}\sim 10^{20}\left(\frac{\Lambda_{3}}{\Lambda_{*}}\right)^{6}\frac{(1-c_{s}^{2})^{2}}{480\pi c_{s}^{7}}\lesssim 1\;,$ (arXiv:1809.03484) [src: Eq. 46], "which implies that $\Lambda_{*}\gg\Lambda_{3}$" [src: Sect. 3.2]. <!-- eq: notation -->
3. In terms of the dimensionless coefficients, in three cells, $\alpha\equiv\frac{2c+4m_{2}^{4}}{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}H^{2}}+\frac{3m_{3}^{6}}{2M_{\rm Pl}^{4}H^{2}}\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\alpha_{\rm B}\equiv-\frac{m_{3}^{3}}{2M_{\rm Pl}^{2}H}\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\alpha_{\rm H}\equiv\frac{2\tilde{m}_{4}^{2}}{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\;.$ [src: Eq. 47], one finds $\left(\frac{\Lambda_{3}}{\Lambda_{*}}\right)^{3}=\frac{\alpha_{\rm H}(1+\alpha_{\rm H})}{\sqrt{2}\,\alpha}\;,$ (arXiv:1809.03484) [src: Eq. 48], so $\alpha_{\rm H}$ "must vanish for any practical purpose"; large α "corresponds to $c_{s}\ll 1$ and further enhances the decay rate", and $\alpha_{\rm H}$ near −1 makes $c_s^2$ negative [src: Sect. 3.2]. "For interesting values of $\tilde{m}_{4}^{2}$ the decay rate of the GWs is so large that no wave will reach the detector" [src: Sect. 3.2]. <!-- eq: notation -->
4. For $c_s=1$ the decay is forbidden by angular momentum, and for $c_s>1$ kinematically; the authors argue from loop corrections to the graviton propagator that "the absence of $\tilde{m}_{4}^{2}$ holds for any value of the scalar speed $c_{s}$" (arXiv:1809.03484) [src: Sect. 3.2; Sect. 5]. <!-- eq: notation -->
5. The second channel, $\Gamma_{\gamma\to\gamma\pi}\sim\alpha_{\rm H}^{2}\omega^{5}/\Lambda_{2}^{4}$, gives "much looser" constraints than $\Gamma_{\gamma\to\pi\pi}\sim\alpha_{\rm H}^{2}\omega^{7}/\Lambda_{3}^{6}$ (arXiv:1809.03484) [src: Sect. 5, inline, text after Eq. 70].
6. The survivor is $L_{c_{T}=1,\text{ no decay}}=f(\phi)\,{}^{(4)}\!R+P(\phi,X)+Q(\phi,X)\Box\phi\;.$ [src: Eq. 71], and its degenerate version $L_{c_{T}=1,\text{ no decay}}=P(\phi,X)+Q(\phi,X)\Box\phi+C(\phi,X){}^{(4)}\!R+\frac{6C_{,X}(\phi,X){}^{2}}{C(\phi,X)}\phi^{;\mu}\phi_{;\mu\nu}\phi_{;\lambda}\phi^{;\lambda\nu}\;.$ (arXiv:1809.03484) [src: Eq. 72], "the most general degenerate theory compatible with $c_{\text{T}}^{2}=1$ and with the absence of graviton decay"; in DHOST language $\alpha_{\rm H}$ and $\beta_1$ do not vanish but are related by $\alpha_{\rm H}=-2\beta_{1}$, "This implies that the screening mechanism based on quartic terms studied in [48, 49, 32] is absent" [src: Sect. 5, footnote 4]. <!-- num: not a value -->
7. Cubic Horndeski is spared: the coupling of $\delta g^{00}\delta K$ to gravitons, $HM_{\text{Pl}}^{2}\delta g^{00}\delta K\sim HM_{\text{Pl}}^{2}\dot{\pi}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\pi\gamma_{ij}\;.$ [src: Eq. 64], is suppressed by $\Lambda_{2}\equiv(H_{0}M_{\text{Pl}})^{1/2}\;.$ (arXiv:1809.03484) [src: Eq. 65], so "a theory with only cubic Galileon/Horndeski is viable since it does not affect the graviton propagation at the scale $\Lambda_{3}$ (or at least it is technically natural to make this assumption)" [src: Sect. 4.2]. <!-- eq: notation -->

The calculation is perturbative: "we have studied the perturbative decay of gravitational waves, neglecting possible coherent effects", expected to be "indeed important" given the occupation numbers (arXiv:1809.03484) [src: Sect. 5].

### Step 7 — the follow-ups with large occupation numbers
<!-- key: step-7-the-follow-ups-with-large-occupation -->

- Resonant decay: with the $m_3^3$ and $\tilde m_4^2$ operators, π obeys a Mathieu equation with exponentially growing bands; for $m_3^3$ the self-interactions "limit the instability", while for $\tilde m_4^2$ they "can be neglected, at least in some regimes"; the modification of the signal "is observable for $3 \times 10^{-20} \lesssim α_{\rm H} \lesssim 10^{-17}$ with a LIGO/Virgo-like interferometer and for $10^{-16} \lesssim α_{\rm H} \lesssim 10^{-10}$ with a LISA-like one" (arXiv:1906.07015, Creminelli et al. 2019) [src: abstract]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- Instabilities: for the braiding operator, the dark-energy scalar "features ghost and/or gradient instabilities for gravitational-wave amplitudes that are produced by typical binary systems"; with the binary populations, "the instability is triggered in the whole Universe for $|α_{\rm B} |\gtrsim 10^{-2}$, i.e. when the modification of gravity is sizeable", down to frequencies "corresponding to $10^{10}$ km", so it "is thus robust, unless new physics enters on even longer wavelengths"; "The fate of the instability ... depend[s] on the UV completion"; the same holds beyond Horndeski "for $|α_{\rm H}| \gtrsim 10^{-20}$", and "the only dark-energy theories with sizeable cosmological effects that avoid these problems are k-essence models, with a possible conformal coupling with matter" (arXiv:1910.14035, Creminelli et al. 2019) [src: abstract]. <!-- eq: notation -->

### Step 8 — vector–tensor and bimetric theories
<!-- key: step-8-vector-tensor-and-bimetric-theories -->

- Generalised Einstein–aether, with $K=c_1\nabla_\mu A_\nu\nabla^\mu A^\nu+c_2(\nabla_\mu A^\mu)^2+c_3\nabla_\mu A_\nu\nabla^\nu A^\mu$ and a free function $\mathcal{F}(K)$: $\alpha_{T}=-(c_{1}+c_{3}){\cal F}_{,K}/[1+(c_{1}+c_{3}){\cal F}_{,K}]$, so the bound implies $c_{1}=-c_{3}$, which on Minkowski space "reduces the theory to the Maxwell action (with a time-like constraint)", while the background still allows modifications (arXiv:1710.06394) [src: inline, text after Eq. 6]. Creminelli & Vernizzi: in Einstein–aether and Hořava gravity "$c_{T}$ is expected to deviate from unity and the bound of GW170817 represents a severe constraint on these models" (arXiv:1710.05877) [src: text after Eq. 16]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- Generalised Proca on $A^\mu=(A,\vec 0)$: $\alpha_{T}=A^{2}\left[2G_{4,X}-(HA-{\dot{A}})G_{5,X}\right]/q_{T},$ [src: Eq. 8] with $q_{T}=2G_{4}-2A^{2}G_{4,X}+HA^{3}G_{5,X}$ (arXiv:1710.06394) [src: inline, text after Eq. 8]. Either the functions are tuned, with "all the considerations about radiative stability, time dependence and background symmetry we discussed for Horndeski", or $G_{4,X}=G_{5,X}=0$, i.e. $\mathcal{L}_4\propto R$ and $\mathcal{L}_5\propto G_{\mu\nu}\nabla^\mu A^\nu$; then tensor ghost freedom enforces $G_4>0$, and on the branch with all three degrees of freedom dynamical $3H^2=(\rho-G_2)/(2G_4)$ (arXiv:1710.06394) [src: text after Eq. 8]. In "beyond" generalised Proca, $\alpha_T=0$ implies $G_{5,X}(HA-{\dot{A}})-2G_{4,X}=2f_{4}A^{2}+6f_{5}HA^{3},$ (arXiv:1710.06394) [src: inline, text after Eq. 8]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- Ezquiaga & Zumalacárregui list couplings such as $R_{\mu\nu}v^\mu v^\nu$ in vector dark energy, Einstein–aether "including some sectors of Hořava gravity", generalised Proca, TeVeS and MOND-like theories as "critically affected", and state that "Massive gravity, bigravity and multi-gravity remain viable as long as the graviton mass is small and matter couples minimally to one of the metrics" (arXiv:1710.05901) [src: Conclusions, text after Eq. 21]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- Bimetric dRGT with matter coupled to one metric and β's of order one: the 1.7 s delay gives $m\lesssim 10^{-22}$ eV, "uncompetitive with Solar System fifth force constraints of order $m\lesssim 10^{-33}$ eV", and "local constraints from GW propagation have no bearing on cosmology" (arXiv:1710.06394) [src: inline, text after Eq. 8]. <!-- eq: notation -->

## The cutoff objection, the replies and the status of each argument
<!-- key: the-cutoff-objection-the-replies-and-the-status -->

**The objection (de Rham & Melville 2018).** For a Horndeski dark-energy EFT the scale is $M\lesssim\Lambda_{\rm Horndeski}\sim(M_{\rm Pl}H_{0}^{2})^{1/3}\sim 260{\rm Hz}$ (arXiv:1806.09417) [src: Eq. 5], "perilously close" to the GW170817 band, and if the theory admits a Lorentz-invariant UV completion the front velocity, $c_{\rm front}=\lim_{k\to\infty}c_{T}(k)\,.$ (arXiv:1806.09417) [src: Eq. 2, footnote 2], "must be luminal". The bound "should therefore be read as a constraint on $c_{T}(k)$ at frequencies on the order of $k\sim 10-100$ Hz" [src: text after Eq. 1]. <!-- eq: notation -->

1. Toy model: $\mathcal{L}_{M}=-\frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}+\frac{1}{2\Lambda^{4}}(\partial\phi)^{4}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{(\partial^{2}\phi)^{2}}{M^{2}}\right)\,,$ [src: Eq. 3] on $\langle\phi\rangle=\alpha\Lambda^2t$ has $c_{S}^{2}=1-\Delta_{0}=1-\frac{4\alpha^{2}}{1+6\alpha^{2}}\,,$ [src: Eq. 4], which near the cutoff runs as $c_{S}^{2}(k)=1-\Delta_{0}+\Delta_{2}\frac{k^{2}}{M^{2}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{4}}{M^{4}}\right)\,,$ (arXiv:1806.09417) [src: Eq. 6]; the higher-derivative operators "are mandated by positivity bounds if this theory is to admit a sensible Wilsonian UV completion" [src: text after Eq. 5]. <!-- eq: notation -->
2. Partial UV completion by a heavy field, $\mathcal{L}_{\Lambda_{*}}=-\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial\chi)^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}M^{2}\chi^{2}+\frac{\chi}{\Lambda_{*}}(\partial\phi)^{2}\,,$ [src: Eq. 7] with $M\ll\Lambda=(M\Lambda_{*})^{1/2}\ll\Lambda_{*}\,.$ [src: Eq. 8]; integrating out χ gives $\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}+\frac{1}{2\Lambda^{4}}(\partial\phi)^{2}\frac{M^{2}}{M^{2}-\Box}(\partial\phi)^{2}\,.\quad$ [src: Eq. 9] and the dispersion relation $\omega^{2}=k^{2}-\frac{4\alpha^{2}}{1+2\alpha^{2}}\frac{\omega^{2}M^{2}}{M^{2}-\omega^{2}+k^{2}}\,,$ (arXiv:1806.09417) [src: Eq. 10], which "matches the leading order EFT sound speed (4) at sufficiently small frequencies $k\ll M$ but leads to luminality at higher frequencies" [src: text after Eq. 10]. <!-- eq: notation --> The illustrative hierarchy $M=10^{-3}\Lambda$ gives $k_{\rm LIGO}>10^{-1}\Lambda\sim 10^{2}M$; "these scales should be taken with a grain of salt" (arXiv:1806.09417) [src: inline, text after Eq. 10].
3. Horndeski: $\mathcal{L}_{\rm H}=\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}R-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{G}^{ab}\partial_{a}\phi\partial_{b}\phi\,,$ [src: Eq. 11] with $\mathcal{G}^{ab}=g^{ab}+c_{2}\frac{M_{\rm Pl}}{\Lambda^{3}}G^{ab}+c_{3}\frac{M_{\rm Pl}}{\Lambda^{6}}L^{a\mu b\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi\,,$ [src: Eq. 12] has, below the cutoff, $c_{T}^{2}(k)=1-\frac{2c_{2}\alpha^{2}\beta^{2}+6c_{3}\alpha^{3}\beta^{3}}{2+c_{2}\alpha^{2}\beta^{2}+6c_{3}\alpha^{3}\beta^{3}}\,+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}}{M^{2}}\right)\,,$ (arXiv:1806.09417) [src: Eq. 14]. Operators $\mathcal{L}^{(n)}_{\rm higher-der}=\left(M_{\rm Pl}^{2}G_{\mu\nu}\right)\frac{\Box^{n}}{M_{n}^{2n+4}}\partial^{\mu}\phi\partial^{\nu}\phi\,,$ [src: Eq. 15], n ≥ 2, change the background by $\frac{\mathcal{E}^{(n)}}{\mathcal{E}_{\rm H}}\sim\frac{H_{0}^{2(n-1)}\Lambda^{6}}{M_{n}^{2n+4}}\,,$ [src: Eq. 16], leave the EFT valid up to $\Lambda_{*}={\rm min}_{n}\left(M_{n}^{2n+4}H_{0}\Lambda^{-3}\right)^{1/(2n+2)}\,.$ [src: Eq. 17], and modify the dispersion, symbolically, in two rows, as $\omega^{2}\sim c_{T}^{2}(0)k^{2}+\mathcal{O}(H_{0}^{2})$ $+\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{c_{n}\Lambda^{6}}{3M_{n}^{2n+4}}\left(-\omega^{2}+k^{2}\right)^{n-1}(\omega^{2}+\mathcal{O}(k^{2},H_{0}^{2}))\,,$ (arXiv:1806.09417) [src: Eq. 19]. <!-- num: not a value -->
4. The conditions they attach: "for the GW speed to be unity at LIGO frequencies, the EFT must breakdown at scales lower that Λ"; for a power-law running "one requires that the cutoff of the theory is some orders of magnitude below the LIGO band"; for their Horndeski example, without a very fast transition, "it would require the higher derivative operators to enter at a scale at least 9 orders of magnitude below the observed scale"; and "By no means do we suggest that every time an observation is performed, one should simply shield the EFT from constraints by invoking a lower cutoff" (arXiv:1806.09417) [src: text after Eq. 19]. Their aim "is not to revive Horndeski or any specific EFT as a particular model for dark energy" [src: text after Eq. 19]. <!-- num: not a value -->

**The replies.**

- In advance (Creminelli & Vernizzi): "one does not expect that high-energy corrections conspire to completely cancel the modification of the GW speed" (arXiv:1710.05877) [src: text before Eq. 1]. De Rham & Melville answer that "from a top-down approach, it is very unlikely that the UV completion knows anything about the special structure of the sLB background", so a return to luminality needs no tuning (arXiv:1806.09417) [src: text after Eq. 19].
- Acceptance as a caveat (Creminelli et al. 2018): "if new states are present at a scale parametrically below $\Lambda_{3}$, the EFT is of no use for the gravitational waves predictions at LIGO/Virgo", and "our conclusions do not hold if the theories at hand break down at a scale parametrically smaller than $\Lambda_{3}$"; they estimate that luminality approached as $c_{\textrm{T}}-1\sim M^{2}/\omega^{2}$ needs $M\lesssim(10^{11}\,\rm{km})^{-1}$, and ask for an example "that successfully reproduces GR on short scales" (arXiv:1809.03484) [src: Sect. 2.1; Sect. 4.2; Sect. 5]. The GW-induced instability is triggered down to "$10^{10}$ km", hence "robust, unless new physics enters on even longer wavelengths" (arXiv:1910.14035) [src: abstract]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- Constraining the transition (Harry & Noller 2022): with $\Lambda\sim 260\;{\rm Hz}.$ (arXiv:2207.10096) [src: Eq. 5], "the largest possible energy/frequency scale", they take the operators ${\cal L}^{(n)}=M_{\rm Pl}^{2}G_{\mu\nu}\frac{\Box^{n}}{M^{2n+4}}(\nabla^{\mu}\phi\nabla^{\nu}\phi),$ [src: Eq. 6] and the dispersion $\omega^{2}\sim c_{\rm GW}^{2}(0)k^{2}+\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{c_{n}\Lambda^{6}}{3M^{2n+4}}(c^{2}k^{2}-\omega^{2})^{n-1}(\omega^{2}+{\cal O}(k^{2})),$ [src: Eq. 7], with the limits $k\ll k_{\star}:c_{\rm GW}-c_{\rm GW}(0)\propto k^{2n-2},$ $k\gg k_{\star}:c-c_{\rm GW}\propto k^{-2}.$ (arXiv:2207.10096) [src: Eq. 8]. From the GW170817 strain, with power-law templates of slope σ, "all samples have $\delta c_{\rm GW}$ smaller than $1\times 10^{-17}$ at 100Hz, and all samples are smaller than $1\times 10^{-18}$ at 500Hz" [src: Sect. IV.4]; a transition below the LVK band is "a smoking-gun signal for large classes of dynamical dark energy theories" (arXiv:2207.10096) [src: abstract]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Lower frequencies, **forecast**: in the LISA band "k/Λ ∼ 10⁻⁵", where a bound of LIGO's precision would make it "very hard for a Horndeski EFT to remain viable" (arXiv:1806.09417) [src: text after Eq. 19]; LISA "will enable us to place stringent independent bounds" on $c_T(f)$ "even in the absence of an electromagnetic counterpart" (arXiv:2203.00566, Baker et al. 2022) [src: abstract] (forecast); "a single joint observation of a GW150914-like event by LISA and a terrestrial interferometer would allow one to constrain the speed of light and gravitons to match to within $10^{-15}$" (arXiv:2209.14398, Baker et al. 2022) [src: abstract] (forecast). <!-- eq: notation -->

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **EFT coefficients (Creminelli & Vernizzi) against the effective tensor metric (Ezquiaga & Zumalacárregui) against direct $\alpha_T$ (Baker et al.).** The first gives operator relations valid around any FRW background (Eqs. 4–6 of arXiv:1710.05877) and the covariant survivor including DHOST (Eq. 16); the second shows why D–E cancellations fail off the background (Eq. 15 of arXiv:1710.05901) and builds survivors by conformal or disformal maps (Eqs. 18–21); the third reads conditions from the cosmological $\alpha_T$ (Eqs. 5–6, 8 of arXiv:1710.06394) and also covers vector–tensor and bimetric theories. Ezquiaga & Zumalacárregui state that their results "agree with the independent derivation presented in Ref. [44]" (arXiv:1710.05901) [src: text after Eq. 21]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Frame.** Creminelli & Vernizzi assume a universal coupling, so that one can go to the frame where matter is minimally coupled; "If one chooses to go to a different disformal frame, both matter and GWs will acquire a common disformal coupling", and the action is no longer of the form of their Eqs. 12 or 16 (arXiv:1710.05877) [src: text after Eq. 16]; Ezquiaga & Zumalacárregui apply their transformation to the gravity sector only, since "A field redefinition of the whole action, including matter, will not change the physical ratio $c_{g}/c$" (arXiv:1710.05901) [src: footnote 1, text after Eq. 17]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- **Perturbative decay against coherent, resonant decay.** Eq. 45 of arXiv:1809.03484 is a single-graviton rate; the resonant calculation with large occupation numbers reaches $\alpha_{\rm H}$ down to the windows quoted in Step 7 but cannot describe the $m_3^3$ self-interactions analytically (arXiv:1906.07015) [src: abstract]. <!-- eq: notation -->

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Quoting the symmetric bound as the measurement.** $\lvert c_g/c-1\rvert\leq5\cdot10^{-16}$ is Ezquiaga & Zumalacárregui's simplification "For simplicity" of the asymmetric interval (arXiv:1710.05901) [src: text after Eq. 1]; the interval itself rests on the lowest distance and a "conservative 10 s delay" [src: text before Eq. 1]. Check: cite the interval with its assumptions. <!-- eq: notation -->
- **The direction of the Cherenkov bound.** As printed, arXiv:1710.05877 [src: text before Eq. 1] says the Cherenkov bounds "only apply to GWs travelling faster, and not slower, than light", while arXiv:1710.06394 [src: text after Eq. 2] ties them to "negative values ($c_{T}<c$)" and arXiv:1710.05901 [src: text after Eq. 1] says GW170817 "applies to $c_{g}>c$ unlike" Cherenkov bounds. Check: take the direction from the two later statements and say which paper is followed. <!-- eq: notation -->
- **Tuning on the background only.** A cancellation that sets $\alpha_T=0$ today on the tracker (quintic Galileon) is broken by a change in matter abundance or by a perturbation, Eq. 15 of arXiv:1710.05901 [src: text after Eq. 15]; check that a proposed luminal model satisfies the conditions for all $H$, $\dot\phi$, $\ddot\phi$ (Eq. 11 of arXiv:1710.05877) or uses the c = 0 exception explicitly [src: text after Eq. 13]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- **Using the LIGO bound at cosmological wavenumbers without the cutoff assumption.** "the current constraints ($m_{4}(k_{\rm LIGO})\approx 0$) may not necessarily imply $m_{4}(k\sim H_{0}\lesssim 10^{-20}k_{\rm LIGO})=0$" (arXiv:1806.09417) [src: text after Eq. 19]; a paper that sets $\alpha_T=0$ in a cosmological fit states, or should state, that it assumes the EFT holds up to the LIGO band. <!-- eq: notation -->
- **Screening and inhomogeneity along the path.** Baker et al.: GWs of 3000 km wavelength "should be sensitive to the inhomogeneities", and the speed measured may be the one cosmological modes "will experience when the universe has emptied out"; the source at $z_s=0.01$ makes the constraint one "on the speed of GWs today" (arXiv:1710.06394) [src: Caveats, text after Eq. 8]. Creminelli & Vernizzi: "it is difficult to believe that this screening effect can persist over distances of order 40 Mpc" (arXiv:1710.05877) [src: text before Eq. 1]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- **Applying the decay bound outside its assumptions.** The decay result holds for an EFT valid up to $\Lambda_3$, in perturbation theory and for luminal theories; it says nothing about operators that do not couple π π to gravitons at $\Lambda_3$ (cubic Horndeski, Eqs. 64–65 of arXiv:1809.03484) [src: Sect. 4.2]. The braiding is constrained instead by GW-induced instabilities (arXiv:1910.14035) [src: abstract]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- **Dropping the matter-coupling assumption.** The survivors of arXiv:1710.05877 are written in the frame where matter couples minimally and universally [src: text after Eq. 16]; a theory with non-universal couplings is not covered.
- **Printed slips.** The Cherenkov direction of arXiv:1710.05877 above; no other physics slip was found in the equations transcribed here.
