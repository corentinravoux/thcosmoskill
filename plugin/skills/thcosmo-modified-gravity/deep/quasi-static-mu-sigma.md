## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- Bellini & Sawicki (arXiv:1404.3713) put Ψ in $g_{00}$: $\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-(1+2\Psi)\mathrm{d}t^{2}+a^{2}(t)(1-2\Phi)\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}^{2}\,.$ (arXiv:1404.3713) [src: Eq. 2]. They define two effective Newton constants, one per potential, $Y\equiv-\frac{2k^{2}\Psi}{a^{2}\tilde{\rho}_{\textrm{m}}\delta_{\textrm{m}}}\,,\qquad Z\equiv-\frac{2k^{2}\Phi}{a^{2}\tilde{\rho}_{\textrm{m}}\delta_{\textrm{m}}}\,.$ (arXiv:1404.3713) [src: Eq. 29], and a slip of their own, $\bar{\eta}\equiv\frac{2\Psi}{\Psi+\Phi}\,.$ (arXiv:1404.3713) [src: Eq. 30], "a non-standard definition of this ratio" chosen because projected Euclid errors "are minimised for this particular combination" [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 30]. Densities carry a tilde, divided by $M_*^2$ (`deep/eft-dark-energy-alpha-basis.md`, Conventions).
- Pogosian & Silvestri (arXiv:1606.05339, Pogosian & Silvestri 2016) use the same line element, $ds^{2}=-(1+2\Psi)dt^{2}+a^{2}(1-2\Phi)d{\bf x}^{2}\ ,$ (arXiv:1606.05339) [src: Eq. 1], the comoving density contrast Δ, and $k^{2}\Psi=-4\pi G\mu(a,k)a^{2}\rho\Delta\ ,$ [src: Eq. 5], $\Phi=\gamma(a,k)\Psi\ ,$ [src: Eq. 6] and $k^{2}(\Phi+\Psi)=-8\pi G\,\Sigma(a,k)\,a^{2}\rho\Delta\ .$ (arXiv:1606.05339) [src: Eq. 7]. Their braiding is Bellini & Sawicki's: "The definition of $\alpha_{B}$ in [56] differs from that in [25] by a factor of −2. We use the original definition of [25]", [25] being arXiv:1404.3713 and [56] arXiv:1509.02191 [src: Sect. IV.3, footnote 4; References]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Gleyzes, Langlois, Mancarella and Vernizzi (arXiv:1509.02191, Gleyzes et al. 2015) write the baryon Euler equation as $\dot{v}_{b}=-\Phi\,,$ (arXiv:1509.02191) [src: Eq. 3.7]. Their braiding is the one Pogosian & Silvestri call "[56]".
  - `[der]` Their Φ is therefore the potential that moves matter, the Ψ of the two papers above.
- Sawicki & Bellini (arXiv:1503.06831) write primes for derivatives with respect to e-folding time and $k_{H}\equiv k/aH$ (arXiv:1503.06831) [src: Sect. II, inline, text after Eq. 1], and assume no slip, $\Phi=\Psi$ (arXiv:1503.06831) [src: Sect. II, inline, text after Eq. 2].
- hi_class formulates its quasi-static scheme in synchronous gauge: "QS has to be discussed in the particular gauge used" (arXiv:1909.01828, Bellini et al. 2019) [src: Sect. 5.1].

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — eliminate the scalar: one equation for the spatial potential
<!-- key: step-1-eliminate-the-scalar-one-equation-for -->

From the Newtonian-gauge Einstein equations of their Sect. 3.4, Bellini & Sawicki "eliminate the scalar field $v_{X}$ using the constraints (18-20) and the time derivative of eq. (20)" (arXiv:1404.3713) [src: Sect. 4, text before Eq. 24] and obtain, for dust, $\ddot{\Phi}+\frac{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}+\beta_{3}\alpha_{\textrm{B}}^{2}\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}}{\beta_{1}+\alpha_{\textrm{B}}^{2}\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}}\dot{\Phi}+\frac{\beta_{1}\beta_{4}+\beta_{1}\beta_{5}\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}+c_{\text{s}}^{2}\alpha_{\textrm{B}}^{2}\frac{k^{4}}{a^{4}}}{\beta_{1}+\alpha_{\textrm{B}}^{2}\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}}\Phi=-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\rho}_{\text{m}}\frac{\beta_{1}\beta_{6}+\beta_{7}\alpha_{\textrm{B}}^{2}\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}}{\beta_{1}+\alpha_{\textrm{B}}^{2}\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}}\delta_{\text{m}}\,,$ (arXiv:1404.3713) [src: Eq. 24]. <!-- num: not a value --> The $\beta_i$ are functions of the $\alpha_i$ and H given in their App. B (Eqs. 58–66), the full equation with matter velocity being their Eq. 56 [src: App. B]. The Newtonian potential follows from the anisotropy constraint with the scalar eliminated, $\alpha_{\textrm{B}}^{2}\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}\left[\Psi-\Phi\left(1+\alpha_{\textrm{T}}+\frac{2\left(\alpha_{\textrm{M}}-\alpha_{\textrm{T}}\right)}{\alpha_{\textrm{B}}}\right)\right]++\beta_{1}\left[\Psi-\Phi\left(1+\alpha_{\textrm{T}}\right)\left(1-\frac{2DH^{2}\left(\alpha_{\textrm{M}}-\alpha_{\textrm{T}}\right)}{\beta_{1}}\right)\right]=\left(\alpha_{\textrm{M}}-\alpha_{\textrm{T}}\right)\left[\alpha_{\textrm{B}}\tilde{\rho}_{\textrm{m}}\delta_{\textrm{m}}-2HD\dot{\Phi}\right]\,.$ (arXiv:1404.3713) [src: Eq. 25], whose row break repeats the "+" as printed, and the dust equations $\dot{\delta}_{\text{m}}-\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}v_{\text{m}}=3\dot{\Phi}\,,\qquad\dot{v}_{\text{m}}=-\Psi\,,$ (arXiv:1404.3713) [src: Eq. 26] close the system. Two of the coefficients: $\beta_{1}\equiv\frac{3\alpha_{\textrm{B}}^{2}}{2}\left(\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{m}}+\tilde{p}_{\mathrm{m}}\right)-D\left[2\mathfrak{H}+\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{m}}+\tilde{p}_{\mathrm{m}}\right]$ [src: Eq. 58] and $\beta_{7}\equiv c_{\text{s}}^{2}-w_{\text{m}}+\frac{\alpha_{\textrm{B}}^{2}\left(1+\alpha_{\textrm{T}}+3w_{\text{m}}\right)+2\left(\alpha_{\textrm{M}}-\alpha_{\textrm{T}}\right)\alpha_{\textrm{B}}}{2D}$ (arXiv:1404.3713) [src: Eq. 64].

The structure of Eq. 24 defines the braiding scale, $\frac{k_{\text{B}}^{2}}{a^{2}H^{2}}\equiv\frac{\beta_{1}}{\alpha_{\textrm{B}}^{2}H^{2}}=\frac{D}{\alpha_{\textrm{B}}^{2}}\left[(1-\tilde{\Omega}_{\text{m}})(1+w_{X})+2\left(\alpha_{\textrm{M}}-\alpha_{\textrm{T}}\right)\right]+\frac{9}{2}\tilde{\Omega}_{\text{m}}\,.$ (arXiv:1404.3713) [src: Eq. 27]; "During matter domination, the braiding scale — if it at all exists, $\alpha_{\textrm{B}}\neq 0$ — typically lies at the cosmological horizon" [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 27].

### Step 2 — the extreme quasi-static limit
<!-- key: step-2-the-extreme-quasi-static-limit -->

Converting to the usual functions, $Y=\frac{\bar{\eta}}{2-\bar{\eta}}Z\qquad\Sigma=\frac{2Z}{2-\bar{\eta}}\,.$ (arXiv:1404.3713) [src: Eq. 31]. Ignoring all scale dependence and taking $k\to\infty$, the two rows of one display give $Z_{\text{QS}}=1+\frac{\alpha_{\textrm{B}}^{2}\left(1+\alpha_{\textrm{T}}\right)+2\alpha_{\textrm{B}}\left(\alpha_{\textrm{M}}-\alpha_{\textrm{T}}\right)}{2Dc_{\text{s}}^{2}}\,,$ and $\bar{\eta}_{\text{QS}}=1+\frac{2\left(\beta_{7}-c_{\text{s}}^{2}\right)\left(\alpha_{\textrm{M}}-\alpha_{\textrm{T}}\right)+\beta_{7}\alpha_{\textrm{B}}\alpha_{\textrm{T}}}{2\left(\beta_{7}-c_{\text{s}}^{2}\right)\left(\alpha_{\textrm{M}}-\alpha_{\textrm{T}}\right)+\beta_{7}\left(2\alpha_{\textrm{B}}+\alpha_{\textrm{B}}\alpha_{\textrm{T}}\right)}\,,\qquad\alpha_{\textrm{B}}\neq 0\,.$ (arXiv:1404.3713) [src: Eq. 32]. Bellini & Sawicki draw two conclusions: "the dark energy clusters at small scales ( $Z\neq 1$ ) only if there is braiding, $\alpha_{\textrm{B}}\neq 0$", so "a detection of clustering of dark energy is unambiguous evidence of the presence of kinetic mixing of the scalar and graviton"; and "the limit for $\bar{\eta}$ taken in (32) does not exist when there is no braiding" [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 32]. <!-- num: not a value --> For f(R), with $\alpha_{\textrm{M}}=-\alpha_{\textrm{B}}$, "at super-Compton scales both Z and $\bar{\eta}$ are very close to their ΛCDM values of 1 (with corrections of order $\alpha_{\textrm{M}}$ ), while inside the Compton scale we have the standard result $Z=2/3$ and $\bar{\eta}=4/3$" (arXiv:1404.3713) [src: Sect. 4.2, inline, text after Eq. 42].

With a negligible standard kinetic term, the small-scale value is $Z=\frac{\beta_{7}}{c_{\text{s}}^{2}}=1+\frac{1}{3c_{\text{s}}^{2}}+\frac{2\alpha_{\textrm{M}}}{3\alpha_{\textrm{B}}c_{\text{s}}^{2}}$ for $k\gg k_{\text{C}}$, against $Z=\frac{\beta_{6}}{\beta_{5}}$ for $aH\ll k\ll k_{\text{C}}$ (arXiv:1404.3713) [src: Eq. 41], $k_{\rm C}$ being the Compton scale of their Sect. 4.2.

### Step 3 — the scale dependence: a ratio of polynomials in k
<!-- key: step-3-the-scale-dependence-a-ratio-of -->

Pogosian & Silvestri start from the quasi-static equations of Bloomfield et al. (their Ref. 22), $A_{1}{k^{2}\over a^{2}}\Phi+A_{2}{k^{2}\over a^{2}}\pi+A_{3}{k^{2}\over a^{2}}\Psi=-\rho\Delta,$ [src: Eq. 46], $B_{1}\Psi+B_{2}\Phi+B_{3}\pi=0,$ [src: Eq. 47] and $C_{1}{k^{2}\over a^{2}}\Phi+C_{2}{k^{2}\over a^{2}}\Psi+\left(C_{3}{k^{2}\over a^{2}}+C_{\pi}\right)\pi=0,$ (arXiv:1606.05339) [src: Eq. 48], with coefficients in the EFT functions in their Eq. 49. Solving the algebraic system gives $4\pi G\mu={\mu\over 2m_{0}^{2}}={f_{1}+f_{2}\ a^{2}/k^{2}\over f_{3}+f_{4}\ a^{2}/k^{2}},$ [src: Eq. 50], $\gamma={f_{5}+f_{6}\ a^{2}/k^{2}\over f_{1}+f_{2}\ a^{2}/k^{2}},$ [src: Eq. 51] and $8\pi G\Sigma={\Sigma\over m_{0}^{2}}={f_{1}+f_{5}+(f_{2}+f_{6})a^{2}/k^{2}\over f_{3}+f_{4}\ a^{2}/k^{2}},$ (arXiv:1606.05339) [src: Eq. 52], the $f_i$ being combinations of the A, B, C of Eq. 53. "In the case of Horndeski theories, with $m_{2}^{2}=0$ and $2{\hat{M}}^{2}={\bar{M}}^{2}_{2}=-{\bar{M}}^{2}_{3}$, the k-dependence in $C_{3}$ disappears, and $f_{1}$,…, $f_{5}$ are functions of time only" (arXiv:1606.05339) [src: App. A, inline, text after Eq. 53].

Rewritten with the mass $M^{2}\equiv C_{\pi}/f_{1}$ and the α functions, $\mu={m_{0}^{2}\over M_{*}^{2}}{1+M^{2}\ a^{2}/k^{2}\over f_{3}/2f_{1}M_{*}^{2}+M^{2}(1+\alpha_{T})^{-1}a^{2}/k^{2}},$ [src: Eq. 19], $\gamma={f_{5}/f_{1}+M^{2}(1+\alpha_{T})^{-1}\ a^{2}/k^{2}\over 1+M^{2}\ a^{2}/k^{2}},$ [src: Eq. 20] and $\Sigma={m_{0}^{2}\over 2M_{*}^{2}}{1+f_{5}/f_{1}+M^{2}[1+(1+\alpha_{T})^{-1}]a^{2}/k^{2}\over f_{3}/2f_{1}M_{*}^{2}+M^{2}(1+\alpha_{T})^{-1}a^{2}/k^{2}},$ (arXiv:1606.05339) [src: Eq. 21]: "M² sets the transition scale in all three phenomenological functions" [src: Sect. IV.1, text after Eq. 21]. Peirone et al. reprint Eqs. 19 and 21 as their Eqs. 8–9 and call them "a ratio of quadratic polynomials in k" (arXiv:1712.00444, Peirone et al. 2017) [src: Sect. II.2].

### Step 4 — the two limits and the consistency relations
<!-- key: step-4-the-two-limits-and-the-consistency -->

Above the Compton wavelength ($k/a\ll M$), $\mu_{0}={m_{0}^{2}\over M_{*}^{2}}(1+\alpha_{T}),$ [src: Eq. 22], $\gamma_{0}={1\over 1+\alpha_{T}}=c_{T}^{-2},$ [src: Eq. 23] and $\Sigma_{0}={m_{0}^{2}\over M_{*}^{2}}\left(1+{\alpha_{T}\over 2}\right).$ (arXiv:1606.05339) [src: Eq. 24]: "on large scales, modifications are either due to change in the background value of the Planck mass, or due to the modified propagation speed of gravity waves", "since the scalar fifth force is suppressed on scales above the Compton wavelength" [src: Sect. IV.2]. Since screening of chameleon or Vainshtein type "only suppress the enhancement in the effective Newton's constant caused by the attractive force mediated by scalar field fluctuations" and does not affect "the super-Compton value of the gravitational coupling", Cavendish-type experiments impose $\mu_{0}(t_{0})=1,\ \Sigma_{0}(t_{0})={1\over 2}\left(2+\alpha_{T}\over 1+\alpha_{T}\right)\ ,$ (arXiv:1606.05339) [src: Eq. 25].

Below the Compton wavelength ($k/a\gg M$), $\mu_{\infty}={m_{0}^{2}\over M_{*}^{2}}(1+\alpha_{T}+\beta_{\xi}^{2}),$ [src: Eq. 26], $\gamma_{\infty}={1+\beta_{B}\beta_{\xi}/2\over 1+\alpha_{T}+\beta_{\xi}^{2}},$ [src: Eq. 27] and $\Sigma_{\infty}={m_{0}^{2}\over M_{*}^{2}}\left(1+{\alpha_{T}\over 2}+{\beta_{\xi}^{2}+\beta_{B}\beta_{\xi}/2\over 2}\right).$ (arXiv:1606.05339) [src: Eq. 28], with $\beta^{2}_{B}={2\over c^{2}_{s}\alpha}\alpha_{B}^{2}$ [src: Eq. 29], $\beta^{2}_{\xi}={2\over c^{2}_{s}\alpha}\left[{\alpha_{B}\over 2}(1+\alpha_{T})+\alpha_{M}-\alpha_{T}\right]^{2}$ [src: Eq. 30] and $\alpha=\alpha_{K}+{3\over 2}\alpha_{B}^{2}\ ,$ (arXiv:1606.05339) [src: Eq. 31], the sound speed being their Eq. 64.

Three conclusions, as Pogosian & Silvestri state them (arXiv:1606.05339) [src: Sect. IV.3]:

- "one generally should have $\mu_{\infty}\geq\mu_{0}$, since $\beta_{\xi}^{2}$ is strictly non-negative. This reflects the fact that the scalar fifth force is always attractive"; if k-dependence is detected, "finding $\mu_{\infty}<\mu_{0}$ would rule out all Horndeski models" (arXiv:1606.05339) [src: Sect. IV.3, inline, text after Eq. 31].
- "finding $\mu-1$ and $\Sigma-1$ to be of opposite signs at any redshift or scale would strongly disfavour all Horndeski models" (arXiv:1606.05339) [src: Sect. IV.3, inline, text after Eq. 31].
- "if $\alpha_{T}$ is either measured … or assumed to be negligible, then $\Sigma_{\infty}\neq\mu_{\infty}$ would amount to a detection of a non-zero $\alpha_{M}$" (arXiv:1606.05339) [src: Sect. IV.3, inline, text after Eq. 31].

The abstract adds that "a measurement of $Σ\ne 1$ would rule out all models with a canonical form of kinetic energy" (arXiv:1606.05339) [src: abstract]. <!-- eq: notation --> Peirone et al. test the sign conjecture by sampling viable Horndeski models with EFTCAMB, requiring $c_{\rm T}=1$ today: it "holds very well for the GBD models" and for the other classes "within the QSA, but the exact calculations show that about 10% of $H_{S}$ and Horndeski models violate the conjecture at k=0.001 h/Mpc, with $\Sigma>1$ and $\mu<1$" (arXiv:1712.00444) [src: Sect. V]. <!-- eq: notation -->

`[der]` In the extreme limit, Eqs. 26–28 of arXiv:1606.05339 and Eq. 32 of arXiv:1404.3713 are two forms of the same result in the same braiding convention, one written for μ, γ, Σ and the other for Z and $\bar\eta$; no paper read prints the conversion between the two sets of symbols.

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **Two ways of taking the limit.** Dropping the time derivatives in the scalar equation and then using the constraints, or taking the limit of Eq. 24: "the two results are identical in the $k\rightarrow\infty$ limit (32). However the term subleading in $k^{2}$ are affected", which gives "non-negligible differences at larger scales. One must therefore be careful about how the quasi-static limit is taken when calculating e.g. the Compton mass" (arXiv:1404.3713) [src: Sect. 4.3].
- **Kobayashi's tensor-function form.** The same limit written with ${\cal G}_T$, ${\cal F}_T$, Θ (arXiv:1901.07183, Eqs. 100–101, in `formalism.md`) drops the mass term and so "cannot be used for f(R) and chameleon models" (see `formalism.md#horndeski-beyond-horndeski-and-dhost`); the Pogosian–Silvestri form keeps $C_\pi$ and the Compton transition.
- **Non-universal coupling (Gleyzes et al. 2015).** With a separate coupling of dark matter, the two Poisson equations read $-\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}\Phi=\frac{3}{2}H^{2}\Omega_{\rm m}\left\{\left(1+\alpha_{\text{T}}+\beta_{\xi}^{2}\right)\omega_{b}\delta_{b}+\left[1+\alpha_{\text{T}}+\beta_{\xi}(\beta_{\xi}+\beta_{\gamma})\right]\omega_{c}\delta_{c}\right\}\,,$ [src: Eq. 3.10] and $-\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}\Psi=\frac{3}{2}H^{2}\Omega_{\rm m}\left\{\left(1+\beta_{\rm B}\beta_{\xi}\right)\omega_{b}\delta_{b}+\left[1+\beta_{\rm B}(\beta_{\xi}+\beta_{\gamma})\right]\omega_{c}\delta_{c}\right\}\,,$ (arXiv:1509.02191) [src: Eq. 3.11], with $\beta_{\rm B}\equiv\frac{\sqrt{2}}{c_{s}\alpha^{1/2}}\alpha_{\text{B}}\;,\beta_{\xi}\equiv\frac{\sqrt{2}}{c_{s}\alpha^{1/2}}\xi\equiv\frac{\sqrt{2}}{c_{s}\alpha^{1/2}}\left[\alpha_{\text{B}}(1+\alpha_{\text{T}})+\alpha_{\text{T}}-\alpha_{\text{M}}\right]\;,$ (arXiv:1509.02191) [src: Eq. 3.12] in their braiding convention. The quasi-static regime is "justified for spatial scales that are smaller than the sound horizon of dark energy, or equivalently for wavenumbers $k\gg aH/c_{s}$" [src: Sect. 3.1]. Cost: the split between a modification of gravity ($\beta_\xi$) and a dark-matter coupling ($\beta_\gamma$) "is not physical and depends on the choice of frame" [src: Sect. 3.1, text after Eq. 3.18].
- **Synchronous-gauge quasi-statics in a Boltzmann code (hi_class).** QS is defined as neglecting the second and first time derivatives of the scalar perturbation in its own equation, not in the Einstein equations, replacing it by $V_{X}=\frac{S\left(\tau,\,k,\,\eta,\,\delta\rho,\,\delta p\right)}{a^{2}H^{2}\mu\left(\tau,\,k\right)^{2}}\,,$ (arXiv:1909.01828) [src: Eq. 5.2], with an analytic time derivative for $V_X'$ [src: Eq. 5.3]. It is switched on per mode only if $\mu^{2}>T_{\mu}^{2}\,\gg 1\,,$ [src: Eq. 5.4] and $\mu_{r}^{2}\equiv\frac{\mu^{2}}{\Omega_{r}^{2}}\times\left(\frac{aH}{c_{r}k}\right)^{2}>T_{r}^{2}\,,$ (arXiv:1909.01828) [src: Eq. 5.5], plus a decay factor and an onset of forced dynamics [src: Sect. 5.2]. Here μ is what hi_class calls "the effective mass μ of the scalar field", not the Poisson function [src: Sect. 5.2]. For a quartic Galileon the automatic scheme gives "a factor ≈ 2.6 improvement in execution time" with differences "well below the sub-percent level"; forcing QS everywhere gives "≈ 6.5" but "considerable deviations again on scales $k\lesssim 10^{-3}h$/Mpc" [src: Sect. 5.3].
- **Quasi-statics on the metric potentials (MGCAMB-like) against quasi-statics on the scalar (hi_class).** "The two QSA strategies are generally consistent, although the methodology based on the metric potentials performs significantly better for the f(R) gravity cosmologies on scales $k\gtrsim 0.1\,h/{\rm Mpc}$" (arXiv:2407.11968) [src: Fig. 1 caption, right panel]. <!-- eq: notation -->
- **An improved quasi-static solution (Sawicki & Bellini).** Instead of dropping time derivatives, the potential is split into an oscillating part and a QS part, $\Phi\equiv\varphi+\Phi_{\text{QS}}\,,$ (arXiv:1503.06831) [src: Eq. 3], with the ansatz $k_{H}^{2}\Phi_{\text{QS}}\equiv-\frac{3}{2}\left(A_{1}+\frac{\epsilon A_{2}}{k_{H}^{2}}\right)\Omega_{\text{m}}\delta-\frac{3\epsilon B_{1}}{2}\Omega_{\text{m}}Hv\,.$ (arXiv:1503.06831) [src: Eq. 5] chosen so that φ has no source; "This is close but not exactly the same definition as is used when time derivatives are just neglected" [src: Sect. II]. With $A_1=\mu_\delta$ (arXiv:1503.06831) [src: Eq. 7], "This choice also matches the standard choice for the leading-order terms of the QS solution" [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 7]; the corrections are in "What goes wrong".

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Outside the dark-energy sound horizon.** "Above the speed of sound, the scalar field develops its own gravitational instability and no longer traces the matter inhomogeneities. In this case, the QSA is definitely not valid. Thus, restricting the QSA to the sub-sound-hoirzon scales is a necessary condition, which may or may not be sufficient" (arXiv:1606.05339) [src: Sect. IV.4]; "In general, however, the validity of the QSA is model-dependent" [src: same place]. Check: compare $c_s k/(aH)$ for the longest mode used.
- **Oscillations that do not decay.** Inside the sound horizon the deviation obeys $\ddot{\varphi}+(\gamma-1)\mathcal{H}\dot{\varphi}+c_{\text{s}}^{2}k^{2}\varphi=0\,,$ (arXiv:1503.06831) [src: Eq. 9] in conformal time; for constant friction and $c_s^2\propto(1+z)^{-p}$ the envelope decays as $(1+z)^\nu$ with $\nu=\frac{p}{4}+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\,,$ (arXiv:1503.06831) [src: Eq. 10]; with a friction coefficient of 3 the deviation decays only for p > −4, "Thus the sound speed must decrease rapidly for φ to grow" (arXiv:1503.06831) [src: Sect. IV]. The friction coefficient is "γ ≈ 3" in models where "the kineticity is small compared to the braiding" (e.g. f(R)) and "γ ≈ 4" in perfect-fluid models [src: Sect. IV]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Friction corrections near the sound horizon.** The corrections to the friction term of the growth equation "are of order $\mu_{\delta}$ and are only suppressed by the sound horizon for the dark energy, $c_{\text{s}}k_{H}$ rather than the cosmological horizon $k_{H}$", so near the sound horizon the growth response "is different than what would be expected from the naive guess, even if the quasi-static solution for Φ were" valid (arXiv:1503.06831) [src: Sect. III]. The minimum sound speed for a survey is $c_{\text{s}}\gtrsim\sqrt{\Omega_{\text{m0}}}\frac{H_{0}}{k_{\text{min}}}\left(\frac{\mu_{0}}{\epsilon}\right)^{1/(s+\nu)}(1+z_{\text{surv}})^{\nu/(s+\nu)}\,.$ (arXiv:1503.06831) [src: Eq. 14], "an order-of-magnitude approximation" [src: Sect. V]; as printed, Eq. 14 writes the tolerance as ϵ, the symbol of the order parameter of Eq. 4, while the tolerance defined in Eq. 11 is ε.
- **Superhorizon history.** Even when surveyed scales are deep in the QS regime, "The validity of the QS solution guarantees the evolution for the growth factor from one redshift to another, but does not necessarily ensure that the amplitude will not have been modified previously by the interaction between the DE degree of freedom and the matter on superhorizon scales" (arXiv:1503.06831) [src: Sect. IV]. Check: normalise with a full Boltzmann solution, not with the QS growth from early times.
- **Large scales within the sound horizon.** In sampled viable Horndeski models the QSA breaks down at the large scales of the Numbers table, "due to the time-derivatives of the metric and the scalar field perturbations, which are neglected under the QSA, becoming comparable to the spatial derivatives" (arXiv:1712.00444) [src: Sect. V].
- **Four failure situations in a code.** "(i) when the coefficients of the perturbations ( $\Omega_{\text{DE}},\alpha_{i}$ ) are varying rapidly"; "(ii) during the radiation era when radiation pressure can make the source S oscillate"; "(iii) when $\mu(\tau,k)^{2}<0$, the solution to the homogeneous equation is tachyonic and does not decay away"; "(iv) when $\mu\left(\tau,\,k\right)^{2}\lesssim 1$" (arXiv:1909.01828) [src: Sect. 5.1, inline, text after Eq. 5.3]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **A hidden instability.** "taking the QS approximation is equivalent to turning a full degree of freedom into a constraint … It may well be that an instability exists which the quasi-static limit would hide" (arXiv:1404.3713) [src: Sect. 4.3].
- **Dropped mass term.** Using a mass-free $G_{\rm eff}$ for f(R) or a chameleon misses the Compton transition (`formalism.md#horndeski-beyond-horndeski-and-dhost`, Kobayashi's warning); use Eqs. 19–21 of arXiv:1606.05339 or the f(R) μ of `formalism.md#f-r-gravity`.
- **Interacting dark sectors.** For interacting quintessence and phantom fields, the sub-horizon approximation's accuracy "progressively deteriorates with increasing interaction strength and towards larger scales", "The QSA generally provides a closer description of the full relativistic evolution than the SHA", and "the validity of the SHA cannot be assumed solely from the sub-horizon condition" (arXiv:2609.30937, Saha et al. 2026) [src: abstract].
- **Non-linear scales.** In ECOSMOG simulations of Hu–Sawicki f(R), time derivatives "make a negligible contribution" for $\lvert f_R\rvert=10^{-4}$ and $10^{-5}$; the $10^{-6}$ model "shows a larger deviation from the quasi-static approximation, but one that diminishes when re-simulated at higher resolution", and the authors conclude that the approximation "is valid for the most practical applications in f(R) cosmologies" (arXiv:1411.6128, Bose et al. 2015) [src: abstract]. The symmetron test is in `approximations.md#quasi-static-approximation`.
- **A printed slip.** Pogosian & Silvestri's App. A prints $HM_{*}^{2}\alpha_{K}$ on the left of their Eq. 58 and Eq. 63 (arXiv:1606.05339) [src: Eqs. 58, 63], where Bellini & Sawicki write $H^{2}M_{*}^{2}\alpha_{\textrm{K}}$ for the same right-hand sides (arXiv:1404.3713) [src: Eq. 53; Table 2]; no erratum was found. <!-- eq: notation --> Use the Bellini–Sawicki form.
