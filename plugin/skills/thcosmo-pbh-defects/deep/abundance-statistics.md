## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- **Compaction normalisation.** Musco 2018 (`deep/collapse-threshold.md`, Step 3) and Ferrante et al. 2022 define the compaction function as twice the mass excess over the areal radius, $\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(r,t)=\frac{2\left[M(r,t)-M_{b}(r,t)\right]}{R(r,t)}=\frac{2}{R(r,t)}\int_{S^{2}_{R}}d^{3}\vec{x}\left[\rho(\vec{x},t)-\rho_{b}(t)\right]=\frac{2}{R(r,t)}\underbrace{\int_{S^{2}_{R}}d^{3}\vec{x}\,\rho_{b}(t)\,\delta(\vec{x},t)}_{\coloneqq\,\delta M(r,t)}\,.$ (arXiv:2211.01728) [src: Eq. 44]. Yoo, Harada, Hirano & Kohri 2020 use $\mathcal{C}:=\frac{\delta M}{R},$ (arXiv:2008.02425) [src: Eq. 3] and state the criterion as $\mathcal{C}^{\rm max}>\mathcal{C}_{\rm th}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\rm th}.$ [src: Eq. 6], with $\mathcal{C}_{\rm th}$ "evaluated as $\simeq 0.267$" in the comoving slicing [src: Sect. II, inline, text after Eq. 6], a value they take from their Ref. [23].
  - `[der]` The same perturbation therefore has a threshold twice as large in the normalisation with the factor 2 as in theirs.
- **Sign of $\zeta$.** Yoo et al. 2020 write the spatial metric as $\mathrm{d}s_{3}^{2}=a^{2}{\rm e}^{-2\zeta}\tilde{\gamma}_{ij}\mathrm{d}x^{i}\mathrm{d}x^{j}$ (arXiv:2008.02425) [src: Eq. 1], so that their compaction function is $\mathcal{C}(r)=\frac{1}{3}\left[1-\left(1-r\zeta^{\prime}\right)^{2}\right].$ [src: Eq. 4]. De Luca et al. 2019 write $\displaystyle\delta(\vec{x},t)=-\frac{4}{9a^{2}H^{2}}e^{-2\zeta(\vec{x})}\left[\nabla^{2}\zeta(\vec{x})+\frac{1}{2}\zeta_{i}(\vec{x})\zeta^{i}(\vec{x})\right]=\frac{4}{9a^{2}H^{2}}e^{-2\nu\sigma_{0}}\left[x\sigma_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\vec{\eta}\cdot\vec{\eta}\right].$ (arXiv:1904.00970) [src: Eq. 4.17].
  - `[der]` The two papers therefore use opposite signs of $\zeta$; the review's sign is in `formalism.md#conventions`.
- **Window functions.** Gow et al. 2020 use a real-space top-hat and "a Gaussian window function modified by a factor of 2 in the exponent as suggested in Ref. [29]", $\displaystyle W_{\text{G}}(k,R)=\exp\left(-\frac{(kR)^{2}}{4}\right).$ (arXiv:2008.03289) [src: Eq. 15; Sect. II, text before Eq. 15], and warn that with it "the compaction referred to by $C$ above is not technically the compaction, but is rather a 'compaction-like' function" [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 15]. Young 2019 normalises each window so that the integrand $4\pi r^{2}W(r,R)$ "should peak at a scale $R$", noting that "the standard Gaussian window function has an extra factor of $1/\sqrt{2}$ inside the exponential compared to equation (27)" (arXiv:1905.01230) [src: Sect. VI, inline, second paragraph and footnote 4]. 
  - `[der]` A smoothing scale $R$ is only defined together with its window.
- **Ferrante et al. 2022** write the gradient-expansion density contrast with a coefficient $\Phi$ and define the linear component $\mathcal{C}_{1}$ accordingly (Eq. 46 below); the value of $\Phi$ is set in a part of the paper not read here.

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — from $\mathcal P_\zeta$ to the variance of the smoothed variable
<!-- key: step-1-from-mathcal-p-zeta-to-the-variance-of -->

Gow et al. 2020 compare three routes with one common chain (arXiv:2008.03289). The abundance today follows from the collapse fraction by $\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathrm{PBH}}=\int\mathrm{d}(\ln R)\ \frac{R_{\text{eq}}}{R}\beta(R),$ [src: Eq. 1], "where $R$ is the horizon scale at the time the PBH is forming", and the mass function by $\displaystyle f(m)=\frac{1}{\Omega_{\mathrm{CDM}}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\Omega_{\mathrm{PBH}}}{\mathrm{d}(\ln m)},$ [src: Eq. 2]. The width parameters are $\displaystyle\sigma_{n}^{2}(R)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}k}{k}\ k^{2n}\,\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{R}}(k),$ [src: Eq. 12] with the compaction power spectrum $\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{R}}(k)=\frac{16}{81}(kR)^{4}W^{2}(k,R)\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}(k).$ [src: Eq. 13], and the real-space top-hat $\displaystyle W_{\text{TH}}(k,R)=3\frac{\sin(kR)-kR\cos(kR)}{(kR)^{3}},$ [src: Eq. 14], "modified ... to remove a ringing effect at large- $R$ " [src: Sect. II, inline, footnote 3]. The PBH mass enters through critical collapse, $\displaystyle m=KM_{H}(C-C_{c})^{\gamma},$ [src: Eq. 16].

The critical values must match the window. The values "$K\approx 3.3$, $C_{c}\approx 0.45$, and $\gamma\approx 0.36$ (commonly referred to as the Musco criteria) were derived for the top-hat window function [37, 38, 27], but are regularly used for other window functions"; Gow et al. take, from their Ref. [26], "$K=4$ and $C_{c}=0.55$ for the top-hat window function, and $K=10$ and $C_{c}=0.25$ for the modified Gaussian window function", with $\gamma=0.36$ for both (arXiv:2008.03289) [src: Sect. II, inline, text after Eq. 16]. Young 2019 makes the same point for the comparison of windows: earlier results are reproduced "correcting for the fact that the critical value for collapse should be calculated with the same smoothing function that the variance is calculated with" (arXiv:1905.01230) [src: Sect. VI, first paragraph]. Young also includes the linear transfer function, writing the smoothed power spectrum for a scale-invariant $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}=\mathcal{A}_{s}$ as $\mathcal{P}_{\delta}=\frac{16}{81}\epsilon^{4}(t)(kr_{m})^{4}\tilde{W}^{2}(k,r_{m})T^{2}(k,\eta)\mathcal{A}_{s},$ [src: Eq. 29], and finds that for the real-space top-hat "the power spectrum oscillates and does not converge to zero until sub-horizon modes are considered and the transfer function becomes important" [src: Sect. VI, inline, paragraph after Fig. 5]. The transfer-function-only route of Musco et al. 2021 is in `deep/collapse-threshold.md` (Step 7).

### Step 2 — Press–Schechter and the factor 2
<!-- key: step-2-press-schechter-and-the-factor-2 -->

In Gow et al.'s form, $\displaystyle\beta(R)=2\int_{C_{c}}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}C\ \frac{m}{M_{H}}P(C),$ (arXiv:2008.03289) [src: Eq. 6] with the Gaussian $\displaystyle P(C)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_{0}(R)}\exp\left(-\frac{C^{2}}{2\sigma_{0}(R)^{2}}\right),$ [src: Eq. 7]; the review's version, with its stated assumptions, is in `formalism.md#pbh-statistics-from-the-power-spectrum-to-the`.

Kushwaha & Suyama recast this as an excursion set for PBHs forming during radiation domination (arXiv:2509.25871, Kushwaha & Suyama 2025). PBH formation "occurs on the scale of the Hubble horizon", so the mass is identified with $M=1/(2GH)$ and the relevant quantity is the density contrast smoothed over the horizon [src: Sect. III, inline, first paragraph], with $\delta(t,{\bm{x}})=-\frac{4}{9a^{2}H^{2}}\triangle\zeta({\bm{x}}).$ [src: Eq. 19]. With a sharp-$k$ filter the variance is $\sigma_{M}^{2}=\int\frac{d{\bm{k}}}{{(2\pi)}^{3}}~\frac{16}{81}{\left(\frac{k}{k_{M}}\right)}^{4}\Theta(1-\frac{k}{k_{M}})P_{\zeta}(k)=\frac{16}{81}\int_{0}^{k_{M}}\frac{dk}{k}{\left(\frac{k}{k_{M}}\right)}^{4}{\cal P}_{\zeta}(k),$ [src: Eq. 20], which, "contrary to $\sigma_{M}$ in the case of the halo formation", "is not a monotonically increasing function of $1/M$" [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 20]; for a sharply peaked spectrum $\sigma_{M}^{2}=\frac{16{\cal A}}{81}{\left(\frac{M}{M_{*}}\right)}^{2}\Theta(M_{*}-M),$ [src: Eq. 22]. Viewed as a random walk in $\tau=a_{M}/GM$, the two-point function is $\langle\delta(\tau)\delta(\tau^{\prime})\rangle=\frac{16}{81}\int\frac{d{\bm{k}}}{{(2\pi)}^{3}}P_{\zeta}(k){\left(\frac{k}{\tau}\right)}^{2}{\left(\frac{k}{\tau^{\prime}}\right)}^{2}\Theta(1-\frac{k}{\tau})\Theta(1-\frac{k}{\tau^{\prime}}).$ [src: Eq. 23], and the noise correlator (Eq. 24) has terms "which do not vanish even for $\tau\neq\tau^{\prime}$ in the present case, meaning that the $\xi$ is colored noise" [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 24]. Assuming Gaussianity of $\delta$, "because of the presence of the correlated term in Eq. (24)", trajectories of the two kinds the paper distinguishes (its B and B′) are "no longer equally likely" [src: Sect. III, paragraph after Eq. 24]; the abstract concludes that for PBHs the equality of the two contributions "no longer holds, and consistent inclusion of both contributions is essential to ensure a positive-definite mass function" [src: abstract]. The paper uses "the traditional quantity $\delta$, rather than more modern alternatives such as the compaction function", stating that "The specific choice of quantity is not essential to our discussion regarding the fudge factor 2" [src: Sect. III, first paragraph].

For a smooth (Gaussian) window, Saito & Tokeshi find that the noise "becomes fully correlated across scales" and that the maximum of the mass function and "its neighbourhood are predominantly determined by the probability that the density contrast exceeds a given threshold at each mass scale" (Saito & Tokeshi 2025, arXiv:2512.22075) [src: abstract].

### Step 3 — peak theory
<!-- key: step-3-peak-theory -->

- **Traditional (BBKS) peaks.** Gow et al. write $\displaystyle\beta(R)=(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}R^{3}\int_{C_{c}}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}C\ \frac{m}{M_{H}}\ n\left(\frac{C}{\sigma_{0}(R)}\right),$ (arXiv:2008.03289) [src: Eq. 8] with the high-peak number density $\displaystyle n(\nu)=\frac{1}{3^{3/2}(2\pi)^{2}}\left(\frac{\sigma_{1}}{\sigma_{0}}\right)^{3}\nu^{3}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\nu^{2}\right).$ [src: Eq. 9], quoted from BBKS (their Ref. [33]), which is not on arXiv. Green, Liddle, Malik & Sasaki had earlier revisited the abundance "using a formation criterion derived by Shibata and Sasaki which refers to a metric perturbation variable rather than the usual density contrast" (Green et al. 2004, arXiv:astro-ph/0403181) [src: abstract].
- **Modified peaks (Young & Musso).** Its starting point is that "For PBHs, the final mass is related to the amplitude and scale of the perturbation from which it forms" (Young & Musso 2020, arXiv:2001.06469) [src: abstract]; in Gow et al.'s form (arXiv:2008.03289) the modified number density enters with $R^4$, $\displaystyle\beta(R)=(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}R^{4}\int_{C_{c}}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}C\ \frac{m}{M_{H}}\ n\left(\frac{C}{\sigma_{0}(R)}\right).$ [src: Eq. 10], "required to counteract an extra inverse spatial dimension in the number density" of their Eq. 11 [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 10], derived in Young & Musso, read here at the abstract level only.
- **Peaks of $\triangle\zeta$ (Yoo et al.).** Yoo, Harada, Hirano & Kohri count peaks of the Laplacian of $\zeta$ with amplitude $\mu_2$ and scale $k_\bullet$, taking the number density from Eq. (58) of their Ref. [1] with the replacements $\mu\rightarrow\mu_{2},~k_{*}\rightarrow k_{\bullet},~\sigma_{n}\rightarrow\sigma_{n+2},~\gamma\rightarrow\gamma_{3}.$ (arXiv:2008.02425) [src: Eq. 30]: $\displaystyle n^{(k_{\bullet})}_{\rm pk}(\mu_{2},k_{\bullet})\mathrm{d}\mu_{2}\mathrm{d}k_{\bullet}={\frac{2}{3^{3/2}(2\pi)^{3/2}}}\mu_{2}k_{\bullet}\frac{\sigma_{4}^{2}}{\sigma_{2}\sigma_{3}^{3}}f\left(\frac{\mu_{2}k_{\bullet}^{2}}{\sigma_{4}}\right)P_{1}\left(\frac{\mu_{2}}{\sigma_{2}},\frac{\mu_{2}k_{\bullet}^{2}}{\sigma_{4}}\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{2}\mathrm{d}k_{\bullet},$ [src: Eq. 25], with $P_{1}\left(\frac{\mu_{2}}{\sigma_{2}},\frac{\mu_{2}k_{\bullet}^{2}}{\sigma_{4}}\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{1-\gamma_{3}^{2}}}\exp\left[-\frac{\mu_{2}^{2}}{2{\tilde{\sigma}(k_{\bullet})}^{2}}\right]$ [src: Eq. 28] and $\frac{1}{\tilde{\sigma}^{2}(k_{\bullet})}:=\frac{1}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\sigma_{4}^{2}(1-\gamma_{3}^{2})}\left(k_{\bullet}^{2}-\frac{\sigma_{3}^{2}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}\right)^{2}.$ [src: Eq. 29]. After the change of variable from $k_\bullet$ to $M$ (Eqs. 31–33), the PBH number density is $n_{\rm BH}\mathrm{d}\ln M=\left[\int^{\infty}_{\mu_{\rm 2b}}\mathrm{d}\mu_{2}~n^{(M)}_{\rm pk}(\mu_{2},M)\right]M\mathrm{d}\ln M.$ [src: Eq. 34] and the collapse fraction follows from Eqs. 35–37 [src: Eqs. 35–37]. "A crucial difference" of their Eq. 37 from Eq. 61 of their Ref. [1] (the abstract says they modify the procedure of arXiv:1805.03946) "is that the expression does not depend on $\sigma_{0}$, which has IR-log divergence for the flat scale-invariant spectrum" [src: Sect. IV, inline, text after Eq. 37]. The formula assumes "that there is only one peak" with the given amplitude inside $r=r_{\rm m}$; if the spectrum "is broad enough or has multiple peaks at far-separated scales, and the typical PBH mass is relatively larger than the minimum scale given by the spectrum", there are "multiple peaks inside $r=r_{\rm m}$" and a window function is needed [src: Sect. IV, paragraph after Eq. 33].
- **The window in the $\triangle\zeta$ route.** Yoo et al. replace $\mathcal{P}$ by $\mathcal{P}_{W}(k)=\mathcal{P}(k)W(k;k_{W})^{2},$ (arXiv:2008.02425) [src: Eq. 45]; "The final PBH mass spectrum is given by the envelope curve of the mass spectra for all values of $k_{W}$", and, for a narrow spectrum, the result is independent of the window in the limit $k_W\to\infty$ [src: Sect. V, inline, first paragraph]. They "expect that a sharp cut-off of the window function would provide a larger value of the peak number density minimizing the extra reduction of the mass spectrum due to the window function" [src: Sect. V, paragraph after Fig. 2].
- **Peaks with a non-Gaussian tail.** Kitajima, Tada, Yokoyama & Yoo "update the peak theory" "particularly by implementing the critical behavior in the estimation of the PBH mass and employing the averaged compaction function for the PBH formation criterion to relax the profile dependence", and, for an exponential tail, confirm an enhancement "even compared to the case of the corresponding nonlinearity parameter $f_\mathrm{NL}=5/2$", with "a characteristic maximal mass which is not seen in the simple Press--Schechter approach" (Kitajima et al. 2021, arXiv:2109.00791) [src: abstract, inline].

### Step 4 — the nonlinear $\delta$–$\zeta$ relation
<!-- key: step-4-the-nonlinear-delta-zeta-relation -->

- **Analytic estimate at the centre of spherical peaks (Gow et al. 2020, Appendix C).** At first order in gradients, "the full non-linear relationship, in polar coordinates and assuming spherical symmetry", is $\delta_{NL}=\frac{\delta\rho}{\rho_{b}}(r,t)=-\frac{4(1+\omega)}{5+3\omega}\left(\frac{1}{aH}\right)^{2}e^{-5\zeta(r)/2}\nabla^{2}e^{\zeta(r)/2},$ (arXiv:2008.03289) [src: Eq. 38], against the linear $\delta_{L}=\frac{\delta\rho_{l}}{\rho_{b}}=-\frac{2(1+\omega)}{5+3\omega}\left(\frac{1}{aH}\right)^{2}\nabla^{2}\zeta.$ [src: Eq. 39]. Smoothing the time-independent part with a top-hat gives "an expression for the compaction function at the centre of spherically symmetric peaks", $C(\mathbf{x},R)=C_{L}-\frac{3}{8}C_{L}^{2},$ [src: Eq. 42], with $C_{L}=\frac{2}{3}R\zeta^{\prime}(R),$ [src: Eq. 43], the prime being "a derivative with respect to the smoothing scale $R$" [src: App. C, text after Eq. 43]. The compaction "has a maximum value, $C_{\mathrm{max}}=2/3$, corresponding to $C_{L}=4/3$"; larger $C_L$ "correspond to a case for which PBH formation has not been simulated" [src: App. C, inline, text after Eq. 43]. The mass becomes $\displaystyle m=kM_{H}(C_{L}-\frac{3}{8}C_{L}^{2}-C_{c})^{\gamma}.$ [src: Eq. 44]. Assuming all collapsing peaks are close to critical and $C_{c}=0.55$, "the critical amplitude for the linear component of the compaction is $C_{c,L}\approx 0.77$", so the power spectrum "should be approximately $1.41^{2}=1.98$ times greater"; for the two lognormal spectra of the paper "the approximation holds to the precision of two decimal places stated above", and "Although this validity may vary with the position of the peak", they assume it holds globally for their constraint figures [src: App. C, inline, paragraph after Eq. 44]. The authors note that with a Gaussian window "there is no analytic solution" and "expect the non-linearities described above to have a similar effect on constraints on the power spectrum, whether a top-hat or Gaussian function is used" [src: App. C, last paragraph].
- **Exact change of variables in threshold statistics (De Luca et al. 2019).** From the joint Gaussian distribution of $\zeta$, its gradient $\vec\eta$ and $x=-\nabla^{2}\zeta/\sigma_{2}$, and the local relation Eq. 4.17 (Conventions), they change variables with $\displaystyle x_{\delta}=x,\quad\vec{\eta}_{\delta}=\vec{\eta},\quad\nu=\frac{1}{2\sigma_{0}}{\rm ln}\left[\frac{4\left(x_{\delta}\sigma_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\vec{\eta}_{\delta}\cdot\vec{\eta}_{\delta}\right)}{9a^{2}H^{2}\delta}\right].$ (arXiv:1904.00970) [src: Eq. 4.18], Jacobian $\displaystyle J=\left|\frac{1}{2\delta\sigma_{0}}\right|.$ [src: Eq. 4.19], to obtain the distribution of $\delta$ (Eqs. 4.20–4.22). The method "does not rely on the fact that spiky peaks of the curvature perturbation coincide with peaks of the overdensity for large thresholds"; "The price to pay is that we will be dealing with the threshold statistics", while "The gain is that the expressions we are going to obtain are exact" [src: Sect. 4, first paragraph]. The variances use the real-space top-hat at $r_m$ [src: Eq. 4.8].
- **Compaction-function form (Ferrante et al. 2022).** Integrating the gradient-expansion density contrast gives $\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(r)=-2\Phi\,r\,\zeta^{\prime}(r)\left[1+\frac{r}{2}\zeta^{\prime}(r)\right]=\mathcal{C}_{1}(r)-\frac{1}{4\Phi}\mathcal{C}_{1}(r)^{2}\,,~~~~~~~~~~~~~\mathcal{C}_{1}(r)\coloneqq-2\Phi\,r\,\zeta^{\prime}(r)\,,$ (arXiv:2211.01728) [src: Eq. 46], which "automatically includes the full non-linear relation between $\delta$ and $\zeta$" [src: Sect. III.1, text after Eq. 46].

### Step 5 — local non-Gaussianity and the non-perturbative tail
<!-- key: step-5-local-non-gaussianity-and-the-non -->

- **All orders in a local map (Ferrante et al. 2022).** For $\zeta=F(\zeta_{\rm G})$, the linear component becomes $\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}(r)=-2\Phi\,r\,\zeta_{\rm G}^{\prime}(r)\,\frac{dF}{d\zeta_{\rm G}}=\mathcal{C}_{\rm G}(r)\,\frac{dF}{d\zeta_{\rm G}}\,,~~~~~~~~{\rm with}~~~\mathcal{C}_{\rm G}(r)\coloneqq-2\Phi\,r\,\zeta_{\rm G}^{\prime}(r)\,.$ (arXiv:2211.01728) [src: Eq. 49] and $\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(r)=\mathcal{C}_{\rm G}(r)\,\frac{dF}{d\zeta_{\rm G}}-\frac{1}{4\Phi}\mathcal{C}^{2}_{\rm G}(r)\left(\frac{dF}{d\zeta_{\rm G}}\right)^{2}\,.$ [src: Eq. 50], so that the compaction depends on two correlated Gaussian variables. Their joint distribution is $\displaystyle\textrm{P}_{\rm G}(\mathcal{C}_{\rm G},\zeta_{\rm G})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)\sigma_{c}\sigma_{r}\sqrt{1-\gamma_{cr}^{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{\zeta_{\rm G}^{2}}{2\sigma_{r}^{2}}\right)\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2(1-\gamma_{cr}^{2})}\left(\frac{\mathcal{C}_{\rm G}}{\sigma_{c}}-\frac{\gamma_{cr}\zeta_{\rm G}}{\sigma_{r}}\right)^{2}\right]\,,$ [src: Eq. 58] with $\displaystyle\gamma_{cr}\coloneqq\frac{\sigma_{cr}^{2}}{\sigma_{c}\sigma_{r}}\,.$ [src: Eq. 59] and the variances of Eqs. 55–57, built with the top-hat $W$ for $\mathcal{C}_{\rm G}$ and $W_{s}(k,r)=\sin(kr)/kr$ for $\zeta_{\rm G}$, at $R=r_m$ and with the transfer function at $\tau=r_m$ [src: Sect. III.1, text after Eq. 57]. The "high-peak limit" of their Ref. [47] sets $\gamma_{cr}\to 1$ and "is strictly valid if one takes $\gamma_{cr}\to 1$ that is the limit in which one takes a monochromatic power spectrum" [src: Sect. III.1, inline, text after Eq. 60]. The general prescription, their Eq. 61, integrates over a type-I domain in $(\mathcal{C}_{\rm G},\zeta_{\rm G})$ and "gives an exact expression in the presence of primordial NGs" in threshold statistics [src: Sect. III.1, text after the prescription]; the display itself is not in the rendering read (see "Not verified").
- **Any one-point distribution (Gow et al. 2022).** Gow et al. 2022 map $\zeta$ to a Gaussian field "by equating the cumulative distribution functions", $\displaystyle F\left[\zeta(r),r\right]=F_{\mathrm{G}}\left[\zeta_{\mathrm{G}}(r),\Sigma_{YY}(r)\right],$ (arXiv:2211.08348) [src: Eq. 6; Sect. II, text before Eq. 6], using that "a variable defined by $F_{X}(X)$ is always uniformly distributed" [src: Sect. II, inline, text before Eq. 6]. For their two examples (an ultra-slow-roll local distribution and "a general piecewise model for $P(\zeta)$ with an exponential tail") they "demonstrate that the enhancement of primordial black hole formation is due to the intermediate regime, rather than the far tail" [src: abstract].
- **Status.** For local non-Gaussianity the perturbative approach is "intrinsically flawed when considering broad spectra" (arXiv:2211.01728) [src: abstract]; uncertainties in $f_{\rm NL}$ "can induce substantial uncertainties in the PBH abundance", which in the forecast of Iovino, Perna & Veermäe for LISA "ultimately limits LISA's capacity to fully probe the asteroid-mass PBH DM window" (Iovino et al. 2025, arXiv:2512.13648) [src: abstract]. How the exponential tail arises is in `thcosmo-inflation` (`deep/stochastic-delta-n-tails.md`).

### Step 6 — how far the routes differ for one spectrum
<!-- key: step-6-how-far-the-routes-differ-for-one -->

Gow et al. 2020 fix $f_{\mathrm{PBH}}=2\times 10^{-3}$ for peaks centred on the LIGO mass range and give the required amplitude $A$ of the lognormal spectrum ${\cal P}_{\zeta}=A\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\Delta}\exp\left(-\frac{\ln^{2}(k/k_{p})}{2\Delta^{2}}\right)$ (arXiv:2008.03289) [src: Eq. 17] for each method and window [src: Table 1]:

| Peak | G, PS | G, TP | G, YM | TH, PS | TH, TP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Delta function | $3.21\times 10^{-3}$ | $2.93\times 10^{-3}$ | N/A | $3.47\times 10^{-3}$ | $2.94\times 10^{-3}$ |
| Lognormal, $\Delta=0.3$ | $4.14\times 10^{-3}$ | $3.78\times 10^{-3}$ | $3.55\times 10^{-3}$ | $4.84\times 10^{-3}$ | $4.13\times 10^{-3}$ |
| Lognormal, $\Delta=1.0$ | $8.92\times 10^{-3}$ | $8.14\times 10^{-3}$ | $7.70\times 10^{-3}$ | $1.11\times 10^{-2}$ | $9.56\times 10^{-3}$ |

Values from arXiv:2008.03289 [src: Table 1]; G is the modified Gaussian and TH the real-space top-hat window, each with its own critical values (Step 1); PS is Press–Schechter, TP traditional peaks, YM the modified peaks of Young & Musso, which "cannot be applied in the case of a delta function peak, or with the top-hat window function" [src: Table 1 caption]. "When changing either the method or the window function while keeping the other fixed, the difference in the required amplitude is $\lesssim 20\%$. The biggest difference when taking both the window function and the calculation method into account is $\sim 32\%$", "when being careful with the combination of the window function and the corresponding critical collapse values" [src: Sect. III.1, inline, paragraph after Table 1]. The mass distributions of PS and TP are "very similar", while YM "yields a marginally taller and narrower mass distribution" [src: Sect. III.1, paragraph after Fig. 1].

Other comparisons:

- **Window function, with matched critical values.** Young finds that "for a given abundance of PBHs" the uncertainty in the amplitude of the power spectrum from the choice of smoothing function "is $\mathcal{O}(10\%)$, an order of magnitude smaller than previous calculations suggest" (arXiv:1905.01230) [src: abstract, inline]. For PBHs of 30 solar masses accounting for the LIGO events, Ando, Inomata & Kawasaki state that these uncertainties "significantly affect the prediction for the stochastic gravitational waves" induced at second order, and that "the pulsar timing array constraints on the produced GWs could disappear for the real-space top-hat window function" (Ando et al. 2018, arXiv:1802.06393) [src: abstract].
- **PS against peaks, narrow against broad.** For formation during radiation domination, Kushwaha & Suyama find that the constraints on $\mathcal P_\zeta$ "remain largely similar for the monochromatic case" but "differ in the case of a broad primordial power spectrum" (arXiv:2603.23025) [src: abstract].
- **Choice of collapse variable.** Using $\mathcal{R}_c$ "in the standard way" overestimates the number "by many orders of magnitude" (arXiv:1405.7023) [src: abstract]; the peak-theory abundance with a local density threshold and no window comes out larger "by many orders of magnitude" (arXiv:1805.03946) [src: abstract]; both are in `results.md#statistics-non-gaussianity-and-clustering`.

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **Press–Schechter.** One Gaussian integral over the smoothed compaction (Step 2); the factor 2 is not justified for PBHs in the excursion-set analysis (arXiv:2509.25871) [src: abstract], and with a Gaussian window the noise correlations matter for the shape of the mass function (arXiv:2512.22075) [src: abstract].
- **Traditional peaks.** Needs $\sigma_1/\sigma_0$; the mass function is close to PS in Gow et al.'s test (Step 6).
- **Modified peaks (YM).** Accounts for scale and amplitude in the mass; not defined for a delta-function peak or with the top-hat window (Step 6).
- **Peaks of $\triangle\zeta$.** No dependence on $\sigma_0$, so a flat spectrum needs no IR cut-off (arXiv:2008.02425) [src: Sect. IV, text after Eq. 37]; broad spectra need the envelope over $k_W$, for which the authors expect a sharp cut-off to give a larger peak number density [src: Sect. V].
- **Threshold statistics on the compaction function.** Exact for the nonlinear relation and for local non-Gaussianity (arXiv:1904.00970) [src: Sect. 4, first paragraph]; (arXiv:2211.01728) [src: Sect. III.1]; threshold, not peak, statistics.
- **CDF matching.** Takes any one-point $P(\zeta)$ (arXiv:2211.08348) [src: abstract].
- **Code.** pbhstat handles "nearly Gaussian initial conditions" only, as recorded in `open-questions.md#which-statistics-turn-mathcal-p-zeta-into-an`.

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Critical values from one window used with another.** The "Musco criteria" are "regularly used for other window functions" though "derived for the top-hat window function" (Step 1). Young reproduces the calculation of his Ref. [28], in which the required amplitude "can vary by more than an order of magnitude" with the smoothing function, "correcting for the fact that the critical value for collapse should be calculated with the same smoothing function that the variance is calculated with" (arXiv:1905.01230) [src: Sect. VI, first paragraph].
- **Compaction normalised two ways.** $\mathcal{C}^{\rm max}>\mathcal{C}_{\rm th}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\rm th}.$ in Yoo et al. 2020 (arXiv:2008.02425) [src: Eq. 6], against $2\delta M/R$ elsewhere (Conventions).
  - `[der]` Check which normalisation a quoted threshold belongs to before comparing it.
- **Top-hat ringing.** In the appendix on "Ringing in the top-hat window function", the "ringing peaks visible in the delta case" in $\sigma_0(R)$ "merge to a constant height as $\Delta$ increases" (arXiv:2008.03289) [src: App. A, Fig. A.1 caption]; with the transfer function, the top-hat-smoothed spectrum "oscillates and does not converge to zero until sub-horizon modes are considered" (arXiv:1905.01230) [src: Sect. VI, paragraph after Fig. 5].
- **IR divergence of $\sigma_0$ for flat spectra.** The earlier procedure depends on $\sigma_0$, which "has IR-log divergence for the flat scale-invariant spectrum"; the $\triangle\zeta$ route avoids it (arXiv:2008.02425) [src: Sect. IV, text after Eq. 37].
- **Several peaks inside $r_m$.** The single-peak assumption fails for broad or multi-scale spectra when the typical PBH mass is well above the minimum scale of the spectrum (arXiv:2008.02425) [src: Sect. IV, paragraph after Eq. 33].
- **Large linear amplitudes.** Perturbations beyond the maximum of the compaction have not been simulated (Step 4).
- **High-peak limit for broad spectra.** Valid only for $\gamma_{cr}\to 1$, the monochromatic case (arXiv:2211.01728) [src: Sect. III.1, text after Eq. 60].
- **Printed slips, copied, not corrected; no erratum looked for.** (i) arXiv:2008.03289 v2 writes the target abundance "in the range $10^{-2}<f_{\mathrm{PBH}}<10^{-3}$" [src: Sect. III.1, third paragraph]: as printed the lower bound exceeds the upper. (ii) The same paper writes the coefficient of critical collapse as $K$ in Eq. 16 and as $k$ in Eq. 44 [src: Eqs. 16, 44]. (iii) arXiv:1904.00970 v2 prints $\displaystyle\langle\zeta_{i}\zeta_{i}\rangle=\frac{\sigma_{1}^{2}}{3}\delta_{ij},$ [src: Eq. 4.5], with repeated index $i$ on the left and $ij$ on the right. (iv) arXiv:2008.02425 v3 integrates over $\mathrm{d}\mu$ with integrand in $\mu_2$ and lower limit $\mu_{\rm 2b}$ in Eq. 36 [src: Eq. 36].
  - `[der]` Each of (i)–(iv) reads as a slip in the paper's own notation.
