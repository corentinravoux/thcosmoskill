## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- **Area parameter, three normalisations.** Saikawa and Hiramatsu et al. write $\rho_{\rm wall}(t)=\mathcal{A}\frac{\sigma}{t},$ (arXiv:1703.02576) [src: Eq. 2.17], with ${\cal A}\equiv\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{wall}}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{wall}}}t.$ (arXiv:1207.3166) [src: Eq. 16]. Ferreira et al. define $\mathcal{A}\equiv\frac{A}{V}\frac{1}{2aH}\,,$ (arXiv:2401.14331) [src: Eq. 3] so that $\rho_{\text{dw}}=\frac{\sigma Aa^{2}}{Va^{3}}=2{\cal A}\,\sigma H\,,$ [src: Eq. 2], with $A$ the comoving area and $V$ the comoving volume. The VOS papers use instead a correlation length $L=\frac{\sigma_{w}}{\rho}$ (arXiv:1602.01322) [src: Sect. III, inline, text before Eq. 17], and Dankovsky et al. "$\xi$" (`approximations.md#convention-traps-and-printed-slips`).
  - `[der]` The first two definitions are the same; Ferreira et al.'s agrees with them in radiation domination. No paper read here states the conversion of $L/t$ into $\mathcal A$, which is not done here.
- **Time variables and signature.** Martins et al. write $ds^{2}=a^{2}(\tau)\left(d\tau^{2}-dl^{2}\right),$ (arXiv:1602.01322) [src: Eq. 7], mostly minus, evolve in conformal time and measure $\frac{\xi_{c}}{\tau}=(1-\lambda)\frac{L}{t}=(1-\lambda)\epsilon\,.$ for $a\propto t^\lambda$ [src: Eq. 21].
  - Ferreira et al. quote annihilation in conformal time $\eta$ (arXiv:2401.14331) [src: Sect. II.2].
  - `[der]` A ratio of times quoted in conformal time is not the same ratio in cosmic time.

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — from the wall action to the averaged (VOS) equations
<!-- key: step-1-from-the-wall-action-to-the-averaged-vos -->

Martins, Rybak, Avgoustidis & Shellard (arXiv:1602.01322, Martins et al. 2016) start from the worldvolume action $S=-\int\mathcal{L}d^{3}\sigma=-\sigma_{w}\int\sqrt{\gamma}d^{3}\sigma,$ [src: Eq. 2], "where $\sigma_{w}$ is a constant mass per unit area" and $\gamma_{ab}$ the induced metric [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 2]. In the FLRW metric of Eq. 7 the equation of motion splits into an energy equation, $\dot{\varepsilon}+3\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\varepsilon\dot{x}^{i}\dot{x}_{i}=0\,.$ [src: Eq. 10], and a normal-acceleration equation driven by the two curvatures of the surface (Eq. 11) [src: Eq. 11]. Averaging with the energy density $\frac{E}{V}=\rho=\frac{\sigma_{w}a^{2}}{V}\int\varepsilon d^{2}\sigma$ [src: Eq. 12] and the rms velocity $\upsilon^{2}=\frac{\int\dot{x}^{2}\varepsilon d^{2}\sigma}{\int\varepsilon d^{2}\sigma}\,,$ [src: Eq. 13] gives $\frac{d\rho}{dt}=-H\rho\left(1+3\upsilon^{2}\right)\,,$ and $\frac{d\upsilon}{dt}=\left(1-\upsilon^{2}\right)\left(\frac{K_{1}+K_{2}}{L}-3Hv\right)\,,$ [src: Eq. 14], under "the assumption that curvature radii have the same averaged value and are equal to the correlation length: $R_{1}=R_{2}=L$" [src: Sect. II, inline, text after Eq. 14]. The model was "first obtained from arguments on energy conservation in [9]" and later "from a microscopic description [16]" [src: Sect. II, first paragraph].

Avelino, Martins & Oliveira introduced "a new phenomenological one-scale model for the evolution of domain wall networks" tested "against high-resolution field theory numerical simulations" (Avelino et al. 2005, arXiv:hep-ph/0507272) [src: abstract].

Energy loss "because of possible intersections and the creation of sphere-like objects that eventually collapse" is added "by analogy to what was originally done by Kibble for cosmic strings", $\frac{d\rho_{loss}}{dt}=-c_{w}\upsilon\frac{\rho}{L}\,,$ (arXiv:1602.01322) [src: Eq. 16; Sect. III, first paragraph], with $c_w$ "the chopping parameter". In terms of $L$ the system becomes $\frac{dL}{dt}=(1+3\upsilon^{2})HL+c_{w}\upsilon\,,$ and $\frac{d\upsilon}{dt}=(1-\upsilon^{2})\left(\frac{k_{w}}{L}-3H\upsilon\right)\,,$ [src: Eq. 17], with $k_{w}=K_{1}+K_{2}$ the momentum parameter, "which we will initially consider as a constant" [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 17].

### Step 2 — the scaling solution and how the parameters are measured
<!-- key: step-2-the-scaling-solution-and-how-the -->

For $a\propto t^{\lambda}$, Eqs. 17 have "the asymptotic scaling solution $L=\epsilon t$ and $\upsilon=v_{0}$", and the two parameters follow from the measured $\epsilon$ and $v_0$: $k_{w}=3\lambda\epsilon v_{0}\,,$ (arXiv:1602.01322) [src: Eq. 18] and $c_{w}v_{0}=\epsilon\left[1-\lambda\left(1+3v_{0}^{2}\right)\right]\,.$ [src: Eq. 19]; "the right hand-sides of Eqs. (18-19) are general expressions for the momentum parameter and energy loss mechanisms that can be measured directly and independently from simulations" [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 19]. The simulations solve, "adopting the Press, Ryden and Spergel procedure [13]", $\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial\tau^{2}}+3\frac{\mathrm{d}\ln a}{\mathrm{d}\ln\tau}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\tau}-\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial x^{i}\partial x_{i}}=-\frac{\partial V}{\partial\phi}\,.$ [src: Eq. 20], in $4096^{3}$ boxes for "radiation era ( $\lambda=1/2$ ), matter era ( $\lambda=2/3$ ) and 10 other expansion rates", ten runs each, stopped "when the horizon becomes half the box size", and averaged between conformal times 500 and 1500, a region "conservatively defined" [src: Sect. III, inline, list after Eq. 20; paragraph after Eq. 21]. The scaling exponents $\frac{1}{\xi_{c}}\propto\tau^{\mu}$ [src: Eq. 22] and $\gamma v\propto\tau^{\nu}\,,$ [src: Eq. 23] should approach $\mu=-1$, $\nu=0$; for $\lambda=1/10$ "the network has not converged to the scaling behavior" [src: Sect. III, inline, text after Eq. 23]. The PRS procedure itself (Press, Ryden & Spergel 1989) is not on arXiv and was not read.

Martins et al. extend the model with a velocity-dependent momentum parameter, $k(v)=k_{0}\frac{1-\left(q\upsilon^{2}\right)^{\beta}}{1+\left(q\upsilon^{2}\right)^{\beta}},$ (arXiv:1602.01322) [src: Eq. 25], and a scalar-radiation loss, $F(v)=c_{w}v+d[k_{0}-k(v)]^{r}\,,$ [src: Eq. 27], since the modified momentum parameter alone "is not sufficient to account for the mismatch between the simulation data and the analytic prediction for the energy losses" [src: Sect. IV.2, first paragraph]; the system becomes $\frac{dL}{dt}=(1+3\upsilon^{2})HL+c_{w}\upsilon+d[k_{0}-k(v)]^{r},$ and $\frac{d\upsilon}{dt}=(1-\upsilon^{2})\left(\frac{k(v)}{L}-3H\upsilon\right),$ [src: Eq. 30]. The scalar-radiation term rests on the statement, from their Ref. [19], that "the uniformly moving wall does not radiate. Only perturbations on the wall surface produce scalar radiation", and on the anticipation, which "looks reasonable", "that the amount of radiation is proportional to the surface perturbations" [src: Sect. IV.2, second paragraph]. Calibrated on the simulations "By using bootstrapping techniques", "the chopping parameter is negligibly small in comparison with the contribution from scalar radiation and may be neglected as a first approximation", $c_{w}=0.00\pm 0.01$, with $d=0.28\pm 0.01$ [src: Eq. 31], $r=1.30\pm 0.02$ [src: Eq. 32], $\beta=1.69\pm 0.08$ [src: Eq. 33], $k_{0}=1.73\pm 0.01$ [src: Eq. 34] and $q=4.27\pm 0.10\,.$ [src: Eq. 35]; "the main energy loss mechanism for the wall network is generically scalar radiation, with the chopping term only becoming important for fast expansion rates" [src: Sect. IV.3, inline, first and last paragraphs].

### Step 3 — the area parameter on a lattice
<!-- key: step-3-the-area-parameter-on-a-lattice -->

Hiramatsu, Kawasaki & Saikawa compute $\mathcal{A}$ two ways (arXiv:1309.5001, Hiramatsu et al. 2013). From the comoving wall area $A$ in a box of comoving volume $V$, $\rho_{\rm wall}=\frac{\sigma_{\rm wall}A}{a(t)V}.$ [src: Eq. 12] and $\mathcal{A}=\frac{At}{a(t)V}.$ [src: Eq. 13], the area being counted on the links where $\phi$ changes sign, $A=\Delta A\sum_{\rm links}\delta_{\pm}\frac{|\nabla\phi|}{|\phi_{,x}|+|\phi_{,y}|+|\phi_{,z}|},$ [src: Eq. 14], with the weighting factor taking "account of the average number of links per area segment" [src: Sect. III.2, text after Eq. 14]; and independently from the field energy inside the wall cores (Eqs. 15–16) [src: Sect. III.2]. With these, $\mathcal{A}$ "becomes almost constant at late times" and "does not depend on the choice of $\lambda$", while its slight late increase "is caused by the finiteness of the simulation box" as presumed by the authors [src: Sect. IV.1, first paragraph]; including "a statistical uncertainty of $\mathcal{O}(0.1)$ due to the different realizations" seen in their runs with $N=512$, they estimate $\mathcal{A}\simeq 0.8\pm 0.1.$ "in the scaling regime" [src: Eq. 29; Sect. IV.1, inline, text before Eq. 29], the value quoted in the review (`results.md#domain-walls`).

Ferreira et al. find for an unbiased network a saturation "for this particular realization to a value $\mathcal{A}\simeq 0.9$", agreeing "roughly" with their Ref. [26] (Ferreira et al. 2024, arXiv:2401.14331) [src: Sect. III.2, inline, first paragraph], in their normalisation of Eq. 3.

For $Z_N$ (axion) string–wall networks, Hiramatsu, Kawasaki, Saikawa & Sekiguchi find that "the value of the area parameter ${\cal A}$ increases for large $N_{\mathrm{DW}}$", in agreement with "the intuitive argument that the number of domain walls increases proportionally to $N_{\mathrm{DW}}$ if the number of strings per simulation box is unchanged", and that their Fig. 3 "also implies that the area of domain walls in the real scalar field theory" ($Z_2$) "evolves similarly to the axionic model with $N_{\mathrm{DW}}=2$" (Hiramatsu et al. 2012, arXiv:1207.3166) [src: Sect. 3.1, inline, paragraphs after Eq. 16]. They note that their simulations do not reproduce the hierarchy between string and wall formation, $\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}/F_{a}\sim 10^{-11}$ in reality against ${\cal O}(0.1)$ in the runs, and "concentrate on the evolution at late times" [src: Sect. 3.1, inline, paragraph before Eq. 15].

The initial-condition dependence of $\mathcal{A}$ found by Dankovsky et al. (arXiv:2509.25367) and its universality over the equation of state (arXiv:2607.28214) are in `results.md#domain-walls`.

### Step 4 — bias, annihilation time and the decay of the network
<!-- key: step-4-bias-annihilation-time-and-the-decay-of -->

- **Analytic estimate.** The review's annihilation time and its $C_{\rm ann}$ are in `formalism.md#domain-walls`; "If the annihilation occurs in the radiation dominated era" [src: Sect. 2.3, text before Eq. 2.28], in temperature, $T_{\rm ann}=3.41\times 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{GeV}\,C_{\rm ann}^{-1/2}\mathcal{A}^{-1/2}\left(\frac{g_{*}(T_{\rm ann})}{10}\right)^{-1/4}\left(\frac{\sigma}{\mathrm{TeV}^{3}}\right)^{-1/2}\left(\frac{V_{\rm bias}}{\mathrm{MeV}^{4}}\right)^{1/2},$ (arXiv:1703.02576) [src: Eq. 2.28]. Ferreira et al. take as the onset of annihilation $H(\eta_{\Delta V})=\Delta V/\sigma\,,$ (arXiv:2401.14331) [src: Eq. 5], the annihilation phase occurring "roughly once the Hubble rate becomes smaller than the pressure acceleration" [src: Sect. II.2, first sentence].
- **Measured decay law.** Monitoring the false-vacuum volume fraction, which is $1/2$ in scaling for $Z_2$, Ferreira et al. find "that the network decays exponentially in the annihilation phase according to" $\mathcal{F}_{\text{fv}}=0.5\exp\left[-\left(\frac{\eta}{\eta_{\text{ann}}}\right)^{p}\,\right]\,,$ (arXiv:2401.14331) [src: Eq. 6; Sect. II.2, last paragraph], fitted only until $\mathcal{F}_{\text{fv}}$ falls below the inverse number of Hubble volumes in the box [src: Sect. III.1, first paragraph]. Averaging over realizations with "box sizes up to $N=3240$" [src: abstract, inline], $p=3.0\pm 0.3.$ [src: Eq. 7], and "a slight delay $\eta_{\text{ann}}\approx 1.5\eta_{\Delta V}$"; a footnote records disagreement "with [23] on the dependence of $\eta_{\text{ann}}$ with $\Delta V$" [src: Sect. III.1, inline, text after Eq. 7 and footnote 3]. "There is some literature on how $\mathcal{F}_{\text{fv}}$ decays during the annihilation phase ... with no current consensus" [src: Sect. II.2, inline, third paragraph].
- **End of gravitational-wave production.** From 3+1 lattice simulations Notari, Rompineve & Torrenti find that GW production "continues to be efficient until a value of the Hubble scale $H_{\text gw}$ that is roughly an order of magnitude smaller than the naive estimate $σH = ΔV$", "thereby leading to a $O(100)$ larger GW signal", robust "when changing the shape of the scalar field potential or including a time-dependent symmetry breaking term" (Notari et al. 2025, arXiv:2504.03636) [src: abstract, inline]. The spectrum itself belongs to `thcosmo-cosmological-gravitational-waves`.
- **Biased potentials against biased initial conditions.** Correia, Leite & Martins recall that their previous work (Correia et al. 2014, not read here) found "that the analytic decay law obtained by Hindmarsh was in good agreement with simulations of biased potentials, but not of biased initial conditions", and confirm that "the validity (or not) of the Gaussian approximation is the key difference between the two cases" (Correia et al. 2018, arXiv:1804.10761) [src: abstract]. The scaling of the annihilation temperature with a population bias found by Barbini et al. is in `results.md#domain-walls`.
- **Formation with a bias.** Ai argues that "the conventional condition for forming a domain-wall network" in the presence of a bias (`[der]` the percolation criterion of the review, Eq. 2.26, `formalism.md#domain-walls`) "cannot be generally valid, since it is insensitive to the dynamics of the phase transition", derives a "substantially stricter criterion" by evaluating the false-vacuum fraction at a freeze-out temperature, and adds the consistency condition $T_{\rm fo}>T_{\rm ann}$ for applying scaling-regime GW estimates (Ai 2026, arXiv:2606.30094) [src: abstract, inline].
- **Dynamical bias without an energy difference.** For scalar field theories "with degenerate vacua (i.e., vacua of equal energy density)", Vilhena, Avelino & dos Santos show, "Using analytical arguments and numerical simulations", that a vacuum-dependent scalar mass makes scalar radiation from walls anisotropic, and that "the resulting recoil (rocket) effect biases the evolution of cosmological domain wall networks in favor of the lower-mass vacuum, thereby promoting network decay"; for non-degenerate vacua this "can either hasten or delay network decay relative to the standard expectation based solely on differences in vacuum energy density" (Vilhena et al. 2026, arXiv:2607.09432) [src: abstract].

### Step 5 — the domain-wall problem and the energy at annihilation
<!-- key: step-5-the-domain-wall-problem-and-the-energy -->

The wall-domination time and the ZKO bound for stable walls are in `formalism.md#domain-walls`. For unstable walls Saikawa requires annihilation before domination, $t_{\rm ann}<t_{\rm dom}$, giving $V_{\rm bias}^{1/4}>2.18\times 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{GeV}\,C_{\rm ann}^{1/4}\mathcal{A}^{1/2}\left(\frac{\sigma}{\mathrm{TeV}^{3}}\right)^{1/2}.$ (arXiv:1703.02576) [src: Eq. 2.29] or, in temperature, $T_{\rm ann}>1.62\times 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{GeV}\,\mathcal{A}^{1/2}\left(\frac{g_{*}(T_{\rm ann})}{10}\right)^{-1/4}\left(\frac{\sigma}{\mathrm{TeV}^{3}}\right)^{1/2}.$ [src: Eq. 2.30]. A footnote qualifies this: "The domain wall domination does not directly imply a cosmological disaster", but "little is known about the detailed dynamics of domain walls in the domain wall dominated universe", so the review uses the domination condition "to indicate the potential uncertainties" [src: Sect. 2.3, footnote 4]. Ramazanov & Zeng study that case: during wall domination "the Universe undergoes accelerated expansion, while the comoving correlation length of the domain wall network remains constant", and the energy density of the resulting gravitational waves "is sufficient to produce a significant impact on the effective number of neutrino species, unless the duration of wall domination is suitably constrained" (Ramazanov & Zeng 2026, arXiv:2611.05504) [src: abstract].

To bound walls whose decay products are "the SM degrees of freedom", Saikawa estimates $\frac{\rho_{\rm wall}}{s}(t)=2.24\times 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{GeV}\,\mathcal{A}\left(\frac{g_{*}(T)}{10}\right)^{3/4}\left(\frac{g_{*s}(T)}{10}\right)^{-1}\left(\frac{\sigma}{\mathrm{TeV}^{3}}\right)\left(\frac{t}{1\,\mathrm{sec}}\right)^{1/2},$ (arXiv:1703.02576) [src: Eq. 2.31], and, from constraints on energy injection at BBN, "the lifetime should be shorter than $t_{\rm ann}\lesssim 0.01\mathrm{sec}$, if we assume that a significant fraction of the energy density of domain walls is converted into energetic particles" [src: Sect. 2.3, inline, text after Eq. 2.31], a constraint "derived under the assumption that the decay products strongly interact with SM particles", which therefore "depends on details of underlying particle physics models" [src: Sect. 2.3, text after Eq. 2.32]. Ferreira et al. write the wall fraction as $\alpha(t)\equiv\rho_{\text{dw}}/\rho_{c}$, which "increases over time", so that the GW spectrum is expected "to have a peak around the annihilation time of the network" (arXiv:2401.14331) [src: Sect. II.1, inline, text after Eq. 4]. How the energy splits into radiation, particles, axions and gravitational waves is not computed in the sections read; the GW share is in the GW skill and the axion share in `thcosmo-dark-matter-models`.

PBHs from the annihilation: Ferreira et al. propose that "rare false vacuum pockets of super-Hubble size collapse to PBHs if their energy density becomes comparable to the background when they cross the Hubble scale", while smaller pockets collapse "only if they are close to spherical", which "introduces very large uncertainties in the final PBH abundance" (arXiv:2401.14331) [src: abstract].

### Step 6 — analytic models against lattice simulations
<!-- key: step-6-analytic-models-against-lattice -->

- **VOS against field theory ($Z_2$).** The extended VOS with the best-fit parameters "provides an excellent agreement with the entire range of numerical simulations" (arXiv:1602.01322) [src: Sect. IV.3, text after Eq. 35]. For the frictionless scaling solutions in cosmologies with $a\propto t^{\lambda}$, $0<\lambda<1$, Avelino's parameter-free model agrees with field-theory simulations "within a few $\%$" for "root-mean-square velocities $σ_v$ smaller than $0.2 \, c$ ($λ\ge 0.9$)", "but significant discrepancies occur for larger values of $σ_v$", and Avelino identifies "problems with the determination of $L$ and $σ_v$ from numerical field theory simulations which might potentially be responsible for these discrepancies" (Avelino 2019, arXiv:1910.07011) [src: abstract, inline]. Comparing the two, Avelino finds that the parametric model "overestimates the damping of the wall motion due to the Hubble expansion and neglects the direct impact of wall decay on the evolution of the root-mean-square velocity", effects that "may be absorbed into a redefinition of the momentum parameter" (Avelino 2020, arXiv:2001.06318) [src: abstract].
- **Anisotropic networks.** The canonical VOS "accurately predicts the linear scaling regime after isotropization" (arXiv:1804.10761) [src: abstract].
- **$Z_N$.** No VOS calibration for $Z_N$ or string–wall networks was read; the lattice trend in $N_{\rm DW}$ is Step 3, and $C_{\rm ann}$ "depending on $N$" is in `formalism.md#domain-walls`.
- **Annihilation.** The lattice annihilation time of Ferreira et al. lags the scaling-based estimate (Step 4), and GW production continues until a Hubble rate "roughly an order of magnitude smaller than the naive estimate" (arXiv:2504.03636) [src: abstract].

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **Constant-parameter VOS (Eq. 17).** Two numbers per expansion rate; Table 1 of Martins et al. shows $k_w$ and $c_w$ varying with the expansion rate (Numbers).
  - `[der]` A single pair therefore does not describe several eras.
- **Extended VOS (Eq. 30).** Five calibrated parameters with negligible chopping, valid over the simulated $\lambda$ (Step 2); the ultra-fast expansion regime is left "for subsequent work" (arXiv:1602.01322) [src: Sect. IV.3, paragraph after Eq. 40].
- **Parameter-free VOS.** No calibration; agrees "within a few $\%$" only at low velocities, the discrepancies being possibly due to how $L$ and $\sigma_v$ are measured in simulations (arXiv:1910.07011) [src: abstract, inline].
- **Lattice, PRS.** Martins et al. use the Press–Ryden–Spergel procedure with a fixed initial wall thickness in grid units (Step 2); the PRS modification itself was not read.
- **Lattice with a $\lambda$-dependent onset.** Hiramatsu et al. observe that the onset of scaling depends on $\lambda$; this formation time scale "might be estimated from the condition that the width of walls" becomes "comparable (or shorter than) the Hubble radius", a condition that "gives $\tau_{\rm form}\propto\lambda^{-1/4}$" (arXiv:1309.5001) [src: Sect. IV.1, inline, paragraph after Fig. 1].
- **Area by link counting or by core energy.** Both used by Hiramatsu et al. (Step 3). Ferreira et al. note that the area-based density of their Eq. 2 "is generally smaller than the total energy density stored by the scalar field" (arXiv:2401.14331) [src: Sect. III.2, first paragraph].

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Area parameters in different normalisations.** The $\mathcal A\sigma/t$ form (arXiv:1703.02576) [src: Eq. 2.17], the $2\mathcal A\sigma H$ form (arXiv:2401.14331) [src: Eq. 2] and the correlation length $L$ (arXiv:1602.01322) [src: Sect. III] are three different parameters.
  - `[der]` Check the definition before comparing values across papers or eras.
- **Box and initial transients.** The late rise of $\mathcal A$ is presumed to be "caused by the finiteness of the simulation box" (arXiv:1309.5001) [src: Sect. IV.1, first paragraph]; Martins et al. note that their initial conditions "will lead to large energy gradients in the early timesteps of the simulation", and average only over $\tau=500-1500$ (arXiv:1602.01322) [src: Sect. III, inline, paragraph before Fig. 2 and paragraph after Eq. 21]; Ferreira et al. note in a footnote that the area parameter "has not achieved a constant value" before the bias acts, so "simulations with even smaller bias sizes would be desirable to exclude residual effects of initial conditions" (arXiv:2401.14331) [src: Sect. III.2, footnote 4].
- **Fitting the decay after Hubble-size pockets are gone.** The decay steepens once false-vacuum regions are sub-Hubble; Ferreira et al. fit only while Hubble-size false-vacuum regions remain in the box (Step 4).
- **Annihilation set by the naive estimate.** Lattice runs place the annihilation and the end of GW sourcing later (Step 4); the review itself notes that sudden termination of GW production at $t_{\rm ann}$ "is not rigorous since the collapse of domain walls is not instantaneous" (arXiv:1703.02576) [src: Sect. 3, footnote 6].
- **Slow expansion rates.** For the slowest expansion rate simulated, the network had not reached scaling in the largest runs of Martins et al. (Step 2).
- **Unphysical hierarchy in string–wall runs.** The simulated ratio of scales is ${\cal O}(0.1)$ instead of $10^{-11}$ (arXiv:1207.3166) [src: Sect. 3.1, paragraph before Eq. 15].
