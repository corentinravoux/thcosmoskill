## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- **Musco 2018 (arXiv:1809.02127).** Comoving (cosmic-time) slicing with zero shift, metric written as $ds^{2}=-A^{2}(r,t)dt^{2}+B^{2}(r,t)dr^{2}+R^{2}(r,t)d\Omega^{2}$ [src: Eq. 7], mostly plus. The curvature profile is written both as $K(r)$ in the comoving radial coordinate $r$ and as $\zeta(\hat{r})$, with $R=a(t)r(1+\epsilon^{2}\tilde{R})=a(t)e^{\zeta(\hat{r})}\hat{r}(1+\epsilon^{2}\tilde{R})$ [src: Eq. 35]. The Friedmann equation is printed as $H^{2}(t)=\frac{8\pi}{3}\rho_{b}(t)-\frac{K}{a^{2}(t)}$ [src: Eq. 13].
  - `[der]` Eq. 35 carries the same sign of $\zeta$ as the review (`formalism.md#conventions`).
  - `[der]` Eq. 13 reads as units in which Newton's constant and the speed of light are set to one, which the sections read do not state in words.
- **Musco et al. 2021 (arXiv:2011.03014).** "Throughout we use $c=G=1$" [src: Sect. I, "Prescription scheme", last sentence]; the prime "denotes spatial derivative with respect to the variable written explicitly in the argument of the function" [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 21].
- **Harada, Yoo & Kohri 2013 (arXiv:1309.4201)** keep $c$ explicit ($p=w\rho c^{2}$) and quote the threshold on the uniform-Hubble slice, $\delta^{\rm UH}_{Hc}$, and as $\tilde\delta_c$, the measure of the comoving-slice simulations [src: abstract, inline; Sect. V.1].
- **Yoo, Harada & Okawa 2020 (arXiv:2004.01042)** write the spatial metric at leading order as the flat metric "multiplied by ${\rm e}^{-2\zeta}a^{2}$" and focus "on a peak in $-\zeta$" [src: Sect. II, first and second paragraphs]. <!-- num: not a value -->
  - `[der]` This is the opposite sign of $\zeta$ to the review and to Musco 2018.
- **Shape-parameter names.** `[der]` The review's $q$ (arXiv:2211.05767, Eq. II.25a) is the same expression as the $\alpha$ of Musco 2018 once $\delta_m=\mathcal C(r_m)$ (its Eqs. 60 and 64). Musco 2018 writes $\alpha=-\frac{\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}(r_{m})r_{m}^{2}}{4\delta_{m}}\,.$ (arXiv:1809.02127) [src: Eq. 88], and Musco et al. 2021 write $\alpha=-\frac{\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}(r_{m})r_{m}^{2}}{4\mathcal{C}(r_{m})},$ (arXiv:2011.03014) [src: Eq. 16]. The $\alpha$ of Musco & Miller 2013 is a different profile parameter: there "Varying $\alpha$ in the range between 0 and 1 gives a centrally-peaked perturbation shape, while for $\alpha$ greater than 1 it is off-centred", and "the simple Mexican hat (with $\alpha=0$ )" is one case (arXiv:1201.2379) [src: Sect. 5, inline, first and fourth paragraphs]. Do not carry an $\alpha$ from one of these papers to the other.

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — the Misner–Sharp–Hernandez equations in comoving gauge
<!-- key: step-1-the-misner-sharp-hernandez-equations-in -->

Musco (arXiv:1809.02127, Musco 2018) sets up the problem in spherical symmetry in the comoving gauge, noting that the simulations of Shibata and Sasaki were performed "using the constant mean curvature gauge" and that "The relation between different gauges in the gradient expansion approximation has been analyzed extensively in [20, 32]" [src: Sect. II.3, first paragraph]. With the metric of Eq. 7 and the operators $D_{t}\equiv\frac{1}{A}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\ \ \ \ \textrm{and}\ \ \ \ D_{r}\equiv\frac{1}{B}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\ ,$ [src: Eq. 9], the two quantities $U\equiv D_{t}R=\displaystyle{\frac{1}{A}\frac{\partial R}{\partial t}}\ \ \ \ \textrm{and}\ \ \ \ \Gamma\equiv D_{r}R=\displaystyle{\frac{1}{B}\frac{\partial R}{\partial r}}\ ,$ [src: Eq. 10] are related to the Misner–Sharp–Hernandez mass by the constraint $\Gamma^{2}=1+U^{2}-\frac{2M}{R}\ ,$ [src: Eq. 11], with $M=\int_{0}^{R}4\pi R^{2}\rho\,dR$ [src: Eq. 12] for a perfect fluid.

The hydrodynamic equations, "obtained from the Einstein equations and the conservation of the stress energy tensor", are $\displaystyle D_{t}U=-\,\displaystyle{\frac{\Gamma}{\rho+p}D_{r}p-\frac{M}{R^{2}}-4\pi Rp}\,,$ [src: Eq. 14], $\displaystyle D_{t}\rho_{0}=-\,\displaystyle{\frac{\rho_{0}}{\Gamma R^{2}}D_{r}(R^{2}U)\,,}$ [src: Eq. 15], $\displaystyle D_{t}\rho=\displaystyle{\frac{\rho+p}{\rho_{0}}D_{t}\rho_{0}\,,}$ [src: Eq. 16], $\displaystyle D_{r}A=-\,\displaystyle{\frac{A}{\rho+p}D_{r}p\,,}$ [src: Eq. 17] and $\displaystyle D_{r}M=\displaystyle{4\pi R^{2}\Gamma\rho}\,,$ [src: Eq. 18], with $\rho_{0}$ "the rest mass density (or the compression factor for a fluid of particles without rest mass)"; the system is closed by $p=w\rho$ [src: Eq. 21] "with $w$ constant" (arXiv:1809.02127) [src: Sect. II.3, text after Eq. 18 and Eq. 21]. The derivation of Eqs. 14–18 is not given there: "see [33, 34, 35] for the details of the derivation" [src: Sect. II.3, text before Eq. 14].

### Step 2 — initial data from the quasi-homogeneous (gradient-expansion) solution
<!-- key: step-2-initial-data-from-the-quasi-homogeneous -->

Polnarev & Musco specify "an initial configuration in terms of a curvature profile" and, "Using an asymptotic quasi-homogeneous solution", relate it to "the density and velocity fields, which at an early enough time, when the length scale of the configuration is much larger than the cosmological horizon, can be treated as small perturbations of the background values"; and they "show that the analytical solutions for the density and velocity profiles as functions of the initial time are pure growing modes" (Polnarev & Musco 2007, arXiv:gr-qc/0605122) [src: abstract].

Musco 2018 reviews this solution. With $a(t)\propto t^{\frac{2}{3(1+w)}}\ \textrm{and}\ H(t)\propto\frac{1}{t}\ \Rightarrow\ \epsilon\propto t^{\frac{1+3w}{3(1+w)}}$ (arXiv:1809.02127) [src: Eq. 32], choosing a particular value of $\epsilon$ "with $k=const$ is equivalent to focusing on a particular value of time" [src: Sect. II.5, text after Eq. 32]. The metric and fluid variables are expanded to $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})$, for example $\rho=\rho_{b}(t)(1+\epsilon^{2}\tilde{\rho})$ [src: Eq. 36], $U=H(t)R(1+\epsilon^{2}\tilde{U})$ [src: Eq. 37] and $M=\frac{4\pi}{3}\rho_{b}(t)R^{3}(1+\epsilon^{2}\tilde{M})$ [src: Eq. 38]; the constraint (Eq. 11) at this order gives $K(r)=a^{2}H^{2}\epsilon^{2}\left(\tilde{M}-2\tilde{U}\right)\ \Rightarrow\ K(r)r_{k}^{2}=\tilde{M}-2\tilde{U}$ [src: Eq. 39], which separates space (the profile) from time ($\epsilon^{2}$); "in the long wavelength approximation perturbations have the same time evolution as that in the linear theory for a pure growing mode" [src: Sect. II.5, text after Eq. 39]. The remaining perturbations follow from $\tilde\rho$ and $\tilde U$, among them $\tilde{A}=-\frac{w}{1+w}\tilde{\rho}$ [src: Eq. 44] and $\tilde{M}=-3(1+w)\tilde{U}$ [src: Eq. 45]; $\tilde B$, zero for $w=0$, is described as "the seeds of pressure gradients which will grow during the non linear evolution, breaking the self similar behaviour of the quasi-homogeneous solution" [src: Sect. II.5, text after Eq. 47]. With $\epsilon(t)=\frac{1}{a(t)H(t)r_{k}}=\frac{1}{a(t)H(t)\hat{r}_{k}e^{\zeta(\hat{r}_{k})}}$ [src: Eq. 48], the density contrast in $K(r)$ is the first row of Eq. 49.

`[der]` That row is the same expression as Eq. II.16a of the review (`formalism.md#pbh-collapse-threshold-and-the-compaction`).

Profiles are compensated when $\int_{0}^{\infty}4\pi r^{2}\tilde{\rho}dr=0$, that is $\lim_{r\to\infty}K(r)r^{3}=0$ (arXiv:1809.02127) [src: Eq. 51]; non-compensated profiles must still satisfy $\Gamma>0$, $K(r)<\frac{1}{r^{2}}\quad\quad\textrm{and}\quad\quad\zeta^{\prime}(\hat{r})>-\frac{1}{\hat{r}}\ .$ [src: Eq. 52].

### Step 3 — the amplitude: mass excess, compaction function and the local-to-global relation
<!-- key: step-3-the-amplitude-mass-excess-compaction -->

Musco 2018 defines the averaged mass excess $\delta(r,t):=\frac{1}{V}\int_{0}^{R}4\pi R^{2}\frac{\rho-\rho_{b}}{\rho_{b}}\,dR$ (arXiv:1809.02127) [src: Eq. 55], which at $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})$ is $\delta(r,t)=\frac{3}{r^{3}}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\delta\rho}{\rho_{b}}r^{2}dr=\epsilon^{2}(t)f(w)K(r)r_{k}^{2}$ [src: Eq. 56]. The edge $r_0$ of the overdensity is rejected as a length scale: "if $r_{0}\to\infty$ we have $\delta\to 0$", so "in general $r_{0}$ is not a good measure of the perturbation lengthscale" [src: Sect. II.6, text after Eq. 58].

The compaction function is defined "according to the $R=2M$ condition for the formation of an apparent horizon" as "twice the mass excess over the areal radius", $\mathcal{C}:=\frac{2[M(r,t)-M_{b}(r,t)]}{R(r,t)}=\frac{r^{2}}{r_{k}^{2}}\tilde{M}+O(\epsilon^{2})$ (arXiv:1809.02127) [src: Eq. 59; Sect. II.6, text before Eq. 59]. At leading order $\mathcal{C}(r)=f(w)K(r)r^{2}=\frac{r^{2}}{r_{k}^{2}}\delta(r)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\mathcal{C}(r_{k})=\delta(r_{k})\,,$ [src: Eq. 60], and $r_k$ is identified with the peak $r_m$, where $K(r_{m})+\frac{r_{m}}{2}K^{\prime}(r_{m})=0$ (first row of Eq. 61) [src: Eq. 61]. A footnote of the same paper states that "This function was for the first time defined by S&S as $\mathcal{C}=(M-M_{b})/R$" [src: Sect. II.6, inline, footnote 2], S&S being Shibata & Sasaki (arXiv:gr-qc/9905064), whose paper was not read here.

Inserting Eq. 61 into the density contrast gives the shape-independent relation $\boxed{\delta(r_{m},t)=3\frac{\delta\rho(r_{m},t)}{\rho_{b}(t)}}$ (arXiv:1809.02127) [src: Eq. 62], which the author describes as "completely independent of the particular shape of the curvature profile" and, "to my knowledge", never "pointed out before" [src: Sect. II.6, text after Eq. 62]. The amplitude is then fixed at $\epsilon(t_{H})\equiv 1$, "which with an abuse of language I am going to call 'horizon crossing time'": $\delta_{m}\equiv\delta(r_{m},t_{H})=f(w)K(r_{m})r_{m}^{2}\,,$ [src: Eq. 64], against $\delta_{0}\equiv\delta(r_{0},t_{H_{0}})=f(w)K(r_{0})r_{0}^{2}\,,$ at the edge [src: Eq. 65]. The paper adds that "the non linear effects when $\epsilon\sim 1$ are not negligible (these will be analyzed in a future work)" [src: Sect. II.6, inline, paragraph beginning "The concept of horizon crossing"]; Step 7 gives the later analysis of Musco et al. 2021.

### Step 4 — evolution and horizon finding
<!-- key: step-4-evolution-and-horizon-finding -->

In Musco 2018 the simulations use "an explicit Lagrangian hydrodynamics code" whose grid "uses logarithmic spacing in a mass-type comoving coordinate"; the initial data are set at $a(t_{i})r_{m}=10\,R_{H}$ ($\epsilon=10^{-1}$), the outer edge at $90\,R_{H}$, "sufficient to ensure that there is no causal contact between it and the perturbed region during the time of the calculations" (arXiv:1809.02127) [src: Sect. IV.1, inline, first and second paragraphs]. The data are evolved with the Misner–Sharp–Hernandez equations "so as to generate a second set of initial data on a null slice", then with the Hernandez–Misner equations, in which "the formation of the apparent horizon is moved to $u\to\infty$, because of the increasing redshift of the null rays emitted by the collapsing shells"; an adaptive mesh refinement resolves black-hole formation "down to extremely small values of $(\delta-\delta_{c})$" [src: Sect. IV.1, inline, second and third paragraphs]. The Hernandez–Misner equations themselves are in that paper's Ref. [12], not read here.

The horizon is found from the trapped-surface condition: "In general the cosmological horizon is a marginally trapped surface within an expanding region, which in spherical symmetry is simply defined by the condition $R(r,t)=2M(r,t)$"; "if we have an expanding medium, this is a cosmological horizon, while if the medium is collapsing then it is a black hole apparent horizon [75, 76]" (Musco et al. 2021, arXiv:2011.03014) [src: Sect. V, inline, third paragraph]. The review's form of the same condition is in `formalism.md#pbh-mass-apparent-horizon-accretion-and`.

Near the threshold the mass follows critical scaling (`formalism.md#pbh-mass-apparent-horizon-accretion-and`); Musco et al. 2021 state that "Numerical simulations have shown that" $1\lesssim{\cal{K}}\lesssim 10$ for the coefficient and that Eq. 15 "is valid with $\gamma$ constant when $\delta_{m}-\delta_{c}\lesssim 10^{-2}$", with "$\gamma\simeq 0.36$ for a radiation dominated fluid" (arXiv:2011.03014) [src: Sect. II.3, text after Eq. 15].

### Step 5 — the threshold against the shape: simulations
<!-- key: step-5-the-threshold-against-the-shape -->

With the shape measured by $\alpha$ (Eq. 88; see Conventions), Musco 2018 finds, for centrally peaked profiles, that $\delta_{0,c}\to 0$ for $r_{0}/r_{m}\to\infty$ "while $\delta_{c}$ is bounded by a minimum value ( $\delta_{min}\simeq 0.41$ )", so that "Choosing $\delta_{m}$ instead of $\delta_{0}$ to measure the amplitude of the overdensity minimizes the variation of the threshold, and should therefore be preferred" (arXiv:1809.02127) [src: Sect. IV.3, inline, paragraph beginning "In the left frame of Figure 4"]. For off-centred profiles the matter redistributes, "converging towards a centrally peaked profile with almost the same amplitude that it would have had if it had been centrally peaked from the beginning", which "suggests that the location of the peak of the energy density is not important, and that what mainly matters is the shape of the compaction function around the peak" [src: Sect. IV.3, paragraph beginning "The right frame of Figure 4"]. The range found is $\boxed{\frac{\delta\rho_{c}}{\rho_{b}}\geq\frac{2}{3}\quad\quad\quad 0.41\lesssim\delta_{c}\leq\frac{2}{3}}$ [src: Eq. 97], "linearly extrapolated from the supra horizon regime ( $\epsilon=1$ )" [src: Sect. IV.3, text before Eq. 97]. The upper limit "corresponds to the limit of validity of the comoving metric $(K(r_{m})r_{m}^{2}=1)$"; the lower limit "is, instead, the analytic solution obtained for $\delta_{c}$ in [44] using a relativistic Jeans argument that takes into account the gravitational role of the pressure, but neglects pressure gradients" [src: Sect. IV.3, inline, paragraph beginning "The upper limit"]; the caption of Fig. 4 names this lower bound "HYK from the names of the authors of [44]" [src: Sect. IV.2, Fig. 4 caption] (Step 6). "The code is not able to evolve with good resolution shapes with $\alpha<0.1$" [src: Sect. IV.3, inline, paragraph beginning "When the perturbation is collapsing"].

The paper's "Proposition" is that the threshold "is related to the role of the pressure gradients which depends on the shape around the peak of the compaction function", a negligible role giving the minimum (broad shape) and "an infinite local value of the pressure gradients" the maximum (peaked shape) (arXiv:1809.02127) [src: Sect. IV.3, "Proposition"]. As a basis it proposes $\frac{\delta\rho}{\rho_{b}}=\frac{\delta\rho_{0}}{\rho_{b}}\left[1-\frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{r}{r_{m}}\right)^{2\alpha}\right]\exp\left[-\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\frac{r}{r_{m}}\right)^{2\alpha}\right]$ [src: Eq. 98], with $\frac{r_{0}}{r_{m}}=\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{1/2\alpha}\,.$ for $\lambda=0$ [src: Eq. 99]. For the profiles of its Eq. 93, only "a few percent change is due to the shape in the region outside $r_{m}$, being completely negligible for $r\gtrsim 2r_{m}$" [src: Sect. IV.3, inline, paragraph beginning "The dotted line of Figure 4"].

### Step 6 — the analytic lower bound: the three-zone model of Harada, Yoo & Kohri
<!-- key: step-6-the-analytic-lower-bound-the-three-zone -->

Harada, Yoo & Kohri (arXiv:1309.4201) treat the overdense region of their three-zone model (Sect. III.1, not read beyond its metric, Eq. 6) as a closed Friedmann region, and adopt "the formation criterion that the sound crossing time over the radius be longer than the free fall time from the maximum expansion to complete collapse" [src: Sect. IV.2.2, paragraph before Eq. 54]. After mapping the perfect-fluid Friedmann equation to dust form with $\displaystyle\tilde{a}=a^{1+3w},\quad d\tilde{t}=(1+3w)\tilde{a}^{3w/(1+3w)}dt,$ [src: Eq. 45], a sound wave obeys $\frac{d\chi}{d\eta}=\pm\frac{\sqrt{w}}{1+3w}$ [src: Eq. 52]. The criterion adopted is "that if and only if the sound wave crosses from the center to the surface outwardly or from the surface to the center inwardly before the maximum expansion, the pressure gradient force prevents the overdense region from becoming a black hole", which "reduces to" $\chi_{a}>\frac{\pi\sqrt{w}}{1+3w}.$ [src: Eq. 54; Sect. IV.2.2, text before Eq. 54], hence $\delta_{Hc}^{\rm UH}=\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi\sqrt{w}}{1+3w}\right)$ [src: Eq. 56], with $\delta_{Hc}^{\rm UH}<\delta_{H}^{\rm UH}\leq 1.$ [src: Eq. 57] "considered as a (roughly) necessary and sufficient condition" [src: Sect. IV.2.2, text after Eq. 57]. Against Carr's value $w$, the formula is approximately "$\pi^{2}$ times the conventionally used Carr's threshold value $w$" if $w\ll 1$, "and almost twice for a radiation fluid $w=1/3$" [src: Sect. IV.2.2, inline, text after Eq. 57]. The paper also notes "some ambiguity in the choice of the Jeans radius in Eq. (39) by a numerical factor of order unity", and develops the argument "to determine the numerical factor of the threshold value" [src: Sect. IV.2.2, first paragraph].

The conversion to the comoving-slice measure of the simulations uses the first-order quasi-homogeneous solution of Polnarev & Musco; for the three-zone model $K(r_{0})r_{0}^{2}=\sin^{2}\chi_{a}=\delta_{H}^{\rm UH}.$ (arXiv:1309.4201) [src: Eq. 61] and $\tilde{\delta}=\frac{3(1+w)}{5+3w}\delta_{H}^{\rm UH}.$ [src: Eq. 63], and "although $\tilde{\delta}$ is defined in terms of the first-order solution of the asymptotic quasihomogeneous solution, the relation (63) between $\tilde{\delta}_{c}$ and $\delta_{H}^{\rm UH}$ is exact" [src: Sect. V.1, last paragraph]. Musco et al. 2021 say of the general-relativistic Jeans-length value "$\delta_{c}\simeq 0.4$ for a radiation dominated Universe" of their Ref. [43] that "This analytical computation gives just a lower bound for the value of the threshold because it is not able to account for the non linear effects of pressure gradients" (arXiv:2011.03014) [src: Sect. I, inline, second paragraph].

### Step 7 — from the power spectrum to a threshold: the averaged compaction function
<!-- key: step-7-from-the-power-spectrum-to-a-threshold -->

Musco et al. 2021 build a prescription for radiation domination "without the need for running numerical simulations" (arXiv:2011.03014) [src: Sect. I, "Prescription scheme"]. As shown in their Ref. [45], the volume average $\bar{\mathcal{C}}(r_{m})=\frac{3}{r_{m}^{3}}\int_{0}^{r_{m}}\mathcal{C}(r)\,r^{2}{\rm d}r\,,$ [src: Eq. 18] "has a nearly constant value when computed at the threshold for PBH formation, which is $\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{c}\simeq 2/5$", which allows "an analytic expression to compute the threshold $\delta_{c}$ as a function of the shape parameter $\alpha$, up to a few percent precision [45]" [src: Sect. III, inline, text after Eq. 18]: $\delta_{c}\simeq\frac{4}{15}e^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}}}\frac{\alpha^{1-\nicefrac{{5}}{{2\alpha}}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{5}{2\alpha}\right)-\Gamma\left(\frac{5}{2\alpha},\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)},$ [src: Eq. 19].

`[der]` This is the expression the review prints as Eq. II.34 with $q$ in place of $\alpha$ (`formalism.md#analytical-threshold-formulae`).

The steps for a given spectrum are: smooth with the linear radiation transfer function $T(k,\eta)=3\frac{\sin(k\eta/\sqrt{3})-(k\eta/\sqrt{3})\cos(k\eta/\sqrt{3})}{(k\eta/\sqrt{3})^{3}}\,,$ (arXiv:2011.03014) [src: Eq. 32], a smoothing that "should be done when ${\hat{r}}_{m}\simeq 10\,r_{H}$ or larger"; their Ref. [68] is credited with "showing that using just the transfer function on superhorizon scales avoids the need of introducing a window function on the scale ${\hat{r}}_{m}$" [src: Sect. IV, text after Eq. 32]; find $\hat r_m$ from $\int dkk^{2}\left[(k^{2}{\hat{r}}_{m}^{2}-1)\frac{\sin(k{\hat{r}}_{m})}{k{\hat{r}}_{m}}+\cos{(k{\hat{r}}_{m})}\right]P_{\zeta}(k,\eta)=0\,,$ [src: Eq. 34], an equation that "in general, has to be solved numerically"; compute the Gaussian shape parameter $\alpha_{\text{\tiny G}}=-\frac{1}{4}\left[1+{\hat{r}}_{m}\frac{\int dkk^{4}\cos{(k{\hat{r}}_{m})}P_{\zeta}(k,\eta)}{\int dkk^{3}\sin{(k{\hat{r}}_{m})}P_{\zeta}(k,\eta)}\right],$ [src: Eq. 37]; for type I solutions, correct it for the non-linear amplitude by solving $F(\alpha)\left[1+F(\alpha)\right]\alpha=2\alpha_{\text{\tiny G}}\,,$ [src: Eq. 29] with $F(\alpha)$ of Eq. 30 [src: Eq. 30]; insert $\alpha$ into Eq. 19. The nonlinear correction vanishes at both ends, "$F(0)\to 1$ $(F(\infty)=1)$" [src: Sect. III, inline, last paragraph of "The non linear component of the shape"].

The amplitude so obtained is still the linear extrapolation to $aHr_m=1$. Measured at the non-linear horizon crossing in the simulations, "$1.3\lesssim\epsilon(t_{H})\lesssim 1.5$"; for $\alpha=1$ the central peak is $\delta\rho_{0}/\rho_{b}\simeq 1.35$ at the linear crossing, $\simeq 1.98$ from the quasi-homogeneous expression evaluated at $\epsilon(t_{H})\simeq 1.46$, and $\simeq 3.34$ "obtained numerically at the non linear horizon crossing", the last step "explained by the higher orders in the gradient expansion"; "This effect is genuinely non linear" (arXiv:2011.03014) [src: Sect. V, inline, fifth paragraph]; the horizon mass changes by "a correction value about equal to 2 (i.e. $1.7\div 2.2$ )" [src: Sect. V, inline, sixth paragraph]. The abstract summarises the effect on the threshold as an increase "by about a factor two with respect to the one computed on superhorizon scales" [src: abstract]. For a very sharp profile, $\alpha=0.15$, the peak amplitude is instead "significantly reduced at the non-linear horizon crossing", because the profile "is not smooth in the center, and there is a significant effect of the local pressure gradients" [src: Sect. V, paragraph beginning "For the very sharp profile"].

### Step 8 — dependence on the equation of state
<!-- key: step-8-dependence-on-the-equation-of-state -->

- **Analytic.** Eq. 56 of Harada, Yoo & Kohri "increases from 0, reaches a maximum value $\sin^{2}(\sqrt{3}\pi/6)\simeq 0.6203$ at $w=1/3$ and decreases to $1/2$, as $w$ increases from 0 to 1" (arXiv:1309.4201) [src: Sect. IV.2.2, inline, text after Eq. 57]. The averaged-compaction formulae in $w$, with their range of validity, are in `formalism.md#analytical-threshold-formulae`.
- **Simulations.** Musco & Miller studied "a range of values for $w$ between 0.01 and 0.6", with "good scaling laws" in all cases with $0.01\leq w\leq 0.6$ and $0\leq\alpha\leq 5$ (in their own $\alpha$, see Conventions); their $\gamma$ agrees with the semi-analytic values of Maison (their Ref. [27]) and its roughly linear trend is "consistent with the limit of $\gamma\rightarrow 0.106$ for $w\rightarrow 0$ obtained by Snajdr [28]"; "The relation between $\delta_{c}$ and $w$ confirms that any epochs in the early universe when the equation of state softens should be favourable for enhancing PBH production" (arXiv:1201.2379) [src: Sect. 5, inline, third and fourth paragraphs]. They caution that "Using $p=we$ for a perfect fluid with positive values of $w$ different from $1/3$ has doubtful physical motivation" [src: Sect. 5, second paragraph]. Values above $w=0.6$ were not used because the void "eventually becomes very challenging for the adaptive scheme" [src: Sect. 5, third paragraph].
- **Beyond $p=w\rho$.** For the lattice-gas equation of state $p=-T\ln(1-\rho)$, "as an analytically tractable proxy" for pressure stiffening, and "Using general-relativistic simulations of spherically symmetric collapse", Bakhti finds that "this nonlinear pressure feedback increases the PBH formation threshold by $0.50\pm0.02\%$ relative to the radiation equation of state within the causal regime of the model", with "$\gamma=0.357\pm0.001$, consistent with the radiation-fluid value to within our numerical precision", which "rather than indicating a universal critical exponent" reflects that this equation of state "approaches the radiation fluid at low density" (Bakhti 2026, arXiv:2606.29590) [src: abstract, inline].

### Step 9 — non-spherical corrections
<!-- key: step-9-non-spherical-corrections -->

- **Spheroidal initial data, 3+1.** Assuming $\zeta$ "is a random Gaussian variable", Yoo, Harada & Okawa draw the ellipticity of a peak of $-\zeta$ from peak theory, with shape variables $\chi_{1}:=\xi_{2}/\xi_{1},~\chi_{2}:=\xi_{3}/\xi_{1}.$ (arXiv:2004.01042) [src: Eq. 9], and evolve them with "the 4th-order Runge-Kutta method with the BSSN ... formalism" and a MUSCL fluid scheme, with "the periodic boundary condition" and "scale-up coordinates" [src: Sect. III, first paragraph]. They find that "$\chi_{2}\sim 0.1$ gives only $\sim 1\%$ difference in the threshold value of the amplitude of the curvature perturbation $\mu$, and we typically have $|\chi_{2}|\ll 1$ for PBH formation in the radiation dominated universe", so that "the effect of ellipticity on the threshold of PBH formation is highly limited and usually negligible in standard situations"; "If the equation of state of the matter field is softer than the radiation fluid, the result would drastically change" [src: Sect. V, inline, first and second paragraphs]. The resolution "is not fine enough to resolve the horizon in our simulation" [src: Sect. V, second paragraph].
- **Ellipsoidal data with prolateness.** For curvature fluctuations "obeying random Gaussian statistics with a monochromatic power spectrum" and a fluid with constant $w$, Escrivà & Yoo find that the critical values $(e_c, p_c)$ at fixed $\mu$ "closely follow a superellipse curve", and that, "for both cases of $w = 1/3$ and $w = 1/10$, small deviations from sphericity can avoid collapsing to a black hole when the amplitude is near its critical threshold" (Escrivà & Yoo 2024, arXiv:2410.03452) [src: abstract, inline].
- **Phenomenological ellipsoidal threshold.** The review's Eq. II.29, with its free parameters, is in `formalism.md#pbh-collapse-threshold-and-the-compaction`. Kühnel & Sandstad, reinvestigating ellipsoidal collapse "For a generic model", state that "the abundance and energy density of the produced primordial black holes will be significantly decreased when the non-sphericity of the overdensities is taken into account" (Kühnel & Sandstad 2016, arXiv:1602.04815) [src: abstract]; whether this paper is the review's Ref. [94] was not checked.
- **Beyond the peak.** For "an asymptotically flat and radiation-dominated" FRW universe, Germani & Montellà state that the threshold "is not solely (mainly) determined by the behaviour of the compaction function at its extrema, as earlier thought, but also by the Ricci scalar of the spatial geometry at smaller (but super-horizon) scales, which we call 'the core'"; in the closed-core case "the core helps the collapse so that the black hole formation threshold is the lowest among all cases" (Germani & Montellà 2025, arXiv:2510.02006) [src: abstract]. Neighbouring peaks and 3+1 Gaussian-profile results are in `results.md#collapse-thresholds-and-the-pbh-mass`.

Musco 2018 states the ranges of its curvature variables $\mathcal K_c$ and $\Phi_c$, as defined there, as $\boxed{0.62\lesssim\mathcal{K}_{c}\leq 1\quad\quad\quad 0.38\lesssim\Phi_{c}\leq 1}$ (arXiv:1809.02127) [src: Eq. 102] and adds that $\Phi_{c}$ "should replace the 'misleading' concept of $\zeta_{c}$ that has been used in the literature for the curvature threshold of PBHs" [src: Sect. V, text after Eq. 102]. For type I perturbations Musco et al. 2021 write $\Phi_{m}=1-\sqrt{1-\frac{3}{2}\delta_{c}},$ (arXiv:2011.03014) [src: Eq. 28]; the definition of their $\Phi$ (their Eq. 12) was not read, nor its identity with the $\Phi$ of Musco 2018 checked.

Two papers choose differently. Young concludes that, "with the tools currently available, the smoothed density contrast should be used rather than the peak value, and should be calculated from the time-independent component of the density contrast in the super-horizon limit (long before perturbations enter the horizon) rather than at horizon crossing" (Young 2019, arXiv:1905.01230) [src: abstract]; Musco et al. 2021 instead include "the non linear effects arising at horizon crossing" (arXiv:2011.03014) [src: abstract].

`[der]` The sections read do not compare the two choices.

`[der]` A threshold should therefore be quoted with its variable, the slice or crossing time at which it is evaluated, the profile family and $w$; for the abundance, the variable must be the one whose statistics are computed (`deep/abundance-statistics.md`).

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **Comoving gauge (Misner–Sharp–Hernandez) against constant mean curvature (Shibata–Sasaki).** Musco 2018 refers the relation in the gradient expansion to its Refs. [20, 32] (arXiv:1809.02127) [src: Sect. II.3, first paragraph]. Harada, Yoo, Nakama & Koga construct long-wavelength solutions "without symmetry in general gauge conditions", derive "the correspondence relation between long-wavelength solutions in the constant mean curvature slicing with conformally flat spatial coordinates and asymptotic quasihomogeneous solutions in the comoving gauge" and "compare the numerical results of PBH formation in these two different approaches" (Harada et al. 2015, arXiv:1503.03934) [src: abstract]. For $p=(\Gamma-1)\rho$ they "suggest" the analytic bounds $\tilde\delta_{c, {\rm min}}\simeq [3\Gamma/(3\Gamma+2)]\sin^{2}\left[\pi\sqrt{\Gamma-1}/(3\Gamma-2)\right]$ and $\tilde\delta_{c, {\rm max}}\simeq 3\Gamma/(3\Gamma+2)$, find that "within compensated models, the sharper the transition from the overdense region to the FRW universe is, the larger the $\tilde\delta_{c}$ becomes", and deduce, "Using simplified models", that the curvature-variable threshold $\psi_{0,c}$ "can be significantly larger (smaller) if the underlying density perturbation of much longer wavelength is positive (negative)" (arXiv:1503.03934) [src: abstract, inline].
- **Lagrangian 1+1 code with null slicing and AMR (Musco 2018) against 3+1 BSSN codes (Yoo et al. 2020; GRChombo in Ning et al. 2026).** The 1+1 code reaches "extremely small values of $(\delta-\delta_{c})$" (arXiv:1809.02127) [src: Sect. IV.1, third paragraph]; the 3+1 code of Yoo et al. treats ellipticity but does not resolve the horizon (arXiv:2004.01042) [src: Sect. V, second paragraph]; a 3+1 code for a spherical Gaussian profile is "consistent with previous spherically symmetric results" (arXiv:2608.13206) [src: abstract].
- **Analytic formulae against simulations.** In Musco 2018 the HYK value is the minimum, approached for a compaction function "converging to a constant behaviour, implying negligible pressure gradients around $r_{m}$" (arXiv:1809.02127) [src: Sect. IV.3, inline, paragraph beginning "The right side of Figure 5"]; Eq. 19 of Musco et al. 2021 holds "up to a few percent precision" as quoted from their Ref. [45] (arXiv:2011.03014) [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 18]; the review's accuracy figures for the $w$-dependent fits are in `results.md#collapse-thresholds-and-the-pbh-mass`.
- **Window function or transfer function.** Musco et al. 2021 use the transfer function on superhorizon scales instead of a window function, noting that a window function "introduces corrections in the calculation of the threshold that, however, are reduced when computing the PBH abundance if the same window function is adopted for evaluating the variance [72]" (arXiv:2011.03014) [src: Sect. IV, text after Eq. 32]; see `deep/abundance-statistics.md`.
- **Stochastic compaction functions.** In stochastic inflation the compaction function can be computed "on stochastic binary trees", which "also determines whether or not the areal radius of a perturbation increases monotonically", separating type I and type II; in "a single-field toy model with a constant potential slope", in the "quantum and near-critical" regimes, "type-II fluctuations outnumber type I" (Animali et al. 2026, arXiv:2606.30087) [src: abstract]. The $\zeta$ tail itself is in `thcosmo-inflation`.

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Quoting $\delta_0$ or $\zeta_c$.** $\delta_{0,c}\to 0$ for $r_0/r_m\to\infty$ (arXiv:1809.02127) [src: Sect. IV.3, paragraph beginning "In the left frame of Figure 4"]; the concept of $\zeta_c$ is called "misleading" there [src: Sect. V, text after Eq. 102]. `[der]` Check that the variable is one of the table of Step 10.
- **Comparing linear and non-linear horizon-crossing amplitudes.** For $\alpha=1$ the central peak of the critical profile is $\simeq 1.35$ at the linear crossing and $\simeq 3.34$ at the non-linear one (arXiv:2011.03014) [src: Sect. V, inline, fifth paragraph].
  - `[der]` A threshold taken in one convention and an amplitude in the other do not compare; check that both are at the linear crossing, as in Eq. 64 of Musco 2018, or both at the non-linear one.
- **Resolution limits.** Shapes with $\alpha<0.1$ are not evolved "with good resolution" in Musco 2018 (arXiv:1809.02127) [src: Sect. IV.3, paragraph beginning "When the perturbation is collapsing"]; the 3+1 code of Yoo et al. 2020 does not resolve the horizon (arXiv:2004.01042) [src: Sect. V, second paragraph]. `[der]` Check that the quoted threshold lies inside the shape range the code resolves.
- **Gradient expansion pushed to $\epsilon\sim 1$.** Musco et al. 2021 write that imposing $aHr_m=1$ extends "the validity of the gradient expansion approximation up to $\epsilon=1$, which is not very accurate" (arXiv:2011.03014) [src: Sect. V, inline, second paragraph]; the initial slice must be superhorizon, "${\hat{r}}_{m}\simeq 10\,r_{H}$ or larger" [src: Sect. IV, text after Eq. 32].
- **Sign of $\zeta$.** Yoo et al. 2020 use $e^{-2\zeta}$ and peaks of $-\zeta$ (arXiv:2004.01042) [src: Sect. II, first and second paragraphs]. `[der]` An amplitude of $\zeta$ carried into the review's convention changes sign.
- **$w\neq 1/3$ as a toy.** "doubtful physical motivation" for a perfect fluid with positive $w\neq 1/3$ (arXiv:1201.2379) [src: Sect. 5, second paragraph]; for an equation of state softer than radiation the non-spherical result "would drastically change" according to Yoo et al. (arXiv:2004.01042) [src: Sect. V, second paragraph].
- **Printed slips, copied, not corrected; none published that was looked for.** (i) In arXiv:1809.02127 v3, the Conclusions justify the local-to-global property "as shown by (88)", where Sect. II.6 derives it as Eq. 62 and Sect. IV.3 cites it as "(62)"; Eq. 88 defines $\alpha$ [src: Sect. V, paragraph after Eq. 102; Eqs. 62, 88]. (ii) In arXiv:2011.03014 v2, Eq. 21 prints the factor $\left[1+{\hat{r}}_{m}\zeta({\hat{r}}_{m})\right]^{2}$ without a prime on $\zeta$ [src: Eq. 21].
  (iii) In arXiv:1809.02127 v3, the second ($\zeta$) row of Eq. 49 carries the coefficient $3(1+w)/(5+3w)$, while the second row of Eq. 40 carries $2(1+w)/(5+3w)$; the text obtains Eq. 49 from Eq. 48 "inserted into (36) and (37)", with $\tilde\rho$ of Eq. 40 [src: Eqs. 40, 49; Sect. II.5, inline, text after Eq. 48].
  - `[der]` In slip (iii) the two rows cannot both hold; the gradient-expansion relation of Gow et al. 2020 (arXiv:2008.03289, Eq. 38) matches the factor of Eq. 40 when expanded, so Eq. 49's second row reads as the slip. Quote Eq. 40 or the first ($K$) row of Eq. 49.
  - `[der]` In slip (ii), the coordinate relation of Musco 2018 (unnumbered display after Eq. 47 of arXiv:1809.02127) carries $\hat r\zeta'(\hat r)$, so this reads as a slip.
