# Formalism of primordial black holes and topological defects

Scope: definitions and governing equations of primordial-black-hole (PBH) formation from the collapse of large curvature perturbations (collapse threshold, compaction function, critical scaling of the mass), the statistics that turn a power spectrum into an abundance, the abundance and mass function, Hawking evaporation, other formation channels; and the formation, dynamics and network evolution of cosmic strings, domain walls and other topological defects. Gravitational-wave signals of PBHs and defects are in `thcosmo-cosmological-gravitational-waves`; PBHs as dark matter in `thcosmo-dark-matter-models`; the inflationary tail of the curvature distribution in `thcosmo-inflation`.

Pointers on conventions that matter in this domain:

- The threshold is quoted in several variables ($\delta_{\mathrm{c}}$ of the compaction function, the amplitude $\mu_{\mathrm{c}}$ of $\zeta$, or the density contrast at horizon crossing in a given slicing); Harada, Yoo & Kohri give both "$\delta^{\rm UH}_{H c}$" on the uniform Hubble slice and "$\tilde\delta_{c}$", "the amplitude measure used in numerical simulations" (arXiv:1309.4201) [src: abstract]. A threshold is meaningful only with its variable, its profile and its $w$. <!-- num: not a value -->
- The sign of $\zeta$ in the metric $\mathrm{e}^{2\zeta}$ is the one of Escrivà, Kühnel & Tada (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Eq. II.7]; papers on non-Gaussian tails may use $\mathcal{R}$ or $-\zeta$: check before combining.

## PBH collapse threshold and the compaction function
<!-- key: pbh-collapse-threshold-and-the-compaction -->

All equations of this section are from the review of Escrivà, Kühnel & Tada 2022 (arXiv:2211.05767), Sects. II.2–II.4, for a perfect fluid $p=w\rho$ with constant $w$ under spherical symmetry, in comoving gauge and $G_{\mathrm{N}}=c=1$. <!-- eq: notation -->

- Superhorizon metric: on scales $R_{\mathrm{m}}\gg R_{H}$ the metric "can be approximated by" $\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-\,\mathrm{d}t^{2}+a^{2}(t)\mathrm{e}^{2\mspace{1.5mu}\zeta(\tilde{r})}\mspace{-2.5mu}\left(\mathrm{d}\tilde{r}^{2}+\tilde{r}^{2}\mathrm{d}\Omega^{2}\right)$ (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Eq. II.7], shown in their Ref. [56] [src: Sect. II.3, inline, text before Eq. II.7]; $\zeta$ and the curvature profile $K(r)$ are "frozen (i.e. constant)" on those scales "considering adiabatic fluctuations" and are related by $K(r)r^{2}=-\,\tilde{r}\zeta^{\prime}(\tilde{r})\big[2+\tilde{r}\zeta^{\prime}(\tilde{r})\big]$ [src: Eq. II.9].
- Gradient expansion: the expansion parameter is the ratio of the Hubble radius to the length scale of the fluctuation, $\epsilon\ll 1$ on superhorizon scales [src: Eq. II.11; Sect. II.3, inline, text after Eq. II.11]. At leading order, $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})$, the density contrast is $\frac{\delta\rho}{\rho_{\mathrm{b}}}(r,\mspace{1.5mu}t)=f(w)\mspace{-1.5mu}\left(\frac{1}{a(t)\mspace{1mu}H(t)}\right)^{\mspace{-6mu}2}\left[K(r)+\frac{r}{3}K^{\prime}(r)\right]$ (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Eq. II.16a], with $f(w)\coloneqq\frac{3(1+w)}{(5+3\mspace{1.5mu}w)}$ [src: Eq. II.17].
- Threshold criterion: "a good criterion for PBH formation is to define the associated threshold as the peak value of the so-called compaction function", following the definition of their Ref. [56], "confirmed in References [53, 68]"; the compaction function is "defined as the average mass excess in a given volume on superhorizon scales" (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Sect. II.3, inline, text after Eq. II.17]. The defining display (Eq. II.18) is not printed in the HTML rendering read; at leading order of the gradient expansion, $\mathcal{C}(r)\simeq f(w)K(r)r^{2}=f(w)\mspace{-1.5mu}\left(1-\big[1+\tilde{r}\zeta^{\prime}(\tilde{r})\big]^{2}\right)$ [src: Eq. II.19], "a time-independent quantity for superhorizon fluctuations as long as $w$ is constant" [src: Sect. II.3, inline, text after Eq. II.19]. The peak $r_{\mathrm{m}}$ is the innermost root of the first derivative of $\mathcal{C}$ [src: Eqs. II.20a–II.20b]; $\mathcal{C}(r_{\mathrm{m}})=\delta_{\mathrm{m}}$, and perturbations "with $\delta_{\mathrm{m}}>\delta_{\mathrm{c}}$" "will not dissipate after entering the cosmological horizon and continue to grow until the formation of a trapped surface" [src: Sect. II.5, inline, first sentence]. Secondary peaks of $\mathcal{C}$ "will not substantially contribute ... as long as those peaks are at least slightly smaller than the main one" [src: Sect. II.3, inline, footnote 4]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Type I and type II fluctuations: Eq. II.20b "yields two types of fluctuations"; type I fulfil $1+\tilde{r}\zeta^{\prime}>0$ for any $\tilde{r}$ (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Sect. II.3, text after Eq. II.20b]. <!-- num: not a value --> <!-- eq: notation -->
- Shape parameter: $q =-\frac{\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}(r_{\mathrm{m}})r^{2}_{\mathrm{m}}}{4\mathcal{C}(r_{\mathrm{m}})}$ (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Eq. II.25a], expressed so in their Ref. [78]; for $q\gg 1$ the peak is sharp and for $q\ll 1$ broad, the limit $q\to\infty$ giving the maximal threshold $\delta_{\mathrm{c,max}}$ and $q\to 0$ the minimal one [src: Sect. II.4, inline, text after Eq. II.25b]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Profile families commonly used: polynomial and exponential curvature profiles $K_{\rm pol}$ and $K_{\rm exp}$, normalised so that the leading-order compaction function peaks at $\delta_{\mathrm{m}}$ (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Eqs. II.24a–II.24b; Sect. II.4, text after Eq. II.24b].
- Amplitude of $\zeta$ at a given compaction peak, for $\zeta(\tilde{r})=\mu g(\tilde{r})$: $\mu=\frac{\pm\sqrt{1-\delta_{\mathrm{m}}/f(w)}-1}{g^{\prime}(\tilde{r}_{\mathrm{m}})\tilde{r}_{\mathrm{m}}}$ (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Eq. II.28], the "+" sign for type I and the "−" sign for type II; "the factor inside the square root is always positive since $\delta_{\mathrm{c}}\leq f(w)$" [src: Sect. II.4, inline, text after Eq. II.28].
- Non-sphericity, phenomenologically, for ellipsoidal overdensities: $\frac{\delta_{\rm ec}}{\delta_{\mathrm{c}}} \simeq 1+\kappa\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\delta_{\mathrm{c}}^{2}}\right)^{\mspace{-6mu}\tilde{\gamma}}$ (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Eq. II.29], derived in their Ref. [94], with $\kappa$ and $\tilde\gamma$ phenomenological parameters that "depend on the characteristics of the scenario under consideration" [src: Sect. II.4, inline, text after Eq. II.29].

### Analytical threshold formulae
<!-- key: analytical-threshold-formulae -->

- Carr's Jeans-length estimate, as quoted by Escrivà, Kühnel & Tada from their Ref. [4]: $\delta_{\mathrm{c},\mspace{1.5mu}\rm Carr}=w$ (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Eq. II.31], obtained "using a Jeans-length approximation" [src: Sect. II.6, inline, first paragraph]. Carr's 1975 paper was not read; it is cited through this review.
- Harada, Yoo & Kohri, from "a three-zone model" for "a homogeneous overdense sphere surrounded by a thin underdense shell": $\delta_{\mathrm{c},\mspace{1.5mu}\rm HYK}=\frac{3(1+w)}{5+3\mspace{1.5mu}w}\,\sin^{2}\!\left(\frac{\pi\sqrt{w}}{1+3\mspace{1.5mu}w}\right)$ (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Eq. II.32; Sect. II.6, text before Eq. II.32]. In the original paper, "Based on a physical argument", the same expression is the threshold of $\tilde\delta_{c}$, "the amplitude measure used in numerical simulations", against the conventional $\tilde\delta_{c}=3w(1+w)/(5+3w)$; "For a radiation fluid, our formula gives" $\tilde\delta_{c}=(2/3)\sin^{2}(\sqrt{3}\pi/6)\simeq 0.4135$ (Harada, Yoo & Kohri 2013, arXiv:1309.4201) [src: abstract, inline].
- Averaged compaction function (Escrivà, Germani & Sheth): $\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathrm{c}}\coloneqq\frac{3}{R^{3}_{\mathrm{m}}}\int_{0}^{R_{\mathrm{m}}}\mathrm{d}\tilde{R}\;\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{c}}\big(\tilde{R}\big)\tilde{R}^{2}$ (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Eq. II.33]; "for a radiation-dominated universe, the universal value $\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathrm{c}}=2/5$ has been found, independently of the underlying profiles" [src: Sect. II.6, inline, text before Eq. II.33], and for the polynomial profile of Eq. II.24a the threshold depends only on $q$, $\delta_{\mathrm{c}}(q)=\frac{4}{15}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mspace{1mu}1/q}\frac{q^{1-5/2\mspace{1.5mu}q}}{\Gamma(5/2\mspace{1.5mu}q)-\Gamma(5/2\mspace{1.5mu}q,\mspace{1.5mu}1/q)}$ [src: Eq. II.34], both from their Ref. [78]. The original letter argues that the threshold "is, to an excellent approximation, universal, whenever given in terms of the compaction function averaged over a sphere of radius $r_m$", and that for radiation "this limit is $\delta_c = 0.40$, which is slightly below the one quoted in the literature" (Escrivà, Germani & Sheth 2019, arXiv:1907.13311) [src: abstract, inline].
- General $w$: the shape dependence through the curvature of $\mathcal{C}$ at its peak "generalizes to all $w\gtrsim 1/3$; for smaller $w$s the knowledge of the full shape of the compaction function is necessary", with analytic approximations "accurate for $w\in [1/3,1]$" (Escrivà, Germani & Sheth 2020, arXiv:2007.05564) [src: abstract, inline]. The review transcribes that formula, $\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathrm{c}}(w)=\frac{3}{R^{3}_{\mathrm{m}}V[\alpha(w)]}\,\int_{R_{\mathrm{m}}[1-\alpha(w)]}^{R_{\mathrm{m}}}\mathrm{d}\tilde{R}\;\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{c}}\big(\tilde{R}\big)\tilde{R}^{2}$ (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Eq. II.35], with the fits of Eqs. II.36–II.39 and the fitting constants in the text after Eq. II.39b [src: Sect. II.6], "valid for $w\geq 1/3$" [src: Sect. II.6, inline, text before Eq. II.35].

### PBH mass: apparent horizon, accretion and critical scaling
<!-- key: pbh-mass-apparent-horizon-accretion-and -->

- Apparent horizon: with the expansions of the null congruences $\Theta^{\pm}=2(U\pm\Gamma)/R$, "the condition for the existence of an apparent horizon is given by $U^{2}=\Gamma^{2}\,\Rightarrow\,2\mspace{1.5mu}M=R$" (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Sect. II.5, inline, fourth paragraph]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Critical scaling: "Numerical simulations have shown that the black hole mass follows a scaling law ... when the amplitude of the perturbation $\delta$ is close to the critical value $\delta_{\mathrm{m}}-\delta_{\mathrm{c}}\lesssim 10^{-2}$", $M=\mathcal{K}M_{H}(t_{H})\big(\delta_{\mathrm{m}}-\delta_{\mathrm{c}}\big)^{\mspace{-2mu}\gamma}$ (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Eq. II.30; Sect. II.5, text before Eq. II.30]; "$\gamma$ is a universal exponent which only depends on the equation-of-state parameter $w$", and $\mathcal{K}$ "has a numerical value of $\mathcal{O}(1)$" and "depends on both the shape of the curvature fluctuation ... as well as on $w$" [src: Sect. II.5, inline, text after Eq. II.30].
- Evans & Coleman observed critical phenomena in this collapse: "A critical exponent $\beta\simeq 0.36$ is found for supercritical ($\eta>\eta_c$) models" in the spherical collapse of radiation fluid (Evans & Coleman 1994, arXiv:gr-qc/9402041) [src: abstract, inline]; Niemeyer & Jedamzik argued that "the universal scaling relation between black hole mass and initial perturbation ... also applies to PBH formation, indicating the possibility of the formation of PBHs with masses much smaller than one horizon mass" (Niemeyer & Jedamzik 1997, arXiv:astro-ph/9709072) [src: abstract, inline].

## PBH statistics: from the power spectrum to the collapse fraction
<!-- key: pbh-statistics-from-the-power-spectrum-to-the -->

Equations from Escrivà, Kühnel & Tada 2022 (arXiv:2211.05767), Sect. III, which, "For definiteness, and since this is the most relevant case", focuses on formation during radiation domination [src: Sect. III, first paragraph]. <!-- num: not a value -->

- Power spectrum and gradient moments: $\Big\langle\tilde{\zeta}^{*}(\bm{k})\tilde{\zeta}(\bm{k}^{\prime})\Big\rangle\equiv\frac{2\mspace{1.5mu}\pi^{2}}{k^{3}}\,\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}(k)(2\mspace{1.5mu}\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}\big(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}\big)$ (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Eq. III.1], $\sigma_{n}^{2}=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}k}{k}\;k^{2\mspace{1.5mu}n}\,\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}(k)$ [src: Eq. III.2]; on superhorizon scales, $\mathcal{P}_{\delta}=\frac{16}{81}\big(kR_{H}\big)^{\mspace{-1.5mu}4}\,\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}$ [src: Eq. III.3], the factor $16/81$ coming from $4(1+w)^{2}/(3w)^{2}$ "evaluated during radiation domination" [src: Sect. III, inline, footnote 17].
- Press–Schechter assumptions, as stated by the review: "(i) the peak value of the nonlinear volume-averaged density perturbation (i.e. the compaction-function peak) $\delta_{\mathrm{m}}$ has a Gaußian distribution"; "(ii) perturbations with compaction-function peaks $\delta_{\mathrm{m}}>\delta_{\mathrm{c}}$ will collapse into a black hole" (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Sect. III.1, inline, first paragraph]. With $p\mspace{1mu}(\delta_{\mathrm{m}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_{0}}\mathrm{e}^{-\delta^{2}_{\mathrm{m}}/(2\mspace{1.5mu}\sigma_{0}^{2})}$ [src: Eq. III.5], the collapse fraction is $\beta=\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{PBH}}}{\rho_{\rm tot}}=2\int_{\delta_{\mathrm{c}}}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\delta_{\mathrm{m}}\;\frac{M}{M_{H}}p\mspace{1mu}(\delta_{\mathrm{m}})$ [src: Eq. III.6, first row], "the factor 2 at the beginning of the integral" being "introduced to avoid the well-know under-counting in Press–Schechter theory" [src: Sect. III.1, inline, text after Eq. III.6]. The closed form in the last row of Eq. III.6 assumes the scaling law of Eq. II.30 "is always accurate even when $\delta_{\mathrm{m}}\gg\delta_{\mathrm{c}}$, which is actually not true" [src: Sect. III.1, inline, text after Eq. III.6]. The monochromatic limit, Eq. III.7, is printed with the error function $\erf$ where the limit $\gamma\to 0$ of Eq. III.6 is stated to give it [src: Sect. III.1, inline, text after Eq. III.7]; `[der]` with the $\erf$ of the review's own definition this reads as a slip for the complementary function, which is not corrected here (see `approximations.md`).
- Peak theory on curvature peaks: "for illustrative purposes" the review uses one method, "the procedure developed in Reference [432]", updated in its Ref. [420] "in order to be able to compute the PBH abundance for an arbitrary power spectrum (including broad shapes)" by counting peaks of $\Delta\zeta$ instead of $\zeta$, which, "As suggested in Reference [420]", allows one "to decouple the effects of the environment from the absolute value of $\zeta$, which can be contaminated by long-wavelength perturbations" (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Sect. III.2, first paragraph and text after Eq. III.12]. The mean profile and peak number density are Eqs. III.8–III.16 there; they are not transcribed here.
- Yoo, Harada, Garriga & Kohri: "A threshold $\zeta_{\rm th}$ for the curvature perturbation is somewhat inappropriate for this purpose, because it depends significantly on environmental effects", while "a threshold $\delta_{\rm th}$ for the density perturbation at horizon crossing seems to provide a more robust criterion" (arXiv:1805.03946) [src: abstract]; Young, Byrnes & Sasaki found that using $\mathcal{R}_{c}$ "in the standard way vastly overestimates the number of PBHs, by many orders of magnitude" and "argue that the density contrast, $\Delta$, should be used instead as super-horizon modes are damped by a factor $k^{2}$" (arXiv:1405.7023) [src: abstract].
- Non-Gaussianity from the nonlinear $\delta$–$\zeta$ relation: "Since the density contrast is related to the comoving curvature perturbation by a nonlinear relation, the overdensity statistics is unavoidably non-Gaussian" (De Luca et al. 2019, arXiv:1904.00970) [src: abstract].
- Local primordial non-Gaussianity: $\zeta(\bm{x})=\zeta_{\mathrm{G}}(\bm{x})+\frac{3}{5}f_{\mathrm{NL}}\zeta_{\mathrm{G}}^{2}(\bm{x})$ (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Eq. II.123], "commonly used as a minimal non-Gaußian correction", "the factor 3/5 is just a convention" [src: Sect. II.10.1, inline, text after Eq. II.123]. For the ultra-slow-roll toy model of a flat inflection, the non-perturbative $\delta N$ mapping is $\zeta(\bm{x})=-\frac{1}{3}\ln\mspace{-1.5mu}\big[1-3\mspace{1.5mu}\zeta_{\mathrm{G}}(\bm{x})\big]$ [src: Eq. II.131]; its derivation and the stochastic exponential tail belong to `thcosmo-inflation` (`deep/stochastic-delta-n-tails.md`). Ferrante et al. compute the abundance "for a completely generic functional form" of local non-Gaussianity using "threshold statistics of the compaction function" (arXiv:2211.01728) [src: abstract, inline].
- Clustering at formation: PBHs, "due to their discrete nature", "would unavoidably undergo Poisson clustering" (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Sect. II.13, inline, first paragraph]; the Poisson contribution to the matter power spectrum, as shown in its Ref. [317], is "scale-invariant and well approximated by" $P_{\rm Poisson}\simeq 2\times 10^{-3}\,\frac{f_{\rm PBH}}{g(z)^{2}}\mspace{-1.5mu}\left(\frac{M}{3\mspace{1mu}M_{\odot}}\right){\rm Mpc}^{3}$ [src: Eq. II.163], $g(z)$ "the growth factor for isocurvature fluctuations" [src: Sect. II.13, inline, text after Eq. II.163].

## PBH abundance and mass function
<!-- key: pbh-abundance-and-mass-function -->

Equations from Carr, Kohri, Sendouda & Yokoyama 2020 (arXiv:2002.12778), Sect. I.3, which "assume that the standard Lambda Cold Dark Matter ($\Lambda$CDM) model applies", with $h=0.67$, and put $c=\hbar=k_{\mathrm{B}}=1$ [src: Sect. I.3, inline, first paragraph]. <!-- num: not a value -->

- Radiation era: $H^{2}=\frac{8\pi\,G}{3}\,\rho=\frac{4\pi^{3}\,G}{45}\,g_{*}\,T^{4}$ (arXiv:2002.12778) [src: Eq. 2].
- Mass at formation, "For PBHs which form during the radiation era (the ones generated before inflation being diluted to negligible density)": $M=\gamma\,M_{\mathrm{H}}=\frac{4\pi}{3}\,\gamma\,\rho\,R_{\mathrm{H}}^{-3}=\frac{\gamma c^{3}t}{G}\approx 2.03\times 10^{5}\,\gamma\,\left(\frac{t}{1\,\mathrm{s}}\right)\,M_{\odot}$ (arXiv:2002.12778) [src: Eq. 4], copied as printed (the exponent $-3$ on $R_{\mathrm{H}}$ in the second expression); "$\gamma$ is a numerical factor (somewhat below 1) which depends on the details of gravitational collapse", the penultimate expression "applies exactly in a radiation Universe (for which $R_{\mathrm{H}}=2ct$)", and Eq. 4 "does not apply for PBHs which form in an early matter-dominated (dust-like) era or for the low mass PBHs forming through critical collapse" [src: Sect. I.3, text after Eq. 4]. Normalising to the particle-horizon mass "is inappropriate if there is an inflationary phase" [src: Sect. I.3, text after Eq. 4].
- Collapse fraction at formation, "Assuming adiabatic cosmic expansion after PBH formation": $\beta(M)\equiv\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{PBH}}(t_{\mathrm{i}})}{\rho(t_{\mathrm{i}})}=\frac{4M}{3T_{\mathrm{i}}}\,\frac{n_{\mathrm{PBH}}(t)}{s(t)}\\ \approx 7.99\times 10^{-29}\,\gamma^{-1/2}\,\left(\frac{g_{*\mathrm{i}}}{106.75}\right)^{1/4}\,\left(\frac{M}{M_{\odot}}\right)^{3/2}\,\left(\frac{n_{\mathrm{PBH}}(t_{0})}{1\,\mathrm{Gpc}^{-3}}\right)$ (arXiv:2002.12778) [src: Eq. 5], with $s=8.54\times 10^{85}\,\mathrm{Gpc}^{-3}$ assumed today [src: Sect. I.3, text after Eq. 5], and in terms of the present density parameter $\beta(M)\approx 7.06\times 10^{-18}\,\gamma^{-1/2}\,\left(\frac{h}{0.67}\right)^{2}\,\left(\frac{g_{*\mathrm{i}}}{106.75}\right)^{1/4}\,\Omega_{\mathrm{PBH}}(M)\,\left(\frac{M}{10^{15}\,\mathrm{g}}\right)^{1/2}$ [src: Eq. 6].
- The rough relation $\Omega_{\mathrm{PBH}}=\beta\,\Omega_{\mathrm{CMB}}\,(1+z)\sim 10^{6}\,\beta\,\left(\frac{t}{\mathrm{s}}\right)^{-1/2}\sim 10^{9}\,\beta\,\left(\frac{M}{M_{\odot}}\right)^{-1/2}$ (arXiv:2002.12778) [src: Eq. 7] is "A less precise form" of Eq. 6, the factor $(1+z)$ arising "because the radiation density scales as $(1+z)^{4}$, whereas the PBH density scales as $(1+z)^{3}$" [src: Sect. I.3, text before and after Eq. 7].
- Reduced collapse fraction: $\beta^{\prime}(M)\equiv\gamma^{1/2}\,\left(\frac{g_{*\mathrm{i}}}{106.75}\right)^{-1/4}\,\left(\frac{h}{0.67}\right)^{-2}\,\beta(M)$ (arXiv:2002.12778) [src: Eq. 8], because "$\beta$ always appears in combination with $\gamma^{1/2}g_{*\mathrm{i}}^{-1/4}h^{-2}$", where "$\gamma$ is rather uncertain"; the $\beta$–$\Omega_{\rm PBH}$ relation "must be modified if the Universe ever deviates from the standard radiation-dominated behaviour – for example, if there is a dust-like stage, a second inflationary phase, a change of dimensionality ... or a variation in the gravitational constant at early times" [src: Sect. I.3, text after Eq. 8].
- Two quantities are constrained: for evaporating PBHs, "the fraction of the Universe collapsing into PBHs at their formation epoch, denoted as $\beta(M)$"; for non-evaporating ones, "the fraction of the DM in PBHs, denoted as $f(M)$" (arXiv:2002.12778) [src: Sect. I.1, fourth paragraph]. Most limits "assume that PBH mass spectrum is quasi-monochromatic (i.e. having a width $\Delta M\lesssim M$) and it could well be extended" [src: Sect. I.1, fourth paragraph].
- Mass function from critical collapse: Niemeyer & Jedamzik argue that sub-horizon-mass PBHs "are expected to form at all epochs" because of "the natural fine-tuning of initial conditions by the exponential decline of the probability distribution" (arXiv:astro-ph/9709072) [src: abstract]; Germani & Musco find that, "although critical collapse gives a broad mass spectrum, the density of primordial black holes formed is dominated by masses roughly equal to the cosmological horizon mass measured at horizon crossing" (arXiv:1805.04087) [src: abstract].
- Thermal history: the review of Escrivà, Kühnel & Tada treats the dependence of the mass function on the softening of the equation of state ("thermal-history-induced mass function") in its Sect. II.11 and the QCD epoch in its Sect. II.12 (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Sects. II.11–II.12, titles]; the equations of those sections were not read (see "Not verified"). <!-- num: not a value -->

## Hawking evaporation
<!-- key: hawking-evaporation -->

From Carr, Kohri, Sendouda & Yokoyama 2020 (arXiv:2002.12778), Sects. I.4–I.4.1.

- Hawking temperature, quoted from Hawking's paper (their Ref. [136]): $T_{\mathrm{BH}}=\frac{\hbar\,c^{3}}{8\pi\,G\,M\,k_{\mathrm{B}}}\sim 10^{-7}\,\left(\frac{M}{M_{\odot}}\right)^{-1}\,\mathrm{K}$ (arXiv:2002.12778) [src: Eq. 9], so black holes "evaporate completely on a timescale" $\tau(M)\sim\frac{G^{2}\,M^{3}}{\hbar\,c^{4}}\sim 10^{64}\,\left(\frac{M}{M_{\odot}}\right)^{3}\,\mathrm{yr}$ [src: Eq. 10]. Hawking's 1974–1975 papers were not read; they are cited through this review.
- In units $M\equiv M_{10}\times 10^{10}$ g: $T_{\mathrm{BH}}\approx 1.06\,M_{10}^{-1}\,\mathrm{TeV}$ (arXiv:2002.12778) [src: Eq. 11], which "assumes that the hole has no charge or angular momentum"; mass loss $\frac{\mathrm{d}M_{10}}{\mathrm{d}t}=-5.34\times 10^{-5}\,f(M)\,M_{10}^{-2}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ [src: Eq. 12], with $f(M)$ "a measure of the number of emitted particle species, normalised to unity for a black hole with $M\gg 10^{17}\,\mathrm{g}$" [src: Sect. I.4.1, text after Eq. 12].
- Lifetime: summing the Standard Model "up to 1 TeV, corresponding to $M_{10}\sim 1$, this gives $f(M)=15.35$" and $\tau\approx 407\,\left(\frac{f(M)}{15.35}\right)^{-1}\,M_{10}^{3}\,\mathrm{s}$ (arXiv:2002.12778) [src: Eq. 14; Sect. I.4.1, text before Eq. 14]. The mass evaporating today is $M_{*}\approx 1.02\times 10^{15}\,\left(\frac{f_{*}}{15.35}\right)^{1/3}\,\mathrm{g}\approx 5.1\times 10^{14}\,\mathrm{g}$ [src: Eq. 16] "For the currently favoured age of 13.8 Gyr", where "the last step assumes $f_{*}=1.9$" [src: Sect. I.4.1, text before and after Eq. 16]; "The more precise calculations of MacGibbon ... give the slightly smaller value $M_{*}=5.00\times 10^{14}\,\mathrm{g}$" [src: Sect. I.4.1, last paragraph].
- Spectra: the emission "is not exactly black-body but depends upon the spin and charge of the emitted particle" (arXiv:2002.12778) [src: Sect. I.4.1, text after Eq. 11]; primary and secondary spectra of any black-hole distribution are computed by the public code BlackHawk (Arbey & Auffinger 2019, arXiv:1905.04268) [src: abstract].
- Memory burden: Dvali, Eisemann, Michel & Zell 2020 state that systems "of enhanced memory capacity" are subject to memory burden, "which suppresses their decay", and, from "a prototype model", that "Applied to black holes, this predicts a metamorphosis, including a drastic deviation from Hawking evaporation, at the latest after losing half of the mass" (arXiv:2006.00011) [src: abstract]; Alexandre, Dvali & Koutsangelas 2024 add that "What happens beyond this time is currently not known. However, theoretical evidence based on prototype models indicates that the evaporation slows down" (arXiv:2402.14069) [src: abstract]. The review of Escrivà, Kühnel & Tada states that "the evaporation dynamics have not yet been entirely resolved, so it remains unclear how long PBHs will eventually survive" (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Sect. V.2, fourth paragraph].

## Other PBH formation channels
<!-- key: other-pbh-formation-channels -->

From the review of Escrivà, Kühnel & Tada 2022 (arXiv:2211.05767), Sect. II.14, unless another paper is named. The transitions themselves (nucleation, $\beta/H$, supercooling) are in `thcosmo-baryogenesis-phase-transitions`; this section keeps only how PBHs form from them. <!-- num: not a value -->

- Early matter domination: "assuming an infinitely long phase of matter domination, the PBH formation threshold formation is zero if non-spherical effects are neglected" (as printed), while a dust phase "could induce angular momentum to the collapsing perturbation, which would effectively increase the threshold value to a nonzero value", as pointed out in their Ref. [347] (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Sect. II.14, paragraph "Collapse in a Matter-Dominated Era"]. The mass function then "acquires a power-law rather than an exponential dependence", $\mathrm{d}n/\mathrm{d}M\propto M^{-2}\delta_{H}(M)^{5}$, as shown in their Ref. [348] [src: Sect. II.14, inline, paragraph "Collapse in a Matter-Dominated Era"].
- Cosmic-string loops that "self-intersect and form closed loops which fall within their Schwarzschild radii" form PBHs "with equal probability at every epoch", with an extended mass spectrum $\beta\sim(G\mspace{1mu}\mu)^{2\mspace{1.5mu}\ell/s-4}$, "where $2<\ell/s<4$", from their Ref. [352] (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Sect. II.14, inline, paragraph "Collapse of Cosmic Loops"].
- Domain walls: collapsing walls from a second-order transition "could ignite the formation of PBHs", with masses that "might span a wide range depending on the specific scenario", possibly "significantly below the horizon mass" (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Sect. II.14, paragraph "Collapse of Domain Walls"].
- Bubble collisions in a first-order transition: "the bubble-formation rate per Hubble volume must be finely tuned, not to be much larger than the Hubble rate", and "can also not be much less than the Hubble rate, because then the bubbles are rare and practically never collide" (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Sect. II.14, paragraph "Collapse through Bubble Collisions"].
- Particle trapping: a fermion $\chi$ with Yukawa coupling to the transition field gets $m_{\chi}=y\braket{\phi}$ in the true vacuum; "If this mass is much larger than the typical kinetic energy of $\chi$ during the phase transition, $\chi$ cannot enter the true vacuum because of kinetic blocking", is trapped in the false vacuum, "which in turn can lead to PBH formation" (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Sect. II.14, inline, paragraph "Particle Trapping by Bubble Walls"].
- Delayed (asynchronous) vacuum decay: "Because of the randomness of the quantum tunneling, there always exists some probability that the vacuum decay is postponed in a whole Hubble volume", and "the postponed vacuum decay then results in overdense regions, which finally collapse into PBHs as indicated by numerical simulations"; the predicted mass function "is nearly monochromatic" (Liu et al. 2021, arXiv:2106.05637) [src: abstract].
- Supercooled-transition remnants: false-vacuum remnants "scale slower with the expansion of the Universe than their surroundings"; Lewicki et al. estimate the collapse "using the hoop conjecture", including sub-horizon regions "that collapse as their compactness grows" (Lewicki et al. 2023, arXiv:2305.04924) [src: abstract].
- A mini-review of PT channels discusses "various model-dependent and independent mechanisms" with model-specific examples (Banerjee, Dey & Khalil 2025, arXiv:2512.22989) [src: abstract].
- Other channels listed by the review: collapse of isocurvature fluctuations, a scalar fifth force, scalar-field fragmentation into Q-balls and oscillons, metric preheating, and baby universes from false-vacuum bubbles nucleated during inflation (arXiv:2211.05767) [src: Sect. II.14, paragraph titles]. For metric preheating with $V\simeq m^{2}\phi^{2}/2$ the instability band is $aH<k<a\sqrt{3\mspace{1.5mu}Hm}$ [src: Sect. II.14, inline, paragraph "Metric Preheating"]. <!-- num: not a value -->

## Defect formation and classification
<!-- key: defect-formation-and-classification -->

From the review of Copeland & Kibble 2009 (arXiv:0911.1345), Sect. 2, natural units $c=\hbar=k_{\text{B}}=1$ [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 1]. <!-- num: not a value -->

- Abelian-Higgs model, "The simplest model in which cosmic strings arise": $L=D_{\mu}\phi^{*}D^{\mu}\phi-\tfrac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}-V(\phi).$ (arXiv:0911.1345) [src: Eq. 1], with $V=\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda(\phi^{*}\phi-\tfrac{1}{2}\eta^{2})^{2}$ and $D_{\mu}\phi=\partial_{\mu}\phi+ieA_{\mu}\phi$ [src: Sect. 2, inline, text after Eq. 1].
- `[der]` The kinetic term with a positive sign and the potential with a negative sign imply the mostly-minus signature, which the review does not state in the part read.
- Formation: below $T_{\text{c}}$ "of order $\eta$" the field "must randomly choose a phase angle", and "this choice may be made differently in widely separated regions"; "if around a large loop $\alpha$ varies by $2\pi$ then somewhere within that loop, $\phi$ must vanish; the locus of such points is a cosmic string" (arXiv:0911.1345) [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 2].
- Tension: "the energy per unit length is equal to the string tension $\mu$", $\mu=F(\beta)\eta^{2},$ (arXiv:0911.1345) [src: Eq. 5], with $F$ "a function of order 1" of $\beta=\frac{e^{2}}{\lambda}=\frac{m^{2}_{\text{v}}}{m^{2}_{\text{s}}}.$ [src: Eq. 6]; "If $\beta>1$, strings with all values of $n$ are stable; if $\beta<1$, only the $n=\pm 1$ strings are stable" [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 8]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Vacuum manifold: $H=\{U\in G:U\phi_{0}=\phi_{0}\}.$ (arXiv:0911.1345) [src: Eq. 7] and $\mathcal{M}=G/H,$ [src: Eq. 8]; "cosmic strings are found if and only there are incontractible loops within $\mathcal{M}$", i.e. $\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M})\neq\mathbf{1}$; "Domain walls exist if the manifold $\mathcal{M}$ is not connected, i.e. if $\pi_{0}(\mathcal{M})\neq\mathbf{1}$, while monopoles exist if there are incontractible 2-surfaces, i.e. if $\pi_{2}(\mathcal{M})\neq\mathbf{1}$" [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 8]. For U(1) broken to $\mathbf{1}$, $\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M})={\mathbb{Z}}$ and the strings are labelled by the winding number [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 8]; "In many models of unification the symmetry group $G$ is simple or semi-simple. In such cases, the fundamental group $\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M})$ is always a finite group" [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 8]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Global strings arise "in theories with no gauge field, where the symmetry is global", and "have rather different properties"; the review does not treat them (arXiv:0911.1345) [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 8]. They are covered below under global strings.
- Gravitational effect: the deficit angle around a straight string is "$\delta=8\pi G\mu$"; for strings created at an energy scale $T_{\text{c}}$, "$G\mu$ is of order $(T_{\text{c}}/M_{\text{Pl}})^{2}$", and "for strings created at a GUT transition, we expect $G\mu$ to be of order $10^{-6}$ or $10^{-7}$" (arXiv:0911.1345) [src: Sect. 10.1, inline, paragraph beginning "The deficit angle"].

## Cosmic strings: dynamics and network evolution
<!-- key: cosmic-strings-dynamics-and-network-evolution -->

From Copeland & Kibble 2009 (arXiv:0911.1345), Sects. 3–4 and 9, unless another paper is named. The velocity-dependent one-scale (VOS) equations of Martins & Shellard, their scaling attractor, the loop production function and the loop number densities used for gravitational-wave spectra are transcribed in `thcosmo-cosmological-gravitational-waves`, `deep/cosmic-string-spectra.md` (Steps 2–4), and are not repeated here. <!-- num: not a value -->

- Nambu–Goto action: $S=-\mu\int d^{2}\sigma\sqrt{-\det(g_{\mu\nu}x^{\mu}_{,a}x^{\nu}_{,b})},$ (arXiv:0911.1345) [src: Eq. 10]; in the conformal gauge in flat space the constraints and equation of motion are $\dot{\mathbf{x}}^{2}+(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})^{2}=1,\qquad\dot{\mathbf{x}}\cdot\mathbf{x}^{\prime}=0.$ [src: Eq. 12] and $\ddot{\mathbf{x}}=\mathbf{x}^{\prime\prime}.$ [src: Eq. 13]. The energy density of a network of correlation length $\xi$ is $\rho_{\text{str}}=\frac{\mu}{\xi^{2}}.$ [src: Eq. 9].
- Friction regime: just after formation "their motion will be heavily damped", and "during this initial friction-dominated regime, the characteristic scale of the tangle will increase with time as $\xi\propto t^{5/4}$", a result of Kibble 1982 (arXiv:0911.1345) [src: Sect. 4, inline, first paragraph].
- Scaling: "There is now general agreement from several simulations, as well as theoretical analyses, that the string network will reach a scaling regime in which the ratio $\gamma=\xi/t$ remains nearly constant", with estimates "typically of order 0.3 and 0.55 in the radiation- and matter-dominated eras respectively"; then $\frac{\rho_{\text{str}}}{\rho}=\frac{8\pi G\mu}{3\nu^{2}}\frac{\xi^{2}}{t^{2}}=\frac{8\pi G\mu\gamma^{2}}{3\nu^{2}},$ (arXiv:0911.1345) [src: Eq. 22; Sect. 4, text before Eq. 22], "a constant of order $G\mu$" [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 22], for $a(t)\propto t^{\nu}$ [src: Sect. 4, inline, text before Eq. 22]. In Minkowski space the r.m.s. velocity "is $1/\surd 2$, though in the expanding universe it is somewhat less" [src: Sect. 4, second paragraph].
- Energy loss: "maintenance of the scaling regime requires some mechanism for removing energy from the tangle of strings. It is widely believed that the formation of closed loops plays a key role, but the questions of how large those loops typically are and of the role of small-scale structure on the strings are still controversial" (arXiv:0911.1345) [src: Sect. 4, third paragraph]; isolated loops "oscillate quasi-periodically, with a period equal to half the length $l$ of the loop" [src: Sect. 4, fourth paragraph].
- Loop lifetime under gravitational radiation, "The original estimate (Vachaspati & Vilenkin 1985)": $t_{l}\approx\frac{l}{\Gamma G\mu},$ (arXiv:0911.1345) [src: Eq. 23], with $\Gamma$ "a numerical factor depending on the shape, but not the size, of the loop, typically of order $10^{2}$. But in some case, the lifetime may be substantially larger than this estimate" [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 23].
- Small-scale structure: with the correlation function $z(s,t)\equiv\langle\mathbf{p}(\tau,u_{1})\cdot\mathbf{p}(\tau,u_{2})\rangle,\qquad s=a\epsilon(u_{1}-u_{2}),$ (arXiv:0911.1345) [src: Eq. 24], "On most scales of observational significance, the small-scale structure does reach a scaling regime", $z(s,t)=1-A(s/t)^{2\chi},$ [src: Eq. 25], from Polchinski & Rocha 2006 [src: Sect. 4, text before and after Eq. 25]. A velocity-dependent two-scale extension finds "quite generally small-scale structure does not prevent the attainment of a linear scaling regime", with "a smaller energy density and root-mean-squared velocity when compared to strings without small-scale structure" (Miranda & Sousa 2026, arXiv:2603.23619) [src: abstract].
- Intercommutation: "field-theory simulations of cosmic strings indicate that the probability of intercommuting is essentially unity, $P=1$, with only ultra-relativistic strings being able to pass through each other without reconnecting" (arXiv:0911.1345) [src: Sect. 9.1, first paragraph]; for fundamental strings, Polchinski (1988) "had shown that in the context of string perturbation theory" it "is of order $g^{2}_{s}$" [src: Sect. 9.1, first paragraph].
- Field theory against Nambu–Goto: in Abelian-Higgs simulations "the strings lose energy into classical radiation of the scalar and gauge fields", and "The scaling of the string separation implies that string loops decay by the emission of massive radiation within a Hubble time in field theory simulations, in contrast to the Nambu-Goto scenario which neglects this" (Hindmarsh, Lizarraga, Urrestilla, Daverio & Kunz 2017, arXiv:1703.06696) [src: abstract]; Hindmarsh, Lizarraga, Urio & Urrestilla 2021 "advocate parametrising the uncertainty as the fraction $f_\text{NG}$ of Nambu-Goto-like loops surviving to radiate gravitationally" (arXiv:2103.16248) [src: abstract].

## Domain walls
<!-- key: domain-walls -->

From the review of Saikawa 2017 (arXiv:1703.02576), Sect. 2, which takes the Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{2}\partial^{\mu}\phi\partial_{\mu}\phi-V(\phi)$ [src: Eq. 2.1] and $M_{\rm Pl}\simeq 2.435\times 10^{18}\,\mathrm{GeV}$, "the reduced Planck mass" [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 2.15].

- `[der]` The minus sign on the kinetic term of Eq. 2.1 implies the mostly-plus signature, which the part read does not state.

- $Z_2$ toy model: $V(\phi)=\frac{\lambda}{4}\left(\phi^{2}-v^{2}\right)^{2}$ (arXiv:1703.02576) [src: Eq. 2.2]; the static planar wall is $\phi(z)=v\tanh\left[\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{2}}vz\right].$ [src: Eq. 2.3], of width $\delta\simeq\left(\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{2}}v\right)^{-1}.$ [src: Eq. 2.4] and tension $\sigma=\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}dzT_{00}=\frac{4}{3}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{2}}v^{3}.$ [src: Eq. 2.6].
- $Z_N$ (axion-like) model: $\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}a\partial^{\mu}a-\frac{m^{2}v^{2}}{N^{2}}\left[1-\cos\left(N\frac{a}{v}\right)\right],$ (arXiv:1703.02576) [src: Eq. 2.7], with $\sigma=\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}dz\left(\frac{da}{dz}\right)^{2}=\frac{8mv^{2}}{N^{2}}.$ [src: Eq. 2.10]; in general "the wall thickness is roughly given by Compton wavelength of the field which causes the spontaneous breaking of discrete symmetry", with $\sigma\sim\delta\cdot V_{0}.$ [src: Eq. 2.11; Sect. 2.1, text before Eq. 2.11].
- Formation: the review "expect[s] that the formation of domain walls can happen" "if either the Hubble parameter during inflation $H_{\rm inf}$ or the maximum temperature after inflation $T_{\rm max}$ is sufficiently larger than the mass $m_{\phi}$"; the review adds "We emphasize that the condition is not robust, and we can consider several loopholes depending on the details of the models" (arXiv:1703.02576) [src: Sect. 2.2, first paragraph and footnote 1].
- Friction regime: tension $p_{T}\sim\frac{\sigma}{R_{\rm wall}},$ (arXiv:1703.02576) [src: Eq. 2.12] against friction $p_{F}\sim\Delta p\cdot n\sim vT^{4},$ [src: Eq. 2.13], the latter "if there is a interaction of the field composing the core of domain walls and particles in thermal bath" [src: Sect. 2.2, text before Eq. 2.13]; the friction force "is exponentially damped when the temperature of the background radiations becomes less than the mass of particles that interact with domain walls" [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 2.16].
- Scaling: once the friction force is irrelevant, "Many numerical studies ... confirmed that the evolution of domain walls in this regime can be described by the scaling solution", parameterised as $\rho_{\rm wall}(t)=\mathcal{A}\frac{\sigma}{t},$ (arXiv:1703.02576) [src: Eq. 2.17; Sect. 2.2, text before Eq. 2.17]; the simulations "imply $\rho_{\rm wall}\propto t^{-\nu}$, where the exponent $\nu$ slightly deviates from $\nu=1$", and "it is unclear whether this deviation represents some physical effect or just a numerical artifact" [src: Sect. 2.2, footnote 2].
- Domain-wall problem: wall domination at $t_{\rm dom}=\frac{3M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{4\mathcal{A}\sigma}\simeq 2.93\times 10^{3}\,\mathrm{sec}\,\mathcal{A}^{-1}\left(\frac{\sigma}{\mathrm{TeV}^{3}}\right)^{-1}$ (arXiv:1703.02576) [src: Eq. 2.19], assuming radiation domination before; "The equation of state for an isotropic gas of non-relativistic domain walls is given by $w=-2/3$", so that $R(t)\propto t^{2}.$ [src: Eq. 2.20; Sect. 2.2, text before Eq. 2.20]. Even if subdominant, $\frac{\delta\rho}{\rho}\sim\frac{\rho_{\rm wall}}{\rho_{c}}\sim G\sigma t_{0}\sim 10^{12}\left(\frac{\sigma}{\mathrm{TeV}^{3}}\right),$ [src: Eq. 2.21], which with $\delta\rho/\rho\lesssim\mathcal{O}(10^{-5})$ from the CMB gives the Zel'dovich–Kobzarev–Okun bound $\sigma^{1/3}\lesssim\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{MeV}).$ [src: Eq. 2.22; Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 2.22] for walls "at the present time".
- Biased walls: with an explicit breaking $\Delta V(\phi)=\epsilon v\phi\left(\frac{1}{3}\phi^{2}-v^{2}\right),$ (arXiv:1703.02576) [src: Eq. 2.23], $V_{\rm bias}\equiv V(-v)-V(+v)=\frac{4}{3}\epsilon v^{4}.$ [src: Eq. 2.24]; this "works if the discrete symmetry is not exact, but holds only approximately" [src: Sect. 2.3, text after Eq. 2.24]. Large-scale walls form "as long as" $\frac{V_{\rm bias}}{V_{0}}<\ln\left(\frac{1-p_{c}}{p_{c}}\right)=0.795.$ [src: Eq. 2.26], with $p_c=0.311$ from percolation on "a three dimensional cubic lattice" [src: Sect. 2.3, text before Eq. 2.26]. Assuming scaling beforehand, the annihilation time is $t_{\rm ann}=C_{\rm ann}\frac{\mathcal{A}\sigma}{V_{\rm bias}}=6.58\times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{sec}\,C_{\rm ann}\mathcal{A}\left(\frac{\sigma}{\mathrm{TeV}^{3}}\right)\left(\frac{V_{\rm bias}}{\mathrm{MeV}^{4}}\right)^{-1},$ [src: Eq. 2.27], "where $C_{\rm ann}$ is a coefficient of $\mathcal{O}(1)$" that "typically takes the value of $C_{\rm ann}\simeq 2\textendash 5$, depending on $N$" and on "the choice of the criterion to determine the decay time of domain walls in the simulations" [src: Sect. 2.3, text after Eq. 2.28].
- Gravitational waves from annihilating walls: `thcosmo-cosmological-gravitational-waves` (`references/` and its domain-wall entries); not repeated here.

## Global strings, monopoles, textures and hybrid defects
<!-- key: global-strings-monopoles-textures-and-hybrid -->

- Global (axion) strings: in simulations of the post-inflationary Peccei–Quinn scenario the network is attracted to "an asymptotic solution ... independently of the initial conditions", with "clear evidence of logarithmic violations of the scaling properties of the attractor solution" (Gorghetto, Hardy & Villadoro 2018, arXiv:1806.04677) [src: abstract]; Kim & Son report "A strong evidence for a logarithmically growing spectral index of the string power spectrum during the evolution" in $4096^3$ simulations of global strings "in the post-inflationary scenario" (Kim & Son 2024, arXiv:2411.08455) [src: abstract]. The axion relic abundance from strings and the domain-wall number belong to `thcosmo-dark-matter-models`. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Monopoles: "Magnetic monopoles are an inevitable feature of post-inflation symmetry-breaking phase transitions in grand unified theories", and "Analytic estimates of their density indicate that they are compatible with standard cosmology only if their mass is less than $10^{11}$ GeV" (Hindmarsh, Lopez-Eiguren, Seppä & Weir 2025, arXiv:2511.14204) [src: abstract]. The "monopole problem" of GUTs in standard cosmology is one motivation for inflation, as Buchmuller, Domcke & Schmitz recall (arXiv:2307.04691) [src: Sect. 5, second paragraph]; Preskill's 1979 estimate and Zel'dovich & Khlopov's were not read.
- Metastable strings: in theories where strings couple to monopoles, strings "can decay by quantum tunneling into string segments connecting monopole–antimonopole pairs"; "In the semiclassical approximation, the decay rate per string unit length is given by" $\Gamma_{d}=\frac{\mu}{2\pi}\exp\left(-\pi\kappa\right)\qquad\text{with}\qquad\kappa=\frac{m_{M}^{2}}{\mu}$ (arXiv:2307.04691) [src: Eq. 1; Sect. 1, text before Eq. 1], quoted from its Refs. [39, 40, 41]. Metastable strings "require two steps of spontaneous symmetry breaking", SU(2) to U(1) (monopoles) and then a U(1) that "must not be orthogonal to the U(1) contained in SU(2)", with inflation in between "to dilute the produced monopoles but to keep the cosmic strings" [src: Sect. 5, first two paragraphs].
- Textures and semi-local strings enter the Planck 2013 defect search as separate templates (arXiv:1303.5085) [src: abstract]; their field theory was not read (see "Not verified").
