## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- Bennett et al. 2020 (arXiv:2012.02726, v3) use the rescaled variables $x\equiv am_{e}$, $y\equiv ap$ and $z\equiv aT_{\gamma}$ (arXiv:2012.02726) [src: Sect. 2.1, inline, text after Eq. 1], comoving densities $\bar{\rho}_{\rm tot}\equiv(x/m_{e})^{4}\rho_{\rm tot}$ (arXiv:2012.02726) [src: Sect. 2.1, inline, text after Eq. 2], the Planck mass $m_{\rm Pl}$ in the Hubble rate, and the flavour basis for the density matrix $\varrho(x,y)$, with the Dirac CP phase "explicitly" set to zero so that "there is no neutrino asymmetry" [src: Sect. 2.2]. "A particle name is taken to refer to both the particle and its anti-particle" [src: Sect. 2.1, footnote 4].
- Bennett et al. 2019 (arXiv:1911.04504, v3), the equation-of-state paper, expand in the elementary charge e and evaluate numbers with $\alpha\equiv e^{2}/4\pi=1/137$ (arXiv:1911.04504) [src: Sect. 4.1.1, inline, text after Eq. 32]; their time variable is normalised to the decoupling temperature, "somewhat different from that used in" earlier work [src: Sect. 2.2, footnote 3]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Cielo et al. 2023 (arXiv:2306.05460, v3) work in cosmic time t with one Fermi–Dirac temperature per neutrino flavour, $T_{\nu_\alpha}$ [src: Sect. III]; Escudero et al. 2025 (arXiv:2511.04747, v2) add an effective chemical potential $\mu_i$ that "should not be confused with a chemical potential parametrizing particle-antiparticle asymmetries" [src: Sect. 2.1, footnote 2].
- `[der]` These are particle-physics conventions in natural units; no metric enters, so the signature and potential conventions of `formalism.md#conventions` do not apply here.

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — the idealised value and the three approximations behind it
<!-- key: step-1-the-idealised-value-and-the-three -->

Bennett et al. 2019 list the three approximations under which T_ν = (4/11)^{1/3} T and N_eff = 3: ideal gases, instantaneous decoupling at a nominal $T_d$, and an electron–positron sector "fully ultra-relativistic at the time of neutrino decoupling" (arXiv:1911.04504) [src: Sect. 2]. "Relaxing any one of the above three approximations will induce a departure from the idealised" value; the first and third are "analytically tractable" while decoupling is kept instantaneous, whereas relaxing instantaneous decoupling "generally necessitates that we solve a system of quantum kinetic equations" [src: Sect. 2].

With instantaneous decoupling, separate conservation of comoving entropy in the QED plasma and in the neutrinos (Eqs. 2–3) gives $\frac{T_{\nu}(a_{2})}{T(a_{2})}=\left(\frac{4}{11}\right)^{1/3}\left(1+\frac{\delta s}{\left.s^{(0)}\right|_{T_{d}/m_{e}\to\infty}}\right)^{-1/3},$ (arXiv:1911.04504) [src: Eq. 9], where δs is the change of the QED entropy at decoupling when the ideal-gas or ultra-relativistic assumption is dropped [src: Sect. 2.1], and hence $\delta N_{\rm eff}=3\left[\left(1+\frac{\delta s}{\left.s^{(0)}\right|_{T_{d}/m_{e}\to\infty}}\right)^{-4/3}-1\right]$ (arXiv:1911.04504) [src: Eq. 11].

The finite electron mass at $T_d$ (the "m_e/T_d correction" of Table 5 of arXiv:2012.02726, "identically the" non-relativistic correction of arXiv:1911.04504 [src: Table 5 caption]) is the largest single term; its leading digit is in `results.md`. The entropy argument "becomes poorly defined if the instantaneous decoupling approximation were relaxed as well", because out-of-equilibrium energy transfer "distorts the neutrino phase space distributions and generates entropy" (arXiv:1911.04504) [src: Sect. 2.2]; from there on the continuity equation is solved (Step 3).

### Step 2 — the QED equation of state to ${\cal O}(e^3)$
<!-- key: step-2-the-qed-equation-of-state-to-cal-o-e3 -->

Bennett et al. 2019 start from the partition function rather than from thermal masses: $\ln Z=\ln Z^{(0)}+\ln Z^{(2)}+\ln Z^{(3)}+\;\cdots$ (arXiv:1911.04504) [src: Eq. 22], known "to n=3 for arbitrary $m_{e}$ and μ" and "to n=5 in the $m_{e}=\mu=0$ limit" [src: Sect. 4, text after Eq. 22]; pressure, energy and entropy densities follow at each order from $P^{(n)}=\frac{T}{V}\ln Z^{(n)},$ (arXiv:1911.04504) [src: Eq. 23] and the standard relations of Eqs. 24–25. <!-- num: not a value -->

At ${\cal O}(e^2)$ (the two-loop diagram), for an isotropic CP-symmetric medium, $P^{(2)}=\frac{T}{V}\ln Z^{(2)}=-\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{12\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}p\;\frac{p^{2}}{E_{p}}n_{D}-\frac{e^{2}}{8\pi^{4}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}p\;\frac{p^{2}}{E_{p}}n_{D}\right)^{2}+\frac{e^{2}m_{e}^{2}}{16\pi^{4}}\iint_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}p\;\mathrm{d}\tilde{p}\;\frac{p\tilde{p}}{E_{p}E_{\tilde{p}}}\;\ln\left|\frac{p+\tilde{p}}{p-\tilde{p}}\right|\;n_{D}\tilde{n}_{D},$ (arXiv:1911.04504) [src: Eq. 28], with $n_{D}\equiv 2f_{D}(E_{p})$ [src: Sect. 4.1, text after Eq. 28]. "with the exception of" one reference, the corrections used in earlier precision calculations "are essentially equivalent to using only the first two, “log-independent” terms"; the third, "log-dependent" term "is usually deemed too small" [src: Sect. 4.1, text after Eq. 28]. The log-independent part enters the continuity equation through two functions $G_1$, $G_2$ (Eqs. 34–35 of arXiv:1911.04504, not transcribed here). <!-- num: not a value -->

At ${\cal O}(e^3)$ the next term is "not $\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2})=\mathcal{O}(e^{4})$", because the resummation of ring diagrams gives the photon a screening mass that "regularises the infrared divergence of the Bose–Einstein distribution", "which explains the odd power of e" (arXiv:1911.04504) [src: Sect. 4.2]. The pressure is $P^{(3)}=\frac{T}{V}\ln Z^{(3)}=\frac{e^{3}T}{12\pi^{4}}I^{{3/2}}(T),$ (arXiv:1911.04504) [src: Eq. 45] with $I(T)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\;\mathrm{d}p\left(\frac{p^{2}+E_{p}^{2}}{E_{p}}\right)n_{D},$ (arXiv:1911.04504) [src: Eq. 46], the Debye–Hückel correction in the non-relativistic Maxwell–Boltzmann limit [src: Sect. 4.2, text after Eq. 46]; the energy and entropy densities are $\rho^{(3)}=\frac{e^{3}T^{2}}{8\pi^{4}}I^{1/2}\partial_{T}I,$ (arXiv:1911.04504) [src: Eq. 47] and $s^{(3)}=\frac{e^{3}}{24\pi^{4}}\left(2I^{{3/2}}+3I^{1/2}T\partial_{T}I\right).$ (arXiv:1911.04504) [src: Eq. 48]. Its $G_1$, $G_2$ functions are Eq. 53 of the same paper (not transcribed).

In the entropy estimate at $T_d=1.3453$ MeV these terms give $\delta N_{\rm eff}^{(2)\not{\ln}}=\delta N_{\rm eff}^{\not{\rm Rel}+(2)\not{\ln}}-\delta N_{\rm eff}^{\not{\rm Rel}}\simeq 0.010121$ (arXiv:1911.04504) [src: Eq. 33], against "the oft-quoted" 0.010594 computed "in the limit $T_{d}/m_{e}\to\infty$" [src: Sect. 4.1.1, text after Eq. 33], and $\delta N_{\rm eff}^{(3)}=\delta N_{\rm eff}^{\not{\rm Rel}+(2)+(3)}-\delta N_{\rm eff}^{\not{\rm Rel}+(2)}\simeq-0.000952$ (arXiv:1911.04504) [src: Eq. 52]. The ${\cal O}(e^3)$ term is negative, "cancelling to a small extent" the ${\cal O}(e^2)$ one, and "it is not obvious on the microphysics level why this term should shift $N_{\rm eff}$ in the direction it does" [src: Sect. 4.2, text after Eq. 53]; across "a broad range of plausible $T_{d}$" it is "larger than—or, at least, comparable to—" the effect of oscillations [src: Sect. 4.2, text after Eq. 53]. Higher orders were "estimated in the instantaneous decoupling limit to contribute at most $\delta N_{\rm eff}\sim 4\times 10^{-6}$" (arXiv:2012.02726) [src: Sect. 3.1.1].

### Step 3 — the continuity equation and the quantum kinetic equation
<!-- key: step-3-the-continuity-equation-and-the-quantum -->

The precision computation solves two sets of equations "in a time frame corresponding to $T\sim{\cal O}(100)\to{\cal O}(0.01)$ MeV" (arXiv:2012.02726) [src: Sect. 2]. The continuity equation, in rescaled comoving variables, is $\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}x}\bar{\rho}_{\rm tot}(x,z(x))=\frac{1}{x}\left[\bar{\rho}_{\rm tot}(x,z(x))-3\bar{P}_{\rm tot}(x,z(x))\right],$ (arXiv:2012.02726) [src: Eq. 2], summing the QED and neutrino sectors; the QED sector, of photons, electrons and muons, is "assumed" to be always in thermodynamic equilibrium [src: Sect. 2.1; composition in footnote 4], so the finite-temperature corrections of Step 2 enter through $\rho_{\rm QED}$ and $P_{\rm QED}$ [src: Sect. 3.1].

The density matrix obeys, schematically, $\partial_{t}\varrho-pH\partial_{p}\varrho=-{\rm i}[\mathbb{H},\varrho]+{\cal I}[\varrho]$ (arXiv:2012.02726) [src: Sect. 2.2, inline, text after Eq. 2], and in the rescaled variables $\frac{{\rm d}\varrho(x,y)}{{\rm d}x}=\frac{1}{m_{e}}\frac{m_{e}^{4}}{x^{4}}\sqrt{\frac{3m^{2}_{\rm Pl}}{8\pi\bar{\rho}_{\rm tot}}}\Bigg(-{\rm i}\Big[\mathbb{H}(x,y,z(x)),\varrho(x,y)\Big]+{\mathcal{I}}[\varrho(x,y)]\Bigg),$ (arXiv:2012.02726) [src: Eq. 3]. As printed, the text below Eq. 3 calls the prefactor written with $(m_{e}/x)^{2}$ "but the inverse Hubble expansion rate"; `[der]` with the definition of the comoving density in Sect. 2.1, the inverse Hubble rate carries the power of x over the electron mass, not its inverse, so the sentence appears to invert the ratio (the equation itself was not checked term by term).

The oscillation Hamiltonian in the flavour basis has a vacuum and an in-medium part: $\mathbb{H}(x,y,z(x))=\frac{x^{6}}{m_{e}^{6}}\frac{U\mathbb{M}U^{\dagger}}{2y}-2\sqrt{2}G_{F}y\left(\frac{{\mathbb{E}}_{\ell}(z(x))+{\mathbb{P}}_{\ell}(z(x))}{m_{W}^{2}}+\frac{4}{3}\frac{{\mathbb{E}}_{\nu}(x,z(x))}{m_{W}^{2}\cos^{2}{\theta_{W}}}\right),$ (arXiv:2012.02726) [src: Eq. 4], with $\mathbb{M}$ the squared-mass-difference matrix, $U=R(\theta_{23})R(\theta_{13})R(\theta_{12})$ [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 4], and the charged-lepton energy and pressure integrals of Eqs. 5–6. The $\mathbb{E}_{\ell}+\mathbb{P}_{\ell}$ form "is in fact the more general result", the usual $(4/3)\,\mathbb{E}_{\ell}$ applying "strictly only when the charged leptons are ultra-relativistic"; using the latter "incurs an error no larger than $10^{-5}$" in $N_{\rm eff}^{\rm SM}$ [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 6].

Only two-body processes are kept: neutrino–electron processes "anyway distributed in the initial and final states" and neutrino–neutrino scattering; the 9D momentum integrals "can be systematically reduced to 2D integrals" at tree level (Eqs. 7–8) [src: Sect. 2.2, text before Eq. 7]. With $\bar{\rho}_{\nu}={\rm Tr}[\mathbb{E}_{\nu}]$ (arXiv:2012.02726) [src: Sect. 2.2, inline, text after Eq. 8], "equations (2) to (8) form a closed system", solved with FortEPiaNO [src: Sect. 2.2, last paragraph]. <!-- num: not a value -->

### Step 4 — the collision integrals
<!-- key: step-4-the-collision-integrals -->

Under homogeneity, isotropy and CP symmetry, and following the reduction procedure of its Ref. 3 (not read), the neutrino–electron term splits into scattering and annihilation, $\mathcal{I}_{\nu e}[\varrho(x,y)]=\frac{G_{F}^{2}}{(2\pi)^{3}y^{2}}\left\{I^{\rm sc}_{\nu e}[\varrho(x,y)]+I^{\rm ann}_{\nu e}[\varrho(x,y)]\right\}\,,$ (arXiv:2012.02726) [src: Eq. 14], whose 2D integrands (Eqs. 15–16) carry the kernels $\Pi$ of Eq. 17, built from the analytic integrals $D_1$, $D_2$, $D_3$ of Eq. 18, multiplying phase-space matrices that hold the quantum statistics: $F_{\rm sc}^{ab}\equiv f_{e}^{(4)}(\mathbb{1}-f_{e}^{(2)})G^{a}\varrho^{(3)}G^{b}(\mathbb{1}-\varrho^{(1)})-f_{e}^{(2)}(\mathbb{1}-f_{e}^{(4)})\varrho^{(1)}G^{b}(\mathbb{1}-\varrho^{(3)})G^{a}+{\rm h.c.}$ and $F_{\rm ann}^{ab}\equiv f_{e}^{(3)}f_{e}^{(4)}G^{a}(\mathbb{1}-\varrho^{(2)})G^{b}(\mathbb{1}-\varrho^{(1)})-(\mathbb{1}-f_{e}^{(3)})(\mathbb{1}-f_{e}^{(4)})G^{a}\varrho^{(2)}G^{b}\varrho^{(1)}+{\rm h.c.},$ (arXiv:2012.02726) [src: Eq. 19]. The coupling matrices are $G^{L}=\text{diag}(g_{L},\tilde{g}_{L},\tilde{g}_{L}),$ and $G^{R}=\text{diag}(g_{R},g_{R},g_{R})$ (arXiv:2012.02726) [src: Eq. 20]; the calculation uses the values "at zero-momentum transfer" of its Table 1 rather than the tree-level expressions [src: App. A, text after Eq. 20]. <!-- num: not a value -->

The neutrino–neutrino term has the same structure, $\mathcal{I}_{\nu\nu}[\varrho(x,y)]=\frac{G_{F}^{2}}{(2\pi)^{3}y^{2}}\left\{I^{\rm sc}_{\nu\nu}[\varrho(x,y)]+I^{\rm pair}_{\nu\nu}[\varrho(x,y)]\right\}\,,$ (arXiv:2012.02726) [src: Eq. 21], with the same kernels and phase-space matrices containing traces over flavour (Eqs. 22–24). The paper is "one of the first studies in which the neutrino–neutrino scattering integral" is solved "as is" without approximations [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 8].

### Step 5 — finite-temperature QED corrections to the weak rates
<!-- key: step-5-finite-temperature-qed-corrections-to -->

Four "qualitatively different" corrections exist: (a) modified dispersion relation, (b) vertex corrections, (c) real emission or absorption, (d) closed fermion loops (arXiv:2012.02726) [src: Sect. 3.1.2, Fig. 2]. Type (a) is implemented by "replacing all occurrences of $m_{e}^{2}$" in the collision integral "with its thermal counterpart $m_{e}^{2}+\delta m_{e}^{2}(p,T)$", the one-loop electron self-energy, dropping its momentum-dependent piece, "shown to contribute less than 10%" of the total [src: Sect. 3.1.2]; the authors "stress however that the procedure is, strictly speaking, inconsistent" [src: Sect. 3.1.2]. Types (b)–(d) "cannot be dealt with in a similar fashion" and were left for later [src: Sect. 3.1.2]. A thermal mass may shift a propagator pole, but "it is not a valid procedure" to obtain bulk thermodynamic corrections by inserting it into ideal-gas expressions [src: Sect. 3.1.2, footnote 5]; that is the equation-of-state route of Step 2.

### Step 6 — the result and what moved it
<!-- key: step-6-the-result-and-what-moved-it -->

Relative to the 2016 calculation, "the main modification" is the ${\cal O}(e^3)$ equation-of-state correction, a change of δN_eff ∼ −0.001 "under the Benchmark A settings", "bringing the 2016 value of 3.045 [20] down to 3.0440" (arXiv:2012.02726) [src: Sect. 5.1]; the ${\cal O}(e^2)$ logarithmic term (δN_eff ∼ −4×10⁻⁵) and the type-(a) rate corrections (|δN_eff| ≲ 10⁻⁴) are "strictly not necessary to achieve a prediction of $N_{\rm eff}^{\rm SM}$ to four-digit significance" [src: Sect. 5.1]. The full breakdown and the benchmark are in `results.md#the-standard-model-value-of-n-rm-eff`.

### Step 7 — why 3.043 differs from 3.0440
<!-- key: step-7-why-3043-differs-from-30440 -->

**The 3.043 calculation.** Because the phase space of the NLO process $e^{+}e^{-}\to\nu\bar{\nu}\gamma$ "is 15 dimensional", Cielo et al. do not solve the momentum-dependent equation but integrated Boltzmann equations with Fermi–Dirac neutrinos at a dynamical temperature (arXiv:2306.05460) [src: Sect. III]: $\frac{dT_{\nu_{\alpha}}}{dt}=-H\,T_{\nu_{\alpha}}+\frac{\delta\rho_{\nu_{\alpha}}}{\delta t}\bigg/\frac{\partial\rho_{\nu_{\alpha}}}{\partial T_{\nu_{\alpha}}}\,\,,$ (arXiv:2306.05460) [src: Eq. 4], with the photon temperature of Eq. 5 and the energy-transfer rate of Eq. 6, where for $m_e=0$ the annihilation term is multiplied by $(1+\xi^{\rm NLO})$ [src: Eq. 8], $\xi^{\rm NLO}$ being "the NLO QED correction for the $e^{+}e^{-}\to\nu\bar{\nu}$ energy transfer rate" [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 8]. The result is $N_{\rm eff}^{\rm NLO}-N_{\rm eff}^{\rm LO}=-0.0007\,.$ (arXiv:2306.05460) [src: Eq. 9], combined with the oscillation correction "of the same size but opposite sign" into $N_{\rm eff}^{\rm SM}=3.0432(2)\,,$ (arXiv:2306.05460) [src: Eq. 10], the uncertainty being "three factors of 0.0001 added in quadrature", one of them added because radiative corrections to $e\nu\to e\nu$ were not included [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 10]. Pauli blocking is not included in the NLO term; the uncertainty this generates "should be at most $10^{-5}$ for $N_{\rm eff}$ and is therefore negligible" [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 10].

**The objections.** Drewes et al. have "reservations about this result": they state that Cielo et al. "took the finite-temperature rate corrections for e⁺e⁻ → ν_α ν̄_α" from a stellar-plasma calculation and appealed to detailed balance, "mapping rate corrections from an incompatible energy regime ( O(1) keV ≪ m_e in a stellar plasma versus O(1) MeV > m_e in the early universe)", and neglected (i) the closed-fermion-loop diagram (d), (ii) corrections to elastic scattering such as ν_α e → ν_α e, and (iii) Pauli blocking of the final-state neutrinos; diagram (d) "contains a t-channel enhancement, which should, at least naïvely, dominate" (arXiv:2402.18481) [src: Sect. 1]. Jackson & Laine's first-principles imaginary-time calculation, with $m_e=0$ and equilibrium distributions, gives corrections to the damping rate "of order 0.2 to 0.3%", as summarised by Drewes et al. (arXiv:2402.18481) [src: Sect. 1].

**The recomputation.** Drewes et al. compute diagram (d) with the electron mass and find, at the mean momentum p = 3.15T [src: Sect. 3.2] for T ∼ 1 → 3 MeV, corrections of "−0.2 → +0.1%" to the $\nu_e$ rate and "−0.0005 → +0.0002%" for $\nu_{\mu,\tau}$, a flavour dependence "not observed in" Cielo et al. (arXiv:2402.18481) [src: Sect. 5]. Propagated to $N_{\rm eff}$ by entropy conservation and by the Boltzmann equation in the damping approximation, δN_eff/N_eff^LO ≃ (−0.5 → −1.1)×10⁻⁵: "we can confirm the sign" of Cielo et al., but "even our most 'optimistic' estimate is a factor of 30 smaller than their claimed correction" [src: Sect. 5]. Setting $m_e=0$ in the rate corrections "can have an ${\cal O}(1)$ impact" on $\delta N_{\rm eff}/N_{\rm eff}^{\rm LO}$ because those corrections diverge relative to the LO rates at $T\lesssim 3m_{e}$, where decoupling is not finished [src: Sect. 5]. Their conclusion: their results "strongly suggest" that the benchmark "is correct within the quoted uncertainties", while "a full numerical solution of the QKEs" with all NLO diagrams "would be highly desirable" [src: Sect. 5].

`[der]` The two numbers therefore differ by the size assigned to the NLO rate correction, not by the equation of state or the oscillation treatment: Cielo et al. add an annihilation-rate correction to a momentum-averaged solution, and Drewes et al. find the diagram-(d) correction at least thirty times smaller (their most optimistic estimate), inside the nominal uncertainty of the benchmark.

The origin of that correction is read two ways. Drewes et al. say Cielo et al. took it from a calculation "computed originally in the context of energy loss in a stellar plasma", an "incompatible energy regime" (arXiv:2402.18481) [src: Sect. 1]; Cielo et al. say they "extended our previous calculation to the relevant region, up to plasma temperatures T∼20 MeV" (arXiv:2306.05460) [src: Sect. II].

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **Entropy conservation with instantaneous decoupling** (arXiv:1911.04504, Sect. 2.1): analytic for each equation-of-state correction, Eqs. 9 and 11 above, but "poorly defined" once decoupling is not instantaneous [src: Sect. 2.2]. Drewes et al. use it as one of their propagation methods (arXiv:2402.18481) [src: Sect. 4.1]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Full QKE on a momentum grid** (FortEPiaNO, arXiv:2012.02726): the reference. Approximations of the weak terms and their cost: setting the diagonal neutrino–neutrino integral to zero "generally underestimates" $N_{\rm eff}^{\rm SM}$ "by about $\delta N_{\rm eff}\simeq 4\times 10^{-4}$"; the "diagonal ϱ" approximation gives "$|\delta N_{\rm eff}|\simeq 2\times 10^{-4}$" and "should hence be avoided when neutrino oscillations are switched on"; the off-diagonal damping approximation (its Eq. 9) gives "$|\delta N_{\rm eff}|\lesssim 10^{-4}$", "may be a reasonable compromise if computing resources need to be diverted elsewhere" (arXiv:2012.02726) [src: Sect. 5.3.1].
- **Adiabatic Transfer of Averaged Oscillations (ATAO)** (Froustey, Pitrou & Volpe, arXiv:2008.01074): keeps only the diagonal of the density matrix in the matter basis, so the state vector has length $NN_{\nu}$ instead of $NN_{\nu}^{2}$ (arXiv:2008.01074) [src: Sect. 4.1, "Momentum grid"]; it gains "at least a factor 5 in time", and "typically a factor N" when "using both a method for the Jacobian and the ATAO approximation" [src: Sect. 4.1, "Numerical optimization via Jacobian computation"]; frozen-out temperatures and $N_{\rm eff}$ differ from the full QKE "by $10^{-6}$" [src: Sect. 4.3]. The same section explains why oscillations raise $N_{\rm eff}$: mixing depopulates $\nu_e$ and "frees some phase space for the reactions which create $\nu_{e}$", which dominate, "hence the larger value" [src: Sect. 4.3].
- **Momentum-averaged equations** (Escudero, Jackson, Laine & Sandner 2025, arXiv:2511.04747): each subsystem obeys $\frac{{\rm d}\rho_{i}}{{\rm d}t}+3H(\rho_{i}+p_{i})\;=\;Q_{i}\;,\quad\sum_{i}Q_{i}=0\;,\quad i\in\{\mathrm{em},\nu_{e},\nu_{\mu},\nu_{\tau}\}\;,$ (arXiv:2511.04747) [src: Eq. 2.1] and $\frac{{\rm d}n_{i}}{{\rm d}t}+3Hn_{i}\;=\;J_{i}\;,\quad i\;\in\{\nu_{e},\nu_{\mu},\nu_{\tau}\}\;,$ (arXiv:2511.04747) [src: Eq. 2.2], solved for an effective temperature and chemical potential per flavour (Eqs. 2.3a–b). The solution takes $t_{\rm CPU}\lesssim 1$ s, and the "differences of all cosmological parameters" from FortEPiaNO are "less than 0.05% in the Standard Model", "below 0.03%" with chemical potentials, and "0.01%" with chemical potentials if oscillations are omitted on both sides [src: Sect. 6]; the remaining spectral distortions relative to a Fermi–Dirac form with temperature and chemical potential are "$\lesssim 0.03\%$" [src: Sect. 6]. Its $N_{\rm eff}$ "agrees very well with the value $N_{\rm eff}\simeq 3.044$" of the momentum-dependent calculations (arXiv:2511.04747) [src: Sect. 6]. Cielo et al. use Fermi–Dirac neutrinos "with a temperature $T_{\nu}$" only, without a chemical potential (arXiv:2306.05460) [src: Sect. III].
- **Higher-order equation of state**: ${\cal O}(e^4)$ and ${\cal O}(e^5)$ terms "shift $N_{\rm eff}$ by $\lesssim 2\times 10^{-5}$" relative to the ${\cal O}(e^3)$ result (arXiv:2511.04747) [src: Sect. 6].

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Thermal masses in ideal-gas formulae.** Inserting the thermal mass into ideal-gas expressions "without Taylor-expanding" is "tantamount to omitting the crucial 1/2 factor" before the ${\cal O}(e^2)$ pressure term and gives a correction "a factor of two larger"; the authors "conclude" that the claimed $N_{\rm eff}=3.052$ "is incorrect", an error that "most probably stemmed from a misinterpretation" of the quasiparticle picture (arXiv:1911.04504) [src: Sect. 4.4.2]. Check: compute bulk quantities from $\ln Z$ (Eqs. 22–28), not from quasiparticle masses. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Ultra-relativistic electrons at decoupling.** At $T_d$ = 1.3453 MeV the log-independent ${\cal O}(e^2)$ term is 0.010121 (arXiv:1911.04504) [src: Eq. 33]; the "oft-quoted" 0.010594 is the same correction "in the limit $T_{d}/m_{e}\to\infty$" [src: Sect. 4.1.1, text after Eq. 33].
- **Setting the diagonal entries of the neutrino–neutrino collision integral to zero, or evaluating it with the diagonal of ϱ only**, with oscillations on: the first "generally underestimates" $N_{\rm eff}^{\rm SM}$ "by about" $4\times 10^{-4}$, the second shifts it by about $2\times 10^{-4}$ (arXiv:2012.02726) [src: Sect. 5.3.1], at least as large as the nominal ±0.0002 of the benchmark [src: abstract].
- **Massless electrons in rate corrections.** Setting $m_e=0$ "can have an ${\cal O}(1)$ impact" on the NLO $\delta N_{\rm eff}/N_{\rm eff}^{\rm LO}$ (arXiv:2402.18481) [src: Sect. 5].
- **Rates from another regime.** Drewes et al. object to stellar-plasma corrections at keV temperatures carried to MeV temperatures (arXiv:2402.18481) [src: Sect. 1]; Cielo et al. say they extended their calculation up to T ∼ 20 MeV (arXiv:2306.05460) [src: Sect. II].
- **Tables that disagree in the last digit.** Table 1 of arXiv:2402.18481, "adapted from" Bennett et al. 2020, prints the non-instantaneous decoupling and spectral distortion term as "-0.006" (arXiv:2402.18481) [src: Table 1], where Table 5 of arXiv:2012.02726 prints "-0.005" (arXiv:2012.02726) [src: Table 5]. Quote Table 5 of the original.
- **Momentum-averaged solutions** against FortEPiaNO, in the comparison of Fig. 3 of arXiv:2511.04747: "differences of up to 0.1%" in the distribution function "in the infrared domain $q_{\nu}/T_{\nu}\lesssim 2.0$", a part "phase-space suppressed in the integrated number and energy densities" [src: Sect. 6; Fig. 3 caption].
