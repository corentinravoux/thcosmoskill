## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- Mogensen, Hannestad & Tram (arXiv:2606.03395, v1) work in synchronous gauge with $\mathrm{d}s^{2}=a^{2}(\tau)\left(-\mathrm{d}\tau^{2}+(\delta_{ij}+h_{ij})\,\mathrm{d}x^{i}\,\mathrm{d}x^{j}\right).$ (arXiv:2606.03395) [src: Eq. 3], "utilising the notation of" Ma & Bertschinger, comoving momentum $\mathbf{q}\equiv a\mathbf{p}$ and energy $\epsilon\equiv aE$ [src: Sect. 2.1, inline, text after Eq. 3], and from Sect. 2.4 on absorb $T_{\nu,0}$ into q [src: Sect. 2.4]. They impose Maxwell–Boltzmann statistics, "1 − f_ν ∼ 1 and 1 + f_φ ∼ 1" [src: Sect. 2.1, text before Eq. 10]. N is the normalisation of the Maxwell–Boltzmann background [src: Eq. 36].
- Oldengott et al. 2017 (arXiv:1706.02123, v2) use the synchronous-gauge notation of their Ref. 24 with the integrated moments ${\cal F}_{\nu\ell}$ and write the opacity rate $\dot{\tau}_{\nu}\equiv-aG_{\rm eff}^{2}T_{\nu}^{5}$ (arXiv:1706.02123) [src: Sect. 2.2, inline, text before Eq. 8]; Mogensen et al. call the same quantity $\dot{\kappa}_{\nu}$ (Eq. 13 below). In the HTML rendering of arXiv:1706.02123 the relaxation hierarchy and the $\alpha_\ell$ values are Eqs. 8 and 10; Mogensen et al. cite them as "equation (2.7)" and "equation 2.9" of the journal numbering (arXiv:2606.03395) [src: Sect. 3.1; Sect. 5]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Barenboim et al. (arXiv:2011.01502, v2) and Chen et al. (arXiv:2203.09075, v3) use the synchronous gauge, conformal time τ, the Legendre convention of their Ref. 37 (arXiv:2011.01502) [src: Eq. 26], the rest-frame decay rate $\Gamma^0_{\rm dec}$ with $\tau_0$ its inverse, and the integrated anisotropic-stress contrast ${\cal F}_{2}$ related to the stress by $\Pi_{\nu\phi}=(\bar{\rho}_{\nu\phi}+\bar{P}_{\nu\phi}){\cal F}_{2}/2$ (arXiv:2011.01502) [src: Sect. 6.1.1, footnote 7]. Their phase-space density carries $g_i/(2\pi)^3$ explicitly, unlike their Ref. 37 [src: Sect. 6.1.1, footnote 6].
- `[der]` The letter τ is conformal time in all four papers, and also the opacity in Oldengott et al.; $\alpha_\ell$ denotes the relaxation coefficients for scattering in arXiv:1706.02123 and arXiv:2606.03395, and different coefficients for decay in arXiv:2203.09075.

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Part A — self-interactions through a massive scalar
<!-- key: part-a-self-interactions-through-a-massive -->

#### Step A1 — the collision term in the first-order Boltzmann equation
<!-- key: step-a1-the-collision-term-in-the-first-order -->

With $f_{i}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{q},\tau)=\bar{f}_{i}(q,\tau)\left[1+\Psi_{i}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{q},\tau)\right].$ (arXiv:2606.03395) [src: Eq. 4] and an interacting background obeying its own homogeneous collision equation (Eq. 5), the linearised Boltzmann equation in Fourier space is $\frac{1}{\bar{f}_{i}}\left(\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial\tau}\right)^{(1)}_{\mathrm{coll}}=\dot{\Psi}_{i}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q},\tau)+\mathrm{i}\frac{qk}{\epsilon}(\hat{k}\cdot\hat{q})\Psi_{i}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q},\tau)+\frac{\partial\ln\bar{f}_{i}(q,\tau)}{\partial\ln q}\left(\dot{\eta}-(\hat{k}\cdot\hat{q})^{2}\frac{\dot{h}+6\dot{\eta}}{2}\right),$ (arXiv:2606.03395) [src: Eq. 6], the collision term for a CP-invariant two-body process being the full integral of Eq. 8 over the other three momenta with quantum-statistical factors. With a time-dependent background (decays), Barenboim et al. carry an extra term $\dot{\bar f}_i/\bar f_i$, which they absorb into the collision operator $\mathcal{C}^{(1)}_{\ell}[\Psi_{i}(q_{i})]\equiv\frac{1}{\bar{f}_{i}}\left(\frac{{\rm d}f_{i}}{{\rm d}\tau}\right)_{C,\ell}^{(1)}(q_{i})-\frac{\dot{\bar{f}}_{i}}{\bar{f}_{i}}\Psi_{i,\ell}(q_{i})$ (arXiv:2011.01502) [src: Eq. 28]; their Eq. 25 is "a generalisation of equation (40) of reference [37] to include a time-dependent background distribution" [src: Sect. 4.3, text after Eq. 25].

#### Step A2 — the contact (heavy-mediator) limit
<!-- key: step-a2-the-contact-heavy-mediator-limit -->

Mogensen et al. "focus on the massive mediator case": a heavy mediator decays or annihilates "way before any times of interest", leaving $\nu\phi\rightarrow\nu\phi$ and $\phi\phi\rightarrow\nu\nu$ irrelevant, and $\nu\nu\rightarrow\phi\phi$ "kinematically suppressed"; with flavour-diagonal universal couplings only $\nu\nu\rightarrow\nu\nu$ remains, through s, t and u channels (arXiv:2606.03395) [src: Sect. 2.2]. The rate per particle is then $\Gamma\sim\mathfrak{g}^{4}T_{\nu}^{5}/m_{\phi}^{4}\equiv G_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}T_{\nu}^{5}.$ (arXiv:2606.03395) [src: Eq. 12], with "the same" temperature dependence as weak interactions, "such that the self-interactions only serve to delay the epoch of neutrino decoupling" [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 12]. The assumption $G_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}\gg G_{F}^{2}$ "is a crucial assumption for this work", defining the opacity rate $\dot{\kappa}_{\nu}\equiv-aG_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}T_{\nu}^{5}.$ (arXiv:2606.03395) [src: Eq. 13]. <!-- num: not a value -->

The abstract of arXiv:2606.03395 describes the process as "mediated by a light scalar" [src: abstract], while its body treats "the massive mediator case" [src: Sect. 2.2] and concludes for "the exchange of a very massive scalar or pseudo-scalar" [src: Sect. 6]. `[der]` The body, not the abstract, states the regime of the calculation. `formalism.md#interacting-and-decaying-neutrinos` quotes the abstract's wording.

#### Step A3 — the exact momentum-dependent hierarchy
<!-- key: step-a3-the-exact-momentum-dependent-hierarchy -->

In this limit every multipole acquires a loss term proportional to $q\Psi_\ell(q)$ and a gain term that is an integral over $q'$ with the kernel $K_\ell(q,q')$, the ℓth Legendre moment of the angular kernel, for example for $\ell>2$ $\dot{\Psi}_{\ell>2}(q)=\frac{k}{2\ell+1}\left[\ell\Psi_{\ell-1}(q)-(\ell+1)\Psi_{\ell+1}(q)\right]-\frac{80}{3}\frac{\mathrm{N}T_{\nu,0}^{5}G_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}}{a^{4}(2\pi)^{3}}q\Psi_{\ell}(q)+\frac{4\mathrm{N}T_{\nu,0}^{5}G_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}}{a^{4}(2\pi)^{3}}\int\frac{q^{\prime}\bar{f}(q^{\prime})}{q\bar{f}(q)}K_{\ell}(q,q^{\prime})\Psi_{\ell}(q^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}q^{\prime},$ (arXiv:2606.03395) [src: Eq. 26] with $K_{\ell}(q,q^{\prime})=\int_{-1}^{1}K(q,q^{\prime},\mu)P_{\ell}(\mu)\,\mathrm{d}\mu.$ (arXiv:2606.03395) [src: Eq. 27]. The ℓ = 0, 1, 2 equations (Eqs. 23–25) carry, besides the metric terms, extra pieces proportional to q²q′²e^{−q} that the paper collects in the function $S_\ell$ of Eq. 31 [src: Sect. 3.1]. As printed, the gain term of Eq. 23 carries $T_{\mu,0}^{5}$ where the other three equations carry $T_{\nu,0}^{5}$ [src: Eqs. 23–26]. Oldengott et al. call this the "exact" hierarchy, whose momentum dependence arises "from the non-negligible energy transfer that accompanies a neutrino-neutrino scattering event" and does not appear "in the Thomson scattering limit" (arXiv:1706.02123) [src: Sect. 2.2]. <!-- num: not a value -->

#### Step A4 — momentum averaging and the definition of $\alpha_\ell$
<!-- key: step-a4-momentum-averaging-and-the-definition -->

Without metric terms each moment obeys $\dot{\Psi}_{\ell}(q)=\frac{k}{2\ell+1}\left[\ell\Psi_{\ell-1}(q)-(\ell+1)\Psi_{\ell+1}(q)\right]+\mathcal{C}[\Psi_{\ell}](q),$ (arXiv:2606.03395) [src: Eq. 29], with $\mathcal{C}[\Psi_{\ell}](q)=\frac{\mathrm{N}T_{\nu,0}^{5}G_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}}{a^{4}(2\pi)^{3}}\left[4\int\frac{q^{\prime}\bar{f}(q^{\prime})}{q\bar{f}(q)}\left[K_{\ell}(q,q^{\prime})-S_{\ell}(q,q^{\prime})\right]\Psi_{\ell}(q^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}q^{\prime}-\frac{80}{3}q\Psi_{\ell}(q)\right]\,,$ (arXiv:2606.03395) [src: Eq. 30]. Multiplying by the energy weight $q\bar f q^2$, integrating, and dividing by the same weight gives the momentum-averaged moments, $\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}=\frac{k}{2\ell+1}\left[\ell\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}-(\ell+1)\mathcal{F}_{\ell+1}\right].$ (arXiv:2606.03395) [src: Eq. 34] without collisions; the time derivative can be taken out of the integral because "the background distribution is time-independent under the assumption of Maxwell-Boltzmann statistics" [src: Sect. 3.1, text before Eq. 32].

Matching to the relaxation form used in CLASS, $\dot{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}=\frac{k}{2\ell+1}\left[\ell\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}-(\ell+1)\mathcal{F}_{\ell+1}\right]+\alpha_{\ell}\dot{\kappa}_{\nu}\mathcal{F}_{\ell},$ (arXiv:2606.03395) [src: Eq. 37], defines the coefficients: $\alpha_{\ell}\dot{\kappa}_{\nu}\mathcal{F}_{\ell}=\frac{1}{6\mathrm{N}}\int q\bar{f}(q)q^{2}\mathcal{C}[\Psi_{\ell}](q)\,\mathrm{d}q.$ (arXiv:2606.03395) [src: Eq. 38]. This is only a definition until the q-dependence of $\Psi_\ell$ is fixed. The "separable ansatz" puts all of it in the background, $\Psi_{\ell}(q)\sim-\frac{1}{4}\frac{\mathrm{d}\ln\bar{f}(q)}{\mathrm{d}\ln q}\mathcal{F}_{\ell}.$ (arXiv:2606.03395) [src: Eq. 39], so that for the Maxwell–Boltzmann background $\Psi_{\ell}(q)\sim\frac{1}{4}q\mathcal{F}_{\ell}.$ (arXiv:2606.03395) [src: Eq. 41]. Inserting this into Eq. 38 gives, as one four-row display, $\alpha_{\ell}=\frac{1}{6\mathrm{N}}\frac{-1}{aG_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}T_{\nu}^{5}\mathcal{F}_{\ell}}\int q\bar{f}(q)q^{2}\frac{\mathrm{N}T_{\nu,0}^{5}G_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}}{a^{4}(2\pi)^{3}}\mathcal{F}_{\ell}\left(\int\frac{q^{\prime 2}\bar{f}(q^{\prime})}{q\bar{f}(q)}\left[K_{\ell}(q,q^{\prime})-S_{\ell}(q,q^{\prime})\right]\,\mathrm{d}q^{\prime}-\frac{20}{3}q^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}q\,,=\frac{1}{6(2\pi)^{3}}\int\left(\frac{20}{3}q^{5}\bar{f}(q)-\int q^{2}q^{\prime 2}\bar{f}(q^{\prime})\left[K_{\ell}(q,q^{\prime})-S_{\ell}(q,q^{\prime})\right]\,\mathrm{d}q^{\prime}\right)\mathrm{d}q\,,=\frac{\mathrm{N}}{6(2\pi)^{3}}\int\left(\frac{20}{3}q^{5}\mathrm{e}^{-q}-\int q^{2}q^{\prime 2}\mathrm{e}^{-q^{\prime}}K(q,q^{\prime},\mu)P_{\ell}(\mu)\,\mathrm{d}\mu\,\mathrm{d}q^{\prime}+\int q^{2}q^{\prime 2}\mathrm{e}^{-q^{\prime}}S_{\ell}(q,q^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}q^{\prime}\right)\mathrm{d}q\,,=\frac{\mathrm{N}}{6(2\pi)^{3}}(800-I_{\ell}+s_{\ell}),$ (arXiv:2606.03395) [src: Eq. 43], with $s_{0}=640\,,\qquad s_{1}=-320\,,\qquad s_{2}=64\,,\qquad s_{\ell>2}=0.$ (arXiv:2606.03395) [src: Eq. 44] and $I_\ell$ the remaining double integral of the kernel.

The ansatz was introduced earlier: in Oldengott et al., the damping term $\alpha_\ell\dot\tau_\nu{\cal F}_{\nu\ell}$ added to the hierarchy "at orders $\ell\geq 2$" is $\dot{\cal F}_{\nu 2}=\frac{8}{15}\theta_{\nu}-\frac{3}{5}k{\cal F}_{\nu 3}+\frac{4}{15}\dot{h}+\frac{8}{5}\dot{\tilde{\eta}}+\alpha_{2}\dot{\tau}_{\nu}{\cal F}_{\nu 2}\ ,$ and $\dot{\cal F}_{\nu\ell}=\frac{k}{2\ell+1}\left[\ell{\cal F}_{\nu(\ell-1)}-(\ell+1){\cal F}_{\nu(\ell+1)}\right]+\alpha_{\ell}\dot{\tau}_{\nu}{\cal F}_{\nu\ell}\ ,\quad\ell\geq 3\ ,$ (arXiv:1706.02123) [src: Eq. 8]; "The monopole and dipole equations remain unaltered on account of energy and momentum conservation" [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 8]. It is equivalent to a relaxation-time approximation ${\cal C}[f]\approx-\frac{{\cal F}}{\tau_{\text{rel}}},$ (arXiv:1706.02123) [src: Eq. 11] with a momentum-independent relaxation rate, which "is, however, also known to be an incomplete description of the dissipative dynamics of generic systems of particles" [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 11].

#### Step A5 — evaluating $\alpha_\ell$: numerical against exact
<!-- key: step-a5-evaluating-alpha-ell-numerical-against -->

Oldengott et al. computed, "For a flavour-independent and diagonal interaction described by the scalar part of" their Lagrangian, $\alpha_{2}=0.40,\alpha_{3}=0.43,\alpha_{4}=0.46,\alpha_{5}=0.47,\alpha_{\ell\geq 6}=0.48.$ (arXiv:1706.02123) [src: Eq. 10], "model-dependent coefficients of order unity" [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 8].

Mogensen et al. integrate the kernel analytically: the direct integration of Eq. 28 "turns out to be equally intractable with direct methods" (arXiv:2606.03395) [src: Sect. 2.4, text after Eq. 28], so they separate the angular problem, carried by three families of rational weights, from the momentum problem, reduced to base integrals of the form "$r_{k}+s_{k}\pi^{2}$" and rational corrections; the result is "a finite algebraic expression" (their Eq. 79) evaluated in exact rational arithmetic, "because the final double-precision value is often obtained after a large cancellation between the two exact pieces" [src: Sect. 6; App. D]. Numerically they find the large-ℓ behaviour $I_{\ell}\sim\frac{5529600}{(\ell+1/2)^{6}}-\frac{656640000}{(\ell+1/2)^{8}}+\frac{64159257600}{(\ell+1/2)^{10}}+\cdots.$ (arXiv:2606.03395) [src: Eq. 128], and give a constrained rational fit in the inverse square of ℓ + 1/2 (Eqs. 129–131) whose error "is below $10^{-6}$ for all $\ell\geq 0$" [src: App. D, text after Eq. 131].

The correction is small: "We do not expect to see any observable effects from the small changes in $\alpha_{\ell}$"; with the exact values in CLASS (fluid approximation off) the effect is "at most one per mille relative change in $C_{\ell}^{\text{TT}}$", "far from observable" and "almost fully correlated with the effective coupling strength $G_{\text{eff}}$" (arXiv:2606.03395) [src: Sect. 5]; the differences between the two sets of $\alpha_\ell$ are described as "the few percent differences" [src: Sect. 5].

#### Step A6 — beyond the contact limit, and the light-mediator side
<!-- key: step-a6-beyond-the-contact-limit-and-the-light -->

Pérez-Castro et al. compute coefficients $\beta_\ell$ as functions of μ_φ = m_φ/T_ν, without imposing the heavy-mediator limit (arXiv:2602.12477) [src: Sect. 4.2, Fig. 6 caption]. An s-channel resonance appears, "as we preliminarily scanned", "roughly in the region given by" μ_φ ∼ 2×10⁻³ − 5×10¹ "for effectively massless neutrinos", which they subtract and leave to future work [src: Sect. 4.2]. For Dirac-like neutrinos "there is a smooth transition" from the heavy-mediator limit at low temperature to a light-mediator regime where $\beta_\ell$ "is effectively a constant at high temperatures", and massive neutrinos "are indistinguishable from massless ones" in these coefficients [src: Sect. 4.2].

For a light mediator ($m_{\varphi}\lesssim 10^{-3}$ eV) earlier work used the relaxation form $\left.\frac{df_{\nu_{i}}}{d\tau}\right|_{\text{coll}}=-a\frac{f_{\nu_{i}}-f_{\nu_{i}}^{(0)}}{t_{c}}=-a\frac{f_{\nu_{i}}^{(0)}\Theta_{\nu_{i}}}{t_{c}}\,.$ (arXiv:2602.12477) [src: Eq. 81], "only valid for $\ell\geq 2$", with the rate $\Gamma_{\nu\nu\to\nu\nu}=\frac{{\rm g}_{s}}{2\pi^{2}}\frac{3}{2}\zeta(3)g_{\rm eff}^{4}T_{\nu}\,,$ (arXiv:2602.12477) [src: Eq. 82], where $g_{\rm eff}=\xi^{1/4}g$ and ξ "accounts for the lack of knowledge about the collision computation and/or thermal effects" [src: Sect. 5, text after Eq. 82]. Their calculation fixes it multipole by multipole, $\xi_{\ell}\equiv\frac{\pi^{2}}{12\zeta(3)}\beta_{\ell}\,.$ (arXiv:2602.12477) [src: Eq. 84], with $\xi_2=3.454\times 10^{-3}$ (Majorana) and $1.728\times 10^{-3}$ (Dirac-like), saturating "for $\ell\geq 20$" [src: Table 1; Sect. 5], and $\xi_{\ell}$(Majorana) ≈ 2 $\xi_{\ell}$(Dirac-like) "due to s-channel suppression" [src: Sect. 5].

### Part B — invisible decay $\nu_H\to\nu_l+\phi$
<!-- key: part-b-invisible-decay-nuh-to-nul-phi -->

#### Step B1 — the old transport rate and its heuristic
<!-- key: step-b1-the-old-transport-rate-and-its -->

The first CMB constraint required the isotropisation ("transport") rate to stay below the Hubble rate at recombination, with $\Gamma_{\mathrm{T}}\simeq\Gamma_{\rm dec}^{0}\left(\frac{m_{\nu H}}{E_{\nu H}}\right)^{3}.$ (arXiv:2011.01502) [src: Eq. 5]: one power of the inverse boost for time dilation, two more because decay products of an ultra-relativistic parent are nearly collinear, so that "some $\sim(E_{\nu H}/m_{\nu H})^{2}$ number of decay and inverse decay events are required" to turn a momentum [src: Sect. 3.1.1, text after Eq. 5]. Later analyses switched free streaming off instantaneously at a fitted redshift, or added a damping term $\frac{\partial{\mathcal{F}}_{\ell}}{\partial\tau}=\cdots-a\Gamma_{\mathrm{T}}\mathcal{F}_{\ell},$ (arXiv:2011.01502) [src: Eq. 7] for $\ell\geq 2$, which in practice "approximates a step-function" because $\Gamma_{\rm T}/H$ grows as a power of a [src: Sect. 3.1.1, "Refinement 2"]. "The central and most critical element in all of the neutrino lifetime constraints discussed above is the transport rate (5)" [src: Sect. 3.1.2]. These bounds also assume the parent stays ultra-relativistic through the CMB epoch and "apply a priori only to neutrino masses satisfying $m_{\nu H}\ll T^{*}\sim 0.2$ eV" [src: Sect. 3.1.2].

#### Step B2 — the leading order cancels
<!-- key: step-b2-the-leading-order-cancels -->

Barenboim et al. write the full first-order hierarchy for $\nu_H$, $\nu_l$ and φ (Eq. 27) with collision terms such as Eq. 29, and build the effective collision integral of the total anisotropic stress of the combined fluid, Eqs. 45–46, which enters $k^{2}(\phi-\psi)=12\pi a^{2}G(\bar{\rho}+\bar{P})\sigma\equiv 12\pi Ga^{2}\,\Pi\,,$ (arXiv:2011.01502) [src: Eq. 44]. Expanding the Legendre kernels in the small inverse boost gives $P_{2}(x)=3P_{1}(x)-2P_{0}(x)+\mathcal{O}(a^{4}m_{\nu H}^{4}/\epsilon_{i}^{4})\,,$ (arXiv:2011.01502) [src: Eq. 47], so that at this order the quadrupole "resembles an admixture of the monopole and the dipole behaviours", which decays and inverse decays exchange losslessly; "we expect the leading-order behaviour of $F_{i,2}$ to be a lossless inter-species transfer as well" [src: Sect. 6.1.1, text after Eq. 48]. The leading-order effective collision integral (Eq. 49) does scale as the old rate, but solving it with the monopole response measured in their modified CLASS (Eqs. 51–53) shows "virtually no loss of anisotropic stress at the leading-order rate" [src: Sect. 6.1.3]: the question was whether Eq. 49 can be "recast into a relaxation form" $\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}(a^{4}\Pi_{\nu\phi})}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\right)_{C}^{\rm LO}\simeq-a\Gamma_{\rm T}(a^{4}\Pi_{\nu\phi}),$ (arXiv:2011.01502) [src: Eq. 50], and the answer is no.

#### Step B3 — the first non-vanishing order
<!-- key: step-b3-the-first-non-vanishing-order -->

Under three assumptions (equilibrium Maxwell–Boltzmann statistics with $\mu_{\nu H}=\mu_{\nu l}+\mu_{\phi}$; the separable ansatz of their Eq. 54; equal contrasts in all three species, Eq. 55), the exact collision integral of the stress reduces to Eq. 56 (arXiv:2011.01502) [src: Sect. 6.1.3]. Expanded in the inverse boost, $\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}(a^{4}\Pi_{\nu\phi})}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\right)_{C}\simeq-a\tilde{\Gamma}_{{\rm dec}}T_{0}^{-4}\left\{\int\mathrm{d}q_{1}\,q_{1}^{3}\,e^{-\epsilon_{1}/T_{0}}\frac{a^{4}m_{\nu H}^{4}}{\epsilon_{1}^{4}}\right\}(a^{4}\Pi_{\nu\phi})=-a\tilde{\Gamma}_{{\rm dec}}\left(\frac{am_{\nu H}}{T_{0}}\right)^{4}{\mathscr{F}}\left(\frac{am_{\nu H}}{T}\right)\,(a^{4}\Pi_{\nu\phi}),$ (arXiv:2011.01502) [src: Eq. 58], with ${\mathscr{F}}(x)=\frac{1}{2}e^{-x}\left[-1+x+e^{x}x^{2}{\rm Ei}(-x)\right]+\Gamma(0,x),$ (arXiv:2011.01502) [src: Eq. 59], which "evaluates to $\sim 20\to 1$" for "$x\sim 10^{-10}\to 0.1$" [src: Sect. 6.1.3, text after Eq. 59]. As printed, the first row of Eq. 58 has $T_0$ and the argument of 𝓕 in the second row has T. "The leading-order anisotropic stress loss associated with the transport rate" $(am_{\nu H}/\epsilon_{1})^{3}\,\Gamma_{\rm dec}^{0}$ "is exactly vanishing"; the first non-vanishing term is always negative and of relaxation form, $\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}(a^{4}\Pi_{\nu\phi})}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\right)_{C}\sim-a\,\Gamma_{{\rm dec}}^{0}\left(\frac{m_{\nu H}}{E_{\nu H}}\right)^{5}(a^{4}\Pi_{\nu\phi}),$ (arXiv:2011.01502) [src: Eq. 60]. The "free-streaming argument" is "phenomenologically correct", but the rate "needs to be updated" [src: Sect. 6.1.3, text after Eq. 60].

#### Step B4 — massive daughters and the $\alpha_\ell$ of decay
<!-- key: step-b4-massive-daughters-and-the-alpha-ell-of -->

Chen et al. repeat the calculation with the same three approximations, "essentially recast" as "a relaxation time calculation in terms of the system's linear response to a small external perturbation", and with $m_\phi=0$ find, for every multipole, $\left(\frac{{\rm d}{\cal F}}{{\rm d}\tau}\right)_{C,\ell}=-\alpha_{\ell}\,a\,\tilde{\Gamma}_{\rm dec}\left(\frac{am_{\nu H}}{T_{0}}\right)^{4}\times\mathscr{F}\left(\frac{am_{\nu H}}{T_{0}}\right)\,\Phi\left(\frac{m_{\nu l}}{m_{\nu H}}\right)\,{\cal F}_{\ell}.$ (arXiv:2203.09075) [src: Eq. 13], with the phase-space factor $\Phi(y)=\frac{1}{1-y^{2}}\left(1-y^{4}+4\,y^{2}\ln y\right),$ (arXiv:2203.09075) [src: Eq. 17], which tends to "$(1/3)(\Delta m_{\nu}^{2}/m_{\nu H}^{2})^{2}$" for a small mass gap because each decay product carries $\Delta m_{\nu}^{2}/(2m_{\nu H})$ in the parent frame, "not $m_{\nu H}/2$" [src: Sect. 4.1, text after Eq. 17], and the decay coefficients $\alpha_{\ell}=\frac{1}{32}\left(3\ell^{4}+2\ell^{3}-11\ell^{2}+6\ell\right)$ (arXiv:2203.09075) [src: Eq. 18], increasing with ℓ because the processes "become more efficient at wiping out anisotropies on progressively smaller angular scales" [src: Sect. 4.1, text after Eq. 18]. Their $\mathscr{F}$ is printed with the incomplete gamma function only (Eq. 15); `[der]` it coincides with Eq. 59 of arXiv:2011.01502 once the exponential integral of negative argument is written as minus the incomplete gamma function. The separable ansatz "is exact in the ultra-relativistic limit" without collisions, and near equilibrium "appears to capture the evolution" of the $\ell\leq 2$ moments well "in the ultra-relativistic limit" [src: Sect. 4.1, item 2]. Mogensen et al. note that for inverse decays the $\alpha_\ell$ "are much larger due to the small angle involved in the inverse scattering process" (arXiv:2606.03395) [src: Sect. 6].

#### Step B5 — why the heuristic failed
<!-- key: step-b5-why-the-heuristic-failed -->

Chen et al. call the cubic rate "not an ab initio result, but rather one based upon a heuristic random walk argument", which "is in fact incomplete" (arXiv:2203.09075) [src: Sect. 4.1, last paragraph]. Their App. C distinguishes two times. After the "coverage time", a single walker has covered all directions and forgotten its origin, but the inverse-decay centres are themselves anisotropic, biasing the emission direction by an amount "of exactly the same order of magnitude" as the anisotropy to be erased; the walker population after one coverage time is only "a slightly less anisotropic version of the background" [src: App. C.2]. Erasing the anisotropy takes $M\sim 1/\theta^{2}$ swaps of one coverage time $T_2$ each, θ being the misalignment angle of the emission, so the isotropisation time is "$\sim M\times T_{2}$", their $T_4$ of Eq. 58 [src: App. C.2, Fig. 4 caption]. The same picture shows why the single-particle argument fails for the dipole: momentum conservation "always preserves the system's dipole" [src: App. C.2].

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **The $(c^2_{\rm eff}, c^2_{\rm vis})$ parameterisation**: Oldengott et al. state that for an isolated ultra-relativistic system "any choice for" c_eff "other than" c_eff² = 1/3 "would not comply with conservation of momentum", that the parameterisation has "no formal interpretation in terms of particle scattering", and that it does not "reproduce the correct CMB phenomenology due to neutrino scattering" (arXiv:1706.02123) [src: Sect. 2.2]. For a massless scalar, which recouples neutrinos that already free-stream, its tight-coupling limit, vanishing c_vis², fails because the $\ell\geq 2$ multipoles are already populated [src: Sect. 2.2]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Exact momentum-dependent hierarchy against the separable ansatz**: Oldengott et al. implement both on a momentum grid in CLASS (their Sect. 3.1); the comparison of CMB spectra is in their Sect. 3.2 (not read in this run).
- **Relaxation coefficients from numerics or from the analytic kernel**: "few percent differences" in $\alpha_\ell$, at most a per-mille change in $C_\ell^{TT}$ (arXiv:2606.03395) [src: Sect. 5].
- **Contact limit against finite mediator mass**: the $\beta_\ell(\mu_\varphi)$ of arXiv:2602.12477 interpolate between the heavy- and light-mediator regimes for non-resonant cases [src: Sect. 4.2].
- **Flavour structure**: hierarchies for mass states derived from the quantum kinetic equations, and a combined-fluid approximation (arXiv:2609.13821) [src: abstract]; in `approximations.md#interacting-neutrinos-contact-limit-collision`.
- **Decay: old transport rate, step function, or first-principles rate**: Eq. 5 with an instantaneous switch, Eq. 7 with exponential damping, or Eq. 60 (arXiv:2011.01502) [src: Sects. 3.1.1, 6.1.3]; with massive daughters, Eq. 13 of arXiv:2203.09075.

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Damping the monopole or dipole.** The relaxation term is added only for $\ell\geq 2$; the low moments are protected "on account of energy and momentum conservation" (arXiv:1706.02123) [src: Sect. 2.2]. A code that damps them violates conservation.
- **Separable ansatz far from equilibrium.** It is exact for collisionless ultra-relativistic particles and "appears to capture" $\ell\leq 2$ near equilibrium "in the ultra-relativistic limit" (arXiv:2203.09075) [src: Sect. 4.1]; the relaxation form is "an incomplete description" in general (arXiv:1706.02123) [src: Sect. 2.2, text after Eq. 11].
- **Contact limit at high temperature or near resonance.** The s-channel resonance region of $\mu_\varphi$ is not modelled by the heavy-mediator coefficients (arXiv:2602.12477) [src: Sect. 4.2]. Check: compare $m_\varphi$ with $T_\nu$ over the redshift range the data constrain.
- **Old decay rate.** The cubic transport rate Eq. 5 gives bounds that must be replaced by Eq. 60 (arXiv:2011.01502) [src: Sect. 6.1.3] and, with massive daughters, by Eq. 13 of arXiv:2203.09075.
- **Decay bounds outside their range.** Anisotropic-stress bounds assume inverse decay active through the CMB epoch and an ultra-relativistic parent; "no lifetime bound can be placed" from anisotropic-stress loss if inverse decay is kinematically suppressed (arXiv:2011.01502) [src: Sect. 3.1.2].
- **Mixing up two meanings of $\alpha_\ell$.** Scattering coefficients from 0.40 to 0.48 (arXiv:1706.02123) [src: Eq. 10] and decay coefficients from Eq. 18 of arXiv:2203.09075 are different objects multiplying different rates.
