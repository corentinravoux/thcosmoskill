# Open questions on neutrinos and light relics

Scope: live debates with both sides cited, unresolved calculations, what the next data or calculation will decide (forecasts labelled), and stale claims with the paper that corrects them.

Snapshot: 2026-11-03

## Live debates
<!-- key: live-debates -->

- **NLO QED corrections to the weak rates and $N_{\rm eff}^{\rm SM}$: 3.043 or 3.0440.** Cielo et al. claim a reduction of $-0.0007$ and N_eff^SM = 3.043 (arXiv:2306.05460) [src: abstract]. Drewes et al. find an effect "about a factor of 30 smaller", below the nominal uncertainty, and keep $3.0440 \pm 0.0002$ (arXiv:2402.18481) [src: abstract]; their analysis is restricted to the enhanced t-channel contributions [src: abstract]. Jackson & Laine, for massless electrons, find the NLO corrections to the energy-transfer rates "as small as those that enter the previously determined neutrino interaction rate" and provide tabulated rates for non-approximate kinetic equations, "which may further reduce the systematic uncertainties" (arXiv:2412.03958) [src: abstract]. Cielo et al. state that their theoretical uncertainty is "much smaller than any projected observational sensitivity" (arXiv:2306.05460) [src: abstract]; Drewes et al. place the effect "below the nominal computational uncertainties" of the benchmark (arXiv:2402.18481) [src: abstract].

## Unresolved calculations
<!-- key: unresolved-calculations -->

- Types (b), (c) and (d) of finite-temperature QED corrections to the weak rates "remain on the table" (arXiv:2012.02726) [src: Sect. 6].
- Positronium formation: a gradual formation "could however yield a larger change" than the $10^{-4}$ bound of the instantaneous scenario (arXiv:2411.14091) [src: abstract].

## What the next data or calculation will decide
<!-- key: what-the-next-data-or-calculation-will-decide -->

- **Forecast:** the CMB-S4 conceptual design reaches $\sigma(N_\mathrm{eff}) < 0.03$ over seven years, and the revised configuration the same precision over a longer time (Trendafilova et al. 2026, arXiv:2608.07453) [src: abstract]. The 2016 target of σ(N_eff) ∼ 0.01 for CMB-S4 (**forecast**, arXiv:1604.08614) [src: Sect. 1], the level needed to reach the minimal thermal contribution (`results.md`).
- **Forecast:** CMB-S4 aims at an uncertainty on $N_{\rm eff}$ of $\leq 0.06$ at 95% CL; with DESI BAO and the Planck optical depth, σ(Σm_ν) = 0.024 eV, improving to 0.014 eV with a better optical depth; PICO with DESI or Euclid BAO, σ(Σm_ν) = 14 meV, "a 4σ detection of the minimum total neutrino mass" for the normal ordering (arXiv:2404.19322) [src: Sect. IV].
- A measured $\Delta N_{\rm eff}$ does not identify its source; CMB spectral distortions from energy exchange between extra radiation and photons, including energy released in neutrinos, can lift the degeneracy for hidden sectors that couple mainly to neutrinos (Li & Chluba 2025, arXiv:2510.04684) [src: abstract].

## Stale claims and misconceptions
<!-- key: stale-claims-and-misconceptions -->

- "N_eff^SM = 3.046": the 2005 value misses the ${\cal O}(e^3)$ finite-temperature QED correction to the equation of state; the current benchmark is 3.0440 (arXiv:2012.02726) [src: Sect. 6; abstract].

## The neutrino-mass tension as seen from theory
<!-- key: the-neutrino-mass-tension-as-seen-from-theory -->

The theory responses to the preference of CMB + DESI for small or "negative" $\sum m_\nu$ are the subject of `thcosmo-tensions-model-building` (planned); the measured bounds are in cosmoskill `cosmo-neutrinos-dark-matter`. Facts a theorist needs here:

- Green & Meyers attribute the preference for negative masses to "larger than expected lensing of the CMB in both the two- and four-point lensing statistics", find it "not easily explained by changes to the expansion history" and "likely distinct" from the DESI preference for dynamical dark energy, and expect it "likely to persist" (arXiv:2407.07878) [src: abstract].
- Elbers et al. define an effective mass parameter that extends consistently to negative values; in ΛCDM with Planck, ACT and DESI they find a 2.8–3.3σ tension with oscillation experiments, and in $w_0w_a$ and mirage dark energy Σm_ν,eff = 0.06 (−0.10/+0.15) eV and $0.04_{-0.11}^{+0.15}$ eV (68%) (arXiv:2407.10965) [src: abstract]. The two papers differ on the role of dark energy: Elbers et al. find that evolving dark energy favours larger masses, Green & Meyers that the lensing excess is likely distinct from it [der].
- Geometry and growth: splitting the expansion rate at the Boltzmann-code level gives $\sum m_\nu^{geo} = -0.07\pm0.10$ eV and $\sum m_\nu^{growth} = -0.13^{+0.10}_{-0.12}$ eV (68% C.L.) with Planck, Planck+ACT+SPT lensing and DESI BAO, a null test of ΛCDM passed "at 0.4σ level"; **forecast**: an incorrect dark-energy model displaces $\sum m_\nu^{geo}$ by 3.1–4.2σ and $\sum m_\nu^{growth}$ by 1.1–1.3σ, while an underestimated optical depth reverses the pattern (Ge et al. 2026, arXiv:2610.20977) [src: abstract].
- Lowering the neutrino abundance does not relax the bound: $T_\nu$ and $\sum m_\nu$ are positively correlated (arXiv:2609.15970) [src: abstract]. Bertólez-Martínez et al. read the bound as "mostly a measurement of the neutrino equation of state", "providing also hints to evade such a bound" (arXiv:2411.14524) [src: abstract].
