# Models of light relics and non-standard neutrinos

Scope: the model landscape beyond three free-streaming Standard-Model neutrinos, one card per model or class, chosen by literature weight in the reviews of Dvorkin et al. (arXiv:2203.07943) and Lesgourgues & Pastor (arXiv:astro-ph/0603494) and in the 2024–2026 literature; formalism in `formalism.md`, model-independent results in `results.md`. Sterile neutrinos as dark matter (keV scale) are in `thcosmo-dark-matter-models`.

Snapshot: 2026-11-19

Bounds cited to Table 1 of Dvorkin et al. (arXiv:2203.07943) are its compilation for Planck 2018 TT,TE,EE + BAO at 95% C.L., with Standard-Model neutrinos fixed at N_eff,fs = 3.046 where stated [src: Table 1]; other Planck 2018 bounds in the cards (decay, majoron) are cited to their own papers. Measured values of $N_{\rm eff}$ and $\sum m_\nu$ belong to cosmoskill `cosmo-neutrinos-dark-matter`.

## Extra radiation
<!-- key: extra-radiation -->

### Free-streaming dark radiation and light thermal relics
<!-- key: free-streaming-dark-radiation-and-light-thermal -->
- **Defining equations:** a decoupled light species X adds $\Delta N_{\rm eff}=\frac{4}{7}g_{X,\star}\left(\frac{T_{X}}{T_{\nu}}\right)^{4}=\frac{4}{7}g_{X,\star}\left(\frac{43}{4g_{\star}(T_{F})}\right)^{4/3}\ ,$ (arXiv:2203.07943) [src: Eq. 2]; its perturbations free-stream like neutrinos.
- **Parameters:** spin (through $g_{X,\star}$), freeze-out temperature $T_F$ or the coupling to the Standard Model that sets it; for pseudo-Nambu–Goldstone bosons (axions, familons, majorons) the scale Λ of the dimension-5 coupling $\mathcal{L}\supset-\Lambda_{i}^{-1}\partial_{\mu}\phi\,\bar{\psi}_{i}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}\psi_{i}$ (arXiv:2203.07943) [src: Fig. 2 caption].
- **Predictions:** $\Delta N_{\rm eff}\geq 0.027$ for any species once in equilibrium, unless the thermal history changes (arXiv:2203.07943) [src: Sect. 2.1]; a drop by "about one order of magnitude" across the QCD transition at T_F ∼ 150 MeV [src: Fig. 1 caption]; a phase shift of the acoustic peaks (`formalism.md`).
- **Consistency:** a thermal abundance is avoided if T_R < T_F, so a null result bounds couplings only given a reheating temperature; recoupling bounds avoid that assumption (arXiv:1604.08614) [src: Sect. 1]; (arXiv:2203.07943) [src: Sect. 2.2]. Reply: none needed; these are conditions, not objections.
- **Observational status:** $\Delta N_{\rm eff,fs}<0.28$, Planck 2018 TT,TE,EE + BAO, 95% C.L. (as compiled in arXiv:2203.07943) [src: Table 1].
- **Status date:** 2026-11-03

### Fluid-like (self-interacting) dark radiation
<!-- key: fluid-like-self-interacting-dark-radiation -->
- **Defining equations:** none universal; the class is defined by perturbations that "propagate with a speed that does not exceed the sound speed of the photon-baryon plasma" and "therefore do not lead to a phase shift" (Dvorkin et al. 2022, arXiv:2203.07943) [src: Sect. 3].
- **Parameters:** $N_{\rm eff,fld}$ alongside $N_{\rm eff,fs}$; in decoupling or recoupling variants, the transition time (arXiv:2203.07943) [src: Sect. 3].
- **Predictions:** the same expansion rate as free-streaming radiation of equal density, different perturbations; "CMB data allows larger $\Delta N_{\rm eff,fld}$" (arXiv:2203.07943) [src: Sect. 3]. Arises from neutrino self-interactions, neutrino–dark-sector interactions or dark-radiation self-interactions [src: Sect. 3]. A 2026 dark-QCD model in which dark radiation confines into light dark pions behaves as "an imperfect fluid with nonzero viscosity" (Buckley, Fernandez & Putney 2026, arXiv:2609.16120) [src: abstract].
- **Consistency:** none found among the sources read.
- **Observational status:** $\Delta N_{\rm eff,fld}<0.38$ with N_eff,fs = 3.046; $N_{\rm eff,fld}<0.6$ when N_eff,fs + N_eff,fld = 3.046 or both vary; $N_{\rm eff,fld}<0.5$ and $<0.51$ with $\sum m_\nu$ varied; Planck 2018 TT,TE,EE + BAO, 95% C.L. (as compiled in arXiv:2203.07943) [src: Table 1]. The dark-QCD model reports $H_0 = 71.60\pm 0.63$ km/s/Mpc with "a 3σ residual tension" across Planck, ACT, BBN, Pantheon+, DESI DR2 and SH0ES (arXiv:2609.16120) [src: abstract]; its place among $H_0$ responses is for `thcosmo-tensions-model-building`.
- **Status date:** 2026-11-03

## Interacting and decaying neutrinos
<!-- key: interacting-and-decaying-neutrinos -->

### Self-interacting neutrinos ($G_{\rm eff}$)
<!-- key: self-interacting-neutrinos-g-rm-eff -->
- **Defining equations:** $\mathcal{L}_{\rm int}=g_{ij}\bar{\nu}_{i}\nu_{j}\varphi,$ (Kreisch, Cyr-Racine & Doré 2019, arXiv:1902.00534) [src: Eq. 1], with the universal choice $g_{ij}\equiv g_{\nu}\delta_{ij},$ [src: Eq. 2], which the authors call "likely unrealistic" but a simple benchmark [src: Sect. II]. In the contact limit $\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}|_{\nu_{i}}^{2}=\displaystyle\sum_{\rm spins}|\mathcal{M}|_{\nu_{i}+\nu_{j}\rightarrow\nu_{k}+\nu_{l}}^{2}=\displaystyle 2G_{\rm eff}^{2}\left(s^{2}+t^{2}+u^{2}\right),$ [src: Eq. 3], with $G_{\rm eff}\equiv|g_{\nu}|^{2}/m_{\varphi}^{2}$ [src: Sect. II, inline, text after Eq. 3].
- **Parameters:** $G_{\rm eff}$; arXiv:1902.00534 introduces the self-interactions "in the presence of a non-vanishing sum of neutrino masses" and fits $N_{\rm eff}$ with them [src: abstract].
- **Predictions:** delayed onset of free streaming; the absence of the phase shift "can be compensated by shifting the value of other cosmological parameters", "an important caveat" to its detections, and a localised feature in the matter power spectrum at scales entering the horizon at the onset of free streaming (arXiv:1902.00534) [src: abstract]. Two regions: G_eff < 1/(56 MeV)² and a strongly interacting mode around G_eff ≃ 1/(10 MeV)², with neutrinos scattering until z ∼ 10⁴ (Cyr-Racine & Sigurdson 2013, arXiv:1306.1536) [src: abstract].
- **Consistency:** building a model with the gauge and flavour structure of the Standard Model "likely requires" a light sterile species mixed with the active neutrinos and coupled to the mediator; for a mediator below 10 MeV, SN 1987A, BBN and IceCube "provide some of the strongest constraints" (arXiv:1902.00534) [src: Sect. II]. Reply: a type-I seesaw model with a keV-scale scalar in which active neutrinos convert resonantly into self-interacting dark radiation after BBN evades laboratory bounds, since cosmology "cannot distinguish between neutrinos and dark radiation with the same abundance and free-streaming properties" (Das et al. 2025, arXiv:2506.08085) [src: abstract]. Flavour-specific interactions treated with mixing weaken CMB bounds on the coupling by $\sqrt{2}$ (two flavours) or $\sqrt{6}$ (one flavour), the combined-fluid approach being "less reliable for only $\nu_e$ interacting" (Barenboim, Drewes & Oldengott 2026, arXiv:2609.13821) [src: abstract].
- **Observational status:** the strongly interacting mode was once favoured with $H_0=72.3 \pm 1.4$ km/s/Mpc and $N_{\rm eff} = 4.02 \pm 0.29$, but "traditional Bayesian analyses penalize the model" (arXiv:1902.00534) [src: abstract]. With the DESI DR1-only FS + BAO + Lyα dataset (no CMB), the moderately interacting region is bimodal, and its sharp intermediate mode near G_eff ≃ 10⁻³ MeV⁻² "is not penalized versus the weaker standard-like mode"; with DESI DR1 full shape + BAO + CMB-SPA (Planck, ACT, SPT), the evidence against the strongly interacting mode is "the strongest thus far (with odds of 5:100000)" (Noriega et al. 2026, arXiv:2610.26654) [src: abstract]. Phase-shift analyses of Planck, ACT and SPT find free streaming "since deep within the radiation-dominated epoch" (arXiv:2509.20363) [src: abstract].
- **Status date:** 2026-11-03

### Invisible neutrino decay
<!-- key: invisible-neutrino-decay -->
- **Defining equations:** a heavier neutrino $\nu_H$ couples to a lighter $\nu_l$ and a massless scalar φ through a Yukawa interaction, decay ν_H → ν_l + φ (Barenboim et al. 2020, arXiv:2011.01502; Chen et al. 2022, arXiv:2203.09075) [src: abstracts].
- **Parameters:** rest-frame lifetime $\tau_0$, parent mass $m_{\nu_H}$, mass gap $\Delta m_\nu^2$ to the daughter.
- **Predictions:** ultra-relativistic decays damp the neutrino anisotropic stress (arXiv:2011.01502) [src: abstract]; non-relativistic decays into dark radiation change the growth of structure over time (Chacko et al. 2020, arXiv:2002.08401) [src: abstract].
- **Consistency:** the commonly used anisotropy-loss rate Γ_T = (1/τ₀)(m_νH/E_νH)³ is wrong; the rate is ∼ (1/τ₀)(m_νH/E_νH)⁵, and the effective perturbation equations used for non-relativistic decay "formally violate momentum conservation and gauge invariance", an effect "likely small" (arXiv:2011.01502) [src: abstract]. A non-zero daughter mass adds a factor ∝ (Δm_ν²/m_νH²)²: Γ_T ∼ (Δm_ν²/m_νH²)² (m_νH/E_ν)⁵ (1/τ₀) (arXiv:2203.09075) [src: abstract].
- **Observational status:** BBN requires τ_ν > 10⁻³ s at 95% CL; Planck 2018 gave τ_ν > (1.3−0.3)×10⁹ s (m_ν/0.05 eV)³ at 95% CL with the old rate (Escudero & Fairbairn 2019, arXiv:1907.05425) [src: abstract]; with the corrected rate the limit becomes τ₀ ≳ (4×10⁵ → 4×10⁶) s (m_νH/50 meV)⁵ (arXiv:2011.01502) [src: abstract], and with mass-spectrum-consistent phase space τ⁰ ≳ (6 → 10)×10⁵ s (atmospheric gap, one channel), τ⁰ ≳ (400 → 500) s (solar gap) and τ⁰ ≳ (2 → 3)×10⁷ s (two channels), "remarkably independent of the parent mass" (Planck 2018, arXiv:2203.09075) [src: abstract].
- **Status date:** 2026-11-03

## Massive light relics
<!-- key: massive-light-relics -->

### Light but massive relics (LiMRs)
<!-- key: light-but-massive-relics-limrs -->
- **Defining equations:** $k_{\rm fs}\approx\frac{0.1\,h\rm Mpc^{-1}}{\sqrt{1+z}}\frac{1.95\,\rm K}{T_{X}}\frac{m_{X}}{0.1\,\rm eV}$ and $\omega_{X}=\Omega_{X}\,h^{2}\approx\frac{m_{X}}{93.14\,\rm eV}\left(\frac{T_{X}}{1.95\,\rm K}\right)^{3}$ (arXiv:2203.07943) [src: Eqs. 3–4].
- **Parameters:** mass $m_X$, temperature today $T_X^{(0)}$, spin; the minimally coupled thermal benchmark is T_X⁽⁰⁾ = 0.91 K (arXiv:2203.07943) [src: Sect. 4.3]. The review reports the constraints of its Ref. [82] on scalars, Weyl fermions, vectors and Dirac fermions [src: Sect. 4.3]. The abstract of arXiv:2107.09664 groups "Weyl (and higher-spin) fermions" with the gravitino [src: abstract]; the s=3/2 gravitino "is cosmologically equivalent to a s=1/2 Weyl fermion" because only two of its four modes are thermally populated at its relativistic decoupling [src: Sect. I; Sect. V].
- **Predictions:** radiation-like at early times, then a suppression of small-scale clustering like massive neutrinos (arXiv:2203.07943) [src: Sect. 4.2]. Future ground-based CMB observatories should distinguish LiMRs from massless dark radiation (**forecast**, arXiv:2607.12021) [src: abstract].
- **Consistency:** none found among the sources read.
- **Observational status:** at T_X⁽⁰⁾ = 0.91 K, the arXiv abstract excludes Weyl fermions with $m_X\geq 2.26$ eV at 95% C.L. and gives limits m_X ≤ 11.2, 1.06, 1.56 eV for scalar, vector and Dirac-fermion relics (Xu, Muñoz & Dvorkin 2021, arXiv:2107.09664) [src: abstract]; the body gives 11 eV for scalars, 2.3 eV for Weyl fermions, 1.6 eV for vectors and 1.1 eV for Dirac fermions [src: Sect. I; Sect. V], and Dvorkin et al. report vectors at 1.56 eV and Dirac fermions at 1.06 eV (arXiv:2203.07943) [src: Sect. 4.3]. Abstract and body disagree on which of the two limits is the vector one and which the Dirac one. Data: Planck 2018, BOSS DR12 full shape and CFHTLenS [src: Sect. III]; weak lensing is "critical for breaking parameter degeneracies" [src: abstract].
- **Status date:** 2026-11-03

### Light (eV-scale and sub-eV) sterile neutrinos
<!-- key: light-ev-scale-and-sub-ev-sterile-neutrinos -->
This card is the standard case: mixing with the active neutrinos and no other interaction; the two cards below change the production.
- **Defining equations:** a 3+1 scheme with a fourth, inert state and a 4×4 mixing matrix, mixing angles $\theta_{14}$, $\theta_{24}$, $\theta_{34}$ and mass splitting $\Delta m^2_{41}$ (arXiv:1905.11290) [src: abstract]. Production by oscillations and collisions follows the QKEs of `formalism.md`, with the matter mixing angle $\sin^{2}2\theta_{\text{m}}=\frac{\sin^{2}2\theta}{\Big(\cos 2\theta+\tfrac{2E_{\nu}}{\Delta m^{2}}V_{\text{eff}}\Big)^{2}+\sin^{2}2\theta}\,,$ (arXiv:2106.05913) [src: Eq. 30].
- **Parameters:** $\Delta m^2_{41}$ and the mixing angles; in cosmology the effective abundance ($N_{\rm eff}$) and the effective mass $m_s^{\rm eff}$, which differs from the physical mass when the state is not fully populated (arXiv:2606.21518) [src: abstract]; the Planck definition of the effective mass and its relation to the physical mass for thermal and Dodelson–Widrow spectra are in `formalism.md` (arXiv:1807.06209) [src: Sect. 7.5.3, text after Eq. 67]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Predictions:** full thermalisation, N_eff ≃ 4, for the short-baseline preferred mixing (arXiv:1905.11290) [src: abstract]. With $\Delta m^{2}_{41}=1.29$ eV², $\lvert U_{e4}\rvert^{2}=0.01$ (the DANSS + NEOS preferred value) and the other two elements zero, the final value is N_eff ≃ 4.05, and $\lvert U_{e4}\rvert^{2}=0.001$ gives N_eff ≃ 3.5, "just outside the 3σ allowed region by DANSS and NEOS" [src: Sect. 3.2, inline]. Without active–sterile mixing the code gives N_eff = 3.044; for one non-zero angle at the same $\Delta m_{41}^{2}$, $\theta_{14}$ "leads always to smaller" N_eff, because the matter potential from $e^\pm$ delays the thermalisation of the mostly sterile state, while the individual effects of $\theta_{24}$ and $\theta_{34}$ are "similar but not equal"; more than one non-zero angle enlarges the tension [src: Sect. 4, inline]. In the 1+1 calculation with zero initial asymmetry, full thermalisation "is justified for eV-mass sterile neutrinos with relatively large mixing (as suggested by short-baseline oscillation data)" (Hannestad, Tamborra & Tram 2012, arXiv:1204.5861) [src: Sect. 4].
- **Consistency:** the review of Dvorkin et al. reports "a tension between the measurements of $N_{\rm eff}$ and the expectation from light sterile neutrinos"; proposed ways out are large asymmetries, secret neutrino interactions and modified thermal histories (arXiv:2203.07943) [src: Sect. 6.2]. Gariazzo et al. conclude that a confirmed active–sterile mixing would require "a new ingredient to suppress the thermalisation", such as an asymmetry or secret interactions (arXiv:1905.11290) [src: Sect. 4]. Dasgupta & Kopp report that joint terrestrial and cosmological constraints, from their Refs. [344–346], strongly disfavour the parameter range motivated by short-baseline experiments (arXiv:2106.05913) [src: Chap. 4, Sect. 3.1, "CMB and Structure Constraints"]. Reply: current data "do not generically exclude sterile neutrinos, but rather place strong pressure on fully thermalized or highly populated scenarios" (Ladeira et al. 2026, arXiv:2606.21518) [src: abstract].
- **Observational status:** Planck 2018: "One thermalized sterile neutrino with $\Delta N_{\rm eff}=1$ is excluded at about 6σ irrespective of its mass, or at about 7σ when assuming a mass $m_{\mathrm{sterile}}^{\mathrm{DW}}\approx 1$ eV" (arXiv:1807.06209) [src: Sect. 7.5.3, text after Eq. 68b]. With Planck, DESI DR2 BAO and supernovae, without a local $H_0$ prior, the fully thermalised case is "strongly disfavored" in ΛCDM and CPL, while Dodelson–Widrow-like and different-temperature realisations remain viable (arXiv:2606.21518) [src: abstract]. With DESI DR2, CMB, lensing, supernovae and SH0ES, $N_{\rm eff} = 3.43 \pm 0.13$ and sterile masses m_s ≃ 0.1–0.2 eV are favoured at $\gtrsim 3\sigma$, eV-scale masses remain "strongly excluded", and for $N_{\rm eff}=3.50$ the limit is $m_s < 0.17$ eV (95% CL) (García Escudero & Abazajian 2025, arXiv:2509.25478) [src: abstract]; the preference rests on SH0ES. For a thermal sterile relic with a different temperature, CMB + DESI BAO + the DESI DR1 Lyman-α one-dimensional power spectrum give $m_s^{\rm eff}<0.061$ eV, the confidence level not stated in the abstract (Silva et al. 2026, arXiv:2609.22581) [src: abstract].
- **Status date:** 2026-11-19

### Light sterile neutrinos with a primordial lepton asymmetry
<!-- key: light-sterile-neutrinos-with-a-primordial -->
- **Defining equations:** the mostly sterile state of the card above, with an initial lepton asymmetry entering the potential $V_{L}=\frac{2\sqrt{2}\zeta(3)}{\pi^{2}}G_{F}T^{3}L^{(a)}.$ (arXiv:1204.5861) [src: Eq. 11], which suppresses the mixing in matter or makes it resonant (`formalism.md`).
- **Parameters:** mass splitting and mixing, the sign of the splitting, and the initial asymmetries $L^{(a)}$ of the active flavours; Hannestad, Tamborra & Tram take $L^{(a)}=10^{-2}$ "For illustrative purposes" (arXiv:1204.5861) [src: Sect. 3.3]. Foot & Volkas found that the standard BBN bounds on the mixing do not apply for $L_ν$ "greater than about $10^{-5}$" (arXiv:hep-ph/9508275) [src: abstract].
- **Predictions:** for $L^{(a)}=10^{-2}$ in the 1+1 approximation, light sterile neutrinos "are not (or only partially) thermalised for almost all the scanned parameter space", and for asymmetries around $10^{-2}$ "almost no thermalisation occurs for the parameters preferred by Solar, reactor and short-baseline data" (arXiv:1204.5861) [src: Sect. 4, inline]; for the mixing parameters of their Ref. [56], $\delta N_{\text{eff}}\sim 0$ for $\delta m^{2}_{s}<0$ and $\delta N_{\text{eff}}=0.05$ for $\delta m^{2}_{s}>0$ [src: Sect. 3.3, inline]. In that case "an eV sterile neutrino will not be in conflict with the cosmological neutrino mass bound", but "the extra energy density preferred by CMB and LSS will then not be associable with the light sterile neutrino" [src: Sect. 3.3]. The authors neglect BBN and state that for large $L^{(a)}$ quantitative exclusion limits "would require solving the full QKEs including all three active species" [src: Sect. 4].
- **Consistency:** an asymmetry also permits resonant production; in (3+1) and (2+1) schemes with dynamical asymmetries, equal asymmetries require $\lvert L\rvert=10^{-3}$ "to start suppressing the resonant sterile production" and opposite ones "an even larger" $\lvert L\rvert\simeq 10^{-2}$ (Mirizzi et al. 2012, arXiv:1206.1046) [src: abstract, inline]. For parameters "inspired by the short-baseline neutrino anomalies", a multi-momentum, multi-flavour treatment raises the asymmetries required "to significantly suppress the sterile neutrino production" to $\lvert L_ν\rvert > O(10^{-2})$, for which conversions happen so late that significant distortions of the electron (anti)neutrino spectra are produced; the larger $\lvert L_ν\rvert$, the more these distortions dominate, "notably increasing the 4 He abundance", and "The standard expression of the primordial yields in terms of the effective number of neutrinos and asymmetries is also greatly altered" (Saviano et al. 2013, arXiv:1302.1200) [src: abstract, inline]. After listing a chemical potential, a low reheating temperature and a late reheating of photons, Dasgupta & Kopp write: "Given that these mechanisms still predict $N_{\textrm{eff}}>3$ at BBN, they are likely ruled out by now" (arXiv:2106.05913) [src: Chap. 4, Sect. 3.2, second paragraph, inline]. Reply: none found.
- **Observational status:** no fit of this scenario to current data was read. For active neutrinos alone, without a sterile state, Froustey & Pitrou find that the space of allowed initial asymmetries "is generically unbound when using currently available primordial abundances and CMB measurements" (arXiv:2405.06509) [src: abstract]; the bounds at the BBN epoch are in `results.md`.
- **Status date:** 2026-11-19

### Secretly interacting light sterile neutrinos
<!-- key: secretly-interacting-light-sterile-neutrinos -->
- **Defining equations:** a vector coupling $\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}=g_{X}\bar{\nu}_{s}\gamma^{\mu}P_{L}\nu_{s}X_{\mu}\,,$ (arXiv:2106.05913) [src: Eq. 45], with contact coupling $G_{X}=g_{X}^{2}/(2\sqrt{2}M_{X}^{2})$ [src: Chap. 4, Sect. 3.2, inline, text after Eq. 45]; or a pseudoscalar coupling ${\cal L}_{\textrm{int}}=g_{X}\overline{\nu}_{s}\gamma_{5}\nu_{s}X\,.$ [src: Eq. 49].
- **Parameters:** $g_X$, $M_X$ (or $G_X$), the sterile mass and the mixing. "Most studies on this topic are agnostic about the mechanism that breaks U(1)$_s$", although a sterile Higgs "is typically needed" to generate active–sterile mixing (arXiv:2106.05913) [src: Chap. 4, Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 45]. BBN is sensitive to $g_X$ and $M_X$ separately, "and not in a fixed combination like $G_{X}$", CMB and structure formation "mainly" to $G_X$ "if $N_{\textrm{eff}}$ is kept unchanged" [src: Chap. 4, Sect. 3.2, paragraph after Fig. 8 caption].
- **Predictions:** new interactions in the sterile sector "can prevent their production in the early Universe" (Hannestad, Hansen & Tram 2013, arXiv:1310.5926) [src: abstract]; with a light hidden boson, a large thermal potential suppresses mixing "even if vacuum mixing angles are large", the abundance "remains very small" and the impact on BBN, CMB and structure "is negligible" (Dasgupta & Kopp 2013, arXiv:1310.6337) [src: abstract]. For $g_X\gtrsim 10^{-2}$ and $M_X\lesssim 10$ MeV, the matter potential suppresses production before neutrino decoupling, but at lower temperatures, when oscillations are no longer matter suppressed, the sterile states are still collisional and a "scattering-induced decoherent production" with a fast rate equilibrates the flavours, and "a signature of strong secret interactions would be a reduction of the effective number of neutrinos $N_{\rm eff}$ at matter radiation equality down to 2.7" (Mirizzi et al. 2014, arXiv:1410.1385) [src: abstract].
- **Consistency:** the same interactions generate strong collisional terms that produce sterile neutrinos "after a resonance in matter is encountered", increasing their contribution to $N_{\rm eff}$, and for resonances at $T\lesssim$ few MeV distort the $\nu_e$ spectra; the deuterium ratio excludes much of the parameter space for the Planck best-fit $\Omega_B h^2= 0.02207$, with weaker bounds for 0.02261, the Planck 95 % C.L. upper bound, while the helium fraction "gives no significant bounds" (Saviano et al. 2014, arXiv:1409.1680) [src: abstract]. For $M_X \gtrsim g_X$ MeV the sterile states "would be free-streaming before becoming non-relativistic", and for this range the authors find "a tension of a eV mass sterile state with cosmological neutrino mass bounds" (arXiv:1410.1385) [src: abstract]. With the short-baseline hints, cosmology and IceCube, flavour equilibration "can be delayed until temperatures of $\sim 1$ MeV, but not below $\sim 100$ keV" (Cherry, Friedland & Shoemaker 2016, arXiv:1605.06506) [src: abstract]. Reply: Chu, Dasgupta & Kopp argue for two regions, "one at very small A' coupling, one at relatively large A' coupling", where BBN, CMB and LSS constraints are all satisfied (arXiv:1505.02795) [src: abstract]; the 2018 update of arXiv:1310.6337 confirms "that much of the previously open parameter space for secret interactions is in fact ruled out" by the bounds on the sum of masses and on active free-streaming, and discusses modifications (Chu et al. 2018, arXiv:1806.10629) [src: abstract]. For the pseudoscalar case the review reports that "for a sterile neutrino mass of 1 eV, a small part of parameter space is allowed by both short baseline data and cosmology", citing its Ref. [115] (arXiv:2106.05913) [src: Chap. 4, Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 49].
- **Observational status:** for self-interactions mediated by massive gauge bosons, with BBN, CMB and BAO data, $\log_{10}(G_X/G_F)\geq 3.6$ "can describe the primordial abundances at 95% CL"; with a wide prior on the sterile mass, $m_{\rm st}\leq 0.91$ eV (95% CL, CMB only) and $m_{\rm st}\leq 0.37$ eV (95% CL, with BAO), "a sterile neutrino mass of 1 eV is still excluded at more than $2σ$ CL"; with the short-baseline mass and mixing fixed, CMB data alone favour the scenario but "including the BAO information severly degrades the agreement with the model" (spelling as printed) (Song, Gonzalez-Garcia & Salvado 2018, arXiv:1805.08218) [src: abstract]. Dasgupta & Kopp conclude that the quantitative BBN and CMB studies they review "clearly rule out the vanilla version of the secret interactions scenario" for the sterile mass ($\sim$eV) and mixing ($\sim 0.1$) suggested by short-baseline anomalies, "for both heavy and light mediators", citing their Refs. [338, 359, 360] (arXiv:2106.05913) [src: Chap. 4, Sect. 3.2, paragraph after Fig. 8 caption]. A coupling to an ultralight dark-matter scalar with $M_{X}<5\times 10^{-17}$ eV suppresses mixing until $T\approx 0.01$ MeV and "the model is safe from both $N_{\text{eff}}$ and $\sum m_{\nu}$ constraints", in models of the review's Refs. [364–366] [src: Chap. 4, Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 52]. No later fit was read.
- **Status date:** 2026-11-19

### Majoron models
<!-- key: majoron-models -->
- **Defining equations:** the majoron is "a pseudo-Goldstone boson arising from the spontaneous breaking of global lepton number" (Escudero & Witte 2019, arXiv:1909.04044) [src: abstract]; the neutrino–majoron coupling has the form of Eq. 1 of arXiv:1902.00534, which "could also arise in models where neutrinos couple to a Majoron" [src: Sect. II].
- **Parameters:** majoron mass and the coupling λ; in the type-I seesaw, the lepton-number breaking scale $v_L$ (arXiv:1909.04044) [src: abstract].
- **Predictions:** majorons that thermalise with neutrinos after BBN through inverse neutrino decays leave imprints in the CMB (arXiv:1909.04044) [src: abstract]; depending on the majoron mass, neutrinos show decoupling (heavy majoron) or recoupling (light majoron) features (Dvorkin et al. 2022, arXiv:2203.07943) [src: Sect. 3].
- **Consistency:** the decoupling case "is disfavored by other terrestrial and cosmological constraints" (arXiv:2203.07943) [src: Sect. 3]; reply: none found.
- **Observational status:** Planck 2018 is sensitive to couplings "as small as λ ∼ 10⁻¹³", corresponding in the type-I seesaw to v_L ∼ O(100) GeV; with an extra $N_{\rm eff}$ the interactions "can ameliorate" the $H_0$ tension (arXiv:1909.04044) [src: abstract]; the recoupling case with an eV-scale majoron "may help mitigate" it (arXiv:2203.07943) [src: Sect. 3]. Its standing among $H_0$ responses is for `thcosmo-tensions-model-building`.
- **Status date:** 2026-11-03
