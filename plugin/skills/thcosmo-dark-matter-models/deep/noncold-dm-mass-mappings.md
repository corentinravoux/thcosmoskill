# Non-cold dark-matter mass mappings — thcosmo-dark-matter-models dossier

Scope: how a bound quoted as a thermal-relic warm-dark-matter mass is translated into a bound on another non-cold model (non-resonant and resonant sterile neutrinos, freeze-in, decay products, other non-thermal spectra), which matching criterion each translation uses, and when a single mass cannot carry the bound.

## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- **Transfer function.** Murgia et al. and Vogel et al. define it against cold dark matter: $T^{2}(k)\equiv\frac{P_{\rm{nCDM}}(k)}{P_{\rm{CDM}}(k)},$ (Murgia et al. 2017, arXiv:1704.07838) [src: Eq. 1]; $T(k)\equiv\sqrt{P_{X}(k)/P_{\mathrm{\Lambda CDM}}(k)}\,,$ (Vogel et al. 2025, arXiv:2507.18752) [src: Eq. 6].
- **Two half-mode definitions.** Murgia et al. define $k_{1/2}$ "by setting $T^{2}\equiv 1/2$" (arXiv:1704.07838) [src: Sect. 2, inline, text before Eq. 5], and Ballesteros, Garcia & Pierre define $k_{1/2}^{\text{WDM}}$ by $\mathcal{P}_{\text{WDM}}(k_{1/2}^{\text{WDM}})=(1/2)\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda\text{CDM}}(k_{1/2}^{\text{WDM}})$ (arXiv:2011.13458) [src: Sect. 2.3, inline, text after Eq. 2.29]. Schneider et al. define the half-mode length as "the length scale at which the amplitude of the WDM transfer function is reduced to 1/2" (arXiv:1112.0330) [src: Sect. 2.1, text before Eq. 8], and Vogel & Abazajian match fits "at $T_{X}(k)=1/2$" (arXiv:2210.10753) [src: Sect. IV, inline]. `[der]` The first pair fixes the power ratio at one half and the second pair the amplitude ratio, so the two conventions name different wavenumbers (and half-mode masses) for the same model; a quoted half-mode scale must say which.
- **Units of the break scale.** Viel et al. give α in Mpc (`formalism.md#linear-signatures-free-streaming-and-the-warm`); Murgia et al. reprint it with a second line in $h^{-1}$Mpc that "holds only in the case of thermal relics" (arXiv:1704.07838) [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 3]; Vogel & Abazajian give α in $h^{-1}$Mpc normalised to $\omega_X/0.12$ and $h/0.6736$ (arXiv:2210.10753) [src: Eq. 9]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Mass names.** The thermal-relic mass is $m_x$ (Viel et al.), $m_{\rm WDM}$ (Murgia et al.; Schneider et al.; Ballesteros et al.), $m_{\rm th}$ (Vogel et al.); the sterile-neutrino mass is $m_{\nu_s}$ (arXiv:1112.0330), $m_{\rm sn}$ (arXiv:1601.07553), $m_s$ (arXiv:2507.18752), $m_N$ (arXiv:1704.07838). Vogel et al. distinguish $m_{\mathrm{th,old}}$ (from the Viel et al. fit) from $m_{\mathrm{th,new}}$ (from the Vogel & Abazajian fit) (arXiv:2507.18752) [src: Sect. II.3].
- **Momentum variables.** Ballesteros et al. write the comoving momentum as q in units of a reference temperature $T_{\star}$, with $\langle p\rangle_{0}=\langle q\rangle\,T_{\star}$ (arXiv:2011.13458) [src: Sect. 2.3, inline, text after Eq. 2.25].

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

Every mapping below answers the same question: which mass $m'$ of model B gives "the same" small-scale suppression as the thermal-relic mass $m$ excluded by a Lyman-α or satellite analysis? The papers differ in what "the same" means. The steps go from the strongest assumption (same distribution shape) to the weakest (no single mass at all).

### Step 1 — the benchmark itself: which thermal mass a bound refers to
<!-- key: step-1-the-benchmark-itself-which-thermal-mass -->

A thermal-relic bound is a bound on the break scale α of the Viel et al. fit (`formalism.md#linear-signatures-free-streaming-and-the-warm`) through the mass dependence of α. Murgia et al. write the Viel et al. relation in both unit systems, $\begin{split}\alpha&=0.24\left(\frac{m_{x}/T_{x}}{1~\rm{keV}/T_{\nu}}\right)^{-0.83}\left(\frac{\omega_{x}}{0.25\cdot(0.7)^{2}}\right)^{-0.16}{\rm{Mpc}}\\ &=0.049\left(\frac{m_{x}}{1~\rm{keV}}\right)^{-1.11}\left(\frac{\Omega_{x}}{0.25}\right)^{0.11}\left(\frac{h}{0.7}\right)^{1.22}h^{-1}\rm{Mpc}~,\end{split}$ (arXiv:1704.07838) [src: Eq. 3], the second line for thermal relics only [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 3].

Vogel & Abazajian (arXiv:2210.10753, Vogel & Abazajian 2022) refit the same form, $T_{X}(k;\alpha,\nu)=[1+(\alpha k)^{2\nu}]^{-5/\nu}\,,$ (arXiv:2210.10753) [src: Eq. 8], with CLASS spectra for fully thermalised spin-1/2 and spin-3/2 relics "at masses of 2, 5, 10, 20, and 100 keV" and Planck 2018 parameters, and with $\alpha=a\,m_{\mathrm{th}}^{b}\left(\frac{\omega_{X}}{0.12}\right)^{\eta}\left(\frac{h}{0.6736}\right)^{\theta}h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}\,.$ (arXiv:2210.10753) [src: Eq. 9]. The Viel et al. fit had analysed "spin-1/2 particles between 0.092 keV and $1.441\,\mathrm{keV}$, resulting in a poorer fit compared to our results in this higher particle mass regime" (arXiv:2210.10753) [src: Sect. IV]. Matching the old and new fits at $T_X(k)=1/2$, "the particle-mass correction goes from 14% at 10 keV, at the range of current constraints, to 26% at 100 keV", "The exact level of the correction on the observable's constraint on particle mass" depending on "which portion of the transfer function that the observable is most sensitive", and "all constraints that are claimed at the $\gtrsim 1\,\mathrm{keV}$ scale that rely on the linear matter transfer function to determine their constraints would need to be corrected in order to map onto the proper dark matter particle mass" (arXiv:2210.10753) [src: Sect. IV]. They add that spin-3/2 masses are "linearly related to an effective spin-1/2 particle rest mass", so that "future constraints based on WDM transfer functions may treat mass bounds as effectively mass-spin bounds" [src: Sect. IV].

Vogel et al. turn this into a conversion between the two thermal masses, $m_{\mathrm{th,new}}\approx 0.83\,m_{\mathrm{th,old}}+0.36\,,$ (arXiv:2507.18752) [src: Eq. 7], masses in keV, which "should be used to properly map any work which utilizes the previous WDM transfer function fit" [src: Sect. II.3, text after Eq. 7]. `[der]` So the first link of any chain is to know which thermal-mass calibration produced the input bound; the reference files quote Viel et al. masses.

### Step 2 — same shape, rescaled: the Dodelson–Widrow map
<!-- key: step-2-same-shape-rescaled-the-dodelson-widrow -->

If the sterile-neutrino distribution is a Fermi–Dirac one scaled by an overall factor, the transfer function of a sterile neutrino equals that of a thermal relic with the same $m/T$ and the same density, which gives the Viel et al. map (`formalism.md#linear-signatures-free-streaming-and-the-warm`, arXiv:astro-ph/0501562, Eq. 5). Schneider et al. reprint it with the density normalised to 0.1225, $m_{\nu_{s}}=4.43{\rm keV}\left(\frac{m_{\rm WDM}}{1{\rm keV}}\right)^{4/3}\left(\frac{w_{\rm WDM}}{0.1225}\right)^{-1/3}\ .$ (arXiv:1112.0330) [src: Eq. 6], their thermal transfer function "assuming that the WDM particle is fully thermalized" [src: Sect. 2.1, text before Eq. 6]. Schutz calls it "The standard mapping", "assuming the sterile neutrino constitutes all of the DM" (arXiv:2608.24990) [src: Sect. V].

The premise is not exact. Murgia et al. note that "contrary to previous statements in the literature [43, 45], non-resonantly produced sterile neutrinos also feature a non-thermal distribution [44], rather than a suppressed thermal spectrum", which "slightly changes the published numerical values of the bounds on this setting" while "the basic conclusion of non-resonant production being excluded remains valid (in fact, it is even made stronger)" (arXiv:1704.07838) [src: Sect. 3.1, footnote 2]. The two conversions in use differ visibly: Schneider (2016) maps a thermal bound of $m_{\rm WDM}\gtrsim 4.35$ keV to $m_{\rm sn}\gtrsim 26.4$ keV with the conversion of Bozek et al. (his Ref. [33]), and notes that the analysis that produced the bound "quote[s] a limit of $m_{\rm sn}\gtrsim 31.7$ keV based on the mass conversion from Ref. [31]", Viel et al. 2005 (arXiv:1601.07553) [src: Sect. 4.1, footnote 3]; the same paper maps $m_{\rm WDM}\gtrsim 3.3$ keV to $m_{\rm sn}\gtrsim 18.5$ keV with the Bozek et al. conversion [src: Sect. 4.1].

### Step 3 — same equation of state: the second-moment map
<!-- key: step-3-same-equation-of-state-the-second-moment -->

Ballesteros, Garcia & Pierre (arXiv:2011.13458, Ballesteros et al. 2020) replace "same distribution" by "same velocity dispersion". In the non-relativistic limit the equation of state of the non-cold component is $w\,\simeq\dfrac{\delta P}{\delta\rho}\,=\,\dfrac{T_{\star}^{2}}{3m_{\text{DM}}^{2}}\frac{\langle q^{2}\rangle}{a^{2}}\,,$ (arXiv:2011.13458) [src: Eq. 2.13], with $\langle q^{n}\rangle\,\equiv\,\dfrac{\int q^{n+2}\bar{f}(q)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}q}{\int q^{2}\bar{f}(q)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}q}\,.$ [src: Eq. 2.14], so that "given a phase space distribution for the DM, determination of its second moment is sufficient to estimate the cutoff of the matter power spectrum" [src: Sect. 2.1, text after Eq. 2.14]. Assuming that w "is the only quantity needed to characterize the cutoff in the linear transfer function", the bound is transferred by solving $\displaystyle w(m_{\text{DM}})=w_{\text{WDM}}(m_{\text{WDM}})\,.$ (arXiv:2011.13458) [src: Eq. 2.20], with, from Eq. 2.13 for the thermal relic, $w_{\text{WDM}}(a)\,\simeq\,6\times 10^{-15}\,a^{-2}\,\left(\dfrac{\text{keV}}{m_{\text{WDM}}}\right)^{8/3}\,,$ [src: Eq. 2.21]. The result is $m_{\text{DM}}\,=\,m_{\text{WDM}}\left(\dfrac{T_{\star}}{T_{\text{WDM}}}\right)\sqrt{\dfrac{\langle q^{2}\rangle}{\langle q^{2}\rangle_{\text{WDM}}}}\simeq\,7.56\penalty\ \text{keV}\,\left(\dfrac{m_{\text{WDM}}}{3\penalty\ \text{keV}}\right)^{4/3}\left(\dfrac{T_{\star}}{T}\right)\sqrt{\langle q^{2}\rangle}\,.$ (arXiv:2011.13458) [src: Eq. 2.25], or, with the present mean momentum, $m_{\text{DM}}\,\simeq\,7.56\penalty\ \text{keV}\,\left(\dfrac{m_{\text{WDM}}}{3\penalty\ \text{keV}}\right)^{4/3}\left(\dfrac{\langle p\rangle_{0}}{T_{0}}\right)\dfrac{\sqrt{\langle q^{2}\rangle}}{\langle q\rangle}\,.$ [src: Eq. 2.26]. The authors trace the idea to their Ref. [69], Colombi, Dodelson & Widrow, which related "a sterile neutrino and a particle that decouples while being relativistic", and to "A similar matching procedure using the mean square of the DM velocity" "proposed in [70] and extended in [71, 72] for several freeze-in models" [src: Sect. 2.3, text after Eq. 2.20]. <!-- num: not a value -->

For the family $f(q)\,\propto\,q^{\alpha}\,\exp{\left(-\beta\,q^{\gamma}\right)}\,,$ (arXiv:2011.13458) [src: Eq. 2.27], with "constant $\alpha>-3$ and $\beta,\gamma>0$", the moments are printed as $\langle q^{n}\rangle\,=\,\beta^{\frac{2-n}{\gamma}}\,\dfrac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1+n+\alpha}{\gamma}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{3+\alpha}{\gamma}\right)}\,.$ [src: Eq. 2.28], and the map becomes $m_{\text{DM}}\,\simeq\,7.56\penalty\ \text{keV}\,\left(\dfrac{m_{\text{WDM}}}{3\penalty\ \text{keV}}\right)^{4/3}\left(\dfrac{\langle p\rangle_{0}}{T_{0}}\right)\,\sqrt{\dfrac{\Gamma\left(\frac{3+\alpha}{\gamma}\right)\,\Gamma\left(\frac{5+\alpha}{\gamma}\right)}{\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{4+\alpha}{\gamma}\right)}}\,,$ (arXiv:2011.13458) [src: Eq. 2.29], "which does not depend explicitly on β", although "the mean momentum $\langle p\rangle_{0}$ depends actually on this quantity" [src: Sect. 2.3, footnote 6]. <!-- num: not a value -->

As printed, Eq. 2.28 does not reduce to one for the zeroth moment and does not follow from Eq. 2.14 with Eq. 2.27, whereas the Gamma-function ratio of Eq. 2.29 does follow from Eq. 2.14 `[der]`. Use Eq. 2.29, or compute the moments from Eq. 2.14 directly.

Validity: the matching needs $w(a)\simeq w_{0}\,a^{-2}$ "at redshift $z<10^{6}$, for which the wavenumbers k relevant for Lyman-α data enters the horizon"; "A different a-dependence of w(a) would affect our matching procedure", as in cannibal dark matter (arXiv:2011.13458) [src: Sect. 2.3, inline]. Accuracy, for $m_{\text{WDM}}=3$ keV: the transfer functions match "mostly with a precision below the percent level" for $k<20\,h\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$, with "a 3% difference or better" at $k_{1/2}^{\text{WDM}}$ for most scenarios and "a ∼10% difference" for the least precise, the decay of a non-thermal relativistic particle and thermal freeze-in with n = 6 (arXiv:2011.13458) [src: Sect. 2.3, text after Fig. 3 caption].

### Step 4 — different shape, best-fitting thermal curve: the Shi–Fuller map
<!-- key: step-4-different-shape-best-fitting-thermal -->

Resonant (Shi–Fuller) production "preferentially populates low momenta, yielding a non-thermal spectrum that is 'colder' than Dodelson-Widrow production", so "A given Shi-Fuller $m_{s}$ therefore maps onto a larger equivalent thermal WDM mass than the same $m_{s}$ produced via the Dodelson-Widrow mechanism" (arXiv:2608.24990) [src: Sect. V]. No moment formula is used: assuming "that all lepton asymmetries are small by the time of structure formation", Vogel et al. compute the distributions with a Boltzmann transport code, pass them to CLASS, form Eq. 6, and "fit the SF transfer functions with thermal WDM transfer functions, where the thermal WDM particle mass is the free parameter" (arXiv:2507.18752) [src: Sect. II.3]. The residuals are "relatively small ($\lesssim 20\%$) for the suppression scale of 1/2", an accuracy that holds "across the examined parameter space, despite many points having PSDs that are highly nonthermal" [src: Sect. II.3]. The resulting map is a power law, $\displaystyle m_{\mathrm{th}}\displaystyle=A\,m_{s}^{a}\,\left[\sin^{2}(2\theta)\right]^{b},\quad\begin{cases}A=1.329\\ a=1.044\\ b=0.055\,,\end{cases}$ (arXiv:2507.18752) [src: Eq. 8], masses in keV, with $m_{\rm th}$ "the more accurate thermal WDM particle mass" of Vogel & Abazajian; the fits "capture the dominant scaling behavior in the high-lepton asymmetry regime and are validated to remain within percentage-level error bounds across the range $15~\mathrm{keV}\leq m_{s}\leq 60~\mathrm{keV}$ and $10^{-15}\leq\sin^{2}(2\theta)\leq 3\times 10^{-13}$", with an average error of 1.3% [src: Sect. III, text after Eq. 9]. The companion fit of the lepton asymmetry needed for the full abundance is Eq. 9 of the same paper. Unlike the maps of Steps 2 and 3, it depends on the mixing angle as well as on the mass `[der]`.

### Step 5 — no single mass: the three-parameter fit and the half-mode scale
<!-- key: step-5-no-single-mass-the-three-parameter-fit -->

When the shape differs, Murgia et al. generalise the thermal fit to $T(k)=[1+(\alpha k)^{\beta}]^{\gamma},$ (arXiv:1704.07838) [src: Eq. 4], with half-mode scale $k_{1/2}=\frac{1}{\alpha}\left[\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{1/\gamma}-1\right]^{1/\beta}.$ [src: Eq. 5]. α "gives the general scale of suppression", while β and γ set "the slope of the transfer function before and after the half-mode scale", and "the larger the absolute value of γ, the sharper is the cut-off" [src: Sect. 2, last paragraph]. "Whereas through Eqs. (2) and (3) we had a one-to-one correspondence between $m_{x}$ and α, constraints on the DM mass are now, by Eqs. (4) and (5), mapped to 3-dimensional surfaces in the {α,β,γ} space", and a value of $k_{1/2}$ that "corresponds to a certain (thermal) WDM mass" is "not yet sufficient to decide about the validity of the point under consideration" [src: Sect. 2, text after Eq. 5]. Resonantly produced sterile-neutrino spectra, which "typically … feature one or more narrow peaks on top of a continuous spectrum", are fitted by Eq. 4 with "excellent fits" for the examples shown, "also for distributions with more than one scale", the distributions tested being "very representative for resonant production" [src: Sect. 3.1]. <!-- num: not a value -->

Marginalising over β and γ gives upper limits on α (Numbers below) which "would correspond, in the old one-to-one parametrisation", to m_WDM ≈ 2 keV for satellites and ≈ 3 keV for the Lyman-α forest, weaker than the thermal limits because "models with shallower transfer functions may be found to be in agreement with Lyman-α forest data even if the corresponding DM candidate mass lies below the current constraints for thermal WDM masses, given that those constraints refer to a very specific shape of the power suppression" (arXiv:1704.07838) [src: Sect. 5.1, text after Eq. 11; Sect. 5.2, text after Eq. 16].

### Step 6 — transferring a Lyman-α bound without a new likelihood
<!-- key: step-6-transferring-a-lyman-alpha-bound-without -->

Two linear-theory recipes compare model B with the thermal model at the excluded mass, using the one-dimensional power $P_{\rm 1D}(k)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{k}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}k^{\prime}P(k^{\prime}),$ (arXiv:1601.07553) [src: Eq. 6].

- **Reference-ratio criterion** (Schneider 2016, arXiv:1601.07553). With $r(k)=\frac{P_{\rm 1D}(k)}{P_{\rm 1D}^{\rm REF}(k)},$ (arXiv:1601.07553) [src: Eq. 7], where REF is the thermal model excluded at 2σ, "We define a model to be excluded (at 2-σ CL), if the ratio is both below one and monotonically decreasing over the entire range probed by Lyman-α"; the slope condition is there because the mean flux is fixed to the observed value in the reference analyses, which renormalises the 1D power [src: Sect. 4.1]. The recipe relies on the bias "differ[ing] very little between CDM and WDM models" [src: Sect. 4.1]; "absolute bounds can only be computed with a full statistical analysis" [src: Sect. 4.1]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Area criterion** (Murgia et al., arXiv:1704.07838). With $r(k)=\frac{P_{1\rm{D}}(k)}{P^{\Lambda\rm{CDM}}_{1\rm{D}}(k)},$ (arXiv:1704.07838) [src: Eq. 12], the estimator $\delta A\equiv\frac{A_{\rm\Lambda CDM}-A}{A_{\rm\Lambda CDM}},$ [src: Eq. 14] and $A=\int\limits_{k_{\rm min}}^{k_{\rm max}}{\rm d}k\ r(k),$ [src: Eq. 15] over the range of the data; a model is excluded "if $\delta A>\delta A_{\rm REF,1}$", the value of the excluded thermal model [src: Sect. 5.2, text after Eq. 15]. The authors regard it "as a first quantitative step towards a more comprehensive analysis" whose bounds "should be robustly checked against full hydrodynamic simulations" [src: Sect. 5.2].

### Step 7 — transferring a satellite bound: half-mode mass and subhalo counts
<!-- key: step-7-transferring-a-satellite-bound-half-mode -->

For satellites, the linear spectrum enters through a sharp-k extended Press–Schechter subhalo function, $\frac{{\rm d}N_{\rm sh}}{{\rm d}\ln M_{\rm sh}}=\frac{1}{C_{\rm n}}\frac{1}{6\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{M_{\rm hh}}{M_{\rm sh}}\right)\frac{P(1/R_{\rm sh})}{R_{\rm sh}^{3}\sqrt{2\pi(S_{\rm sh}-S_{\rm hh})}}\,,$ (arXiv:1601.07553) [src: Eq. 8], with $S_{i}=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{1/R_{i}}{\rm d}k\ k^{2}P(k),\hskip 14.22636ptM_{i}=\frac{4\pi}{3}\Omega_{\rm m}\rho_{c}(cR_{i})^{3},\hskip 14.22636ptc=2.5,\hskip 28.45274pti=\{\rm sh,hh\},$ (arXiv:1601.07553) [src: Eq. 9], and $C_{\rm n}$ = 45 or 34 for host haloes defined at 200 times the background or the critical density [src: Sect. 4.2, text after Eq. 9]. Murgia et al. print the same function per unit mass with the factor 1/44.5 (arXiv:1704.07838) [src: Eq. 9]. A model is excluded when the predicted count above the satellite-mass threshold falls below the estimated number of satellites (Numbers below); "Eq. (8) solely depends on the linear power spectrum P(k) for a given DM scenario" (arXiv:1601.07553) [src: Sect. 4.2], so any model B with a computed P(k) is tested directly, without a mass map `[der]`. <!-- num: not a value -->

The half-mode mass is how a thermal satellite bound is usually quoted. From the thermal fit, Schneider et al. define $M_{\rm fs}=\frac{4\pi}{3}\overline{\rho}\left(\frac{\lambda^{\rm eff}_{\rm fs}}{2}\right)^{3},$ (arXiv:1112.0330) [src: Eq. 7], with $\alpha\equiv\lambda_{\rm fs}^{\rm eff}$ [src: Sect. 2.1, text after Eq. 6], $\lambda_{\rm hm}=2\pi\lambda^{\rm eff}_{\rm fs}\left(2^{\mu/5}-1\right)^{-1/2\mu}\approx 13.93\lambda^{\rm eff}_{\rm fs}\ .$ [src: Eq. 8] and $M_{\rm hm}=\frac{4\pi}{3}\overline{\rho}\left(\frac{\lambda_{\rm hm}}{2}\right)^{3}\approx 2.7\times 10^{3}M_{\rm fs}\ .$ [src: Eq. 9], the scale "where we expect the WDM to first affect the properties of dark matter haloes" [src: Sect. 2.1, text after Eq. 9]. As first noted by Smith & Markovic, rescaled by $M_{\rm fs}$ "or equivalently by $M_{\rm hm}$", the theory mass functions "for a wide variety of different values of $m_{\rm WDM}$ all appear to fall upon the same locus", and the scaling "also works surprisingly well for the mass function measured from the simulations" (arXiv:1112.0330) [src: Sect. 4.1]. `[der]` A bound on the half-mode mass therefore transfers to another model only to the extent that its suppression has the thermal shape around that scale, which Step 5 shows is not guaranteed.

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

| Matching criterion | What it assumes | What it changes | Compared in |
|---|---|---|---|
| Same distribution shape (Viel et al. Eq. 5; Schneider et al. Eq. 6) | sterile spectrum is a scaled Fermi–Dirac one | none if the premise holds; the premise fails for Dodelson–Widrow (arXiv:1704.07838, Sect. 3.1, footnote 2) | conversions of Viel et al. and Bozek et al. differ (arXiv:1601.07553, Sect. 4.1, footnote 3) |
| Same equation of state, $\langle q^2\rangle$ (Ballesteros et al. Eqs. 2.20–2.29) | w alone sets the cut-off; $w\propto a^{-2}$ when Lyman-α modes enter | no numerical power spectrum for distributions "well described by (2.27)" (Eq. 2.29; footnote 6), Eq. 2.25 otherwise | CLASS transfer functions, Fig. 3 of arXiv:2011.13458 (Sect. 2.3) |
| Best-fitting thermal curve (Vogel et al. Eqs. 6–8) | the model is associated with the thermal $T(k)=1/2$ suppression scale (arXiv:2507.18752, Fig. 6 caption) | depends on mixing angle as well as mass | residuals $\lesssim 20\%$ at suppression 1/2 (arXiv:2507.18752, Sect. II.3) |
| Three-parameter fit and $k_{1/2}$ (Murgia et al. Eqs. 4–5) | shape described by α, β, γ | bound is a surface, weaker than the thermal one | true against fitted transfer functions, Sect. 6 of arXiv:1704.07838 |
| Linear 1D-power criteria (Schneider Eqs. 6–7; Murgia et al. Eqs. 12–15) | bias and mean-flux effects as in the thermal reference | no likelihood rerun | non-linear spectra give "consistent results" (arXiv:1704.07838, Sect. 5.2) |
| Satellite counts from P(k) (Schneider Eqs. 8–9) | sharp-k EPS, host mass, satellite mass threshold | model tested directly | simulated mass functions "in good agreement" with the formalism, App. B of arXiv:1704.07838 (Sect. 5.1) |

Schutz warns that "recasting a WDM limit for these models requires choosing which feature of the transfer function to match, which can affect the quoted strength of the resulting limit", and that compressing the transfer function "to a single WDM mass eliminates the information that distinguishes between different models" (arXiv:2608.24990) [src: Sect. V]. Murgia et al. find that the two probes "probe slightly different scales": model nCDM35 "is allowed by the Lyman-α forest, while it is excluded by satellite counts" (arXiv:1704.07838) [src: Sect. 5.2, text after Eq. 16]. <!-- num: not a value -->

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Thermal masses from two calibrations.** A bound obtained with the Viel et al. fit and a model map calibrated on Vogel & Abazajian (Eq. 8 of arXiv:2507.18752 uses the latter) mix two thermal masses; Vogel & Abazajian put the difference at 14% at 10 keV for spin-1/2, matched at $T_X(k)=1/2$ (arXiv:2210.10753) [src: Sect. IV]. Check: convert with Eq. 7 of arXiv:2507.18752 first.
- **Half-mode convention.** $T^2=1/2$ (arXiv:1704.07838; arXiv:2011.13458) against $T=1/2$ (arXiv:1112.0330; arXiv:2210.10753) [src: Sect. 2; Sect. 2.3; Sect. 2.1; Sect. IV]. <!-- eq: notation --> Check: state which, and recompute $k_{1/2}$ or $M_{\rm hm}$ from the paper's own definition.
- **Dodelson–Widrow treated as a scaled thermal spectrum.** Murgia et al. say it is non-thermal and that bounds shift (arXiv:1704.07838) [src: Sect. 3.1, footnote 2]; two published conversions give 26.4 against 31.7 keV for the same input (arXiv:1601.07553) [src: Sect. 4.1, footnote 3]. Check: name the conversion with every sterile-neutrino mass.
- **A printed slip in a moment formula.** As printed, Eq. 2.28 of arXiv:2011.13458 (v2) is not the moment of Eq. 2.27 defined by Eq. 2.14; Eq. 2.29 is consistent with Eq. 2.14 `[der]`. No erratum was looked for.
- **Second-moment map outside its premise.** It needs $w\propto a^{-2}$ when the Lyman-α modes enter, and "A different a-dependence of w(a) would affect our matching procedure"; for cannibal dark matter, with $w\propto 1/\log a$ while number-changing processes are active, the paper reports from its Refs. [36, 74] that the power spectrum "can be matched to a WDM one with a good precision" "by introducing a similar matching procedure up to a correction factor of order one" (arXiv:2011.13458) [src: Sect. 2.3, inline, text before Fig. 3 caption]; it is least accurate for non-thermal relativistic decays and n = 6 thermal freeze-in [src: Sect. 2.3]. Strongly coupled freeze-in spectra are "not captured by the common αβγ-parametrization" either (`formalism.md#linear-signatures-free-streaming-and-the-warm`).
- **A thermal shape assumed for a model that does not have it.** Bounds quoted for the thermal shape "refer to a very specific shape of the power suppression" (arXiv:1704.07838) [src: Sect. 5.2]. Check: fit Eq. 4 of arXiv:1704.07838 to the model's transfer function, or rerun the likelihood.
- **A thermal history that is not radiation dominated.** If an early matter-dominated era dilutes the relic, "the perturbations would have evolved through an expansion history that was different from the radiation-dominated one assumed in deriving WDM transfer functions" (arXiv:2608.24990) [src: abstract]. Models that enhance small-scale power cannot be expressed by a parametrisation "whose single degree of freedom interpolates between 'suppressed' and 'not suppressed'" (arXiv:2608.24990) [src: Sect. V, footnote 5].
- **Linear proxies used as a final bound.** Both 1D-power criteria are approximations of the flux power, and "the derived bounds should be robustly checked against full hydrodynamic simulations" (arXiv:1704.07838) [src: Sect. 5.2].
