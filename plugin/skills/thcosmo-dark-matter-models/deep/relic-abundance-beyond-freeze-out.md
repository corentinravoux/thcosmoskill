## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- Edsjö & Gondolo (arXiv:hep-ph/9704361, Edsjö & Gondolo 1997) order the N coannihilating species as $m_{1}\leq m_{2}\leq\cdots\leq m_{N-1}\leq m_{N}$, write the scale factor R, use $x=m_{1}/T$, and parametrise the energy and entropy densities with $g_{\rm eff}$ and $h_{\rm eff}$; their relative velocity $v_{ij}$ is the Møller-type combination of their Eq. 31, and they use Maxwell–Boltzmann statistics throughout (arXiv:hep-ph/9704361) [src: Sects. 3.1–3.3]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- Binder, Bringmann, Gustafsson & Hryczuk (arXiv:1706.07433, Binder et al. 2017) write the entropy degrees of freedom $g^{s}_{\rm eff}$ and an effective Hubble rate $\tilde{H}\equiv H/[1+\tilde{g}(x)]$ [src: Sect. II.1, text after Eq. 17], and stress that $\langle\sigma v\rangle$ is "a thermal average of $\sigma v_{\rm lab}$ rather than any other combination of cross section and velocity"; for $\sigma v_{\rm CMS}$ constant, $\langle\sigma v\rangle\to\sigma v_{\rm CMS}$ "only in the limit $T\to 0$" (arXiv:1706.07433) [src: Sect. II.1, text after Eq. 12].
- Biondini & Vogl (arXiv:2610.03718, Biondini & Vogl 2026) count particles and antiparticles in one density, $n_{\textrm{eq}}=2n_{X,\textrm{eq}}$ (arXiv:2610.03718) [src: Sect. 3, inline, text after Eq. 21], so that their Boltzmann equation, Eq. 21, carries a factor one half in front of the effective cross section [src: Eq. 21]. <!-- num: not a value --> Their Hubble rate in radiation domination is $H=\sqrt{4\pi^{3}g_{{\mbox{\scriptsize eff}}}(T)/45}T^{2}/m_{{\mbox{\tiny\rm{Pl}}}}$ (arXiv:2610.03718) [src: Sect. 3, footnote 4, inline]. <!-- num: not a value -->
  - `[der]` Their $m_{\rm Pl}$ enters as the non-reduced Planck mass in this form, while Safdi's $m_{\rm pl}$ is the reduced one (`formalism.md#conventions`); check which one a coefficient assumes.

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — several species: from N Boltzmann equations to one
<!-- key: step-1-several-species-from-n-boltzmann -->

Griest & Seckel (Phys. Rev. D 43 (1991) 3191, doi:10.1103/PhysRevD.43.3191) are pre-arXiv; their content enters here through readable papers that cite them. D'Agnolo, Pappadopulo & Ruderman summarise the paper as one that "enumerates three “exceptions” to thermal relic cosmology: (1) mutual annihilations of multiple species (coannihilations), (2) annihilations into heaver states (forbidden channels), and (3) annihilations near a pole in the cross section" (arXiv:1705.08450) [sec: Introduction, text after the abstract], the misspelling "heaver" as printed. <!-- num: not a value --> Edsjö & Gondolo state that "Griest and Seckel [5] have worked out the Boltzmann equation when coannihilations are included", review those expressions and recast them (arXiv:hep-ph/9704361) [sec: Sect. 3].

For N species $\chi_i$ with conserved R-parity, the number density of species i obeys $\frac{dn_{i}}{dt}=-3Hn_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle\sigma_{ij}v_{ij}\rangle\left(n_{i}n_{j}-n_{i}^{\rm{eq}}n_{j}^{\rm{eq}}\right)-\sum_{j\neq i}\big[\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{Xij}v_{ij}\rangle\left(n_{i}n_{X}-n_{i}^{\rm{eq}}n_{X}^{\rm{eq}}\right)-\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{Xji}v_{ij}\rangle\left(n_{j}n_{X}-n_{j}^{\rm{eq}}n_{X}^{\rm{eq}}\right)\big]-\sum_{j\neq i}\big[\Gamma_{ij}\left(n_{i}-n_{i}^{\rm{eq}}\right)-\Gamma_{ji}\left(n_{j}-n_{j}^{\rm{eq}}\right)\big].$ (arXiv:hep-ph/9704361) [src: Eq. 27], the three rows of one display: annihilations, conversions by scattering off the bath, and decays [src: Sect. 3.1, text after Eq. 27]. Since every heavier species decays into the lightest, only the sum $n=\sum_{i=1}^{N}n_{i}.$ [src: Eq. 35] matters, and the conversion and decay rows "cancel in the sum" [src: Sect. 3.1, text after Eq. 36].

The step that closes the system is an assumption on the conversion rates: scattering off the relativistic bath is much faster than annihilation, because the bath density is not Boltzmann suppressed, so the species stay in relative equilibrium, $\frac{n_{i}}{n}\simeq\frac{n_{i}^{\rm{eq}}}{n^{\rm{eq}}}.$ (arXiv:hep-ph/9704361) [src: Eq. 37]. Then $\frac{dn}{dt}=-3Hn-\langle\sigma_{\rm{eff}}v\rangle\left(n^{2}-n_{\rm{eq}}^{2}\right)$ [src: Eq. 38] with $\langle\sigma_{\rm{eff}}v\rangle=\sum_{ij}\langle\sigma_{ij}v_{ij}\rangle\frac{n_{i}^{\rm{eq}}}{n^{\rm{eq}}}\frac{n_{j}^{\rm{eq}}}{n^{\rm{eq}}}.$ (arXiv:hep-ph/9704361) [src: Eq. 39]: the single-species equation of `formalism.md#thermal-freeze-out` with an effective cross-section.

### Step 2 — the thermal average as a one-dimensional integral (Gondolo–Gelmini, generalised)
<!-- key: step-2-the-thermal-average-as-a-one -->

Edsjö & Gondolo write the annihilation rate per unit volume covariantly with the Lorentz-invariant rate $W_{ij}=4p_{ij}\sqrt{s}\sigma_{ij}=4\sigma_{ij}\sqrt{(p_{i}\cdot p_{j})^{2}-m_{i}^{2}m_{j}^{2}}=4E_{i}E_{j}\sigma_{ij}v_{ij}.$ (arXiv:hep-ph/9704361) [src: Eq. 44], change variables to $E_{+}$, $E_{-}$ and s (their Eqs. 49–56) and integrate $E_-$ and $E_+$ analytically, which is possible because the Maxwell–Boltzmann product depends only on $E_+$ and $W_{ij}$ only on s "due to the neglect of final state statistical factors" [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 54]. Summing the channels into one effective rate, $W_{\rm{eff}}=\sum_{ij}\frac{p_{ij}}{p_{11}}\frac{g_{i}g_{j}}{g_{1}^{2}}W_{ij}=\sum_{ij}\sqrt{\frac{[s-(m_{i}-m_{j})^{2}][s-(m_{i}+m_{j})^{2}]}{s(s-4m_{1}^{2})}}\frac{g_{i}g_{j}}{g_{1}^{2}}W_{ij}.$ [src: Eq. 60], equivalently $\sigma_{\rm eff}=\sum_{ij}\frac{p^{2}_{ij}}{p^{2}_{11}}\frac{g_{i}g_{j}}{g_{1}^{2}}\sigma_{ij}.$ [src: Eq. 61], the thermal average becomes $\langle\sigma_{\rm{eff}}v\rangle=\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}dp_{\rm{eff}}p_{\rm{eff}}^{2}W_{\rm{eff}}K_{1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{s}}{T}\right)}{m_{1}^{4}T\left[\sum_{i}\frac{g_{i}}{g_{1}}\frac{m_{i}^{2}}{m_{1}^{2}}K_{2}\left(\frac{m_{i}}{T}\right)\right]^{2}}.$ (arXiv:hep-ph/9704361) [src: Eq. 65], which "correctly reduces to the formula in Gondolo and Gelmini" without coannihilations [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 65].

What this buys: $W_{\rm eff}(p_{\rm eff})$ does not depend on T, so it is tabulated once per model before the thermal average [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 65]; and "coannihilations appear as thresholds at $\sqrt{s}$ equal to the sum of the masses of the coannihilating particles", exactly like final-state thresholds [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 65]. Resonances, thresholds and coannihilations are thus all features of one function of s, integrated exactly rather than expanded in velocity.

### Step 3 — the abundance equation and the conversion to Ωh²
<!-- key: step-3-the-abundance-equation-and-the -->

With $Y=n/s$, entropy conservation, and the radiation-dominated Friedmann equation, Eq. 38 becomes $\frac{dY}{dx}=-\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{45G}}\frac{g_{*}^{1/2}m_{1}}{x^{2}}\langle\sigma_{\rm{eff}}v\rangle\left(Y^{2}-Y_{\rm{eq}}^{2}\right)$ (arXiv:hep-ph/9704361) [src: Eq. 76], with $g_{*}^{1/2}=\frac{h_{\rm{eff}}}{\sqrt{g_{\rm{eff}}}}\left(1+\frac{T}{3h_{\rm{eff}}}\frac{dh_{\rm{eff}}}{dT}\right)$ [src: Eq. 78], and the relic density follows from the asymptotic yield, $\Omega_{\chi}h^{2}=2.755\times 10^{8}\frac{m_{\chi}}{\mbox{GeV}}Y_{0}.$ (arXiv:hep-ph/9704361) [src: Eq. 80] for $T_0$ = 2.726 K [src: Sect. 3.3, text before Eq. 80]. The derivative term in Eq. 78 is where the temperature dependence of $h_{\rm eff}$, and so the Standard-Model equation of state, enters (see "Variants"). Binder et al. print the same chain in the form $\frac{Y^{\prime}}{Y}=\frac{sY}{x\tilde{H}}\left\langle\sigma v\right\rangle\left[\frac{Y_{\rm eq}^{2}}{Y^{2}}-1\right]\,.$ (arXiv:1706.07433) [src: Eq. 17] with $\tilde{g}\equiv\frac{1}{3}\frac{T}{g^{s}_{\rm\text{eff}}}\frac{dg^{s}_{\rm\text{eff}}}{dT}\,.$ [src: Eq. 18], and note that Eq. 17 "is the basis for the implementation of relic density calculations in all major numerical codes" (arXiv:1706.07433) [src: Sect. II.1, text after Eq. 19].

Numerically, the equation is stiff: Edsjö & Gondolo integrate it from x = 2 "with an implicit trapezoidal method with adaptive stepsize" (arXiv:hep-ph/9704361) [src: Sect. 5].

### Step 4 — forbidden channels: the thermal average by detailed balance
<!-- key: step-4-forbidden-channels-the-thermal-average -->

When dark matter ψ annihilates dominantly into heavier states, the channel "vanish[es] at zero temperature" and proceeds only through the thermal tail (arXiv:1505.07107, D'Agnolo & Ruderman 2015) [src: Introduction]. The rate is obtained not by integrating the tail but from the inverse process, which is open at zero temperature: detailed balance gives $\left<\sigma_{\psi\bar{\psi}}\,v\right>=\frac{(n_{\gamma_{d}}^{eq})^{2}}{(n_{\psi}^{eq})^{2}}\left<\sigma_{\gamma_{d}\gamma_{d}}\,v\right>\approx 8\pi f_{\Delta}\frac{\alpha_{d}^{2}}{m_{\psi}^{2}}\,e^{-2\Delta x},$ (arXiv:1505.07107) [src: Eq. 3], with Δ the relative mass splitting and $f_\Delta$ given in the text after Eq. 3 [src: text after Eq. 3], assuming the dark photon stays in equilibrium during freeze-out [src: text before Eq. 3]. Two statements on the 1991 treatment, as the paper prints them: "the approximation of the forbidden cross section in Ref. [8] has an incorrect exponential dependence on Δx", and "The standard lore is that forbidden channels are only relevant in highly degenerate scenarios, Δ≪1 (this was stated by Ref. [8] which implicitly assumes weak scale DM)", whereas "light DM calls for an $\mathcal{O}(1)$ splitting" (arXiv:1505.07107) [src: text after Eq. 3; text after Eq. 4], Ref. [8] being Griest & Seckel [src: References]. <!-- num: not a value -->

### Step 5 — coscattering: the fourth exception
<!-- key: step-5-coscattering-the-fourth-exception -->

Step 1 assumed conversions $\chi\phi\leftrightarrow\psi\phi$ faster than annihilations. D'Agnolo, Pappadopulo & Ruderman keep Griest & Seckel's other assumptions (initial equilibrium, 2-to-2 annihilations, dark-matter temperature tracking the photons) and reverse the order: "In the coannihilation phase, it is assumed that process (2) decouples before process (1)"; in "coscattering, … process (1) shuts off before process (2), such that the DM abundance is determined by the freeze-out of inelastic scattering", generically "if DM scatters against massive states, $m_{\phi}\sim m_{\chi}$" (arXiv:1705.08450) [src: Introduction]. <!-- num: not a value --> Eq. 37 of Edsjö & Gondolo then fails and the conversion row of their Eq. 27 must be kept. In the coscattering paper the kinetic-equilibrium solution (their Eq. 23) and the full Boltzmann equation (their Eq. 11) are compared in their Fig. 2 (arXiv:1705.08450) [src: Fig. 2 caption]; neither equation was transcribed here.

### Step 6 — Sommerfeld enhancement and bound-state formation
<!-- key: step-6-sommerfeld-enhancement-and-bound-state -->

With a light mediator, the tree-level s-wave rate is multiplied by a velocity-dependent factor, $\sigma v_{\rm lab}=S(v_{\rm lab})\times(\sigma v_{\rm lab})_{0}\,,$ (arXiv:2103.01944, Binder et al. 2021) [src: Eq. 24]. For a Yukawa potential approximated by a Hulthén potential, $S(v_{\rm lab})=\frac{\frac{\pi}{\epsilon_{v}}\sinh\!\Big[\frac{12\epsilon_{v}}{\pi\epsilon_{A}}\Big]}{\cosh\!\Big[\frac{12\epsilon_{v}}{\pi\epsilon_{A}}\Big]-\cos\!\Big[2\pi\sqrt{\frac{6}{\pi^{2}\epsilon_{A}}\!-\!\left(\frac{6\epsilon_{v}}{\pi^{2}\epsilon_{A}}\right)^{2}}\Big]},$ (arXiv:2103.01944) [src: Eq. 25], with $\epsilon_{v}\equiv v_{\text{lab}}/(2\alpha_{\chi})$ and $\epsilon_{A}\equiv m_{A}/(\alpha_{\chi}m_{\chi})$ [src: Sect. 4.2, inline, text after Eq. 25]. Zero-energy bound states exist "precisely at threshold for $m_{A}/(\alpha_{\chi}m_{\chi})=6/(n^{2}\pi^{2})$" and near these parametric resonances "$S(v_{\rm lab})\propto 1/v_{\rm lab}^{2}$ for $v_{\rm lab}\lesssim m_{A}/m_{\chi}$, leading to a second period of annihilation after kinetic decoupling"; away from them "$S(v_{\rm lab})\propto 1/v_{\rm lab}$" (arXiv:2103.01944) [src: Sect. 4.2]. <!-- num: not a value -->

Bound-state formation adds a second depletion channel. Neglecting transitions between bound states, the Boltzmann equation keeps one density, $(\partial_{t}+3H)n=-\frac{1}{2}\langle\sigma_{\textrm{eff}}\,v_{\textrm{rel}}\rangle(n^{2}-n^{2}_{\textrm{eq}})\,,$ (arXiv:2610.03718) [src: Eq. 21], with $\langle\sigma_{\textrm{eff}}\,v_{\textrm{rel}}\rangle=\langle\sigma_{\textrm{ann}}v_{\textrm{rel}}\rangle+\sum_{n}\left(\frac{1}{4}\langle\sigma^{n}_{\textrm{bsf}}\,v_{\textrm{rel}}\rangle\,\frac{\Gamma^{n,\hbox{\scriptsize para}}_{\textrm{ann}}}{\Gamma^{n,\hbox{\scriptsize para}}_{\textrm{ann}}+\Gamma_{\textrm{bsd}}^{n}}+\frac{3}{4}\langle\sigma^{n}_{\textrm{bsf}}\,v_{\textrm{rel}}\rangle\,\frac{\Gamma^{n,\hbox{\scriptsize ortho}}_{\textrm{ann}}}{\Gamma^{n,\hbox{\scriptsize ortho}}_{\textrm{ann}}+\Gamma_{\textrm{bsd}}^{n}}\right).$ (arXiv:2610.03718) [src: Eq. 22], the paradarkonium and orthodarkonium terms weighted 1/4 and 3/4 [src: Eq. 22]; the two "are treated on the same footing, as orthodarkonium and paradarkonium decay at the same order in the coupling" [src: Sect. 3, text after Eq. 22]. <!-- num: not a value --> Eliminating the formation cross-section by detailed balance gives $\langle\sigma^{n}_{\textrm{bsf}}\,v_{\textrm{rel}}\rangle\,\frac{\Gamma^{n,\hbox{\scriptsize para}}_{\textrm{dec}}}{\Gamma^{n,\hbox{\scriptsize para}}_{\textrm{dec}}+\Gamma_{\textrm{bsd}}^{n}}=\frac{\Gamma^{n,\hbox{\scriptsize para}}_{\textrm{dec}}\,\Gamma_{\textrm{bsd}}^{n}}{\Gamma^{n,\hbox{\scriptsize para}}_{\textrm{dec}}+\Gamma_{\textrm{bsd}}^{n}}e^{\frac{|E_{n}|}{T}}\left(\frac{2\pi}{\mu_{{\mbox{\tiny\rm{DM}}}}T}\right)^{3/2}\,\equiv\Gamma^{n,\textrm{para}}_{\textrm{red}}e^{\frac{|E_{n}|}{T}}\left(\frac{2\pi}{\mu_{{\mbox{\tiny\rm{DM}}}}T}\right)^{3/2}\,,$ (arXiv:2610.03718) [src: Eq. 26]: a "reduced" width that "approaches the smaller of the two rates when a strong hierarchy exists between them", so the contribution follows the decay width at high T and the dissociation rate at low T [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 26]. For $T\ll|E_{1}|$ the bound-state term "scales as $1/\sqrt{T}$", like the Sommerfeld-enhanced annihilation, and "their relative importance is reduced to a constant $\mathcal{O}(1)$ factor" (arXiv:2610.03718) [src: Sect. 3.2.1]. <!-- num: not a value --> The structure of Eq. 22 is kept when transitions are included, but "a matrix involving combinations of decay, dissociation, and transitions widths of bound state also enters" [src: Sect. 3, text before Eq. 21].

### Step 7 — early kinetic decoupling: from one moment to two, or to the full phase space
<!-- key: step-7-early-kinetic-decoupling-from-one -->

Every step above integrates the phase-space Boltzmann equation over momentum with the ansatz $f_{\chi}=A(T)f_{\chi,{\rm eq}}=\frac{n_{\chi}}{n_{\chi,{\rm eq}}}f_{\chi,{\rm eq}}\,,$ (arXiv:1706.07433) [src: Eq. 11], justified when "DM-SM scattering typically proceeds at a much faster rate than DM-DM annihilation" [src: Sect. II.1, text after Eq. 11]; with Maxwell–Boltzmann statistics this gives the standard equation $\frac{dn_{\chi}}{dt}+3Hn_{\chi}=\langle\sigma v\rangle\left(n_{\chi,{\rm eq}}^{2}-n_{\chi}^{2}\right)\,,$ (arXiv:1706.07433) [src: Eq. 12]. When the momentum-exchange rate is small (resonances, Boltzmann-suppressed scattering partners, light mediators), the ansatz fails.

Binder et al. add the second moment, $y\equiv\frac{m_{\chi}}{3s^{2/3}}\left\langle\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}}{E}\right\rangle=\frac{m_{\chi}}{3s^{2/3}}\frac{g_{\chi}}{n_{\chi}}\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}}{E}f_{\chi}(\mathbf{p})\,.$ (arXiv:1706.07433) [src: Eq. 20], which defines a dark-matter temperature $T_{\chi}=ys^{2/3}/m_{\chi}\,.$ [src: Eq. 21], and obtain the coupled system $\frac{Y^{\prime}}{Y}=\frac{sY}{x\tilde{H}}\left[\frac{Y_{\rm eq}^{2}}{Y^{2}}\left\langle\sigma v\right\rangle-\left\langle\sigma v\right\rangle_{\rm neq}\right]\,,$ [src: Eq. 27] and $\frac{y^{\prime}}{y}=\frac{\gamma(T)}{x\tilde{H}}\left[\frac{y_{{\rm eq}}}{y}-1\right]+\frac{sY}{x\tilde{H}}\left[\left\langle\sigma v\right\rangle_{\rm neq}-\left\langle\sigma v\right\rangle_{2,{\rm neq}}\right]+\frac{sY}{x\tilde{H}}\frac{Y_{\rm eq}^{2}}{Y^{2}}\left[\frac{y_{{\rm eq}}}{y}\left\langle\sigma v\right\rangle_{2}-\left\langle\sigma v\right\rangle\right]+\frac{H}{x\tilde{H}}\frac{\langle p^{4}/E^{3}\rangle_{\rm neq}}{3T_{\chi}}\,.$ (arXiv:1706.07433) [src: Eq. 28], the two rows of one display, γ(T) being the "momentum exchange rate" of their Eqs. 5–6 [src: Sect. II]. The system does not close in Y and y; it is closed by evaluating the out-of-equilibrium averages at the dark-matter temperature of Eq. 21 [src: Eqs. 31–33], which is exact for a Maxwellian at a temperature of its own, $f_{\chi}=\frac{n_{\chi}(T)}{n_{\chi,\text{eq}}(T_{\chi})}\exp\left(-\frac{E}{T_{\chi}}\right)\bigg|_{T_{\chi}=ys^{2/3}/m_{\chi}}\,,$ (arXiv:1706.07433) [src: Eq. 34], as "expected, e.g., if the DM particles exhibit significant self-scattering" [src: Sect. II.2, text after Eq. 34]. The third option is the full phase-space solution of their Sect. II.3 [src: Sect. II.3, title].

Applied to the scalar singlet near the Higgs resonance: "Outside the resonance region, the coupled Boltzmann equations lead to identical results compared to the standard approach"; inside it "the two methods can give significantly different results", and a smaller scattering rate "leads to an even larger deviation" (arXiv:1706.07433) [src: Sect. III.2]. The coupled system "provides a qualitatively very good description", "even though for high-precision results it seems mandatory to actually solve the full Boltzmann equation in phase space" [src: Sect. IV].

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **Three Boltzmann solvers in one code (DRAKE).** DRAKE implements "the traditionally adopted equation for the dark matter number density" (nBE), "fluid-like equations that couple the evolution of number density and velocity dispersion" (cBE) "and a full numerical evolution of the phase-space distribution" (fBE); the traditional treatment "can result in an estimate of the relic density that is wrong by up to an order of magnitude" in its three examples, narrow resonance, Sommerfeld enhancement and forbidden annihilation (arXiv:2103.01944) [src: abstract]. Between cBE and fBE, for the Sommerfeld case, "the difference between cBE and fBE is as expected rather small, independently of the vicinity to a parametric resonance" [src: Sect. 4.2]; for narrow resonances the fBE resolves phase-space distributions that "strongly differ from thermal ones" [src: Sect. 4.1].
- **Momentum-dependent solution near resonances (Ala-Mattinen & Kainulainen).** For sharp resonances "the usual momentum averaged approximations for computing the DM abundance may not be accurate"; their accuracy "depend[s] strongly on the strength of the elastic interactions", but in the singlet-scalar model "the standard model scatterings induce an efficient kinetic equilibrium that validates the momentum averaged computation to 20 per cent accuracy" (arXiv:1912.02870) [src: abstract].
- **Semi-analytic post-freeze-out integral (Biondini & Vogl).** Once a freeze-out point is fixed by $(Y-Y_{\rm eq})/Y_{\rm eq}=c$, the yield is a single integral (their Eq. 58); "A conventional choice is $c=\sqrt{2}$", while "in micrOMEGAs one finds $c=1.5$", and "We compared the choice $c=\sqrt{2}$ with $c=1.5$ and find that the difference in the freeze-out temperature obtained from the numerical and semi-analytical determinations remains below 0.2% over the range of $\alpha$ considered in this work" (arXiv:2610.03718) [src: Sect. 4.2]. The semi-analytic yield agrees with the numerical one "to better than 1%" for pure s-wave, Sommerfeld-enhanced and Sommerfeld plus ground-state bound-state cases [src: Sect. 5]; with their approximate rates the error "remains below 5% over the entire range of couplings considered" [src: Sect. 5]. Cost: it applies "directly to the ground-state, no-transition case" [src: Sect. 4.2].
- **Standard-Model equation of state.** Updating $g_{\rm eff}$ and $h_{\rm eff}$ to lattice-QCD and electroweak-corrected values changes the abundance by "nearly 10% … in the 1-1000 GeV dark matter abundance, compared to the conventional estimate of the EOS" (arXiv:2005.03544, Saikawa & Shirai 2020) [src: abstract]; "depending on the treatment of the lattice data and perturbative calculation, the estimated effective degrees of freedom can vary by about 10%" [src: Sect. 2]. Their tables can be loaded into micrOMEGAs v5, which "seems to adopt $g_{s,0}=3.906$" against their $g_{s,0}=3.931$, so that "multiplying the factor $3.931/3.906\simeq 1.006$ by the micrOMEGAs v5 output with eos2020.dat reproduces our result quite accurately" (arXiv:2005.03544) [src: Sect. 4, inline].
- **Which code does what.** All public codes listed by the DRAKE paper (DarkSUSY, MadDM, micrOMEGAs, SuperIso Relic) "currently rely on the standard approach", which "can be re-cast in the form of a single Boltzmann equation for the DM number density even when including sharp resonances, thresholds or co-annihilations (all of which were initially considered complications [23])", [23] being Griest & Seckel (arXiv:2103.01944) [src: Sect. 1; References]. "As of version 6.2.5, along with the release of DRAKE, DarkSUSY additionally provides numerical routines to solve the coupled system" of number density and velocity dispersion [src: Sect. 1, footnote 5]. <!-- num: not a value --> DarkSUSY 6 tabulates $W_{\rm eff}$ once per model "with a particularly dense sampling of potentially critical regions like thresholds and resonances" (arXiv:1802.03399, Bringmann et al. 2018) [src: Sect. 4.1]. micrOMEGAs 7 adds "user-defined modifications of the Hubble expansion rate, entropy evolution, and non-thermal dark matter production from late-decaying cosmological components", for "low-temperature reheating, early matter domination, and kination" (arXiv:2606.06645, Belanger et al. 2026) [src: abstract]; its paper was not found to treat Sommerfeld or bound-state effects in the relic calculation (the one match for "Sommerfeld" is in its CMB-constraint section) [src: Sect. 5.1].

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Velocity expansion near a pole or threshold.** The effective rate has resonances and thresholds as sharp features in s (arXiv:hep-ph/9704361) [src: Sect. 3.2, Fig. 1 caption]; Edsjö & Gondolo split the thermal-average integral "at each sharp threshold" and "explicitly check for each MSSM model that the spline routine behaves well at thresholds and resonances" [src: Sect. 5]. `[der]` An expansion of $\sigma v$ in powers of $v^2$ cannot represent such features. Check: integrate Eq. 65 with the exact $W_{\rm eff}$.
- **t- and u-channel singularities in coannihilation.** "In rare cases, we find resonances in the t- or u-channels", regulated "by assigning a small width of a few GeV to the neutralinos and charginos"; "Our results are not sensitive to the choice of this width" (arXiv:hep-ph/9704361) [src: Sect. 5].
- **Kinetic equilibrium assumed but not checked.** Local thermal equilibrium at freeze-out is "the usual assumption, though rarely explicitly tested", including for semi-annihilations, modified expansion histories and processes beyond 2 → 2 (arXiv:1706.07433) [src: Sect. IV]. <!-- num: not a value --> Check: solve the coupled system of Eqs. 27–28, or run the cBE of DRAKE, and compare with the nBE.
- **Fokker–Planck scattering term with heavy partners.** The approximate elastic collision term "relies on the assumption of small momentum transfer compared to the average DM momentum – which is typically not satisfied for scattering partners that are close in mass to the DM particle" (arXiv:2103.01944) [src: Sect. 4.3]; DRAKE's App. B gives a full term for a constant amplitude [src: Sect. 4.3].
- **Old approximations for forbidden channels.** Griest & Seckel's approximation "has an incorrect exponential dependence on Δx" (arXiv:1505.07107) [src: text after Eq. 3]; use the detailed-balance form of Eq. 3 or a numerical solution. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Coannihilation assumed where coscattering holds.** If conversions decouple before annihilations, Eq. 37 of arXiv:hep-ph/9704361 fails (arXiv:1705.08450) [src: Introduction]; check the conversion rate against H at freeze-out.
- **Bound-state transitions dropped.** The no-transition effective cross-section is not enough "if a precision of better than 15% is required" when excited states and transitions matter (arXiv:2610.03718) [src: Sect. 4.2].
- **Sommerfeld enhancement and kinetic decoupling together.** Near parametric resonances a second annihilation era follows kinetic decoupling, with self-heating of the dark matter (arXiv:2103.01944) [src: Sect. 4.2; Fig. 6 caption]; an nBE treatment with $T_\chi=T$ misses it.
- **Equation of state and $g_{s,0}$.** The default degrees of freedom of a code and the value of $g_{s,0}$ shift the abundance at the level of the Numbers table (arXiv:2005.03544) [src: Sect. 4]; state the tables used.
