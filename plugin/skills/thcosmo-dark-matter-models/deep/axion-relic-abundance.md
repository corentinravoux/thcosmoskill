## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

- Three parametrisations of the temperature dependence are in use, each stated by its paper. Safdi writes $m_a(T)^2\propto T^{-n}$ with $n\approx 8.2$ (`formalism.md#misalignment-axions-and-axion-like-particles`). Di Luzio, Giannotti, Nardi & Visinelli (arXiv:2003.01100, Di Luzio et al. 2020) write the mass itself as $m_{a}(T)\simeq\beta\,m_{a}\left(\frac{T_{C}}{T}\right)^{\gamma}\,,$ (arXiv:2003.01100) [src: Eq. 150] with "$\gamma\approx 4$, $T_{C}\approx 160$ MeV" and β "of the order of a few × 10⁻²" in the dilute instanton gas approximation (DIGA) [src: Sect. 3.2, text after Eq. 150]; they relate the two by stating that the lattice result of their Ref. [126] has "$2\gamma=8.2$" at $T\gg T_{C}$, against "$2\gamma=6.68$" for the interacting instanton liquid model [src: Sect. 3.7.1]. Gorghetto, Hardy & Villadoro (arXiv:2007.04990) use an index α of the axion potential, set to "$\alpha=8$, which corresponds to the prediction of the dilute instanton gas approximation at weak coupling" (arXiv:2007.04990) [src: Sect. 4]; Correia et al. (arXiv:2609.20801) write the susceptibility index as $n_\tau$ (arXiv:2609.20801) [src: Sect. VI]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- The misalignment angle is θ = a/f_a throughout; Safdi and Di Luzio et al. write the scale factor R (arXiv:2303.02169, Eq. 60; arXiv:2003.01100, Eq. 152). <!-- num: not a value --> Di Luzio et al. write the Hubble rate with the non-reduced Planck mass, "$H(T)=1.66\,g_{*}^{1/2}\;T^{2}/m_{\rm Pl}$" (arXiv:2003.01100) [src: Sect. 3.3, inline, text after Eq. 167], and Borsanyi et al. write the Friedmann equation with $M_{Pl}$, $H^{2}=\frac{8\pi}{3M_{Pl}^{2}}\rho$ (arXiv:1606.07494) [src: Eq. S32]; Safdi's $m_{\rm pl}$ is the reduced one (`formalism.md#conventions`).
- Post-inflationary string simulations measure time by the logarithm of the scale hierarchy, $\log(m_{r}/H)$ with $m_r$ the radial-mode mass (arXiv:2007.04990) [src: Sect. 2], written ℓ by Saikawa et al. (arXiv:2401.17253) [src: Sect. VII]; the physical value is "$\log(m_{r}/H)=60\div 70$" (arXiv:2007.04990) [src: Fig. 1 caption, inline]. <!-- num: not a value -->

## The derivation
<!-- key: the-derivation -->

### Step 1 — the zero mode: frozen, then oscillating
<!-- key: step-1-the-zero-mode-frozen-then-oscillating -->

Truncating the periodic potential at quadratic order, the angle obeys $\ddot{\theta}+3H\dot{\theta}-\frac{1}{R^{2}(t)}\nabla^{2}\theta+m_{a}^{2}(t)\theta=0\,,$ (arXiv:2003.01100) [src: Eq. 153]. Super-horizon modes with negligible mass stay frozen, and the field starts to oscillate when $m_{a}(t_{\rm osc})\approx 3H(t_{\rm osc})\,,$ (arXiv:2003.01100) [src: Eq. 154], where "The factor three … serves as a crude approximation", which "should be matched to a numerical solution" [src: Sect. 3.3, footnote 33]. An initial velocity of the field matters only if $\frac{\dot{\theta}_{\rm PQ}}{H_{\rm PQ}}>\frac{6\ T_{\rm PQ}}{T_{\rm osc}}$ [src: Eq. 161], that is "unless the axion velocity at the PQ breaking scale is at least eight orders of magnitude larger than the expansion rate of the Universe, the results for axion DM are indistinguishable from what is obtained by setting … $\dot{\theta}_{i}=0$" (arXiv:2003.01100) [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 161]. <!-- num: not a value -->

After $T_{\rm osc}$ the comoving axion number is conserved. With $n_{a}(T_{\rm osc})=\frac{b}{2}m_{a}(T_{\rm osc})\,f_{a}^{2}\,\langle\theta_{i}^{2}\rangle\,.$ (arXiv:2003.01100) [src: Eq. 165], b "a factor of order one" that absorbs the use of Eq. 154 instead of a numerical solution [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 165], and Eq. 154 combined with the power law of Eq. 150, the oscillation temperature is $T_{\rm osc}^{2+\gamma}\simeq\left(\frac{\beta\,T_{C}^{\gamma}\,m_{\rm Pl}}{4.98\,g_{*}(T_{\rm osc})^{1/2}}\right)\;m_{a}\,.$ (arXiv:2003.01100) [src: Eq. 168], about "$T_{\rm osc}\approx 800\left(\frac{m_{a}}{1\,\mu{\rm eV}}\right)^{1/6}$ MeV" for $g_{*}(T>T_{C})$ = 61.75, γ = 4, $T_C$ = 160 MeV and β ≈ 0.026 [src: Sect. 3.3, inline, text after Eq. 168]. "Neglecting the contribution from the topological defects" [src: Sect. 3.3, text before Eq. 167], the illustrative result is $\Omega_{a}^{\rm mis}h^{2}\approx 0.12\,\left(\frac{28\;\mu\text{eV}}{m_{a}}\right)^{\frac{7}{6}}=0.12\,\,\left(\frac{f_{a}}{2.0\times 10^{11}{\rm\;GeV}}\right)^{\frac{7}{6}}\,,$ (arXiv:2003.01100) [src: Eq. 170], with $\langle\theta_{i}^{2}\rangle=(2.15)^{2}$, b = 1 and the parameters above, "assuming a standard cosmology and canonical properties for the axion" [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 170], which "should be taken only as an illustrative approximation"; more reliable estimates need the temperature dependence of the mass, the anharmonic terms and "the details of the quark-hadron phase transition" [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 170]. The pre-inflationary counterpart with a single $\theta_i$ is Safdi's Eq. 72 (`formalism.md#misalignment-axions-and-axion-like-particles`).

### Step 2 — the lattice χ(T) and a numerical solution
<!-- key: step-2-the-lattice-t-and-a-numerical-solution -->

Borsanyi et al. replace the power law by the lattice topological susceptibility, $V(\theta)=m_{A}^{2}(T)(1-\cos\theta)=\chi(T)/f_{A}^{2}(1-\cos\theta)$ (arXiv:1606.07494) [src: Sect. S11, inline, text after Eq. S31], computed "up to 3 GeV" so that "one does not have to rely on the dilute instanton gas approximation"; "a posteriori the exponent predicted by DIGA turned out to be compatible with our finding but its prefactor is off by an order of magnitude" (arXiv:1606.07494) [src: main text, paragraph after Fig. 3 caption]. They integrate the full cosine equation in temperature, with their lattice equation of state for $t(T)$, $\frac{d^{2}\theta}{dT^{2}}+\left[3H(T)\frac{dt}{dT}-\frac{d^{2}t}{dT^{2}}/\frac{dt}{dT}\right]\frac{d\theta}{dT}+\frac{\chi(T)}{f_{A}^{2}}\left(\frac{dt}{dT}\right)^{2}\sin\theta=0$ (arXiv:1606.07494) [src: Eq. S36], starting at $T=5T_{\rm osc}$ with vanishing first derivative and averaging $n_A/s$ "for the temperature range 0.8 – 0.2$T_{s}$", $T_s$ being the first sign change of θ [src: Sect. S11]. The equation of state matters even below the QCD transition: "a coupling close to the Planck scale results in a $T_{\rm osc}$ below the QCD phase transition", and "Below the transition $T_{\rm osc}\propto m_{A}^{0.47}$ while above the transition $T_{\rm osc}\propto m_{A}^{0.17}$" (arXiv:1606.07494) [src: Sect. S11, text after Eq. S36]; their Fig. S30, which shows $T_{\rm osc}$ against $m_A$, assumes "that all the observed dark matter comes from the axions", "Within this pre-inflation scenario" with "a single $\theta_{0}$ angle" [src: Fig. S30 caption].

### Step 3 — anharmonic corrections and the post-inflationary average
<!-- key: step-3-anharmonic-corrections-and-the-post -->

For a uniform distribution of initial angles and a quadratic potential, $\theta_{i}\equiv\sqrt{\langle\theta_{i}^{2}\rangle}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{3}}\simeq 1.81\,,$ (arXiv:2003.01100) [src: Eq. 162]. For the periodic potential this is modified by non-harmonic terms, parametrised by a function $F(\theta_i)$ that "accounts for the anharmonic corrections in the axion potential": $\langle\theta_{i}^{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta_{i}\,F(\theta_{i})\theta_{i}^{2},$ (arXiv:2003.01100) [src: Eq. 163], with "numerically … $\theta_{i}\approx 2.15$", a value taken from their Ref. [47], Grilli di Cortona et al. [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 163].

Visinelli & Gondolo (arXiv:0903.4377, Visinelli & Gondolo 2009) give an analytic form. The factor has limits $\lim_{\theta_{i}\rightarrow\,0}f(\theta_{i})=1\,\,;\quad\quad\lim_{\theta_{i}\rightarrow\pm\pi}f(\theta_{i})=+\infty.$ (arXiv:0903.4377) [src: Eq. 39], and they adopt $f(\theta_{i})=\left[\ln\left(\frac{e}{1-\theta_{i}^{2}/\pi^{2}}\right)\right]^{7/6}.$ (arXiv:0903.4377) [src: Eq. 40], which "extends Lyth's formula to values of $\theta_{i}<0.9\pi$"; the exponent 7/6 instead of Lyth's 1.175 "comes from the different dependence of the axion mass $m_{a}(T)$ on T", $T^{-3.7}$ for Lyth against $T^{-4}$ for them (arXiv:0903.4377) [src: Sect. V, text after Eq. 40]. The post-inflationary average is $\langle\theta_{i}^{2}f(\theta_{i})\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\theta_{i}^{2}\,f(\theta_{i})\,d\theta_{i}.$ [src: Eq. 36], giving $\langle\theta_{i}^{2}f(\theta_{i})\rangle=8.77.$ (arXiv:0903.4377) [src: Eq. 37] against $\pi^2/3$ without the factor [src: Sect. V, text after Eq. 36]. With the lattice χ(T) and the full cosine, Borsanyi et al. find, "For the whole $m_{A}$ range which is relevant for the post-inflation scenario", that "up to a few per mil the average angle of $\theta_{0}=2.155$ can be used", $\overline{R_{A}}(m_{A})=R_{A}(\theta_{0}=2.155,m_{A})$ (arXiv:1606.07494) [src: Eq. S42], the average being $\overline{R_{A}}(m_{A})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}R_{A}(\theta_{0},m_{A})d\theta_{0}$ [src: Eq. S41]. `[der]` This average is the misalignment part only: it omits strings and walls, which Step 6 adds.

### Step 4 — pre- against post-inflationary breaking
<!-- key: step-4-pre-against-post-inflationary-breaking -->

The case depends on two conditions: "a) The PQ symmetry is spontaneously broken during inflation; b) The PQ symmetry is never restored after its spontaneous breaking occurs", realised respectively when "$f_{a}>H_{I}$" and when the PQ scale exceeds "the maximum temperature reached in the post-inflationary Universe" (arXiv:2003.01100) [src: Sect. 3.3, inline]. If both hold (pre-inflationary), "inflation selects one patch … [with] a homogeneous value of the initial misalignment angle", defects are inflated away, and isocurvature "severely constrain[s] this scenario"; if either fails (post-inflationary), θ takes all values across patches and the proper treatment is "to solve numerically the equation of motion of the PQ field", "including the contribution from topological defects" (arXiv:2003.01100) [src: Sect. 3.3]. <!-- num: not a value --> `[der]` The pre-inflationary case has one free parameter more ($\theta_i$) and a bound from isocurvature (Step 5); the post-inflationary case has a unique mass for which axions are all the dark matter, set by the string-wall network (Step 6).

### Step 5 — the isocurvature bound in the pre-inflationary case
<!-- key: step-5-the-isocurvature-bound-in-the-pre -->

Safdi's chain (arXiv:2303.02169, Sect. 6.4.1, "Cosmological considerations for PQ broken before inflation"):

1. Curvature perturbations shift matter and radiation together, $\begin{split}{\delta\rho_{\rm matter}\over\rho_{\rm matter}}={3\over 4}{\delta\rho_{\rm rad}\over\rho_{\rm rad}}\,,\end{split}$ (arXiv:2303.02169) [src: Eq. 73].
2. A massless axion in de Sitter space has a scale-invariant spectrum, $\begin{split}\langle\delta a({\bm{k}}^{\prime})\delta a({\bm{k}})\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}({\bm{k}}^{\prime}+{\bm{k}})P_{a}({\bm{k}})\,,\qquad\Delta_{a}^{2}\equiv{k^{3}\over 2\pi^{3}}P_{a}={H_{\rm inf}^{2}\over 4\pi^{2}}\,,\end{split}$ (arXiv:2303.02169) [src: Eq. 74], whose variance is $\begin{split}\sigma_{a}^{2}\equiv\langle\delta a({\bm{x}})\delta_{a}({\bm{x}})\rangle=\int{dk\over k}\Delta_{a}^{2}=\Delta_{a}^{2}\log(k_{\rm UV}/k_{\rm IR})\,,\end{split}$ [src: Eq. 76], the second factor printed as $\delta_a$ rather than $\delta a$. <!-- num: not a value --> As printed, Eq. 74 defines $\Delta_a^2$ with $2\pi^3$ in the denominator (arXiv:2303.02169) [src: Eq. 74]. The hub's Fourier convention (`theoretical-cosmology/references/conventions.md#fourier-convention-and-power-spectrum`) should be checked before reusing the normalisation `[der]`.
3. The axion perturbations are uncorrelated with curvature and induce "a relation closer to $\delta\rho_{\rm matter}=-\delta\rho_{\rm rad}$" [src: Sect. 6.4.1, text before Eq. 77]; the matter isocurvature is $\begin{split}\delta\rho_{\rm matter}^{\rm iso}\sim{1\over 2}m_{a}^{2}\left(\theta_{i}f_{a}+\sqrt{\Delta_{a}}\right)^{2}-{1\over 2}m_{a}^{2}\theta_{i}^{2}f_{a}^{2}\sim m_{a}^{2}\theta_{i}f_{a}{H_{\rm inf}\over 2\pi}\,,\end{split}$ [src: Eq. 77] and $\begin{split}{\delta\rho_{\rm matter}^{\rm iso}\over\rho_{\rm matter}}\sim{1\over\pi}{H_{\rm inf}\over|\theta_{i}|f_{a}}\,.\end{split}$ (arXiv:2303.02169) [src: Eq. 78].
4. Planck limits the isocurvature variance to "less than around 1% of $\Delta_{\mathcal{R}}^{2}$", which "implies that ${\delta\rho_{\rm matter}^{\rm iso}\over\rho_{\rm matter}}\lesssim 5\times 10^{-6}$" (arXiv:2303.02169) [src: Sect. 6.4.1, inline, text after Eq. 78]. Eliminating $\theta_i$ with Eq. 72 (the angle that gives the full dark-matter density) yields $\begin{split}H_{\rm inf}\lesssim 2\times 10^{9}\,\,{\rm GeV}\left({f_{a}\over 10^{16}\,\,{\rm GeV}}\right)^{{n+3\over 2n+4}}\approx 2\times 10^{9}\,\,{\rm GeV}\left({f_{a}\over 10^{16}\,\,{\rm GeV}}\right)^{0.45}\,.\end{split}$ (arXiv:2303.02169) [src: Eq. 79]. The text announces combining the Planck limit "with (77)", although the ratio it bounds is that of Eq. 78 [src: Sect. 6.4.1, text after Eq. 78]; the slip is copied, not corrected.
5. Consequence, as stated: "if high-scale inflation is discovered … then this would effectively rule out GUT-scale axions"; at present "$H_{\rm inf}\lesssim 6\times 10^{13}$ GeV" (arXiv:2303.02169) [src: Sect. 6.4.1, inline, text after Eq. 79].

The steps rest on Eq. 72, whose $g_{*s}$ assumption is "not a great approximation" (`formalism.md#misalignment-axions-and-axion-like-particles`), so Eq. 79 is an order-of-magnitude bound `[der]`. A 2026 analysis with Planck, ACT and SPT finds that the combined data improve the constraint on the isocurvature spectral index "moderately" "compared to Planck alone", "while the bounds on the isocurvature amplitudes for the fixed spectral indices we consider do not get tighter" (arXiv:2606.11312, Petretti et al. 2026) [src: abstract].

### Step 6 — the post-inflationary case: strings, then walls
<!-- key: step-6-the-post-inflationary-case-strings-then -->

The string network stores $\rho_{\rm s}\left(t\right)=\xi\frac{\mu_{\rm eff}}{t^{2}}\,,$ (arXiv:2007.04990) [src: Eq. 2], with ξ the string length per Hubble patch and an effective tension that at late times is "approximately equal to" "$\mu=\pi v^{2}\log(m_{r}/H)$" [src: Sect. 2.1, inline, text after Eq. 2]. Energy conservation gives an emission rate "$\Gamma\simeq\xi\mu_{\rm eff}/t^{3}\simeq 8\pi H^{3}f_{a}^{2}\xi\log(m_{r}/H)$" (arXiv:2007.04990) [src: Sect. 2.2, inline]. The axion number produced depends on how this energy is shared among momenta, through the instantaneous spectrum $F\sim 1/k^{q}$ between Hubble and the core scale: for a single power law on $k\in[x_{0}H,m_{r}/2]$, "$n_{a}=8\mu_{\rm eff}\xi H\nu(q)/x_{0}$ where the function $\nu(q)$ rapidly interpolates between $1-1/q$ for $q>1$ and $(H/m_{r})^{1-q}$ for $q<1$" (arXiv:2007.04990) [src: Sect. 2.2, inline]. Two inputs therefore have to be extrapolated from simulations to the physical value of $\log(m_r/H)$: ξ and q `[der]`.

- **Gorghetto, Hardy & Villadoro (2020).** ξ grows logarithmically, fitted by $\xi=c_{1}\log+c_{0}+\frac{c_{-1}}{\log}+\frac{c_{-2}}{\log^{2}}\,,$ (arXiv:2007.04990) [src: Eq. 3] with "$c_{1}=0.24(2)$", so that "A straight extrapolation to $\log=70$ would give $\xi=15(2)$", while "we cannot exclude the possibility that the observed growth of $\xi$ saturates at larger values of the log" [src: Sect. 2.1]; q grows linearly in the log and the data "strongly suggests that the spectrum becomes IR dominated ($q>1$) within one or two e-foldings beyond the simulation reach" [src: Sect. 2.2]. Requiring that the abundance "does not exceed the current observed dark matter value", with $\xi_{\star}=15$ (which "assumes that the linear-log behavior observed in simulations extends beyond the simulation range by another order of magnitude"), $x_{0}=10$, $q>2$, α = 8, $\log_{\star}=64$ they obtain, for N = 1, $m_{a}\gtrsim 0.5~{\rm meV}\,,\qquad f_{a}\lesssim 10^{10}~{\rm GeV}\,,$ (arXiv:2007.04990) [src: Eq. 6], and for N = 6 $m_{a}\gtrsim 3.5~{\rm meV}\,,\qquad f_{a}\lesssim 2\cdot 10^{9}~{\rm GeV}\,.$ [src: Eq. 7]; they "do not think that it would be fair to associate an error": "shifts of ${\cal O}(1)$ could be expected, but we would be surprised if these bounds relax by significantly more than a factor of two" (arXiv:2007.04990) [src: Sect. 4]. These are lower bounds: the extra contribution of strings and walls after $t_\star$ is "expect[ed] … to add further to the axion abundance", though the authors "cannot exclude a partial destructive interference" with the earlier spectrum, while "it would be highly unlikely that it could weaken the bounds … beyond an ${\cal O}(1)$ factor" [src: Sect. 4].
- **Buschmann et al. (2021), adaptive mesh refinement.** The fiducial fit gives "$q=1.02\pm 0.03$" (arXiv:2108.05368) [src: Fig. 3 caption]. Writing $\langle H/k\rangle^{-1}=\delta\sqrt{\xi}$, the number density at the QCD epoch is, "to leading order in $\log_{*}$, $n_{a}^{\rm string}\approx(8\pi f_{a}^{2}H/\delta)\sqrt{\xi_{*}}\log_{*}$"; for an exactly scale-invariant spectrum, q = 1, δ grows like the log and "$n_{a}^{\rm string}\approx(8\pi f_{a}^{2}H/\delta_{1})\sqrt{\xi_{*}}$" with "$\delta_{1}=6.2\pm 0.4$"; allowing for the 1σ range of q, "δ is in the range $\delta\in(106,870)$" (arXiv:2108.05368) [src: "Dark matter density" section, inline]. The result is the "(40,180) microelectronvolts" of `open-questions.md#the-axion-mass-in-the-post-inflationary-scenario`, obtained "Adding in the contribution from the QCD phase transition" (domain walls and misalignment), "assuming $q\in(0.98,1.06)$" and "a domain wall number of unity" [src: "Dark matter density" section].
- **Saikawa et al. (2024), static lattices to $11264^3$.** The spectral index "can be over/under estimated" when the string density is lower/higher than the attractor, "can be biased" by oscillations in the spectrum unless they are "properly handled", and is distorted by discretisation effects "which bias q toward larger values" and "blow up rapidly at large $\ell=\ln(m_{r}/H)$" [src: Sect. VII, inline, text before Eq. 7.1]; both a plateau model with q → 1 and a linear-increase model fit the data, and the difference between the two extrapolations is taken as the uncertainty of the prediction $95\,\mu\mathrm{eV}\lesssim m_{a}\lesssim 450\,\mu\mathrm{eV}.$ (arXiv:2401.17253) [src: Eq. 7.1], the main uncertainty being "the different scenario on the value of q at large ℓ" [src: Sect. VII]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Correia et al. (2026), minimal scaling model on $12\,288^3$ grids.** The simulations follow the strings into the QCD epoch, where they bound domain walls that "draw the strings together and annihilate them"; the comoving number density is "$9.48(23)f_{\rm a}^2 H_*$", which with "the most commonly quoted lattice results for the topological susceptibility" gives, "in this minimal model", "$17.46(84)\,μ\text{eV}$" (arXiv:2609.20801) [src: abstract]. They extrapolate within a scaling framework and state that the apparent scaling violations claimed by the other groups "cannot be distinguished from the evolution towards the same scaling fixed point from the low density initial conditions favoured by those groups" (arXiv:2609.20801) [src: Sect. VI].

### Step 7 — why the prediction is disputed
<!-- key: step-7-why-the-prediction-is-disputed -->

The disagreement is mainly about the network rather than misalignment `[der]`. As Di Luzio et al. put it in 2020, uncertainties "are primarily related to the serious difficulties that one encounters in trying to assess the contribution to the population of cold axions from the decay of topological defects", with additional ones from χ(T), and "differences between different estimates can easily be several times larger than the quoted errors"; their conservative range is "$m_{a}\approx\left(10-100\right){\rm\,\mu eV}$, and likely with an even larger upper value if $N_{\rm DW}>1$" (arXiv:2003.01100) [src: Sect. 3.7.1, inline]. The 2024–2026 papers isolate three points of contention `[der]`:

1. **Scaling or a growing ξ.** Gorghetto et al. extrapolate a logarithmic growth of ξ (arXiv:2007.04990, Sect. 2.1), and Saikawa et al. find "a scaling solution but with logarithmic (ℓ) corrections to the parameters" (arXiv:2401.17253) [src: Sect. I]; Correia et al. argue for scaling and attribute the growth to transients from low-density initial conditions (arXiv:2609.20801) [src: Sect. VI].
2. **The spectral index at large log.** For q > 1 the abundance "only has a very weak dependence on its precise value", for q < 1 it carries the factor $(H/m_{r})^{1-q}$ of ν(q) (arXiv:2007.04990, Sect. 2.2), and for q = 1 Buschmann et al. find δ ∝ log(m_r/H) if the spectrum "is exactly scale invariant at large k" (arXiv:2108.05368, "Dark matter density"); Saikawa et al. cannot separate a plateau at q ≈ 1 from continued growth (arXiv:2401.17253) [src: Sect. VII]. <!-- num: not a value -->
3. **Misalignment added on top.** Correia et al. state that "adding the number density produced by the misalignment mechanism is double-counting, as pointed out in Ref. [85]", because "There is no global misalignment angle: the phase of the field is a local quantity" and its misalignments are "already included in the numerical simulation" (arXiv:2609.20801) [src: Sect. VI]. Di Luzio et al., citing their Ref. [212], report that a factor-of-ten discrepancy of an earlier calculation "could be partially due to the explicit separation of the misalignment and string decay production mechanisms", "which leads to an over-counting of the axion energy density" (arXiv:2003.01100) [src: Sect. 3.7.1].

Correia et al. find consistent with their observations the estimate of their Ref. [51], Benabou et al. (arXiv:2412.08699), "that the string-wall collapse boosts the number density by a factor of about 5 relative to strings", while that mass estimate "is much greater than ours because of the extrapolation" (arXiv:2609.20801) [src: Sect. VI].

## Variants and what they cost
<!-- key: variants-and-what-they-cost -->

- **Power-law mass and Eq. 154 against lattice χ(T) and a numerical solution.** The analytic route (arXiv:2003.01100, Eqs. 165–170) hides order-one factors in b and in the factor 3 of Eq. 154 [src: Sect. 3.3, footnote 33]; the numerical route of arXiv:1606.07494 (Eq. S36) uses the lattice χ(T) and equation of state and is needed when $T_{\rm osc}$ falls near or below the QCD transition [src: Sect. S11].
- **Indirect against direct simulation of high-tension strings.** Static-lattice and AMR simulations of the physical PQ field reach $\log(m_r/H)\sim 8$–9 and extrapolate (arXiv:2007.04990; arXiv:2108.05368; arXiv:2401.17253); the alternative "simulate[s] high-tension strings directly by introducing extra field degrees of freedom", and its $m_{a}=26.2\pm 3.4\,\mu\mathrm{eV}$ "does not agree" with the indirect extrapolation, and "Understanding the origin of such a discrepancy should be a crucial step to improve the robustness of our indirect method" (arXiv:2401.17253) [src: Sect. VII, inline]. <!-- num: not a value -->
- **Kalb–Ramond string models.** Estimates of "125 μeV" (standing waves on long strings) or "160 μeV" (oscillating loops), with other parameter choices able to "bring the prediction in line with" the scaling result (arXiv:2609.20801) [src: Sect. VI], the underlying paper being their Ref. [54], not read.
- **Pre-inflationary fluctuations instead of misalignment.** If inflationary fluctuations rather than a mean displacement set the abundance, "a fluctuation-dominated abundance carries an isocurvature perturbation even when the mean displacement vanishes"; for a canonical minimally coupled axion it "exceeds the Planck isocurvature limit by about eight orders of magnitude", and for the QCD axion they "estimate that any single power-law spectrum blue enough to satisfy the pivot limit gives too little fluctuation abundance, and misalignment dominates" (arXiv:2610.36033, Lee, Verner & Yu 2026) [src: abstract].
- **Domain walls seeded during inflation.** When many vacua are populated at oscillation, axions from wall collapse "can dominate when the walls enter the scaling regime before annihilation", and "sizable isocurvature perturbations can therefore survive even after the walls annihilate" (arXiv:2608.13650, Kitajima et al. 2026) [src: abstract].

## What goes wrong
<!-- key: what-goes-wrong -->

- **Double counting misalignment and defects.** Correia et al. state that adding a misalignment term to a field simulation of the post-inflationary case is double-counting, the local phase being "already included in the numerical simulation" (arXiv:2609.20801) [src: Sect. VI]; Di Luzio et al. report an over-counting that "could be partially" behind one discrepancy (arXiv:2003.01100) [src: Sect. 3.7.1]. Check: in a simulation-based abundance, no separate misalignment term.
- **χ(T) index inconsistent with the simulations quoted.** Several string results used the instanton-liquid index "$2\gamma=6.68$", milder than the lattice "$2\gamma=8.2$", and "These results strongly depend on the choice of the temperature dependence of the QCD topological susceptibility" (arXiv:2003.01100) [src: Sect. 3.7.1, inline]. Check: quote the susceptibility used with every mass.
- **Spectral-index estimates biased by the simulation.** q "can be over/under estimated" when the string density is below or above the attractor, "can be biased" by spectral oscillations, and is pushed up by discretisation (arXiv:2401.17253) [src: Sect. VII].
- **Order-of-magnitude isocurvature bound used as precise.** Eq. 79 of arXiv:2303.02169 inherits the approximations of Eq. 72 (see Step 5); take current amplitude limits from a CMB analysis such as arXiv:2606.11312.
- **Initial velocity of the field.** Negligible unless Eq. 161 of arXiv:2003.01100 holds; kinetic-misalignment models are a separate case [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 161].
- **A printed slip in the lattice paper.** As printed, the friction term of Eq. S31 of arXiv:1606.07494 reads $3H(T)\theta\frac{d\theta}{dt}$, with a factor θ that does not appear in the temperature form of Eq. S36 [src: Eqs. S31, S36]; no erratum was looked for. Use Eq. S36.
- **Two different abstracts for one paper.** The arXiv listing of arXiv:0903.4377 (v2) gives "$m_a =67\pm2{\rm μeV}$" for the post-inflationary case [src: abstract, arXiv listing, inline], while the abstract printed in the rendered v2 body gives "$m_{a}=(85\pm 3){\rm~\mu eV}$" [src: abstract, rendered body]; the rendered body states that it includes in $\Omega_a$ "the contribution from axionic strings decay, Eq. (45)" [src: Sect. VI]. Quote the version and place.
