### The axion mass in the post-inflationary scenario
<!-- key: the-axion-mass-in-the-post-inflationary-scenario -->

- The question: if the PQ symmetry breaks after inflation there is "a unique mass that gives rise to the observed DM abundance", but "computing this mass accurately has been a challenge for four decades" (Buschmann et al., arXiv:2108.05368) [src: abstract].
- Many axions from strings: Gorghetto, Hardy and Villadoro find "clear evidence of scaling violations", whose "most conservative extrapolation" suggests "a large number of axions from strings", and a lower bound on the mass "substantially stronger than the naive one from misalignment" (arXiv:2007.04990) [src: abstract].
- Scale-invariant emission: with adaptive mesh refinement, strings radiate "with a scale-invariant spectrum, to within ∼5% precision", giving "(40,180) microelectronvolts" (arXiv:2108.05368) [src: abstract]; the same abstract describes earlier static-lattice predictions "spanning the range from 25 microelectronvolts to over 500 microelectronvolts".
- Larger static lattices: Saikawa et al. identify overlooked systematics (initial conditions, oscillations in the spectrum, discretisation), "some of which could explain the discrepancy in the literature", cannot tell whether the spectral index saturates, and find "$m_a \approx 95$-$450\,μ\mathrm{eV}$" under a simple power-law extrapolation (arXiv:2401.17253) [src: abstract, inline]. Kim, Park and Son independently confirm the logarithmic growth of the string number and of the spectral index (arXiv:2402.00741) [src: abstract, inline].
- 2026: a minimal scaling model in which domain walls pull the strings together gives "17.46(84) μeV", and its authors "argue that field theory simulations of non-minimal models where the strings have a Higgs condensate or gauge flux, and are annihilated by domain walls, will give axion mass predictions below about $40\,μ\text{eV}$" (Correia et al., arXiv:2609.20801) [src: abstract].
- `[der]` The disagreement is about the extrapolation of string dynamics from simulated to physical string tensions, and about the treatment of the QCD-era non-linearities and walls; the papers above do not yet agree on one range.
- What decides it: simulations with a larger dynamic range in the string tension (arXiv:2401.17253) [src: abstract]; a haloscope detection at a definite mass would test each prediction.

### The 3.5 keV line and decaying sterile neutrinos
<!-- key: the-3-5-kev-line-and-decaying-sterile-neutrinos -->

- Claim: candidate ∼3.5 keV X-ray lines are "consistent with the decay of a ∼7 keV sterile neutrino dark matter particle" (Abazajian review, arXiv:1705.01837) [src: abstract].
- Against: more than 30 Ms of XMM-Newton blank-sky observations find "no evidence of the 3.5-keV line emission from the Milky Way halo", with an upper limit on the decay rate "inconsistent with the possibility that the 3.5-keV line originates from dark matter decay" (Dessert, Rodd & Safdi 2020, arXiv:1812.06976) [src: abstract].
- Reply: Boyarsky et al. find these bounds "overestimated by more than an order of magnitude due to improper background modeling", so that "the dark matter interpretation of the 3.5 keV signal remains viable" (arXiv:2004.06601) [src: abstract].
- Status: unresolved between these papers; no later reply was read in this run. Measured X-ray limits: cosmoskill `cosmo-neutrinos-dark-matter`.

### Self-interacting dark matter and gravothermal collapse
<!-- key: self-interacting-dark-matter-and-gravothermal -->

- A 2026 lensing analysis "hints" at core collapse and favours cross-sections of $10^2$–$10^4$ cm² g⁻¹ at 10 km s⁻¹ (arXiv:2610.40766) [src: abstract]; such large, velocity-dependent cross-sections are the regime where collapse times computed for idealised NFW halos differ from those of halos taken from cosmological simulations "by up to a factor of a few" (arXiv:2610.28745) [src: abstract].
- What decides it: calibrated collapse times across Knudsen numbers (arXiv:2610.28745) and more lenses (arXiv:2610.40766); `[der]` the inference depends on the halo model as much as on the particle model.

### Stellar heating bounds on fuzzy dark matter
<!-- key: stellar-heating-bounds-on-fuzzy-dark-matter -->

- For: ultra-faint dwarf kinematics bound $m > 3 \times 10^{-19}$ eV, conservative against Schrödinger–Poisson simulations, and $m > 8 \times 10^{-18}$ eV if Ursa Major III/UNIONS I is a galaxy (arXiv:2509.02781) [src: abstract, inline].
- Caveat: the diffusion approximation behind earlier simulation-based bounds is "valid only when the de Broglie wavelength is much smaller than the galaxy's half-light radius"; at lower masses tidal heating dominates, and soliton mass and tidal stripping can suppress heating (arXiv:2604.26393) [src: abstract].
- What decides it: whether Ursa Major III/UNIONS I is a galaxy, and more precise sizes and velocity dispersions (arXiv:2509.02781) [src: abstract].

### Primordial black holes: the asteroid-mass window and fine-tuning
<!-- key: primordial-black-holes-the-asteroid-mass-window -->

- The window is quoted with different edges (arXiv:2006.02838) [src: abstract]; (arXiv:2606.12775) [src: abstract]; (arXiv:2510.00246) [src: abstract]: quote the edges with the paper and its assumptions.
- Fine-tuning: Profumo finds three "naturalness universality classes" and argues that the generic fine-tuning claim "conflates the worst case with a landscape spanning every naturalness tier", while single-field ultra-slow-roll collapse "is severely tuned" (arXiv:2606.12775) [src: abstract]; he reconciles the Barbieri–Giudice and Iovino–Riotto measures as answering "complementary questions" [src: abstract].
- What decides it: Hawking-radiation signatures at the low edge of the window, for example in the 21 cm signal (arXiv:2510.00246) [src: abstract], and the gravitational-wave background of the production mechanism (arXiv:2604.27496) [src: abstract].

## Unresolved calculations
<!-- key: unresolved-calculations -->

- Bound states and Sommerfeld enhancement in freeze-out: full treatments are "technically intricate, requiring careful treatment or the use of specialized tools"; a 2026 semi-analytic prescription aims to bring them into standard codes (arXiv:2610.03718) [src: abstract].
- Non-thermal momentum distributions: freeze-in at stronger coupling (arXiv:2606.20815) [src: abstract] and Shi–Fuller production (arXiv:2507.18752) [src: abstract] need their own mapping onto the warm-dark-matter benchmark.
- Gravitational production depends on the reheating equation of state (arXiv:2602.10215) [src: abstract] and on the initial state for spin-1 (arXiv:2603.03430) [src: abstract].
- Post-inflationary axion mass: see above.

## Forecasts and the next data
<!-- key: forecasts-and-the-next-data -->

- Higgsino and wino WIMPs: Safdi's lectures state that "the next generation of indirect detection searches will discover or rule out this particle" (arXiv:2303.02169) [src: Sect. 1.1] — a **forecast**.
- Shi–Fuller sterile neutrinos and sub-GeV thermal relics: electron-recoil experiments "are beginning to test" thermal targets (arXiv:2603.03444) [src: abstract]; the Lyman-α free-streaming bound is "limited by the availability of high-quality quasar spectra" (arXiv:2309.04533) [src: abstract].
