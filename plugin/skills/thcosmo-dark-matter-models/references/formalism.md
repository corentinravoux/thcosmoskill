# Formalism of dark-matter models

Scope: definitions and governing equations of dark-matter production (freeze-out, freeze-in, misalignment) and of the linear signatures of non-cold candidates.

Snapshot: 2026-11-03

## Conventions
<!-- key: conventions -->

The skill draws its production formalism from the TASI lectures of Safdi (arXiv:2303.02169), the review of Arbey and Mahmoudi (arXiv:2104.11488), the freeze-in review of Bernal et al. (arXiv:1706.07442) and the axion review of Marsh (arXiv:1510.07633). Arbey and Mahmoudi set $\hbar=c=k_B=1$; Marsh "(mostly)" uses the same units (arXiv:1510.07633) [src: Notes]; Safdi and Bernal et al. print no units statement. The hub's atlas (`theoretical-cosmology/references/conventions.md`, sections "Units, Planck mass and where 8πG appears" and "Initial conditions: adiabatic or isocurvature") collects the choices of other works. <!-- eq: notation -->

## Thermal freeze-out
<!-- key: thermal-freeze-out -->
<!-- sources: Safdi arXiv:2303.02169 Sect. 4.1 -->

- Boltzmann equation for the number density of a particle χ annihilating with its antiparticle, with the inverse process restored by detailed balance: $\begin{split}{dn_{\chi}\over dt}+3Hn_{\chi}=-\langle\sigma v\rangle(n_{\chi}^{2}-n_{\rm eq}^{2})\,,\end{split}$ (Safdi 2023, arXiv:2303.02169) [src: Eq. 16]. Safdi notes that "the best way to compute how $n_{\chi}$ freezes out is to numerically evolve the non-linear differential equation" [src: Sect. 4.1, text after Eq. 16].
- Freeze-out happens when $\langle\sigma v\rangle n_{\rm eq}\lesssim H$, which the lectures show occurs for $x_{\rm fo}\sim 10$, "roughly independent of $m_{\chi}$ and $\langle\sigma v\rangle$" (arXiv:2303.02169) [src: Sect. 4.1, text after Eq. 16].
- Parametric estimate: $\begin{split}\langle\sigma v\rangle\sim{T_{\rm fo}^{2}\over m_{\rm pl}n_{\rm eq}^{\rm fo}}\sim{x_{\rm fo}\over m_{\rm pl}T_{\rm MRE}}\,.\end{split}$ (arXiv:2303.02169) [src: Eq. 19], which is independent of $m_{\chi}$ "at least to the extent that $x_{\rm fo}$ is independent of $m_{\chi}$" [src: Sect. 4.1, text after Eq. 19]. The estimate approximates the two $g_{*s}$ factors of Eq. 18 as equal, although for a TeV relic $g_{*s}$ may be "around 10 - 30 times larger" at freeze-out than at equality [src: Sect. 4.1, text after Eq. 18].
- Freeze-out condition solved for $T_{\rm fo}$: $\begin{split}{T_{\rm fo}^{2}\over m_{\rm pl}}\sim\langle\sigma v\rangle n_{\rm eq}^{\rm fo}\sim\langle\sigma v\rangle\left({m_{\chi}T_{\rm fo}\over 2\pi}\right)^{3/2}e^{-m_{\chi}/T_{\rm fo}}\,,\end{split}$ with spin degrees of freedom left out (arXiv:2303.02169) [src: Eq. 20].
- Velocity dependence: for $v\ll 1$ the lectures write $\sigma v\sim v^{2L}$, with L the orbital angular momentum of the initial state; at freeze-out $v\sim 1/\sqrt{x_{\rm fo}}\sim 0.3$, against $v\sim 10^{-3}$ in the Milky Way today, so p-wave rates are "tens of thousands of times weaker today, at best" (arXiv:2303.02169) [src: Sect. 4.1.1, inline].
- Precise solution for a generic WIMP: the required cross-section "varies significantly with mass at masses below 10 GeV, reaching a maximum of 5.2*10^-26 cm^3s^-1 at masses around 0.3 GeV, and is 2.2*10^-26 cm^3s^-1 with feeble mass-dependence for masses above 10 GeV" (Steigman, Dasgupta & Beacom 2012, arXiv:1204.3622) [src: abstract].
- Generalisation to an arbitrary freeze-out process, with an analytic estimate of the abundance that says "when instantaneous freezeout is appropriate and how it can be corrected when freezeout is slow", covering WIMPs, SIMPs, coannihilation, coscattering, inverse decays and forbidden channels (Frumkin et al. 2022, arXiv:2207.01635) [src: abstract].
- Freeze-out inside a sector decoupled from the Standard-Model plasma: the required cross-section differs from the canonical value by an amount that is "generally sizeable, and can reach orders of magnitude" (Bringmann et al. 2020, arXiv:2007.03696) [src: abstract].

## Freeze-in
<!-- key: freeze-in -->
<!-- sources: Bernal et al. arXiv:1706.07442 Sect. 3.3; Hall et al. arXiv:0911.1120 -->

- Definition: a Feebly Interacting Massive Particle (FIMP) interacts "so feebly with the thermal bath that it never attains thermal equilibrium"; its "freeze-in yield is IR dominated by low temperatures near the FIMP mass and is independent of unknown UV physics, such as the reheat temperature after inflation" (Hall et al. 2009, arXiv:0911.1120) [src: abstract].
- Couplings: freeze-out cannot happen if the coupling to the visible sector is "typically $y\simeq\mathcal{O}(10^{-7})$ or less" (Bernal et al. 2017, arXiv:1706.07442) [src: Sect. 3.3, inline].
- Production by decays of a bath particle σ → χχ, back-reaction neglected, Maxwell–Boltzmann statistics for σ: $\frac{{\rm d}n_{\chi}}{{\rm d}t}+3\,H\,n_{\chi}=2\,\Gamma_{\sigma\to\chi\chi}\frac{K_{1}(m_{\sigma}/T)}{K_{2}(m_{\sigma}/T)}n_{\sigma}^{\rm eq}\,,$ (arXiv:1706.07442) [src: Eq. 7], with $K_{j}$ modified Bessel functions of the second kind [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 7].
- With $Y\equiv n_{\chi}/s$, $x\equiv m_{\sigma}/T$ and constant relativistic degrees of freedom, $\frac{x}{Y_{\sigma}^{\rm eq}}\frac{\mathrm{d}Y}{\mathrm{d}x}=2\,\frac{\Gamma_{\sigma\to\chi\chi}}{H}\frac{K_{1}(x)}{K_{2}(x)}.$ (arXiv:1706.07442) [src: Eq. 8].
- Approximate solution, evaluated around $T\simeq m_{\sigma}$: $\Omega_{\rm\chi}h^{2}\simeq 4.48\times 10^{8}\frac{g_{\sigma}}{g_{*s}\sqrt{g_{*}}}\frac{m_{\rm\chi}}{\rm GeV}\frac{M_{\rm P}\,\Gamma_{\sigma\to\chi\chi}}{m_{\rm\sigma}^{2}}\,,$ (arXiv:1706.07442, after Hall et al.) [src: Eq. 9].
- Coupling needed, for $g_{*s}\simeq g_{*}$ and $\Gamma\simeq y^{2}m_{\sigma}/(8\pi)$: $y\simeq 10^{-12}\left(\frac{\Omega_{\rm\chi}h^{2}}{0.12}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{g_{*}}{100}\right)^{3/4}\left(\frac{m_{\sigma}}{m_{\rm\chi}}\right)^{1/2}.$ (arXiv:1706.07442) [src: Eq. 10]; if the initial abundance was not negligible, Eq. 10 "should be taken as an upper bound for $y$" [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 10].
- Production by $2\to 2$ scattering through the operator $\zeta\chi\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{3}$, bath masses negligible: $\Omega_{\rm\chi}h^{2}\simeq 1.01\times 10^{24}\frac{\zeta^{2}}{g_{*s}\sqrt{g_{*}}}\,,$ (arXiv:1706.07442) [src: Eq. 11].
- Timing: freeze-out produces the abundance at $m_{\chi}/T\simeq 10\dots 30$, freeze-in "during the epoch $m/T\simeq 2\dots 5$", with m the relevant mass scale of the process (arXiv:1706.07442) [src: Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 12].
- IR character: $\frac{n_{\chi}}{T^{3}}\simeq t\,\Gamma_{\sigma\to\chi\chi}\simeq y^{2}\frac{M_{\rm P}}{m_{\sigma}},$ (arXiv:1706.07442) [src: Eq. 13]; "the standard freeze-in involving only renormalizable operators is almost entirely an IR process" [src: Sect. 3.3, text before Eq. 13].
- `[der]` Opposite monotonicity: in freeze-in a larger coupling gives a larger abundance, in freeze-out a smaller one (arXiv:1706.07442, Sect. 3.3, text after Eq. 10, and Fig. 3).
- Ultraviolet freeze-in: when the hidden and visible sectors are coupled "only via non-renormalisable operators", which "is arguably a more generic realisation" since it needs no diminutive renormalisable couplings (Elahi, Kolda & Unwin 2014, arXiv:1410.6157) [src: abstract].

## Production in non-standard cosmological histories
<!-- key: production-in-non-standard-cosmological -->
<!-- sources: Arbey & Mahmoudi arXiv:2104.11488 Sect. 8 -->

- The relic abundance depends on the expansion rate at production, which is not measured before BBN: freeze-out at "a temperature around $M_{\chi}/10$, typically about 10-100 GeV for standard thermal relics" corresponds to $a/a_{0}\sim 10^{-15}-10^{-14}$, and "at this epoch we have no constraint on the expansion rate" (Arbey & Mahmoudi 2021, arXiv:2104.11488) [src: Sect. 8.1, inline].
- Kination-dominated era of a quintessence field: $H^{2}=\frac{8\pi G}{3}(\rho_{rad}+\rho_{\phi})\,,$ (arXiv:2104.11488) [src: Eq. 78] with $\frac{d\rho_{\phi}}{dt}=-3H(\rho_{\phi}+P_{\phi})\,,$ [src: Eq. 79]. A faster expansion makes freeze-out occur earlier and increases the relic density [src: Sect. 8.1]; in their pMSSM example a higgsino with $\Omega h^{2}=5.6\times 10^{-3}$ in the standard history can be raised by "two orders of magnitude" without disrupting BBN, but standard-history densities "smaller than $3\times 10^{-4}$" cannot be raised enough [src: Sect. 8.1]. Their conclusion: the limits on the relic density of Sect. 6.2 "cannot be used to constrain particle physics scenarios, because the cosmological properties of the early Universe may be different" [src: Sect. 8.1].
- A 2026 example: in a kination epoch "the enhanced expansion rate causes earlier freeze-out and increases the relic abundance", opening scotogenic scalar dark-matter masses "from approximately $200~\mathrm{GeV}$ to $1~\mathrm{TeV}$" (Chun et al. 2026, arXiv:2611.00930) [src: abstract].
- Arbey and Mahmoudi also treat a scalar field decaying in the early Universe, with no assumption on its nature: "it may be an inflaton, a scalar field participating in a phase transition, in leptogenesis or baryogenesis" (arXiv:2104.11488) [src: Sect. 8.2]. For freeze-in during a non-adiabatic matter-dominated reheating "DM comoving yield is inversely proportional to Hubble", so the coupling must be enhanced (arXiv:2610.29543) [src: abstract].

## Misalignment: axions and axion-like particles
<!-- key: misalignment-axions-and-axion-like-particles -->
<!-- sources: Safdi arXiv:2303.02169 Sect. 6.4; Marsh arXiv:1510.07633 -->

Safdi writes the scale factor R and the axion field a; Marsh's review (arXiv:1510.07633) covers background evolution, misalignment for the QCD axion and ALPs, and perturbations, including the axion sound speed, the Jeans scale and transfer functions [src: abstract].

- Classical-field description: with local $v\sim 10^{-3}$ and $\rho_{\rm DM}\sim 0.4$ GeV/cm³ the occupation number is ${\mathcal{N}}\sim 10^{27}(10^{-6}\,{\rm eV}/m_{a})^{3}$, and quantum corrections are suppressed by $\sim 1/\sqrt{\mathcal{N}}$ (arXiv:2303.02169) [src: Sect. 6.4, inline].
- Field equation: $\begin{split}\ddot{a}+3H\dot{a}-{1\over R^{2}}\nabla^{2}a+{\partial V(a,T)\over\partial a}=0\,,\end{split}$ (arXiv:2303.02169) [src: Eq. 60], with spatial derivatives in comoving coordinates.
- Temperature-dependent QCD axion mass, "a reasonable approximation" away from $T\sim\Lambda$: $\begin{split}m_{a}(T)^{2}={\rm min}\left[{\alpha_{a}\Lambda^{4}\over f_{a}^{2}(T/\Lambda)^{n}},m_{a}^{2}\right]\,,\end{split}$ (arXiv:2303.02169) [src: Eq. 61], with Λ = 400 MeV and $n\approx 8.2$ from the lattice reference the lectures cite [src: Sect. 6.4, text after Eq. 61]. For ALPs the mass "can usually" be taken temperature-independent [src: Sect. 6.4, text after Eq. 60].
- Two cosmologies: Peccei–Quinn (PQ) symmetry restored after inflation (strings, patch-to-patch initial angles, numerical solution of the full non-linear equations) or not restored (one homogeneous initial misalignment angle $\theta_{i}$) (arXiv:2303.02169) [src: Sect. 6.4].
- PQ broken before inflation, linearised for $|\theta_{i}|\ll\pi$: $\begin{split}\ddot{a}+3H\dot{a}+m_{a}(T)^{2}a=0\,,\end{split}$ (arXiv:2303.02169) [src: Eq. 62]; the field is frozen until $m_{a}\gtrsim 3H$ [src: Sect. 6.4.1]. For constant mass in radiation domination, $\begin{split}{a(t)\over f_{a}}=\theta_{i}\left({2\over m_{a}t}\right)^{1/4}\Gamma\left({5\over 4}\right)J_{1/4}(m_{a}t)\,,\end{split}$ [src: Eq. 63], whose energy density scales as $T^{3}$, like non-relativistic matter, on scales larger than the de Broglie wavelength [src: Sect. 6.4.1, text after Eq. 64].
- Constant-mass relic abundance, order-unity factors dropped: $\begin{split}\Omega_{a}h^{2}\sim 0.1\theta_{i}^{2}{f_{a}^{2}\over T_{\rm MR}m_{\rm pl}}\sqrt{{m_{a}\over m_{\rm pl}}}\sim 0.1\theta_{i}^{2}{f_{a}\over 10^{17}\,\,{\rm GeV}}\sqrt{{m_{a}\over 10^{-22}\,\,{\rm eV}}}\,.\end{split}$ (arXiv:2303.02169) [src: Eq. 69].
- QCD axion, with $m_{a}f_{a}\approx 75$ MeV and oscillation above Λ: $\begin{split}T_{\rm osc}\sim\left(m_{a}m_{\rm pl}\Lambda^{n}\right)^{1/(n+2)}\sim{\rm GeV}\left({10^{12}\,\,{\rm GeV}\over f_{a}}\right)^{0.1}\,,\end{split}$ (arXiv:2303.02169) [src: Eq. 71] and $\begin{split}\Omega_{a}h^{2}\sim 0.1\theta_{i}^{2}\left({f_{a}\over 3\times 10^{11}\,\,{\rm GeV}}\right)^{1+{1\over n+2}}\sim 0.1\theta_{i}^{2}\left({f_{a}\over 3\times 10^{11}\,\,{\rm GeV}}\right)^{1.1}\,.\end{split}$ [src: Eq. 72], which assumes equal $g_{*s}$ at oscillation and equality, "not a great approximation but which is good enough for an order of magnitude estimate"; the correct abundance follows for θ_i of order unity and "an axion mass around 10 to 20 μeV" [src: Sect. 6.4.1, text after Eq. 72]. The form of Eq. 61 is not appropriate for $f_{a}\gtrsim 10^{16}$ GeV, where $T_{\rm osc}\lesssim\Lambda$ [src: Sect. 6.4.1, text after Eq. 71].
- Isocurvature: if PQ is broken before or during inflation, the axion fluctuations become isocurvature perturbations, uncorrelated with the curvature perturbation, and are "strongly constrained by CMB data"; they are also non-Gaussian, though "for GUT scale axions the constraint from the isocurvature two-point function is currently stronger" (arXiv:2303.02169) [src: Sect. 6.4.1, "Cosmological considerations for PQ broken before inflation"].
- PQ restored after inflation: the complex PQ field obeys $\begin{split}\ddot{\Phi}+3H\dot{\Phi}-{\nabla^{2}\over R^{2}}\Phi+{m_{r}^{2}\over v_{a}^{2}}\Phi\left(|\Phi|^{2}-{v_{a}^{2}\over 2}\right)=0\,,\end{split}$ (arXiv:2303.02169) [src: Eq. 80], with $m_{r}=\sqrt{2\lambda}v_{a}$; axion strings "are currently thought to be the dominant source of axion DM" in this case, and with domain-wall number N > 1 the walls do not collapse quickly [src: Sect. 6.4.2].

## Ultralight (fuzzy, wave) dark matter: linear theory
<!-- key: ultralight-fuzzy-wave-dark-matter-linear-theory -->
<!-- sources: Hu, Barkana & Gruzinov arXiv:astro-ph/0003365; Eberhardt & Ferreira arXiv:2507.00705 -->

Non-linear wave dynamics (solitons, interference granules, Schrödinger–Poisson simulations) belongs to `thcosmo-nonlinear-structure`; here only the field equations and the linear cut-off.

- Field and non-relativistic wavefunction, with $\hbar=c=1$: $\Box\phi=m^{2}\phi\,,$ (Hu, Barkana & Gruzinov 2000, arXiv:astro-ph/0003365) [src: Eq. 1] and, for $\phi=A\cos(mt-\alpha)$ and $\psi\equiv Ae^{i\alpha}$, $i(\partial_{t}+{3\over 2}{\dot{a}\over a})\psi=(-{1\over 2m}\nabla^{2}+m\Psi)\psi\,,$ [src: Eq. 2].
- In the generalised-dark-matter fluid mapping the field has an effective sound speed $c_{\rm eff}^{2}=k^{2}/4a^{2}m^{2}$, k comoving; the comoving Jeans wavenumber is "constant during radiation domination" and $\propto a^{1/4}$ during matter domination, with $k_{J\rm eq}=9\,m_{22}^{1/2}\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$ at equality (arXiv:astro-ph/0003365) [src: inline, text before Eq. 8].
- Linear suppression found numerically: $P_{\rm FCDM}(k)=T_{\rm F}^{2}(k)P_{\rm CDM}(k)\,,\,\,\,T_{\rm F}(k)\approx{\cos x^{3}\over 1+x^{8}}\,,$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0003365) [src: Eq. 8], with $x=1.61\,m_{22}^{1/18}k/k_{J{\rm eq}}$ [src: inline, text after Eq. 8].
- As printed, the 2025 review of Eberhardt and Ferreira writes the same fit as $P_{\mathrm{ULDM}}(k)=T_{\mathrm{ULDM}}^{2}(k)P_{\mathrm{CDM}}(k)\,,\qquad T_{\mathrm{ULDM}}^{2}(k)\approx\frac{\cos(x^{3})}{1+x^{8}}\,,$ (arXiv:2507.00705, v1) [src: Eq. 78] with $x=1.61m_{22}^{-1/18}k/k_{J}$ [src: Sect. V.2, inline, text after Eq. 78]: the fit is assigned to $T^{2}$ rather than to T, and the exponent of $m_{22}$ carries the opposite sign to Hu et al.'s Eq. 8. Quote the fit from Hu et al., not from this display.
- Jeans length, as the review prints it: $r_{J}=\pi^{3/4}(G\delta\rho)^{-1/4}m^{-1/2}$ (arXiv:2507.00705) [src: Eq. 76] and $\approx 50\left(\frac{m}{10^{-22}\,\mathrm{eV}}\right)^{-1/2}\left(\frac{\rho}{\rho_{b}}\right)^{-1/4}\left(\Omega_{m}h^{2}\right)^{-1/4}\,,$ [src: Eq. 77], the two rows of one display; no length unit is printed with the 50, and the background density in the text after Eq. 77 is printed with units "M⊙ kpc³".
- As printed, the text before Eq. 76 of arXiv:2507.00705 gives the growth rate with the quantum term added, $\gamma^{2}=4\pi G\delta\rho_{k}+(k^{2}/2m)^{2}$ [src: Sect. V.2, inline, text before Eq. 76], although the same paragraph says the quantum pressure "resists the collapse of small scales"; `[der]` a term that resists collapse lowers the growth rate, so the sign as printed is a slip. Use the effective sound speed of Hu et al. above.
- Boltzmann codes for the linear spectrum named by the review: axionCAMB, its successor axionECAMB, and AxiCLASS (arXiv:2507.00705) [src: Sect. V.2].

## Linear signatures: free streaming and the warm cut-off
<!-- key: linear-signatures-free-streaming-and-the-warm -->
<!-- sources: Viel et al. arXiv:astro-ph/0501562 Sect. II -->

- Temperature of an early-decoupled thermal relic: ${T_{x}\over T_{\nu}}=\left({10.75\over g_{*}(T_{D})}\right)^{1/3}<1~,$ (Viel et al. 2005, arXiv:astro-ph/0501562) [src: Eq. 1]; density $\omega_{x}=\Omega_{x}h^{2}=\beta\left({m_{x}\over 94\,\mathrm{eV}}\right),$ [src: Eq. 2], with $\beta=(T_{x}/T_{\nu})^{3}$ for thermal relics and $\beta=\chi$ for sterile neutrinos made by oscillations [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 2].
- Free-streaming wavenumber, "roughly": $k_{\rm FS}={2\pi\over\lambda_{\rm FS}}\sim 5\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}\left({m_{x}\over 1\,\mathrm{keV}}\right)\left({T_{\nu}\over T_{x}}\right)~,$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0501562) [src: Eq. 3]; the authors note "there exist different definitions of the free-streaming or damping scale" [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 3].
- Transfer function defined against ΛCDM at fixed cosmological parameters: $T(k)=[P(k)_{\Lambda\mathrm{WDM}}/P(k)_{\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}}]^{1/2}$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0501562) [src: Eq. 4].
- Fit: $T(k)=[1+(\alpha k)^{2\nu}]^{-5/\nu}$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0501562) [src: Eq. 6], with ν = 1.12 "the best fit for k<5 h Mpc⁻¹" [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 6], and break scale $\alpha=0.24\left({m_{x}/T_{x}\over 1\,\mathrm{keV}/T_{\nu}}\right)^{-0.83}\left({\omega_{x}\over 0.25(0.7)^{2}}\right)^{-0.16}\mathrm{Mpc}$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0501562) [src: Eq. 7].
- Thermal-relic to non-resonant sterile-neutrino mass map (equal $\omega_{x}$ and $m_{x}/T_{x}$): $m_{\mathrm{sterile\,\nu}}=4.43\,\mathrm{keV}\left({m_{\mathrm{thermal}}\over 1\,\mathrm{keV}}\right)^{4/3}\left({0.25(0.7)^{2}\over\omega_{x}}\right)^{1/3}.$ (arXiv:astro-ph/0501562) [src: Eq. 5]. It holds for sterile neutrinos that "share the same phase–space distribution" as active neutrinos with an overall suppression [src: Sect. II, text after Eq. 1]; resonant (Shi–Fuller) production gives non-thermal distributions that need their own mapping, for which a 2025 paper gives "a refined $m_{\mathrm{th}}$ fitting function" (Vogel et al., arXiv:2507.18752) [src: abstract]; freeze-in at stronger coupling also gives distributions "not captured by the common $αβγ$-parametrization" (arXiv:2606.20815) [src: abstract].
- Shape is not universal: the transfer functions of ultralight, warm and interacting dark matter "are all slightly different therefore there is no way to make a one-to-one correspondence between the diffrent microphysics that works for all observations" (Eberhardt & Ferreira, arXiv:2507.00705) [src: Sect. V.2, Fig. 4 caption].

## Linear signatures: interactions with baryons and dark radiation
<!-- key: linear-signatures-interactions-with-baryons-and -->
<!-- sources: Boddy & Gluscevic arXiv:1801.08609 Sect. III.1; ETHOS arXiv:1512.05344; atomic DM arXiv:1209.5752 -->

- Dark matter–baryon scattering adds a drag term to the fluid equations. In the synchronous gauge, conformal time, the four rows of one display read $\dot{\delta}_{\chi}=-\theta_{\chi}-\frac{\dot{h}}{2}$, $\dot{\theta}_{\chi}=-\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\theta_{\chi}+c_{\chi}^{2}k^{2}\delta_{\chi}+R_{\chi}(\theta_{b}-\theta_{\chi})$, $\dot{\delta}_{b}=-\theta_{b}-\frac{\dot{h}}{2}$, $\dot{\theta}_{b}=-\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\theta_{b}+c_{b}^{2}k^{2}\delta_{b}+R_{\gamma}(\theta_{\gamma}-\theta_{b})+\frac{\rho_{\chi}}{\rho_{b}}R_{\chi}(\theta_{\chi}-\theta_{b})$ (Boddy & Gluscevic 2018, arXiv:1801.08609) [src: Eq. 14], with $R_{\chi}$ the momentum-transfer rate coefficient from dark matter–baryon scattering [src: Sect. III.1, text after Eq. 14].
- The momentum change per collision is $|\Delta\vec{p}_{\chi}|=\frac{m_{\chi}m_{B}}{m_{\chi}+m_{B}}|\vec{v}_{\chi}-\vec{v}_{B}|\left(\hat{n}-\frac{\vec{v}_{\chi}-\vec{v}_{B}}{|\vec{v}_{\chi}-\vec{v}_{B}|}\right)\ ,$ (arXiv:1801.08609) [src: Eq. 15]. As printed, the Maxwellian distributions of Eqs. 16–17 are normalised with $(2\pi)^{3/2}\bar{v}^{3/2}$ in the denominator, while the text defines $\bar{v}^{2}$ as the velocity dispersion [src: Sect. III.1, text after Eq. 17]. `[der]` A three-dimensional Maxwellian is normalised by the cube of the dispersion's square root, so the printed exponent is a slip of the display.
- Velocity dependence: interactions with "a stronger dependence on velocity" keep dark matter and baryons coupled longer but are suppressed at the low temperatures relevant for Planck and are "thus less constrained", while being "more constrained in the limit of small dark matter mass" (arXiv:1801.08609) [src: abstract].
- Dark matter–dark radiation interactions (ETHOS): microphysical models are mapped onto "physical effective parameters that shape the linear matter power spectrum and the self-interaction transfer cross section"; models with similar effective parameters "would nevertheless result in similar dark matter distributions" (Cyr-Racine et al. 2016, arXiv:1512.05344) [src: abstract].
- Dark acoustic oscillations: atomic dark matter coupled to dark photons until late imprints "a new length scale, the Dark-Acoustic-Oscillation (DAO) scale, on the matter density field" (arXiv:1209.5752) [src: abstract].

## Gravitational and other non-thermal production
<!-- key: gravitational-and-other-non-thermal-production -->
<!-- sources: Kolb & Long arXiv:2312.09042; Chung, Kolb & Riotto arXiv:hep-ph/9802238; Graham, Mardon & Rajendran arXiv:1504.02102 -->

- Definition: cosmological gravitational particle production (CGPP) "is the creation of particles in an expanding universe due solely to their gravitational interaction", a consequence of quantum fields in a time-dependent background; Kolb and Long catalogue spectra and abundances "of gravitationally produced particles of various spins" (Kolb & Long 2023, arXiv:2312.09042) [src: abstract]. For the scalar field of their Sect. III, the mode functions are related by Bogoliubov transformations whose coefficients satisfy $|\alpha_{k}|^{2}-|\beta_{k}|^{2}=1$ [src: Sect. III.2, inline, text after Eq. 17]; the relation is the bosonic one.
- Superheavy dark matter: in large-field inflation, stable particles "whose mass is of the order of the inflaton mass (presumably around $10^{13}$GeV)" are produced at the end of inflation in sufficient abundance "quite independently of any details of the non-gravitational interactions" (Chung, Kolb & Riotto 1998, arXiv:hep-ph/9802238) [src: abstract].
- Vector dark matter from inflationary fluctuations: the longitudinal mode of a massive vector is produced "with a power spectrum peaked at intermediate wavelengths", which suppresses long-wavelength isocurvature, and the abundance "can be calculated precisely from the mass and the Hubble scale of inflation"; saturating the dark-matter density gives "m = 10^-5 eV (10^14 GeV/H_I)^4" (Graham, Mardon & Rajendran 2016, arXiv:1504.02102) [src: abstract].
- Dependence on reheating: gravitational production of decoupled scalars "is efficient and can lead to over-production"; for an inflaton potential $V_{\rm inf}\propto φ^k$ an extended reheating phase dilutes the relics for k < 4, while for k > 4 the abundance "is enhanced during the reheating phase" (Costa & Kim 2026, arXiv:2602.10215) [src: abstract, inline].
- Dependence on the initial state: for particles whose only source of conformal-symmetry breaking is a mass term (spin-1/2 fermions and conformally coupled scalars), non-Bunch–Davies initial conditions have "little impact on the mass range relevant to dark matter", while the longitudinal mode of a spin-1 field shows "a large deviation from the standard computation" (Bertuzzo, Salla & Tesi 2026, arXiv:2603.03430) [src: abstract].
- Gravitational production as a background to other mechanisms: it "mars" the predictivity of non-thermal models unless a low reheating temperature dilutes it (arXiv:2306.13061) [src: abstract].
- Production by evaporating primordial black holes: stable dark-sector particles emitted by Hawking evaporation inherit isocurvature if primordial non-Gaussianity couples long and short modes (Franciolini & Racco 2026, arXiv:2603.02322) [src: abstract].
